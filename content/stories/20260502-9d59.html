#为什么我们会怪自己？


心理学中有个概念叫 “公正世界假设”——我们的大脑不愿意相信坏事会随机发生在好人身上。于是你心里会出现一个声音：“如果是我哪里没做好，那我改掉就好了，以后就不会再发生了。”——这个想法把责任放错了地方。


很多人在被骚扰的几秒钟内，身体僵住、说不出话、动不了——那不是你软弱，是你的大脑在保护你。

“

“Sexual harassment is about one person only—the one who failed to control their behavior. It has nothing to do with what you wore, what you said, or how quickly you reacted.”

”


#Why do we blame ourselves?


Psychology describes a concept known as the “Just-World Hypothesis.” People are inclined to believe that the world is fair, and that bad things do not happen randomly to good people. As a result, survivors may think:

“If I had done something differently, this wouldn’t have happened.”


This is a misplaced attribution of responsibility.

Many individuals experience a freeze response—being unable to speak or move in the moment. This is not weakness; it is a neurobiological survival response.

三个小建议，帮你卸下羞耻感


换主语：不再说“我当时为什么不……”，改成“他当时为什么没有经过我的同意就……”——主语一换，心里的自责就会松动。


找一个人说出来：羞耻最怕见光。找朋友、家人、心理咨询师，安安静静听你说完就行。你不用一个人扛着。


每天跟自己默念：“被骚扰不是我的错。害怕、发懵、没反应过来，都是正常的。错的是他，不是我。”


Three practical steps to release shame


Shift the subject of responsibility

Replace “Why didn’t I…” with
→ “Why did they act without my consent?”
This reframing helps reduce self-blame.


Speak to someone you trust
Shame thrives in silence. Sharing your experience with a trusted friend, family member, or therapist can be deeply relieving.


Use self-affirmation
Remind yourself daily:
“This was not my fault. My reactions—fear, freezing, confusion—were normal. The responsibility lies entirely with the perpetrator.”

场景一：公共场合 + 陌生人 → 直接开撕，大声指认


不要讲礼貌，不要委婉。你的第一句话是报警级别的公开指认：


“他在性骚扰！我不认识他！这里有人在性骚扰，请大家帮忙拍下来，我现在就要报警！”


 核心 把对方从偷偷摸摸的黑暗里拖到阳光下，围观人群会成为你的保护。


场景二：饭局/酒局 + 熟人 → 当场定性，戳破“玩笑”外衣


对方用氛围、酒局做掩护，赌你不好意思说破。你要做的是当场捅破窗户纸：


“你是在对我开黄腔吗？请注意，你的话已经是性骚扰了。”


“刚刚你的行为属于性骚扰，请自重。”


  • 对方说“开个玩笑，你怎么那么敏感” → “你认为性骚扰是开玩笑？我不认为。”

  • 对方说“你想多了” → “你不这样做我就不会这样想，如果不想被误会，请注意自己的行为。”

  • 对方打哈哈转移话题 → 收住，但已经表明你不好惹。如果不收敛，直接硬刚：“我有录音的习惯，再这样我立马报警。”


“一个连基本尊重都给不到你的人，不可能给你任何好处。止损才是明智。”


场景三：私密空间 + 一对一 → 第一优先级：离开，建立外部链接

  • 移动到出口或公共区域：“告辞，我朋友已经过来找我了。”

Scenario 1: Public Space + Stranger


Respond loudly and clearly. Public exposure increases safety.


“This person is sexually harassing me! I do not know them! Please help document this—I am calling the police!”


 Core principle Bring the behavior into public visibility.


Scenario 2: Social Gatherings + Acquaintances


Name the behavior directly; reject the ‘joke’ framing.


 “Are you making a sexual remark? That is inappropriate.”


“Your behavior constitutes sexual harassment. Please stop.”


 Common responses & replies 

  • “It’s just a joke.”
    → “Sexual harassment is not a joke.”

  • “You’re overreacting.”
    → “If you hadn’t acted this way, I wouldn’t interpret it this way.”

  • If dismissed repeatedly:
    → “I document interactions. Continued behavior will be reported.”


“Someone who cannot show basic respect is not someone you owe tolerance to.”


Scenario 3: Private Setting + One-on-One


Priority: Ensure safety and exit immediately.

  • Simulate a call:
    “I’m with [name] at [location].”

  • Move toward exits or public spaces

  • Leave promptly and establish external awareness

  • Once safe, you may later formally define and report the behavior.

#什么是性骚扰？


地铁公交上故意紧贴摩擦、触碰隐私部位；言语低俗讲黄段子、性暗示；社交软件发送色情信息；职场校园利用权力进行压迫式骚扰；网络上偷拍传播隐私、恶意P图……这些在全球范围内都可以被认定为性骚扰。

“

“Any unwanted, sexually related conduct that violates your will constitutes unlawful behavior in most jurisdictions.”

”


#What constitutes sexual harassment?


  • Unwanted physical contact in public spaces

  • Verbal sexual remarks or innuendo

  • Sending explicit content without consent

  • Abuse of power in workplaces or educational settings

  • Non-consensual recording, image manipulation, or distribution

These behaviors are widely recognized as legally actionable misconduct.

第一道：性骚扰的民事保护

  • 认定标准：以你的意愿为准。不是对方觉得“没事”，只要你感到不适、被冒犯、不愿意，就构成性骚扰。

  • 单位有法定责任：机关、公司、学校必须采取防范、受理投诉、调查处理等措施。知情不报、敷衍推诿，本身就是违法。

  • 你可以依法要求：停止侵害、赔礼道歉、消除影响、损害赔偿。造成严重精神伤害，还可主张精神损害赔偿。


第二道：强制猥亵、侮辱（刑事犯罪）

以暴力、威胁、灌酒、恐吓等方式强行触碰、猥亵、侮辱他人，将面临监禁。在公共场所、对多人、多次实施或针对未成年人，刑罚更重。


第三道：强奸（最严重的法律红线）

违背意愿，以暴力、胁迫或其他手段实施强奸，判处长期监禁。情节恶劣、造成重伤死亡，最高可判无期甚至死刑。


Level 1: Civil Liability

  • Standard: Based on the victim’s subjective experience of discomfort or violation

  • Employers and institutions have a legal duty to prevent and address harassment

  • Survivors may claim:

    • cessation of harm

    • formal apology

    • reputational restoration

    • financial compensation (including emotional distress damages)


Level 2: Criminal Offense — Indecent Assault / Coercion

  • Use of force, threats, intoxication, or coercion

  • More severe penalties in public settings, repeated offenses, or cases involving minors


Level 3: Rape (Severe Criminal Offense)

  • Non-consensual sexual acts involving force, coercion, or incapacitation

  • Subject to severe criminal penalties, including long-term imprisonment

夏律师的“五步维权法”

1. 明确拒绝：直接说出“我不愿意，这是骚扰，请立即停止”。你的拒绝是最关键的法律事实。


2. 优先保安全：能离开马上离开，到有人、有监控的地方。生命安全永远第一。


3. 第一时间固定证据：不删不改，保存聊天记录、语音、图片、视频；记住时间、地点、监控位置；有伤立即就医，保留病历、伤情照片。


4. 立刻维权：刑事犯罪直接报警；职场校园骚扰向单位投诉，同时联系妇联、法律援助机构；民事维权可申请法律援助。


5. 别怕过去很久：刑事犯罪追溯期较长，越早报案证据越充分，但维权永远不晚。**正义可以迟到，但绝不会缺席。

Five-Step 

Rights Protection Framework

1. Clearly refuse
State explicitly: “I do not consent. This is harassment. Stop immediately.”


2. Prioritize safety
Move to secure environments with visibility and surveillance


3. Preserve evidence
Save messages, recordings, timestamps, locations, and medical records


4. Seek legal recourse promptly
Report to law enforcement or institutional bodies; access legal aid


5. Remember: it is never too late
Statutes of limitation may apply, but justice remains accessible

破除“完美受害者”谬误


很多女性被侵犯后会担心：“我不是完美受害者，是不是也有我默许的成分？”


这是基本的归因错误。追求“完美受害者”理念，会让人们忽略权力关系不平等、施害者动机等客观因素，变成“受害者有错论”。


你在极端威胁下的反应——僵住、发抖、失眠、心慌


——都是所有人在威胁下的正常反应，不是你有问题。


Challenging the “Perfect Victim” Myth


Survivors often worry:

“Was I not clear enough? Did I somehow allow it?”


This reflects a fundamental attribution error that shifts responsibility away from the perpetrator.


Human responses under threat—freezing, fear, confusion—are normal physiological reactions, not indicators of consent or fault.
