This privacy notice explains how **{{< org uk legalName >}}** ("AWDPI United Kingdom", "we" or "us") collects and uses personal data about people who ask us for support, donate, volunteer, work with us or contact us, and the rights you have. We are the controller of this personal data under the UK General Data Protection Regulation (UK GDPR) and the Data Protection Act 2018.

We also run awdpi.org for the AWDPI network. How the website handles information about visitors is explained on our [Privacy](/privacy/) page.

## The personal data we collect

What we collect depends on how you deal with us.

- **If you ask us for support:** the name or nickname you give us, your contact details (for example your WeChat ID, WhatsApp number or email address), the town or city where you live, and what you choose to tell us about your situation and needs. This can include:
  - special category data, such as information about your physical or mental health, your sex life or sexual orientation, your racial or ethnic origin, or your religious beliefs;
  - information about criminal offences, such as violence committed against you;
  - your immigration status and information about your children.
- **About other people:** when you tell us about other people, such as a partner, a family member or the person who harmed you, we record only what we need to support you.
- **If you donate:** your name, contact details, the amount, date and purpose of your gift, and the information we need to send you a receipt.
- **If you volunteer with us:** your name, contact details, languages, skills and availability, and records of the work you do with us.
- **If you work with us as a partner or contact us:** your name, your organisation, your contact details and the content of your messages.

We collect this information from you directly, through messages, calls and emails. Sometimes another organisation, such as a service that refers you to us, or another AWDPI organisation, gives us your details, but only with your agreement.

## Staying anonymous

You do not need to give us your real name to get support. You can use a nickname, and you decide what you tell us. You are not required by law or by any contract to give us personal data, but if you leave some out, we may not be able to help in every way, for example by referring you to another service.

## How we use your data and our lawful bases

| What we use it for | Lawful basis |
| --- | --- |
| **Giving you the support you ask for**, such as language support, legal information, emotional support, referrals to refuges, solicitors or other services, and follow-up. | Our legitimate interests in providing the support you ask for, in line with our charitable purposes (UK GDPR Article 6(1)(f)). For special category data and information about criminal offences: your explicit consent (Article 9(2)(a); Data Protection Act 2018, Schedule 1, paragraph 29). |
| **Protecting someone at risk**, where someone's life or safety is at risk and we cannot get consent, or asking for it would put someone at risk. | Recognised legitimate interests in safeguarding vulnerable individuals and responding to emergencies (Article 6(1)(ea) and Annex 1), and vital interests (Articles 6(1)(d) and 9(2)(c)). For special category data and information about criminal offences: substantial public interest in confidential counselling and support, and in safeguarding individuals at risk (Article 9(2)(g); Data Protection Act 2018, Schedule 1, paragraphs 17 and 18). |
| **Sharing your story**, for example in an interview published in our Stories section, only if you agree, and only in the form you agree to. | Your explicit consent (Articles 6(1)(a) and 9(2)(a)). |
| **Processing donations** and sending receipts. | Our legitimate interests in receiving and acknowledging donations (Article 6(1)(f)). |
| **Sending you news or fundraising emails.** | Your consent (Article 6(1)(a)), as the Privacy and Electronic Communications Regulations require. |
| **Organising volunteering**, answering enquiries and working with partner organisations. | Our legitimate interests in running our charity and working with others (Article 6(1)(f)). |
| **Running our charity and meeting our legal obligations**, such as keeping accounts, reporting to the Charity Commission, dealing with complaints and responding to court orders. | Legal obligation (Article 6(1)(c)) and our legitimate interests (Article 6(1)(f)). Where special category data is needed to establish, exercise or defend legal claims: Article 9(2)(f) and Schedule 1, paragraph 33. |
| **Producing research and statistics** on gender-based violence. We publish only figures that do not identify anyone. | Our legitimate interests in understanding and preventing gender-based violence (Article 6(1)(f)). For special category data: research and statistics in the public interest, with safeguards (Article 9(2)(j); Schedule 1, paragraph 4). |

Where we rely on your consent, you can withdraw it at any time by telling us. This does not affect what we did before you withdrew it. After that, we stop using the data for that purpose, but we may keep a record of the support we gave where another lawful basis applies (see [How long we keep it](#how-long-we-keep-it)).

We do not make decisions about you based solely on automated processing, and we do not profile donors or screen their wealth.

## Information about other people

When you tell us about someone else, such as the person who harmed you, we do not tell that person that we hold information about them, because doing so could put you at risk and would seriously impair our work (UK GDPR Article 14(5)(b)).

## Who we share it with

What you tell us about your situation is confidential. We share it outside AWDPI United Kingdom only with your agreement, except where the law requires it or where it is necessary to protect someone at risk. If we have to share information without your agreement, we share only what is necessary and, where it is safe to do so, we tell you.

- **Our team.** The trustees, staff and volunteers handling your request see only what they need, and they must keep it confidential.
- **Support organisations**, such as refuges, solicitors, counsellors or interpreters, with your agreement.
- **Other AWDPI organisations**, only when this is needed to help you, for example if you move to another country, and with your agreement.
- **Service providers** that process data on our instructions, such as our email and document storage provider. They are bound by contract to use it only to provide their service to us.
- **Professional advisers and regulators**, such as our accountants, auditors and lawyers, and the Charity Commission, where this is necessary.
- **Police, courts and safeguarding authorities**, only where the law requires it or to protect someone at risk.

We do not sell personal data, and we do not share it for advertising.

## Messaging apps

Many people contact us through WeChat or WhatsApp. These apps are run by other companies under their own privacy policies:

- **WeChat** is run by Tencent. Messages to our WeChat accounts are processed by Tencent, including in mainland China, where the authorities may have access to them.
- **WhatsApp** is run by Meta. Messages are end-to-end encrypted, but Meta processes information about your use of WhatsApp, including in the United States.

If you prefer, you can contact us by email or phone instead. If someone else may look at your phone, consider deleting your conversations with us after reading them.

## Where we store it and transfers outside the UK

We keep our records, including our email, with service providers that store them in the European Union. Transfers to the European Economic Area are approved by UK regulations.

We transfer personal data outside the UK in these cases:

- **To other AWDPI organisations**, only when this is needed to help you and with your agreement. AWDPI Belgium is in the European Union. For AWDPI United States and AWDPI Australia, we use the International Data Transfer Agreement or the UK Addendum to the European Commission's Standard Contractual Clauses; where no such agreement is in place, we transfer your data only with your explicit consent, after telling you about the risks.
- **To service providers outside the UK**, where UK regulations approve the transfer, such as the UK–US "data bridge" for companies certified under the Data Privacy Framework, or under the International Data Transfer Agreement or the UK Addendum.
- **Through WeChat.** No UK regulations approve transfers to China. When you contact us on WeChat, we will tell you about the risks described under [Messaging apps](#messaging-apps) and offer you other ways to reach us, and we discuss your situation there only with your explicit consent.

You can ask us for a copy of the safeguards we use.

## How long we keep it

We keep personal data only as long as we need it, and then delete it or anonymise it:

- **Support records:** while we are supporting you, and then for up to 3 years after your case is closed, so that we can deal with any complaint or legal claim and protect people at risk. You can ask us to delete them sooner.
- **Donation and financial records:** for 6 years from the end of the financial year they relate to, as the Charities Act 2011 requires.
- **Enquiries and partner correspondence:** up to 2 years after our last contact.
- **Volunteer records:** while you volunteer with us and up to 2 years afterwards.

If you ask us to stop contacting you, we keep your contact details on a "do not contact" list, so that we respect your request.

## Your rights

Under the UK GDPR, you have the right to:

- **access** the personal data we hold about you and get a copy of it;
- have it **corrected** if it is inaccurate or incomplete;
- have it **erased**;
- **restrict** how we use it;
- **object** to how we use it where we rely on our legitimate interests, and object at any time to our use of it for fundraising or other direct marketing;
- **receive it in a portable format**, or have it sent to another organisation, where we use it on the basis of your consent and by automated means;
- **withdraw your consent** at any time, where we rely on consent.

Some of these rights do not apply in every case. For example, we may withhold information that would reveal personal data about someone else or put someone at risk, as the Data Protection Act 2018 allows.

To use any of these rights, write to [{{< org uk email >}}](mailto:{{< org uk email >}}). This is free. We reply within one month; if your request is complex, we may extend this by up to two further months and will tell you why. If we need to confirm who you are, we will do it in a way that keeps you safe.

## Complaints

You have the right to complain to us if you think we have not handled your personal data in line with data protection law. Write to [{{< org uk email >}}](mailto:{{< org uk email >}}). We will acknowledge your complaint within 30 days, look into it without undue delay and tell you the outcome.

You also have the right to complain to the Information Commission, the UK's data protection regulator, which still uses the name ICO: 4th Floor, No.3 Circle Square, 5 Hawkshaw Street, Manchester M1 7BL; [ico.org.uk/make-a-complaint](https://ico.org.uk/make-a-complaint/); helpline 0303 123 1113. The Commission will usually expect you to have raised your complaint with us first.

## Children

Our services are for adults. If you are under 18 and need help, please tell us and we will help you find services for young people. You can also call Childline free on 0800 1111 at any time.

## How we protect it

We limit access to personal data to the people who need it, keep case information confidential, and use appropriate technical and organisational measures to protect it against loss, misuse and unauthorised access. If a personal data breach is likely to result in a high risk to you, we will tell you without undue delay, and we report breaches to the Information Commission where the law requires.

## Your safety

If you are in immediate danger, call 999. If you cannot speak, call 999 from a mobile, stay on the line and press 55 when prompted, so the police know it is a genuine emergency.

You can also call the free 24-hour National Domestic Abuse Helpline on 0808 2000 247.

The **Quick exit** button in the bottom-right corner of every page takes you straight to Google, and so does pressing Esc twice. Leaving a page this way does not erase it from your browser history, so if someone else uses your device, consider using a private window or clearing your history.
