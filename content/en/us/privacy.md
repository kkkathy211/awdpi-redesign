This privacy notice explains how **{{< org us legalName >}}** ("AWDPI United States", "we" or "us") collects, uses, shares and protects personal information about people who ask us for support, donate, volunteer, work with us or contact us, and the choices you have.

AWDPI United States is an independent organisation within the AWDPI international network. AWDPI United Kingdom, AWDPI Belgium and AWDPI Australia are separate organisations. Each is responsible for the personal information it handles and has its own privacy notice.

## The information we collect

What we collect depends on how you deal with us.

- **If you ask us for support:** the name or nickname you give us, your contact details (for example your WeChat ID, WhatsApp number or email address), the state and city where you live, and what you choose to tell us about your situation and needs. This can include sensitive information, such as your health, your immigration status, your children, or violence you have experienced.
- **About other people:** when you tell us about other people, such as a partner, a family member or the person who harmed you, we record only what we need to support you.
- **If you donate:** your name, contact details, the amount, date and purpose of your gift, and the information we need to send you a receipt. If you give through an online fundraising platform, the platform shares these details with us under its own privacy policy.
- **If you volunteer with us:** your name, contact details, languages, skills and availability, and records of the work you do with us.
- **If you work with us as a partner or contact us:** your name, your organisation, your contact details and the content of your messages.

We collect this information from you directly, through messages, calls and emails. Sometimes another organisation, such as a service that refers you to us, or another AWDPI organisation, gives us your details, but only with your agreement.

## Staying anonymous

You do not need to give us your real name to get support. You can use a nickname, and you decide what you tell us. You do not have to give us any information, but if you leave some out, we may not be able to help in every way, for example by referring you to another service.

## How we use it

- To give you the support you ask for, such as language support, legal information, emotional support, referrals to shelters, legal services or other support, and follow-up.
- To protect someone's life or safety in an emergency.
- To share your story, for example in an interview published in our Stories section, only if you agree, and only in the form you agree to.
- To process donations, send receipts, and keep the records that federal and state law requires.
- To send you news or fundraising messages, if you have agreed to receive them.
- To organise volunteering, answer enquiries and work with partner organisations.
- To run our organisation, for example to keep accounts, deal with complaints and meet our legal obligations.
- To produce research and statistics on gender-based violence, using only information that no longer identifies anyone.

We do not use computer programs to make decisions about you.

## Confidentiality for people we support

What you tell us about your situation is confidential. We do not share information that identifies you with anyone outside AWDPI United States without your informed consent, which you can withdraw at any time, except where:

- the law or a court order requires it, for example where state law requires certain people to report suspected abuse of a child, or of an elder or dependent adult; or
- it is necessary to prevent a serious and imminent threat to someone's life or safety.

If we have to share information without your consent, we share only what is necessary and, where it is safe to do so, we tell you.

## Who we share it with

- **Our team.** The staff and volunteers handling your request see only what they need, and they must keep it confidential.
- **Support organisations**, such as shelters, lawyers, counsellors or interpreters, only with your consent.
- **Other AWDPI organisations**, only when this is needed to help you, for example if you move to another country, and with your consent.
- **Service providers** that handle information for us, such as our email and document storage provider, payment processors and fundraising platforms. They may use it only to provide their service to us.
- **Professional advisers**, such as our accountants, auditors and lawyers, who must keep it confidential.
- **Authorities**, such as the police or courts, only in the cases described under [Confidentiality for people we support](#confidentiality-for-people-we-support).

We do not sell or rent personal information, share it for targeted advertising, or exchange donor lists with other organisations.

When you donate online, your card details are handled by the payment processor. We do not receive or store your full card number.

## Messaging apps

Many people contact us through WeChat or WhatsApp. These apps are run by other companies under their own privacy policies:

- **WeChat** is run by Tencent. Messages to our WeChat accounts are processed by Tencent, including in mainland China, where the authorities may have access to them.
- **WhatsApp** is run by Meta. Messages are end-to-end encrypted, but Meta processes information about your use of WhatsApp, including in the United States.

If you prefer, you can contact us by email or phone instead. If someone else may look at your phone, consider deleting your conversations with us after reading them.

## Where we store it

We keep our records, including our email, with service providers that store them in the United States. Some service providers, and the messaging apps described above, may process information in other countries.

## How long we keep it

We keep personal information only as long as we need it, and then delete it or remove anything that identifies you:

- **Support records:** while we are supporting you, and then for up to 3 years after your case is closed. You can ask us to delete them sooner.
- **Donation and financial records:** as long as federal and state tax and nonprofit law requires.
- **Enquiries and partner correspondence:** up to 2 years after our last contact.
- **Volunteer records:** while you volunteer with us and up to 2 years afterwards.

If you ask us to stop contacting you, we keep your contact details on a "do not contact" list, so that we respect your request.

## Your choices

You can ask us to:

- tell you what personal information we hold about you and give you a copy;
- correct it if it is wrong;
- delete it, unless the law requires us to keep it;
- stop sending you news or fundraising messages, which you can also do with the unsubscribe link in any email we send;
- stop contacting you.

Write to [{{< org us email >}}](mailto:{{< org us email >}}). We reply within 30 days. If we need to confirm who you are, we will do it in a way that keeps you safe. If a state privacy law gives you further rights and applies to us, we will honour those rights too.

## Our website

awdpi.org is run for the whole AWDPI network by AWDPI United Kingdom. How the website handles information about visitors is explained on our [Privacy](/privacy/) page. The website does not track visitors over time or across other websites, and we do not knowingly allow other parties to do so through it. Because of this, it does not respond differently to "Do Not Track" signals.

## Children

Our services are for adults. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children under 13 through our website. If you are under 18 and need help, please tell us and we will help you find services for young people. You can also call the Childhelp National Child Abuse Hotline at 1-800-422-4453 at any time.

## How we protect it

We limit access to personal information to the people who need it, keep case information confidential, and use reasonable technical and organisational safeguards to protect it against loss, misuse and unauthorised access. If a security breach affects your personal information, we will notify you as required by applicable state law.

## Your safety

If you are in immediate danger, call 911.

You can also call the National Domestic Violence Hotline at 1-800-799-7233, or text START to 88788, at any time.

The **Quick exit** button in the bottom-right corner of every page takes you straight to Google, and so does pressing Esc twice. Leaving a page this way does not erase it from your browser history, so if someone else uses your device, consider using a private window or clearing your history.

## Changes to this notice

We may update this notice. When we do, we post the new version on this page and change the date below. If the changes are significant, we will also take reasonable steps to tell the people we are in contact with.
