New Year's Eve mass shooting in Monterey Park, California, USA

(1) Incident Summary

According to U.S. media reports, on the evening of January 21 local time, a mass shooting occurred in Monterey Park, a city in Los Angeles, California with a large Chinese population. The incident has so far resulted in at least 10 deaths and 10 others hospitalized. That evening was New Year's Eve, and thousands had gathered to begin a two-day celebration—the region's largest Chinese Lunar New Year event.


The suspect was a 72-year-old man named Huu Can Tran. Witnesses reported that the shooter carried a semi-automatic firearm and multiple rounds of ammunition. Police confirmed that all the deceased were of Asian descent. Ten people died at the scene—five women and five men—and 10 others were injured, with injuries ranging from minor to severe. On January 22 local time, the suspect died from a self-inflicted gunshot wound.


A celebration event attended by tens of thousands was being held near the shooting site. After the incident, all related events were canceled.


According to U.S. media, this is one of the deadliest shootings in the Los Angeles area in nearly a decade and the deadliest mass shooting in the United States so far this year.



(2) Commentary Perspectives

1. The incident has drawn widespread attention. The U.S. President ordered flags to be flown at half-staff in mourning, and the Chinese Consulate General urges enhanced vigilance.


U.S. President Joe Biden announced that the White House and all federal government buildings would fly the American flag at half-staff until sunset on January 26 to mourn the victims of the deadly mass shooting in Monterey Park, Los Angeles County, California.


Given the severity of the incident and its significant impact on the Chinese community, the Chinese Consulate General urges Chinese citizens within its jurisdiction to enhance risk awareness, strengthen safety precautions, and ensure personal safety. In case of any threat to personal safety, call 911 immediately.


2. California has failed to stop the flow of illegal firearms. Frequent hate crimes, gun violence, and anti-Asian discrimination have created a grim situation for Asian communities.


On January 24 local time, this was the second mass shooting in California within three days. Two related shootings occurred at a farm and a trucking company in Half Moon Bay, San Mateo County on the San Francisco Peninsula, resulting in seven deaths. The suspect, aged 67, was also a farm worker, and some of the victims were his coworkers. Police found a semi-automatic handgun in his vehicle.


After the Half Moon Bay shooting, Dave Pine, Chair of the San Mateo County Board of Supervisors, said in a statement: "Tragically, the scourge of gun violence has struck again. We haven't even had time to mourn those lost in the horrific Monterey Park shooting. Gun violence must stop. California has some of the strictest gun laws in the United States, and we have strengthened them through local actions, but we must do more. The current situation is unacceptable."


3. Asians will not remain silent. Overseas Asian communities must unite and courageously speak out and resist malicious slander.


Although Asian communities remain vulnerable overseas, vulnerability does not mean they should be bullied. Acting within one's capacity does not mean "minding one's own business" or "avoiding trouble." We can start by correcting foreigners' deeply rooted stereotypes about Asians, and by no longer remaining silent in the face of rumors and slanders about China and Asia. Beyond that, we must strive to unite with other Asian communities, ensuring that those facing racial discrimination are not left isolated. We must also bravely support Chinese people who are unjustly attacked or slandered, proving through action: the Chinese people are one whole—we will not turn a blind eye to injustice suffered by our compatriots just because it does not directly affect us.


In the face of racial discrimination or related threats, we can seek help from embassies, universities, and all those who recognize that racism is a common enemy of humanity. We can unite to resist the forces of racism.

According to the WeChat account "Huaren Information Media," around 6 a.m. on January 3, a 25-year-old Chinese man living in Italy and a female friend were robbed by two Moroccans on San Massimo Avenue while heading to the city center.

Asian woman robbed on New York's 7 train

According to LIC Post on January 9, an Asian woman was robbed with a knife on the 7 train in Sunnyside.


Police said the 32-year-old victim boarded the train at 46th Street–Bliss Street Station around 12:40 a.m., and was approached by an unidentified man as the train headed toward Manhattan. The suspect then shouted, "Don't move, give me your money." Police said the woman had already handed over her iPhone 13 before the suspect pulled out a utility knife and demanded cash. The woman then tried to flee, but the man chased her and slashed her hand and face with the knife. When the train arrived at 33rd Street Station, the suspect fled the car. The Asian woman received medical treatment at the scene.


According to police information, the suspect is a Black man, approximately 40 years old, about 5 feet 11 inches tall, weighing around 200 pounds, last seen wearing a black jacket and a gray hat. He has not yet been arrested.

Indiana University student stabbed due to Asian identity

According to CBS News on January 16, a 56-year-old woman has been charged with repeatedly stabbing an 18-year-old Indiana University student, with the university stating the suspect's motive was the student's Asian identity.


Bloomington police said in a press release that the victim told investigators she was standing on a Bloomington bus waiting for the exit door to open on Wednesday afternoon when another passenger suddenly began hitting her head. Surveillance footage showed no interaction between the two women before the attack.


A witness on the same bus contacted police, who then arrested Billie R. Davis of Bloomington. According to court records, Davis is charged with attempted murder and aggravated battery. The victim, who sustained multiple stab wounds, was treated at a hospital; her name was not released. Court documents indicate Davis stated she attacked the victim because of her race. This case is a blatant anti-Asian hate crime.

Asian female teacher shot dead by boyfriend

According to the New York Post on January 13, Wendy Duan, an elementary school teacher in Texas, was murdered by her boyfriend, Charvas Thompson.


According to the victim's mother, the couple had known each other for only one week through the dating app "MeetMe." On the day of the incident, Wendy had an argument with her boyfriend at her home in Sugar Land, after which he shot and killed her.


The mother said Thompson mistook her daughter's kindness for weakness and hoped he would rot in prison. "He is a devil who took my daughter's life and should be severely punished by law."


Wendy was a third-grade language arts teacher in the Alief Independent School District and was named ESL (English as a Second Language) Teacher of the Year in December 2021.


The suspect has since been arrested and is currently being extradited back to Texas. A warrant was issued on Sunday charging him with murder, and his bail has been set at $500,000.

Sierra Leone President Signs

Landmark Gender Equality Law

On January 19, 2023, Sierra Leonean President Julius Maada Bio signed the Gender Equality and Women's Empowerment Act, 2022—now known as the Gender Equality Act, 2022—and outlined transformative government reforms and gender policies to be implemented during the final four and a half years of his presidency.

 

"For me, empowering women economically and protecting them is not political rhetoric. Empowering women is essential to the health and social development of families, communities, and nations. Women can only reach their full potential when they live safe, fulfilling, and productive lives."

 

He said, "When I became president, I firmly placed gender issues on my Agenda for a New Direction. Sierra Leone's future is female; we must do everything possible to promote women's timely, full, and unconditional participation in our national life, politics, and development."

ILO Launches First-Ever

Gender Equality and Social Inclusion Handbook

To promote social inclusion and prevent sexual violence in academic settings, the International Labour Organization (ILO) launched a set of guidelines and a training handbook on January 24 in Jakarta, aiming to "mainstream gender equality, disability, and social inclusion, and to prevent and address sexual violence in vocational education and training institutions." This initiative is part of the UK government-funded ILO Indonesia Prosperous Skills Program.

 

The first publication of its kind, along with the guidelines and handbook, was developed through a series of literature reviews and focus group discussions (FGDs) with students, faculty, and administrative staff from four polytechnics in coastal provinces known for marine studies. The publication supports the Indonesian government's commitment to reducing violence and harassment in educational institutions, as stipulated by the Minister of Education, Culture, Research, and Technology. These four polytechnic partners have become pilot institutions for implementing the guidelines and training handbook. The initiative is expected to be adopted and scaled up across 40 other polytechnics and additional vocational institutions.

International Women's Day for Multilateralism

On November 23, 2021, the UNESCO General Conference adopted Resolution 41 C/57, proclaiming January 25 as the International Women's Day for Multilateralism. The day aims to recognize women's vital role in advancing human rights, peace, and sustainable development within the multilateral system.

 

The International Women's Day for Multilateralism advocates for greater representation of women in key decision-making roles that shape and implement the multilateral agenda, and ensures multilateralism serves women through gender-responsive reforms and agreements. It also provides a global platform for intergenerational dialogue between women leaders within the multilateral system and the next generation of women leaders in global governance.

UNESCO Celebrates International Day of Education 2023 for Afghan Girls and Women

UNESCO Director-General Audrey Azoulay decided to dedicate International Day of Education 2023 (January 24) to Afghan girls and women. As events are held at the United Nations headquarters, UNESCO will renew its call for the immediate restoration of their fundamental right to education.

 

On the occasion of International Day of Education on January 24, UNESCO will host an event at the United Nations in New York, with contributions from the UN Secretary-General, the President of the UN General Assembly, and the UNESCO Director-General. The first panel discussion will focus specifically on education for Afghan girls and women.

 

Currently, 2.5 million (80%) school-aged Afghan girls and young women are out of school, with 1.2 million barred from secondary and higher education following decisions by the de facto authorities. Since August 2021, UNESCO has adapted its interventions to support continued education in challenging environments.

Early-Career Research Fellowships Support

25 Women Scientists from Developing Countries

The Organization for Women in Science for the Developing World (OWSD) announced that 25 women have been awarded the 2022 Early-Career Research Fellowships. These scientists will receive up to $50,000 to lead research projects and establish research groups within their home institutions, maintaining international research standards and attracting scholars from around the world for collaboration.

 

Fellowship funding is generously provided by Canada's International Development Research Centre (IDRC). Additionally, the Aspen Institute Italia is funding one "Early-Career Fellow" for the first time this year, marking an initial step in a partnership that will build bridges for future opportunities supporting women in science from developing countries. OWSD is a program of The World Academy of Sciences (TWAS), based in Trieste, Italy, under UNESCO.

California Makes Lunar New Year an Official Holiday

According to Yahoo News, California became the first U.S. state to designate Lunar New Year as an official public holiday, following Governor Gavin Newsom's signing of Assembly Bill 2596 (AB 2596) last September. The Lunar New Year is not only an important festival for Chinese communities in Asia, but also a significant celebration for Asian American communities in the United States—especially in California, which has a large Asian population. Governor Newsom signed AB 2596 into law last September, and it officially took effect this year. California has designated the day as a state government holiday to recognize the cultural diversity and significance that Asian Americans bring to the state, and to provide all Californians with an opportunity to participate in Lunar New Year celebrations. Prior to California's legislation, school districts in New York and San Francisco had already granted Lunar New Year holidays, but California is the first state government to officially declare it a public holiday—demonstrating growing recognition of Asian culture.

AMD Makes History with Two Asian Women Leading the Company

According to Sina Finance on January 13, AMD announced on Wednesday afternoon local time that it would hire Jean Hu (Jin Hu) from Marvell Technology as its new Chief Financial Officer (CFO). The Silicon Valley chip giant said Hu will start on January 23, succeeding Devinder Kumar, who is retiring and will remain at AMD until April to ensure a smooth transition. AMD is currently led by Lisa Su, who serves as both Chair and CEO. This means AMD will become the first U.S. publicly traded company to be simultaneously led by Asian women as both CEO and CFO. Given the barriers Asian women face in reaching top corporate leadership in the U.S., this moment is highly significant.

"Top U.S. High School" Deliberately Conceals Student Awards

Preliminary investigations revealed that for years, administrators at Thomas Jefferson High School for Science and Technology had concealed multiple students' (most of whom were Asian American) rightful receipt of the "U.S. Presidential Scholars" award, under the guise of "fairness." The award is widely recognized and significantly benefits students' college applications, and the concealment affected 1,200 students. Dave Lu, founder of Hyphen Capital, tweeted: "They decided to screw over all the kids who worked hard to earn recognition, denying them the chance to use these honors in college applications. Fairness at the expense of Asians is not fairness at all!" "These two individuals need to be fired—this deception has harmed so many children's lives." Additionally, the school's admissions process has shown bias against Asian students: the admitted proportion of Asian students dropped sharply from 73% last year to 54%, while Black students' share rose from under 2% to 7%, and Hispanic students' share increased from 3% to 11%. The school is openly marginalizing Asian students, prompting many parents to file lawsuits; the case remains unresolved.
