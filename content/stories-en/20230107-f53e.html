  Event Introduction  

Out with the old, in with the new—2023 has arrived as 2022 becomes history. During this special season, Avoice has curated five diverse and wide-ranging cinematic masterpieces to celebrate the Lunar New Year. They are The Fabelmans, Elvis, Everything Everywhere All at Once, She Said, and Tár. Except for She Said, which will be screened on Monday, January 9 at 7:30 PM, the other five films will be broadcast every Saturday at 8:00 PM Beijing time—don't miss it!



1. She Said (2022) 

The story centers on New York Times female journalists Jodi Kantor (played by Zoe Kazan) and Megan Twohey (played by Carey Mulligan). Adapted from the bestselling book She Said: Breaking the Sexual Harassment Story That Helped Ignite a Movement, the film follows how one investigative report sparked the globally renowned #MeToo movement, exposing decades of tolerated sexual abuse in Hollywood and transforming American culture.

Since its inception, #MeToo has exerted influence worldwide. It has unveiled industry hidden rules and untold truths. She Said is also a film about journalists, objectively portraying their professional ethics, while its documentary-style cinematography reflects deep respect for news professionals.

2. The Fabelmans (2022)   Director: Steven Spielberg

The Fabelmans takes a semi-autobiographical approach, telling the story of how Spielberg fell in love with filmmaking during childhood and eventually became a director. The young Spielberg's confusion and disappointments regarding family, dreams, friendship, and love are interwoven with occasional humor, blending the bittersweet experiences of his journey, ultimately achieving his dream. As a Spielberg production, the film's use of light and shadow and its retro visual texture are exceptional. "Though my belt grows wider, I never regret"—The Fabelmans embodies this steadfast dedication and pursuit of art.

During a high school ditch day film shoot, the protagonist faithfully recreates the athletic prowess of a school bully who once tormented him. Film is Spielberg's language. Although his daily life may seem mundane and unremarkable, when it comes to cinema, he is fully capable of stepping into the spotlight and becoming the center of public attention. Dreamers are beautiful, but those who persist in their dreams are the most beautiful.

3. Elvis (2022) Director: Baz Luhrmann

Directed by legendary filmmaker Baz Luhrmann, known for Moulin Rouge! and The Great Gatsby, and starring Austin Butler—Hollywood's rising heartthrob from Once Upon a Time in Hollywood—alongside Oscar-winning actor Tom Hanks, this film brings the life of the late rock 'n' roll icon Elvis Presley to the big screen.

From an unknown rural boy to a globally adored superstar, witness how Elvis navigated fame, fortune, and complex human relationships, and explore the life behind the legendary rock king's timeless hits. With stunning visuals, this film is a must-watch for fans of lavish cinematic styles!

4. Everything Everywhere All at Once (2022)

Everything Everywhere All at Once has surpassed Uncut Gems at the North American box office, becoming the highest-grossing film under A24 in North America. In terms of critical acclaim, it has entered the IMDb TOP 250, currently ranking 64th.

Using the multiverse as its entry point, Everything Everywhere All at Once encompasses multiple film genres, blending sci-fi and action elements while presenting comedy and romance in character development. To describe it in just one sentence would be reductive. The directors have embedded numerous tribute Easter eggs—those with strong curiosity, sci-fi enthusiasts, and fans of cosmic philosophy may particularly enjoy this work.

5. Tár (2022) Director: Todd Field Lead Actor: Cate Blanchett

The film was nominated for the Golden Lion at the Venice Film Festival and ultimately earned Cate Blanchett—the actress affectionately known as "The Great Demon Queen" among Chinese film fans—the Best Actress award at Venice. The story follows a female conductor, Lydia Tár, who suddenly faces a media storm over negative workplace allegations. This scandal impacts her family and conducting career, leading her to resign before a major performance and ending her professional ascent.

Many assume this is a "strong female lead" film, but Tár is neither a motivational tale of perseverance, patience, and determination, nor a portrait of an artist who sacrifices everything for art. Tár is about public pressure, workplace political correctness, and cancel culture. Should a public figure's private life be tied to their profession? Should personal morality and values be balanced with one's career? To what extent should they be separated, and to what degree should they be used to interpret or stigmatize her/his career and creative work?
