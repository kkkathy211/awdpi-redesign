The origin of the International Day for the Elimination of Violence against Women on November 25


On November 25, 1960, Rafael Trujillo, the dictator of the Dominican Republic, brutally murdered the Mirabal sisters—political activists—out of fear they would challenge his authority and to consolidate his power. After the end of Trujillo's 30-year dictatorship, the First Latin American Feminist Congress in July 1981 declared that November 25—the day the Mirabal sisters were killed—November 25 should be designated as a day against violence, to honor their resistance against tyranny and violence, and to help countless women living under the shadow of domestic violence transform the shame of "family disgrace should not be made public" into the courage to "firmly say 'no' to violence".


In 1993, the United Nations issued the Declaration on the Elimination of Violence against Women , which defined the scope of "violence against women" as "any gender-based violence that results in, or is likely to result in, physical, sexual or psychological harm or suffering to women, including threats of such acts, coercion or arbitrary deprivation of liberty, whether occurring in public or in private life." The Declaration also stated that domestic violence against women is a violation of women's human rights and fundamental freedoms, and a consequence of gender inequality.


In 2021, marking the 30th anniversary of the Day against Violence, under the leadership of the United Nations, the theme for the 2021 International Day for the Elimination of Violence against Women was set as: "Orange the World: End violence against women now!", focusing on the connections between crisis-related, rapidly changing environments and all forms of violence against women.

The "Avoice Overseas Chinese Women Protection Project" aims to assist overseas Chinese women suffering from domestic violence and improve the living conditions of vulnerable groups of overseas Chinese women, building a bridge between women in need and various resources, becoming their solid support and warm shelter..

We emphasize self-empowerment among the target group of women, while also providing multi-dimensional support, including language, psychological, legal, medical, shelter, channels, education, economic assistance, storytelling and amplification, and community support, expanding the breadth of assistance while creating a virtuous cycle of mutual support.
