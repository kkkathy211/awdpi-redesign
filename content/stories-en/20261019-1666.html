Background Introduction


The lingering echoes of the last time-traveling concerto have just faded from early summer, and now New York has already stepped into autumn. The air on weekend mornings carries a gentle chill; Manhattan has not yet fully awakened: Where are the people who once listened to music with you? This concert begins with a quiet prayer, like making a wish at the start of a new day for someone you hold dear.


(Autumn scenery of Central Park, Manhattan |Image source: Public domain)


Autumn might be the perfect season to write a love letter—though it need not begin with "Dear…" Some people remain by your side, while others live on only in songs. As dusk passes and Halloween quietly arrives, skeletons begin to dance and old ghosts come knocking. The music carries a southern breeze, evoking memories of a distant hometown. Then, at midnight, a tango begins—will the next turn still be a farewell?




(Autumn scenery of Central Park, Manhattan | Image source: Public domain)


From morning prayers to daytime love, from twilight phantoms to midnight tango, this concert, *Songs of Autumn*, is a letter composed by violin and piano: filled with classical elegance, the mystery of autumn nights, and the passionate sorrow of tango. Only when the final note fades do we realize—perhaps there's a line in this letter meant for you.


(Autumn scenery of Central Park, Manhattan | Image source: Public domain)

Event & Program


At this PINEASE concert, we will follow the whispered dialogue between violin and piano—from morning prayers to daytime love, from twilight phantoms to midnight tango—and together listen to a love letter written for autumn:

PROGRAM | Repertoire


PART I — Songs of Autumn

First Half · Songs of Autumn


1. Charles Gounod — Ave Maria
Gounod — *Ave Maria*

In the morning, the city has not yet awakened. May this quiet prayer carry my blessings to you…


2. Ottorino Respighi — Five Pieces for Violin and Piano
Respighi — *Five Pieces for Violin and Piano (excerpts)*

I. Romanza | I. Romance
Some words, written into a letter and then crossed out—finally leaving only: "I missed you today…"

II. Aubade | II. Aubade
The light outside the window brightens; the streets gradually come to life. Just thinking about seeing you today makes my steps lighter…


3. Niccolò Paganini — Cantabile, Op. 17
Paganini — *Andantino cantabile, Op. 17*

If strings could sing, I'd ask them to speak a few words from my heart. It doesn't need to be perfect—just so long as you can hear it…


4. Amy Beach — Romance for Violin and Piano, Op. 23
Amy Beach — *Romance for Violin and Piano, Op. 23*

At first, I only wanted to softly say I missed you. But as I spoke, long-hidden feelings began to rise…


5. Fritz Kreisler — Schön Rosmarin
Kreisler — *Liebesleid (Love's Sorrow)*

There are also these light-hearted moments: seeing you, I forget all the words I prepared. I just want to invite you to dance, and find an excuse to stay a little longer…


6. Fritz Kreisler — Praeludium and Allegro
Kreisler — *Prélude et agitato*

There's something I want to tell you seriously. But as the music surges, I realize there's no need to hesitate—there are so many beautiful moments still ahead, worth experiencing together…

-

PART II — Autumn Nights
Second Half · Autumn Night


1. Camille Saint-Saëns — Danse macabre, Op. 40
Saint-Saëns — *Danse Macabre, Op. 40*

The midnight bell tolls, and skeletons come to the ball. Tonight, let us make a jest of death and dance until dawn…


2. William Bolcom — Graceful Ghost Rag
Bolcom — *Graceful Ghost Rag*

Some ghosts are well-mannered—they only come to sit a while when a familiar melody plays. Like an old friend long unseen, still wearing the smile you remember…


3. Astor Piazzolla — La Muerte del Ángel
Piazzolla — *Muerte del Ángel (The Death of the Angel)*

The music suddenly grows urgent; even the gentle night gains an edge. If farewell must come, at least for now, I still want to hold your hand tightly…


4. Astor Piazzolla — Vuelvo al Sur
Piazzolla — *Oblivion (Return to the South)*

The night deepens, and suddenly I long to return south, to a street that remembers my footsteps. Home is far, but the door in memory remains slightly ajar…


5. Astor Piazzolla — Libertango
Piazzolla — *Libertango (Free Tango)*

Let's dance one more tango together. This time, no questions about the future, no rush to say goodbye—just dance freely to the music, until the very end…

Guest Introductions


🎻


          Dr. Jia Wei Violin

An internationally acclaimed violin soloist and chamber musician, Dr. Jia Wei has performed across China, the United States, Japan, Singapore, Germany, France, and Luxembourg, earning wide praise for his deeply expressive artistry. He has been invited to perform at numerous prestigious institutions and universities, including the Kauffman Center for the Performing Arts in Kansas City, Fordham University School of Law in New York, Chichibu Hall in Japan, Guiyang Grand Theater, Blue Lake Fine Arts Camp, Michigan State University, Marlboro College, University of Central Oklahoma, University of Erlangen-Nuremberg in Germany, Xinghai Conservatory of Music, and Minzu University of China.


In addition to his performance career, Dr. Jia Wei is deeply committed to music education and the development of young musicians. He currently serves as undergraduate and graduate violin and chamber music instructor at the University of Central Oklahoma. As an active educator, he has been invited to give lecture-recitals and masterclasses at Minzu University of China, South China Normal University, Huizhou University, Guizhou Normal University, Xinghai Conservatory of Music, University of Central Oklahoma, University of Wisconsin–Green Bay, Central Michigan University, and Marlboro College. He has also served as a visiting professor at Neijiang Normal University in Guizhou.


Dr. Jia Wei is also a co-founder of PINEASE Association Inc., a nonprofit arts organization based in Manhattan, New York. Through innovative programming, cross-disciplinary collaboration, and educational outreach, he strives to bridge the gap between classical music and audiences.


A grandson of Ji Zhou, a renowned composer from Guizhou, he began studying violin at age six under his father, later studying with teachers Hu Shuang, Professor Xie Yu, Professor He Minnan, Professor Zhu Hong, and in his doctoral studies with renowned violinist Walter Verdehr and Dmitri Berlinsky, gold medalist of the Paganini International Violin Competition.

🎹


          Chen Qiao, Piano

A young Chinese-American collaborative pianist based in New York, actively performing on the city's music scene. Her artistic practice centers on collaborative piano, encompassing opera, art song, and chamber music, and extends into broader musical and cultural domains.


As a collaborative pianist, Chen Qiao has participated in major concerts and opera productions, performing on iconic New York stages such as Carnegie Hall, and joining European tours. She has been active at international festivals and young artist programs including the Miami Music Festival, Kyoto International Music Festival, and Classic Lyric Arts, where she has served as harpsichordist, orchestral pianist, and vocal coach, demonstrating deep understanding of operatic language and dramatic structure. She has also served as official collaborative pianist at multiple international music competitions, providing professional support to emerging musicians.


Chen Qiao holds a Master of Music in Collaborative Piano from Manhattan School of Music and a Bachelor of Music from Shanghai Conservatory of Music. She currently serves as piano accompanist and vocal coach at Manhattan School of Music, actively involved in vocal and instrumental teaching, rehearsals, and performances.


For Chen Qiao, music is always connected to language, literature, and the humanities. She views performance as a convergence of musical interpretation and narrative art. Beyond the concert hall, she engages in curation and interdisciplinary artistic projects, exploring the connections between music and visual art, architecture, and spatial experience.
