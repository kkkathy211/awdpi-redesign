 Summary:


Recently, there have been frequent reports about unfair treatment toward minority students, particularly Asian American applicants, in admissions processes at Ivy League and other prestigious North American universities. The challenges faced by Asian students in college admissions have become an open secret. To reduce unfair treatment of minorities during admissions, Common Application has adopted a method of hiding racial information, but this is merely a superficial solution that does not address the root problem. However, the growing engagement of Asians in North America may gradually help change this situation. Recently, eight Chinese scientists were elected as members of the U.S. National Academy of Sciences, and Canada is set to open its first Chinese Canadian museum. As graduation season is underway, Asian students traveling abroad should pay extra attention to their personal safety, stay vigilant, and ensure their well-being. The path toward global gender equality remains long and difficult. In Senegal, hip-hop artist Sister LB hopes to empower women through her music and fight against injustices faced by women in her own way. In Guam, although abortion is legal, women still struggle to gain control over their reproductive rights. For global news on crimes against Asians, Asian representation, and gender equality, please refer to this month's newsletter.

Two Asian female high-achieving students on a graduation trip were followed and harassed by a white man they thought was a fellow countryman, who pushed them off a cliff,

resulting in one death and one injury.


On June 14, a brutal murder at Neuschwanstein Castle, a famous tourist site in Germany, drew media attention worldwide. Two Asian female tourists in their early twenties were pushed off a steep 50-meter cliff along a mountain trail by a 30-year-old white male, resulting in one death and one injury. The suspect committed the crime in broad daylight with extremely vicious methods, and many tourists present at the scene witnessed the attack. Subsequent reports pieced together details of the case, sparking outrage among netizens globally. Eva Liu and Kelsey Chang (both names transliterated), American university friends, had just graduated from computer science and computer engineering programs respectively at the University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign.


After graduation, they decided to take a European trip, never expecting to encounter a demon while visiting Neuschwanstein Castle in Germany. The suspect is a white man with a goatee, aged 30, also from the United States. According to information released by police, after Kelsey was pushed off, Eva, now isolated, was sexually assaulted and then pushed down as well. Kelsey, who was pushed first, was miraculously caught by a fallen tree on the cliff and survived with only minor scrapes. Eva, who was sexually assaulted before being pushed, died from her severe injuries after being taken to the hospital. May the deceased rest in peace, may the surviving Kelsey live without fear for the rest of her life, and may the suspect receive the punishment he deserves under the law.

On June 18, a Chinese national shared on Twitter a terrifying incident she experienced during her stay at the luxury Ritz-Carlton resort in the Maldives. In a lengthy post, the 26-year-old Chinese tourist accused resort employee Usham of sexually assaulting her. She also accused the resort management and Maldivian police of covering up the incident and failing to take any coercive measures to investigate her case. The victim shared photos showing her injuries, a photo of the accused Usham, and a copy of the complaint she filed with Maldivian police to substantiate her allegations. Local media reported that Maldivian police stated they had launched an investigation into the allegations and confirmed that necessary samples for the investigation had been collected. Police further stated that no arrests had been made in connection with the case so far. Meanwhile, the Chinese national is receiving treatment at a health center on Thulusdhoo Island in Kaafu Atoll, Maldives.

A 12-year-old girl at a McDonald's in Oakland

was beaten bloody


Recently, a Filipino family who relocated to New Zealand said they felt unsafe after their 12-year-old child was viciously assaulted at a McDonald's in Auckland. The young girl and her three classmates were enjoying a meal and chatting happily at the Glenfield McDonald's when two other girls "mistakenly" believed they were being mocked. They demanded the young girl apologize, and even after she did, they continued to physically attack her. They kicked her legs, pinned her down, threw her to the ground, and repeatedly kicked her face and forehead, causing her to bleed.


After the attack, the young girl tearfully called her mother. When her family arrived at the McDonald's, the girl was bleeding profusely, with blood everywhere. The family had only moved to New Zealand last August and had never experienced such an event in the Philippines. A police spokesperson confirmed they received a report of a serious assault at 1:33 p.m. on Saturday but could not confirm whether they were searching for any suspects or whether any arrests had been made. "There is no further information available at this time," the spokesperson said. The Glenfield McDonald's location did not respond to media outlet Stuff's request for comment.

An Asian pregnant woman in the U.S. was shot four times, resulting in the death of both mother and unborn child


According to reports, at around 11 a.m. local time on the 13th, Eina Kwon (transliterated as Kwon Eun-a), a 34-year-old Asian expectant mother eight months pregnant, was shot multiple times inside a Tesla at a downtown Seattle intersection. She died after being hospitalized, and her baby, delivered by emergency cesarean section, also died shortly afterward. Her husband was also injured by gunfire but is not in life-threatening condition. The 30-year-old gunman has been arrested and is awaiting charges. Police officers quickly located the suspect based on eyewitness descriptions and later identified him as Gosby. Gosby told officers he had a history of mental illness, and responding officers noted he appeared to be in a state of distress at the time of arrest. The semi-automatic firearm used in the shooting of the couple was reported to be a stolen weapon. Eina Kwon and her husband previously ran a restaurant together. Many residents, customers, and local businesses visited to mourn after the tragedy. The restaurant announced it would temporarily suspend operations. On the 16th, the King County prosecutor charged Gosby with first-degree murder, first-degree assault, and second-degree unlawful possession of a firearm.

Women in Senegalese hip-hop music

carving out space for themselves


"No matter what, everyone knows I am a rapper who speaks for women," says Senegalese hip-hop artist Sister LB. "We are seen as the weaker group, but we are the strong group—we are the ones who give life. If I don't amplify women's voices, it would be like suicide for me."


34-year-old Sister LB hopes to empower women through her music and fight against social injustice. In doing so, she has become a key figure in Senegal's thriving community of female rappers and hip-hop artists.


Sister LB has been rapping since she was 13. She released her first solo single, "Ji Gën," in 2019, and said she went solo to ensure more women's voices would be heard and to gain greater control over her own career.


"Ji Gën" means "woman" in Wolof, the most widely spoken language in Senegal. The song tells women that their happiness matters, and their lives matter. Her latest single, "Do Xool," released in March, challenges the traditional expectation that a woman's role in life is solely to bear children. She said: "Women have their own lives. They have other things to do, and we must respect that."

A Delaware state legislator plans to become the first openly transgender member of the U.S. House of Representatives


Delaware State Senator Sarah McBride recently announced she will run for the U.S. House of Representatives seat in Delaware. If elected, she would become the first openly transgender member of the U.S. Congress.


At 32, McBride is no stranger to being a "first": in 2012, as an intern in the Obama administration, she became the first openly transgender person to work in the White House. In 2020, she won a state Senate seat in the Wilmington district with over 70% of the general election vote, becoming the first openly transgender state senator in the United States, and was re-elected last year.


Ms. McBride is also a former national press secretary for the Human Rights Campaign, the country's largest LGBT advocacy organization. She may face some tough challenges in her solidly blue (Democratic) district, but she has ample political capital in the state, bolstered by her relationship with current U.S. President Biden. Perhaps Ms. McBride can leverage this advantage to achieve her goals in next year's election.

Trinidad, in the Caribbean

Is grappling with a "national crisis" of violence


Trinidad and Tobago has seen a record high in the number of women and girls murdered, while police and politicians have failed to address the rising crime rate.


As of May this year, 280 people had been killed in Trinidad and Tobago. With a population of about 1.5 million, this Caribbean nation currently has the sixth-highest crime rate in the world.


Once the wealthiest country in the Caribbean, Trinidad and Tobago has been in turmoil for the past two decades due to mismanagement of oil revenues, corruption, drug trafficking, and surging gun violence.


In this harsh environment, the number of women being killed has risen sharply, and photos of missing women frequently appear on social media. Headlines about shootings emerge every week.


Dr. Gabrielle Hosein, writer and lecturer at the Institute for Gender and Development Studies at the University of the West Indies, says this is a "national crisis" for women. Violence is so widespread here that reducing and preventing it should be treated as a national emergency.

In Guam, abortion is legal,

but difficult to access


Guam, a U.S. territory in the western Pacific, is thousands of miles from the nearest U.S. state. While abortion is legal here, there are no resident physicians trained to perform abortions. A recent court ruling may further reduce women's access to abortion pills, which have already become their only remaining legal option.


For decades, the Pregnancy Control Clinic handled most abortions on Guam, a U.S. territory located 1,600 miles south of Japan. The clinic was housed in a low, beige building around the corner from a bowling alley.


But the doctor who ran the clinic retired seven years ago, and the clinic now appears abandoned. A letter from Dr. Edmund A. Greely is posted on the front door—"My last day seeing patients is November 18, 2016," he wrote. "I suggest you begin looking for a new doctor as soon as possible."


Although abortion is legal in Guam during the first 13 weeks of pregnancy, the last doctor qualified to provide abortion services left the island in 2018. The nearest abortion clinic on U.S. territory is in Hawaii, an eight-hour flight away. An ongoing court case could soon cut off access to abortion pills, the last legal pathway to abortion for most women in Guam. This hinges on whether the U.S. Ninth Circuit Court of Appeals will reinstate a territorial law requiring women to see a doctor in person to obtain the pills.

Eight Chinese scientists newly elected

to the U.S. National Academy of Sciences


The U.S. National Academy of Sciences (NAS) recently announced its new members, including eight Chinese scientists elected as Members of the National Academy of Sciences: Chen Gang (MIT), Lin Xihong (Harvard University), Pan Duojia (University of Texas Southwestern Medical Center), Dai Biguan (Cornell University), Wang Michelle D (Cornell University), Xiao Shuhai (Virginia Tech), Zeng Hongkui (Allen Institute for Brain Science), and Zhou Min (UCLA).


Six of the eight members studied at well-known universities in mainland China: Chen Gang graduated from Huazhong Institute of Technology (now Huazhong University of Science and Technology) in 1983 and earned a master's degree in power engineering in 1987; Lin Xihong received her bachelor's degree in applied mathematics from Tsinghua University in 1989 and has served as a Changjiang Scholar Distinguished Visiting Professor at Tsinghua since 2007; Pan Duojia earned a bachelor's degree in biochemistry from Peking University in 1988; Michelle D. Wang completed her undergraduate studies in nuclear physics at Nanjing University and pursued her Ph.D. at the Institute of Physics, Chinese Academy of Sciences; Xiao Shuhai graduated from Peking University with a degree in geology in 1988 and earned a master's degree in geology in 1991; Zhou Min received her bachelor's degree in English language and literature from Sun Yat-sen University in 1982.

Ottawa, Canada holds a national reflection ceremony

marking the 100th anniversary of the Chinese Exclusion Act,

with about 3,000 Chinese Canadians gathering to call for action

On June 23, Canada's national reflection ceremony marking the 100th anniversary of the Chinese Exclusion Act was held in the Senate chamber in Ottawa, the nation's capital. Mary Ng, Canada's Minister of International Trade, Export Promotion, Small Business and Economic Development, expressed pride in the achievements of Chinese Canadians across various fields.


On June 24, Canadian Senator Yuen Pau Woo delivered an appeal to approximately 3,000 participants at a large gathering in front of Parliament Hill in Ottawa, reflecting on the centenary of the Chinese Exclusion Act. Senators Victor Oh, Lillian Dyck, Member of Parliament Chandni Chopra, and representatives from Chinese Canadian communities across the country also spoke out, calling for a more inclusive, diverse, and discrimination-free Canada. Organized jointly by Chinese Canadian community groups from across the country, this rally was the largest event in a recent series of centenary reflection activities.

22-year-old Chinese American guard Isaiah Wang from New Jersey

enters the NBA

Chinese American basketball player Isaiah Wang was born on January 28, 2001, in New Jersey. His father is Terrence Wang and his mother is Rachel. Selected by the Indiana Pacers as the 55th pick in the 2023 NBA Draft, Isaiah Wang may become a target for naturalization as a Chinese national team player. With a Chinese great-grandfather, he meets the basic criteria set by the Chinese Basketball Association for naturalized players.


Isaiah Wang showed strong basketball talent in high school. In his junior year, he led Bishop McDevitt Catholic High School to win the Philadelphia High School League regular season championship and was named MVP. In his senior year, he led the team to back-to-back MVP honors and further secured a state championship. During the 2022–23 season, Isaiah Wang led the University of Miami to the Final Four, and although they lost to the University of Connecticut in the semifinals, he was named to the All-ACC team and was awarded ACC Player of the Year.

Canada's first Chinese Canadian Museum to open soon

Canada's first Chinese Canadian Museum will open on July 1, commemorating the 100th anniversary of the Chinese Immigration Act of 1923 through a national opening exhibition titled "Seeking Traces: The 1923 Chinese Exclusion Act," while also highlighting the life and evolution of Chinese Canadian communities in Canada.


For a long time, the lived experiences of Chinese Canadians have not been well known in mainstream society. This exhibition documents the difficult conditions early Chinese immigrants faced under systemic discrimination, their resilience and perseverance, as well as their cultural heritage and lessons learned.


Through its exhibitions, educational programs, and special events, the Chinese Canadian Museum will become an important part of Vancouver's Chinatown and Canada's cultural landscape. Looking ahead, it aims to foster respect, understanding, inclusion, and collaboration, serving as a bridge across cultures, communities, and generations.
