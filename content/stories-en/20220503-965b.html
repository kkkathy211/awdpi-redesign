There are voices claiming that Generation Z is the latest "lost generation," while others say they are the surging tide of the future. But what exactly are they doing, and what role are they playing in social issues?


On the issue of domestic violence, which has seen rising attention in recent years, Generation Z is far from silent.


No. 1 

When idol groups perform "anti-domestic violence"



"In the urban jungle of millions of households, there are always people living in shadows.


When children witness domestic violence and endure fear and anxiety, how can they protect themselves and their mothers?"


In the stage performance *Matriarchal Society* by TF Family's third-generation trainees, four teenagers with an average age of just 14 brought the heavy topic of "domestic violence" onto the stage, shining a light on its darkest, most hidden corners.


Two families, two pairs of brothers, each reenact experiences of domestic violence on opposite sides of the stage.

At the beginning of the performance, the time shared between mothers and children is warm and beautiful, but the wounds on their mouths, nose bridges, and arms seem to suggest that this is merely the calm before the storm.


When the father stumbles home drunk, the atmosphere shifts dramatically. Facing the irate father, the mother silently carries out household chores while the children huddle together, too afraid to speak. Even so, they cannot appease him—he begins punching and kicking the mother.



The contrast between warm and cold lighting on either side of the stage also hints at the two families' different attitudes toward domestic violence—endurance or resistance—and foreshadows their divergent fates.

In the end, only one family's child bravely stands up, resists the abuser, and calls the police.


The other family chooses silence and endurance. Everything appears to return to normal, seemingly unchanged from the beginning, but soon may fall back into the cycle of domestic violence.



"Only by bravely speaking out is there a chance to become strong."



Meanwhile, the seniors (members of Teens in Times: Song Yaxuan, Liu Yaowen, Yan Haoxiang, He Junlin) also used original rap lyrics in the same themed performance to express their reflections on domestic violence:


A raised hand, a mistake committed

Once an angel, now turned demon


You call it love, yet she bears bruises around her eyes

She tries to resist 

To let her child live openly, her cheeks have already turned yellow with injury

She doesn't wish

For her child's mental harm, disguising pressure as happiness



Image source: Screenshot from Weibo video


The official concept description from TF Family is truly unique: "The overall presentation evokes the texture of the ocean—an oppressive ocean, but also a healing ocean, and ultimately a free ocean.


The four teenagers are like a small boat adrift in the deep sea, a faint light, using their small yet firm strength to speak up for women."


If the younger trainees' performance focused specifically on "domestic violence," then the members of Teens in Times cast their care toward broader social issues, highlighting the unfair scrutiny women face in today's society, such as being pressured into marriage with demands for exorbitant dowries, or being blamed after sexual assault.


For these young performers, they hope to convey the inequalities they observe in life through original artistic expression. "Using our small strength to let people know, to spread the idea of respecting women and equal rights for all," said He Junlin of Teens in Times in an interview.


Once focused solely on singing and dancing, these young men now prove through their actions that Generation Z has never stopped fulfilling their social responsibilities..


The promotion of gender equality awareness and women's empowerment requires not only women themselves to act, and not only youth to "rise above the cold and move forward," but everyone in society to shoulder this shared responsibility.


Even if the road ahead remains long and there is still much to change, as long as we keep moving forward, everything will develop in a better direction—just as the lyrics at the end of the rap say:: "She knows she has suffered much disrespect, yet still holds hope."



No. 2 

"The 'Xiao Juans' are real, living individuals"



Songs, dances, short films—these diverse forms are how Generation Z expresses their thoughts on social issues, adapting to the times and reaching the public. The joys and sorrows on stage are grounded in reality; taking domestic violence as an example, the dance piece *Xiao Juan (a pseudonym)* stands out as an outstanding work.


((Official promotional image of the work – authorized)


"Heavy footsteps, a lowered head, a high-necked shirt covering the neck and body."

This is the impression Xiao Juan leaves on people.

"I'm so afraid. I don't dare go home. He's going to beat me again."

This is Xiao Juan's repeated monologue and cry.


The place he calls home was once her newlywed chamber, filled with hopes and dreams of love and family. But later, she realized it was actually a prison, a living hell, and ultimately became her funeral hall.

Countless hands reach toward her—through oppression, persuasion, and doubt—urging her to close her eyes and silently endure.

From homes to news headlines, what is that invisible gaze?

Is it fear? Resentment? Sorrow? Or endless tears and struggles?

We do not know.


The dance piece *Xiao Juan (a pseudonym)* begins here. Xiao Juan's story unfolds through text and sound, brought to life through dance.


The nightingale turns mute, the vibrant becomes a puppet—under various forms of domestic violence, Xiao Juan gradually turns into a marionette.

She learns to tread carefully, watching every expression, afraid that one misstep will bring fists, acid, or gasoline rushing toward her.

She has tried to resist, but physical and mental pain, along with control tactics, have gradually emptied her life. She even begins to grow accustomed to this existence.



Oh God, have you seen my suffering? Have you heard my cries?

The sunlight that falls on me every morning isn't light—it's just another repetition of the same nightmare.

I want to escape, but I never succeed. I grow increasingly hopeless, pretending this is just how life is.


Suddenly, I hear someone speaking.

Wait, footsteps.

It's him! He's coming again!

I frantically grab the person beside me, begging them to help me, save me.

Didn't they say someone would come to help me? Didn't they say someone would speak for me? Didn't they say someone would rescue me?

You'll stand by my side, right?

But they don't. They just stand silently, avoiding my gaze. I can see it—they are afraid too.


The next second, he rushes over, grabs me by the collar, dragging me along.

The pain from last time still lingers, and new slaps come one after another.

I scream and struggle, as if that could make him stop.



Passersby watch the spectacle with interest, while others dare not look or refuse to look.

They all silently sew their own lips shut.


I run desperately, trying to break free, to escape this nightmarish life.

Xiao Juan's story seems to end here. Whether the fleeing girl finds a happy ending, we do not know. But what we do know is this: Xiao Juan has never been just one person's name.

It isn't even really a name—it's a pseudonym.

"Juan" means graceful, delicate, and bright.


Yet when the high-necked shirt is finally lifted, countless scars reveal the loss of that brightness.

"Know my name. Remember my name."

The Xiao Juans uncover their scars and look back at you and me.

The same high-necked shirts, the same scars, the same tragedy.



They may have considered ending it all, or still wait for a sudden ray of light, or may already be a handful of dust, fodder for others' conversations.

In the end, they pull the high-necked shirt back on and retreat into that so-called "home."

Which will arrive first—redemption or a gravestone?


This dance piece powerfully presents the story of Xiao Juan (a pseudonym) in a deeply moving way. Its narrative-rich choreography, emotional depth, and strong sense of social responsibility leave audiences profoundly shaken and touched. After seven weeks and over two hundred hours of intense rehearsal by the performers, the piece won the 2021 JD Sports Cup Street Dance Championship.


"What is hidden from the Xiao Juans is not only their names, not only their suffering, but also their dignity as human beings, their joys and sorrows, their once-beautiful dreams and hopes for love," said singer Tan Weiwei.

The voices of the Xiao Juans must be heard, and their reasonable demands must be acknowledged and fulfilled.

This path is full of thorns and bloodshed—never easy or simple—but no matter what, remember this:

Those scars are not the shame of the Xiao Juans, but the shame of the abusers.




No. 3

     Global Status of Domestic Violence



The issue of domestic violence, which draws significant attention from Generation Z youth, is a concrete manifestation of various forms of "inequality" persisting in society, reflected within family life.


"Home," which should be a sanctuary in everyone's heart, instead becomes, for victims of domestic violence, a dark nest where crises erupt and escape is impossible.


With the global outbreak of COVID-19, rising unemployment, increased psychological anxiety, and financial pressure have made family conflicts more likely to escalate. Social isolation measures have forced victims to live in close quarters with their abusers… All these factors have turned domestic violence into an invisible time bomb, quietly detonating around the world.


At the onset of the COVID-19 pandemic, domestic violence cases against women in Brazil surged by 50%, nearly 30% in countries such as France and New Zealand, around 25% in the United Kingdom, and approximately 20% in Spain. According to a survey by the European Institute for Gender Equality, the average daily number of domestic violence hotline calls during the COVID-19 outbreak was significantly higher than in the same period in previous years. Since the beginning of this year, the global pandemic situation remains severe, and domestic violence cases under the shadow of the pandemic continue to rise.


Domestic (intimate relationship) violence against women has long been a serious global social issue. Research and data indicate that women are more likely than men or other groups to experience prolonged, more severe physical and psychological abuse, as well as violence that can result in serious injury or death.


On July 12, 2021, Italy defeated England to win the European Championship, yet women in the UK felt deeply fearful—some even began organizing self-help efforts, with women who had the means proactively offering shelters to protect others at risk of domestic violence.


This is because as early as 2018, the UK's National Centre for Domestic Violence released data showing that after England lost to Croatia, the national domestic violence rate spiked by 38%.


"If England gets beaten, so will she."

No. 4

     How to define domestic violence




Domestic violence incidents appearing in the public eye are merely the tip of the iceberg, with the "silent majority" still hidden beneath the surface. When we attempt to explore the reasons behind this silence, one question inevitably arises—"What is the definition of domestic violence?"


The World Health Organization (WHO) has provided a clear definition of "Intimate Partner Violence" (IPV): intimate partner violence refers to behaviors by current or former intimate partners that cause physical, sexual, or psychological harm to victims, including physical assault, forced sexual acts, psychological abuse, and controlling behaviors.


It should be noted that domestic violence is not limited to physical harm and assault, but also includes emotional abuse, coercion, and humiliating behaviors. We can use the following questions to assess whether we are currently experiencing or have previously experienced domestic violence.

| TIPS |

1) Are you afraid of your partner most of the time? Do you avoid discussing certain topics out of fear?

 

2) Do you feel that you are being treated unfairly or harmed?

 

3) Have you ever thought, "It's my fault"?

 

4) Do you feel numb or helpless? Do you feel unsure about how to change your partner's behavior?

 

5) Does your partner criticize, humiliate, blame, ignore your achievements, or belittle your abilities?

 

6) Has your partner ever harmed you or made threats against you?

 

7) Does your partner have a bad and unpredictable temper?

 

8) Does your partner threaten to commit suicide if you leave? Do they threaten you with losing custody of your children?

 

9) Does your partner show excessive jealousy, strong controlling tendencies, prevent you from contacting family or friends, or restrict your spending, internet use, or phone calls?


At the same time, abusers may promise to change or temporarily stop violent behaviors, but this does not mean they will truly change.


If the abuser exhibits any of the following behaviors, it indicates they have not made substantive changes, and your safety remains at risk.


| TIPS |

1) The abuser apologizes and promises to reform, but repeats the abusive behavior after some time.

 

2) The abuser downplays the severity of their violence.

 

3) The abuser shifts blame onto others, including you.

 

4) The abuser says they deserve a second chance.

 

5) The abuser only agrees to let you seek outside help under strong pressure, in order to control their own behavior.

 

6) The abuser pressures you to make decisions about the relationship.


When you determine that the abuser will not change, you can take the following measures to ensure your safety:


| TIPS |

1. Seek help from outside. Legal action is the primary means of combating domestic violence. Victims should report domestic violence incidents to local police when experiencing or witnessing abuse. When making a report, it is best to use a phone the abuser does not know about.

 

2. Plan a safety evacuation strategy. Prepare an emergency kit, ideally stored in a place easily accessible when leaving but unlikely to be discovered by other family members.

 

3. Plan escape routes and flee danger promptly. There should be multiple exits leading to safe areas around the house, no items that could be used as weapons (such as kitchen knives), and ideally, access to communication devices like phones. Practice escape routes in advance.

 

4. Collect evidence of domestic violence. This includes, but is not limited to: call logs, text messages, exchanged emails and attachments, messages from communication apps such as WhatsApp, WeChat, or Line, information from social media platforms such as Facebook, Instagram, Snapchat, or Weibo, and photo/audio/video records.

 

5. Protect your legal rights and interests. When living in a domestic violence/abuse environment, it is recommended to consider protecting your legal rights and interests through various means in advance, including property rights and benefits that belong to your share.

 

6. Post-evacuation precautions. Leaving the home does not guarantee absolute safety. You should try to leave the area or city if possible. If such a change is difficult to implement, you should alter your daily routines (such as changing your phone number) and avoid disclosing personal information, including your address, to others.

Avoicers from Generation Z




"A violence-free family life is a right for everyone, requiring the collective effort of all." We should anticipate that domestic violence will continue to exist for a long time to come, and addressing it remains a challenging and long-term mission.


The Avoice Overseas Chinese Women Protection Project is jointly implemented by more than 400 young volunteers from around the world. Avoicers from Generation Z are committed to advancing the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals, eliminating gender-based violence, and promoting gender equality. Through implementing public welfare projects, participating in academic research, and engaging in international exchanges and cooperation, they aim to share China's stories with the international community, demonstrate the responsibility and action of Chinese youth, amplify the voices of Chinese youth, and work towards building a "home of love and equality for overseas Chinese women."
