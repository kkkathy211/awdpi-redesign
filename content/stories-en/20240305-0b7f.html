In today's fast-paced and diverse society, UK-based women's aid organisations Shakti Women's Aid have emerged as an undeniable force, dedicated to supporting and assisting Black and minority ethnic women in the UK who suffer from domestic violence. This volunteer-driven organisation, driven by a deeply resonant mission, provides a safe haven for women from non-European countries, including those from Africa, South Asia, and Arab regions. Shakti Women's Aid reaches out to victims facing the shadows of domestic violence, honour-based abuse, forced marriage, sexual harassment, and sexual assault, helping them address challenges ranging from financial hardship to legal assistance, safeguarding their safety and dignity.
















Shakti Women's Aid

1. Redefining the Narrative: Speaking Up for "Domestic Violence" Across Different Perspectives


In defining the scope of domestic violence, Shakti Women's Aid adopts a broader perspective, extending beyond spousal violence alone. Within South Asian cultures, family relationships are complex, and acts of domestic violence may come from any family member. This expanded definition reflects a deep understanding of cultural differences and a commitment to addressing victims' comprehensive needs.





"The concept of 'family' for women is not simply about 'marrying out' and moving into a new household. Most women live with their husband's parents after marriage, and may even reside with his siblings and their families—essentially living in a joint family. Typically, the husband is the perpetrator of domestic violence, but within a joint family, it could be someone else." Joint families are common in Pakistan and India, and many of the women supported in the UK live in such arrangements. In addition, Shakti Women's Aid has assisted female victims from China, who are often taught to respect elders. Violence originating from within a joint family "may be related to culture, but it is difficult to determine the exact causal relationship." explained the head of Shakti Women's Aid.





Domestic violence does not only occur within joint family households. Some women, even before marriage, may not have a husband to inflict abuse, but could already be suffering harm from those around them—such as through forced marriage. Currently, the Scottish government has included such cases within the definition of domestic violence. It is precisely for this reason that Shakti Women's Aid stresses that perpetrators can be mothers, uncles, brothers, or any other family member.


Domestic violence can be direct or indirect. However, in actual cases, most female victims tend to only mention their ex-husbands or current partners, failing to associate abuse with other family members. Unlike other mutual aid organisations that remain superficial, Shakti emphasises reframing the experience for victims, never limiting itself to the traditional definition of "domestic violence." The organisation's leader says this is precisely the meaning of its existence.




"We once supported a woman living in Edinburgh with her family, who wanted to leave Edinburgh due to domestic violence. Initially, she sought help from mainstream white-led organisations, but they generally believed only spousal violence qualified as domestic violence, which did not match her situation—she was ultimately turned away. It is precisely because of this that society needs organisations like ours to advocate for policy change and meet victims' needs."



Shakti Women's Aid

2. Diverse Choices, Empowering Women's Inner Strength



The organisation is named " Shakti Shakti ", meaning " women's power ". The name draws inspiration from the Hindu mother goddess, symbolising a divine force that destroys evil. This name perfectly reflects the organisation's core values and mission—



to awaken and empower women's inner strength, helping them escape the shadows of violence and oppression. To better provide support, and based on deep insights into victims' psychology, all staff at Shakti Women's Aid are women. The leader explains that during the process of seeking help, victims often prefer to communicate with those who can empathise with them, which offers deeper emotional safety and trust. Through this approach,



Shakti creates an environment of support and understanding, enabling women to feel less alone when facing hardship. "When living abroad, given the choice, one naturally feels more comfortable approaching people who are similar to oneself. After all, if both parties speak the same language, we don't have to worry about whether the other person understands us—this brings greater inner security." However, some service users may prefer to speak with someone from a different background, while others express concerns about receiving help motivated by financial gain or fear of privacy breaches.


Shakti Women's Aid

Therefore, understanding service users, identifying required staff, and offering diverse choices is Shakti Women's Aid's approach.


3. Compassionate Care, Illuminating Hope and Change for "Them"



In practical measures to support victims, Shakti Women's Aid employs multiple strategies, including collaborating with other voluntary organisations, avoiding direct use of the term "domestic violence", and hosting various themed workshops and seminars. These initiatives aim to reduce psychological barriers for victims and enhance their self-protection capabilities by providing information and resources. "We consistently avoid using the term 'domestic violence' and instead, together with our partners, run workshops and seminars on topics such as 'women's safety', 'women's health', or 'women's wellbeing'... We conduct training because many people don't know how—or are afraid—to start conversations with victims. Victims themselves also fear talking to strangers due to various concerns, such as language barriers, feelings of shame, or fear of being separated from their children…"



Through such training, Shakti and its partners offer services that maximise empathy and resonance with victims. When asked about potential challenges, the leader stated that despite facing issues such as the lack of official recognition of the definition of "domestic violence" and funding shortages, Shakti Women's Aid remains committed to its mission, striving to provide essential support and protection for Black and minority ethnic women in the UK.




Through relentless effort and collaboration, Shakti Women's Aid stands like an unextinguished lamp, continuously illuminating the future for more female victims, bringing hope and change to them, their families, and their communities.
