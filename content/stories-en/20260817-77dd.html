Many girls are interested in AI, technology, and product design, yet rarely have the opportunity to systematically understand what STEM actually includes, how this knowledge applies to real life, and how they can continue exploring it in the future.


From August 23 to 27, 2026, the "Her × Digital Future" Innovation Camp will offer a 5-day online project-based STEM learning program for girls at the middle school level.


The program is initiated by the United Nations Development Programme (UNDP) China, hosted by 1kgBox, implemented by the Horizon Global Youth Development Program, and co-organized by AWDPI Asian Women Development Plan International. This Innovation Camp is a public welfare project; participants do not need to pay any fees.


Over five days, students will begin by learning about STEM and sustainable development, gradually engaging with artificial intelligence, community research, user research, product design, market analysis, proposal writing, and public presentation, and will complete a team project under mentorship focused on "How digital technology can promote sustainable development."


What kind of innovation camp is this?


This Innovation Camp adopts a small-group, project-based teaching model. It plans to recruit 8–15 girls, with each group consisting of 3–5 members, and each group will be continuously guided by 1–2 mentors.


Students will go through a relatively complete project process over the five days:

Identifying a problem → Conducting research → Analyzing needs → Designing a product → Considering business models → Finalizing a proposal → Creating a PPT → Public pitch


Course content includes:

  • STEM and the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs)

  • Basic principles and applications of artificial intelligence

  • AI writing, AI art, and AI image generation

  • User research and community investigation

  • Questionnaire design and data collection

  • Product design and minimum viable product (MVP)

  • Market research, branding, and business models

  • AI-assisted programming and product design

  • Business proposal writing

  • PPT creation and public pitching


We hope that by the end of the five days, students will not just have "attended some lectures," but will have a more concrete understanding of the various fields within STEM, and will know how a real-world problem can be observed, researched, analyzed, and transformed into a solution.


What exactly will be learned each day?


DAY 1 | August 23

Getting to know STEM, and recognizing problems around us

  • Opening ceremony and team collaboration: understanding the overall curriculum, learning objectives, and group cooperation methods.

  • Introduction to STEM: exploring the connections between science, technology, engineering, and mathematics.

  • Sustainable development and daily life: learning about the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) and their applications in everyday life.

  • STEM and social issues: discussing how technology can respond to real-world challenges through case studies on gender equality, marginalized groups, and sustainable product design.

  • Digital cultural heritage and cultural outreach cases: observing how STEM contributes to the preservation and dissemination of traditional culture.

DAY 2 | August 24

From AI basics to learning how to conduct research

  • Applications and fundamental principles of artificial intelligence.

  • AI art, AI writing, and AI image generation.

  • User research and community investigation: learning basic methods such as observation, interviews, note-taking, and data collection.

  • Understanding research ethics and learning to identify problems from real needs.

  • Organizing research findings and further clarifying the direction of the group project.

DAY 3 | August 25

Transforming problems into product solutions

  • Market research: functional deconstruction using mobile apps as an example.

  • User surveys: learning how to create and distribute questionnaires.

  • Product design: understanding how to build a minimum viable product (MVP) from 0 to 1.

  • Fundamentals of marketing, branding, and business models.

  • Continuing to refine and deepen the group project under mentor guidance.

DAY 4 | August 26

Organizing ideas into a presentable proposal

  • Using AI tools for programming and product design.

  • Understanding the structure and key points of writing a business proposal.

  • Creating and refining the pitch PPT.

  • Group mock pitch, followed by adjustments based on mentor feedback.

DAY 5 | August 27

Public pitch and expert feedback

  • Team public pitch: presenting the solution, explaining the problem, and sharing the approach.

  • On-site expert comments and suggestions.

  • Closing ceremony and distribution of participation certificates.

What can girls take away after the five days?


01 A complete team project proposal

Completing the entire process from research and analysis to solution design around a real social issue.

02 A set of project pitch PPT slides

Students actively participate in structuring project logic, organizing content, and visual presentation.

03 A public pitch experience

Explaining the team's identified problem, analysis process, and solution to experts and receiving on-site feedback.

04 A set of reusable methods

Including the use of AI tools, user research, questionnaire and interview techniques, product design, market analysis, project writing, and public speaking.

05 An official participation certificate

Students who complete all courses and successfully participate in the project pitch will receive a participation certificate jointly issued by the United Nations Development Programme (UNDP) and 1kgBox.

06 A genuine team collaboration experience

Fully experiencing group project cooperation from team formation, discussion, task division, proposal revision, to joint presentation.
