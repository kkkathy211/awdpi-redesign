In 2025, on April 8, a molestation incident occurred in a carriage of Line 1 of the Shanghai Metro.

  • On April 8, during evening rush hour on Shanghai Metro Line 1, a man sexually molested a female passenger. The woman and surrounding passengers noticed, recorded video, and reported the incident to police.

  • On April 9, the Shanghai Public Security Bureau issued a statement confirming the man had been administratively detained.

  • On April 10, false information spread online claiming the man and the victim knew each other and had conspired to stage the act for internet attention.

  • On April 11, the Shanghai Public Security Bureau issued another statement clarifying that this claim was a rumor, and the rumor-monger would be dealt with according to law.


Unsurprisingly, online discussions were still filled with attacks on the female passenger's appearance and clothing, disclosures of the man's family and employment, and expressions of empathy for the man's so-called "social death"; there was no shortage of male-chauvinist self-media accounts using this incident to launch new platforms.

This was not a staged performance for internet traffic, nor was it a case of social death.

This was molestation and sexual harassment. This was an incident of sexual harassment in a public space that demands widespread attention and discussion.

At the same time, nearly every woman around us has experienced sexual harassment—some in public places, some in enclosed spaces; some involving groping, some exhibitionism, and some revolting verbal harassment; some from strangers, some from acquaintances, even family members.

We need to talk more about sexual harassment, and talk about it loudly, so it cannot be diverted by other topics or twisted by others.

In this episode of *MoreforGood*, we invited a legal professional specializing in gender-based violence and a psychotherapist with over ten years of experience to discuss how we might react, and what we can do, when facing sexual harassment in public—as bystanders or as victims.

Program reference materials

  • "System for Eliminating Sexual Harassment in the Workplace (Reference Text)"

Article 2: "Sexual harassment as referred to in this system means any act that violates another person's will and causes them discomfort related to sex, through language, expressions, gestures, text, images, video, audio, links, or any other means, regardless of whether the perpetrator has any intent to harass or other improper purpose."


Article 7: "The following behaviors are prohibited within the workplace: (1) unwelcome verbal teasing, telling dirty jokes, sharing personal sexual experiences, or using unwelcome nicknames; (2) intentionally touching, bumping, or kissing another person's sensitive body parts, inappropriately exposing one's private body parts, or making sexual contact or touching oneself in the presence of others; (3) sending or directly showing pornographic, provocative texts, images, audio, video, etc., via information means such as WeChat, text messages, or emails; (4) placing obscene images or advertisements around the workplace that cause embarrassment; (5) repeatedly expressing or conveying sexually suggestive content through stalking, harassing messages, or sending items; (6) any other acts of sexual harassment."
