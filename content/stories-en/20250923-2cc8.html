Domestic violence is not just physical—it often hides in language, emotions, economic control, and subtle power dynamics.


The film *Kim Ji-young, Born 1982*traces the life of an ordinary woman, gently revealing many forms of oppression that are often "invisible." It presents not only the realities of South Korean society but also reflects the shared experiences of many women.


  • Being ignored and invalidated at home,

  • facing systemic barriers in the workplace,

  • and having choices stripped away in intimate relationships—


these scenes represent the very issues we need to bravely face and discuss in real life.


This film screening and discussion is honored to invite Tina Ji, Partner at Central Land Lawyers, as our guest.


@Tina Ji


She will use her professional legal perspective to guide us through three key scenes from the film, delving into the issue of domestic violence prevention,:


the boundaries of verbal abuse,

  • and whether persistent verbal belittlement, negation, and sarcasm in daily life constitute domestic violence under the law.


Evidence and seeking help:

  • If someone experiences domestic violence in Australia, how should they collect, document, and preserve evidence?

  • What legal aid options and protection mechanisms are available in Australia?


Economic control and employment rights:

  • Does a partner or family member restricting a woman's employment qualify as "economic abuse"?

  • Under Australian law, how can women protect their right to work?
