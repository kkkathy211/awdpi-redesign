Global data on femicide due to domestic violence and the International Day for the Elimination of Violence against Women

During the 2025 High-Level Political Forum (HLPF), UN Women led global action, placing gender equality at the core of the sustainable development agenda. The forum conducted a comprehensive review of Sustainable Development Goal 5 (SDG 5), emphasizing its role as a key driver for achieving the 2030 Agenda. UN Women called for urgent action to fill the annual $420 billion gender equality funding gap in developing countries, and to increase investment in care systems, gender-inclusive policies, and support for vulnerable women.


The forum also marked the 30th anniversary of the Beijing Declaration, hosting a multi-stakeholder hearing on July 24 that brought together feminist leaders and government representatives to push for accelerated reforms toward gender equality. Additionally, UN Women launched a global alliance to support women facing caste and racial discrimination (such as Dalit women in South Asia and Quilombola communities in Brazil), and organized a series of side events discussing land rights, climate action, and ending gender-based violence. Sport as a catalyst for equality was also included in discussions, highlighting diverse pathways for collaborative action.


By reviewing SDG 5, calling for the $420 billion funding gap to be filled, and linking actions across sport, anti-violence, and other fields, the forum directly drove policy change and resource allocation, providing critical momentum for accelerating global gender equality, especially benefiting marginalized women.

Afghan women's resistance in the dark: survival and hope under Taliban rule

Under four years of Taliban rule, Afghan women's rights have suffered systematic deprivation: 80% of young women are out of school and unemployed, no women in the cabinet, and over 80 bans have excluded women from public life. The freedom to run for president or attend university has vanished entirely, making it the world's most severe gender crisis.


Yet women's resistance has never ceased: they secretly run online businesses, serve as humanitarian workers, and are supported by UN Women through 130 rural literacy committees (such as the Nuristan province project) and over 200 women's organizations at the grassroots level. The "Rebuild the Women's Movement" initiative launched in 2024 provides skills training and survival support to 16,000 women across 34 provinces, preserving resilience and flickers of hope in the darkness.


This report exposes the collapse of women's rights under Taliban oppression, while highlighting grassroots resistance and international support, igniting urgent global attention to gender equality, preserving the flame of resistance for Afghan women, and pushing for international intervention.

Kanem steps down as Executive Director of UNFPA: a battle to protect the dignity of millions of "forgotten girls"

Dr. Natalia Kanem, Executive Director of the United Nations Population Fund (UNFPA), concluded her eight-year tenure, having used the symbol of the "ten-year-old girl" to represent the rights crisis faced by marginalized women globally, leading the agency to breakthroughs against the odds. During her term, she drove the agency's budget growth to $1.7 billion, covering over 150 countries: training hundreds of thousands of midwives, distributing billions of contraceptive supplies, and delivering "dignity kits" in conflict zones like Gaza and Sudan to protect women's basic needs.


She reshaped the agency's intellectual leadership, refuting the "population collapse" narrative and revealing that delayed youth fertility is driven by economic pressures; she also brought together religious leaders to jointly oppose harmful practices such as female genital mutilation. At her departure, she reaffirmed: "Only when the most vulnerable girl succeeds, does a better world truly arrive."


Kanem's use of the "ten-year-old girl" symbol focused attention on the plight of marginalized women; "dignity kits" in conflict zones redefined gender-sensitive humanitarian aid standards; data-driven challenges to fertility rate myths advanced policy reform; and collaboration with religious leaders to end FGM set a resilient, practical model for global gender equality.

350-year gender barrier broken: Imperial College's Doherty becomes UK's first female Royal Astronomer

On July 30, 2025, King Charles III formally appointed Professor Michele Doherty, space physicist at Imperial College London, as Royal Astronomer, ending a 350-year history since the position's establishment in 1675, during which 15 men held the role. Doherty also serves as Executive Chair of the UK's Science and Technology Facilities Council. Her leadership of the "Jupiter Icy Moons Explorer" mission is probing liquid water in the outer solar system, and she previously contributed to NASA's Cassini mission, which discovered potential for life on Enceladus. She responded modestly to her groundbreaking appointment: "I hope to inspire more women to pursue science." Both of the UK's Royal Astronomer positions are now held by women, with Scotland's Royal Astronomer, Catherine Heymans, calling this change a reflection of academic progress.


Doherty shattered 350 years of male monopoly, proving scientific authority is unrelated to gender; her achievements in deep-space exploration and her humble demeanor have redefined role models in the field, forcing academia to dismantle structural biases and inspiring women worldwide to break through ceilings in STEM.

Chinese national severely injured in iron rod attack on Tokyo street, suspect flees by car, police in pursuit

At 9 a.m. on July 31, two Chinese men were attacked with iron rods by four unidentified men on the street in Kanda-Sudachō, Chiyoda Ward, Tokyo, suffering serious head injuries. Witnesses reported the attackers wore work uniforms and fled toward Akihabara in a vehicle after the assault. The victims stated they had no prior relationship with the attackers and were not robbed. Police discovered the suspect vehicle (with Kansai license plate) in a parking lot in Adachi Ward, 9 kilometers away, on the same morning and are actively pursuing the suspects. The incident occurred in a densely commercial area, and the motive remains under investigation.


This case exposes the security risks faced by Asians overseas; unprovoked attacks intensify community-wide fear. The incident sounds an alarm, underscoring the urgent need for cross-border cooperation to strengthen public area surveillance, strictly punish hate crimes, and build a safety net for ethnic minorities.

Racial discrimination incident in Toronto park: white boys shout "destroy your country," parents' argument escalates conflict

A netizen reported a racial discrimination incident at Ashton Meadows Park in Markham, Canada: a group of white boys aged 6 to 8 shouted at children of other ethnicities, "We are Team Canada, we will destroy your country," accompanied by toy-snatching and shoving. When other parents intervened, the perpetrators' parents not only failed to stop them but engaged in a heated argument with present parents, revealing classic "passive-aggressive racism." This child racial bullying incident strikes at the core of Asian community safety concerns: "destroy your country"-style hate speech can easily trigger targeted violence, and parental tolerance further fuels hostility in public spaces. Asians must push for enhanced patrols in high-risk areas, rapid hate crime reporting mechanisms, and anti-discrimination legal education for children to strengthen personal safety defenses.

Chinese woman visiting relatives killed at Costco: Porsche-riding robber shoots her, escapes to Africa via legal loophole, finally captured

In January 2024, 67-year-old Chinese woman Huang Mingyuan, visiting the U.S. to reunite with family, was targeted by robbers in a Costco parking lot in Tacoma, Washington, while loading groceries with her sister. Two suspects drove a stolen Porsche toward them and, brandishing guns, robbed them as they loaded their vehicle. Huang Mingyuan fought back to protect her sister and was shot in the chest by 20-year-old suspect Salman Haji, dying at the scene.


After the incident, Haji exploited a delay in the police manhunt, was briefly penalized and released for driving a damaged Honda, then fled overseas, reaching Africa within five days. The FBI spent one year tracking him across borders; the suspect was eventually captured in Kenya and extradited back to the U.S. He is now detained in King County Jail alongside his 19-year-old accomplice Abdi, both facing charges including first-degree murder, with bail set at $5 million and $6 million respectively.


This case reveals how elderly Asian individuals have become "hunted targets" for violent criminals—this 67-year-old Chinese woman was fatally attacked due to her ethnic identity. Security flaws in parking areas and delayed judicial responses expose systemic protection gaps, underscoring the urgent need for targeted legislation to strengthen hate crime sentencing, implement one-touch emergency alert systems in high-risk locations, and establish transnational joint prevention mechanisms to end the cycle of targeted violence against Asians.

Former Italian Prime Minister Prodi: Openness and win-win cooperation are the foundation of China-EU prosperity; differences should not be barriers to collaboration

Romano Prodi, former Prime Minister of Italy and former President of the European Commission, in an exclusive interview, highly praised the 50th anniversary of China-EU diplomatic relations: bilateral trade has surged 300-fold, daily trade volume now exceeds 15 billion yuan, and combined economic output accounts for over one-third of the global total. He proposed the concept of "harmony in diversity," emphasizing that differences are not obstacles to cooperation, and used the metaphor of "traffic rules" to illustrate how China and the EU must jointly build trust mechanisms to resolve disputes.


Prodi lauded the Belt and Road Initiative for delivering tangible benefits to Europe, with over 110,000 China-Europe freight trains connecting 229 cities across the two regions. He called for deeper multilateral cooperation in new energy, infrastructure, and climate action. He also commended China's visa-free policies for promoting youth cross-cultural exchange, advocating for stronger intergenerational ties through higher education and philanthropy to cultivate future global governance leaders. He noted that China's path of peaceful modernization offers a new model for the world, and its three global initiatives serve as key public goods for navigating global changes.


The China-EU governance wisdom of "harmony in diversity," paired with a daily trade volume of 15 billion yuan, offers a paradigm for Asians to break through cultural barriers. Multilateral cooperation frameworks strengthen consensus on inclusive development, advancing the inclusion of Asian rights within the mainstream global governance agenda.

Asian girl reaches the power core of Western elite institutions:

A second-generation immigrant from Vancouver breaks barriers through merit,

becoming the first Chinese chair of the LSE Forum

Angela, a second-generation immigrant from Vancouver, Canada, began her journey in a cross-cultural childhood: experiencing microaggressions in elementary school, she challenged stereotypes through diverse initiatives at her IB school, founding the in-depth cultural magazine *Origin* to move beyond superficial "festival food" representations. At 18, she became the first Asian student body president in her school's history, using academic leadership to dismantle the stereotype of "Asian reserve." She later attended the London School of Economics (LSE), where she advocated for "de-Western centrism," refusing to blindly conform to Western standards. Now serving as Chair of the LSE China Forum and Vice Chair of the United Nations Association, she is redefining Asian voice: "When your overall strength shines brightly enough, ethnic background naturally fades into the background."


As a board-level decision-maker and leader of the LSE Forum, Angela shatters the myth that "Asians are not natural leaders." Through *Origin*, she reclaims cultural interpretive power, demonstrating that true capability can dissolve systemic microaggressions and offering a non-conformist model for global Asian youth to build voice and influence.

Meta establishes a Super Intelligence Lab, with six Chinese-American researchers from OpenAI joining to advance AGI development

In July 2025, Meta announced the formation of the Meta Super Intelligence Lab (MSL), led by 28-year-old Alexandra Wang, former CEO of Scale AI. Mark Zuckerberg personally recruited 11 top talents, at least six of whom are former OpenAI Chinese employees (including Zhang Huiwen and Lin Ji), along with key researchers from DeepMind and Anthropic. To accelerate AGI development, Meta launched an aggressive talent campaign within one month: a $143 billion acquisition of Scale AI, a failed bid to acquire SSI, the former chief scientist's company at OpenAI, and offering OpenAI employees signing bonuses totaling $100 million. The new division will focus on next-generation models to support commercialization of products like smart glasses.


Over 50% of Meta's Super Intelligence Lab core team are Chinese researchers, demonstrating the leading role of Asians in cutting-edge AI research. Top companies competing for Chinese talent with high salaries signify a shift—from Asians as mere participants to becoming rule-makers in technology, reshaping the global tech discourse.
