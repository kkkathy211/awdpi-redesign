UN Women Urges

Placing Gender Equality at the Core of Social Development


The Second World Summit on Social Development was held in Qatar in early November 2025. Sima Bahous, Executive Director of UN Women, led a delegation advocating that gender equality be positioned as the foundation of inclusive development.


The summit focused on three core themes: eradicating poverty, promoting full employment, and fostering social inclusion. Data shows that one in ten women worldwide lives in extreme poverty, and the gender gap in labor force participation stands at 27.7%.


UN Women proposed four key action areas: addressing women's poverty, investing in the care economy, advancing women's employment, and safeguarding girls' rights. Multiple panel discussions were held during the summit, emphasizing the elimination of women's poverty through decent work, social protection, and equal pay, and advocating for redefining care politics as a pillar of sustainable development.


Advancing gender equality is not only a fundamental human right but also the cornerstone of sustainable social development. Poverty must be eradicated, workplace barriers broken, and caregiving responsibilities fairly distributed to build a society where all women can enjoy dignity, opportunity, and the right to full development.

Sanae Takaichi Elected LDP President,

Set to Become Japan's First Female Prime Minister


On October 4, 2025, the results of the Liberal Democratic Party (LDP) leadership election in Japan were announced, with Sanae Takaichi defeating several rivals. As the LDP holds the majority in parliament, she is expected to be elected Japan's first female prime minister in the upcoming parliamentary vote.


Takaichi holds conservative political views, has visited Yasukuni Shrine multiple times, and advocates constitutional revision and military expansion, earning her the nickname "female Abe." She will face multiple challenges upon taking office, including maintaining coalition stability, tackling economic inflation, and cooperating with opposition parties. Her far-right policy orientation may accelerate Japan's political shift to the right and impact regional dynamics.


Women assuming leadership roles represents major progress in gender equality. We must continue to push for women to break through glass ceilings in all fields, ensure equal opportunities, eliminate structural barriers, and build a truly inclusive and diverse development environment.

The UN Agenda on "Women, Peace and Security" Marks 25 Years, Advancing Women's Participation in Building Lasting Peace


2025 marks the 25th anniversary of the adoption of UN Security Council Resolution 1325, which established the "Women, Peace and Security" agenda. This agenda was the first to recognize that gender equality is essential to lasting peace and committed to ensuring women's equal participation as peacebuilders in decision-making.


However, current conflicts are reversing gender equality gains: in 2024, only one in ten peace negotiations included female negotiators; global military spending is rising, while funding for gender equality remains severely inadequate. At a time when conflicts have surged to their highest level since 1946, the 25th anniversary must be seized as an opportunity to renew commitments and investments in this agenda, placing women's leadership at the heart of peace processes.


These findings underscore the critical importance of advancing women's equal participation in peace processes. When women are excluded from the negotiating table, conflicts are more likely to persist. Women's leadership in decision-making must be ensured, as it directly affects whether societies can achieve lasting stability and true prosperity.

Five Unprovoked Attacks on Asians in One Day in U.S. Chinatown


On October 29, 2025, at least five unprovoked attacks on Asians occurred in New York's Chinatown. A 33-year-old suspect, Clive Porter, assaulted multiple Asian individuals on the street, including a 72-year-old Chinese man, shouting hate speech such as "F*** Asians." Porter has over 80 prior convictions and attacked police after his arrest. Authorities have opened a hate crime investigation and urged more victims to come forward. Porter currently faces multiple charges, including assault and harassment, and the case is ongoing.


Such incidents have caused deep fear and anger within Asian communities. We must firmly oppose all forms of racial hatred, call for legislation to strictly punish hate crimes, strengthen community security, and collectively safeguard the safety and dignity of the Asian community.

43 Years Since Vincent Chin's Murder, the Shadow of Anti-Asian Hate Remains


2025 marks the 43rd anniversary of the murder of Vincent Chin, a Chinese American youth. In 1982, he was beaten to death with a baseball bat by two white auto workers who mistakenly believed he was Japanese. The perpetrators received only probation. The case triggered massive protests across the Asian American community nationwide and became a pivotal moment in the civil rights movement.


Yet 43 years later, anti-Asian violence persists. Data shows that over 10,000 hate incidents targeting Asians have occurred in the U.S. since 2020. Experts point out that whenever U.S. relations with Asian countries become tense, Asian Americans easily become scapegoats. This "perpetual foreigner" stereotype remains a deep-rooted cause of anti-Asian violence.


Anti-Asian violence not only threatens individual safety but also tears at the foundation of a diverse and coexisting society. We must remember the lessons of history, resolutely eliminate hatred through legislation, education, and community action, and collectively safeguard a just society where everyone is equal.

Chinese Woman Randomly Attacked in Australia, Safety Alarm Rings Again


In October 2025, Wan Lai, a 36-year-old Chinese sushi chef, was unprovokedly attacked while on her way to work in downtown Melbourne. A 32-year-old woman, who was on bail, suddenly stabbed her in the right chest, the wound mere centimeters from her heart.


Diagnosed with hemopneumothorax, Wan Lai is out of life danger but recovering slowly. She has lost her ability to work and, due to psychological trauma, moved from her original residence, living in constant fear. The local community is deeply alarmed and has increased security patrols. The suspect is currently in custody awaiting trial, facing multiple charges including intentional assault.


This unprovoked attack against a Chinese woman has deeply unsettled overseas Asian communities, undermining their sense of safety. We must firmly oppose all acts of violence against Asians, push for improved legal protections and community support systems, and collectively safeguard the dignity and safety of every life.

After Harvard Abolishes Racial Preferences,

Asian Freshman Enrollment Soars to 41%


Following the U.S. Supreme Court's 2023 ruling banning race-based considerations in college admissions, Harvard University released data on its Class of 2029 on October 23, revealing significant changes: Asian student enrollment surged to 41%, a record high, while Black student enrollment dropped to 11.5%, the lowest in a decade.


The abolition of "racial preferences" is seen as a breakthrough for Asian students, but has also sparked new debates on educational equity. Supporters argue it achieves "merit-based admissions," while critics worry campus diversity will suffer. Meanwhile, the retention or elimination of other admissions criteria, such as standardized testing, has become a new focal point in the debate over fairness.


Asians have proven their merit in Harvard's admissions process. We must extend this educational fairness to all areas of society and actively pursue political voice and representation to break stereotypes and fundamentally curb systemic discrimination and violence against Asians.

Third RICE Asian Food Festival Brings Asian Flavors to Paris,

Promoting Asian Cultural Exchange


The third RICE Asian Food Festival was successfully held in Paris on October 4, 2025. Organized jointly by the European Times Media and Culture Group and the Paris 13th District Government, the event brought together 45 restaurants and 15 cultural associations from 11 Asian countries, including China, Vietnam, and Thailand.


Paris 13th District Mayor Anne Hidalgo praised the festival in her speech for showcasing the diverse charm of Asian cultures. The event also featured traditional performances such as singing, dancing, and lion dances, becoming an important platform for young Asians to express creativity and promote multicultural exchange, further enhancing French public understanding of and identification with Asian cultures.


The success of the Asian Food Festival demonstrates the power of cultural integration. Asian communities need more platforms like this to speak up, break stereotypes, and claim equal voice and representation, transforming cultural influence into positive energy for social equity.

Chinese-American pianist Lu Yixuan wins top prize at Chopin Competition


At the recently concluded 19th International Chopin Piano Competition, Chinese-American pianist Lu Yixuan claimed the championship, continuing the outstanding tradition of Chinese musicians at this prestigious event. Chinese competitors also delivered strong performances, with Wang Zitong winning third place and 17-year-old Lü Tianyao securing fourth place.


Although Lu Yixuan grew up in the United States, he has deep roots in Chinese music: his mother is from Shanghai, and he studied under renowned Chinese pianists such as Yin Chengzong. This victory once again highlights the exceptional achievements and influence of Chinese diaspora artists in the international classical music scene.


Chinese-American pianist Lu Yixuan's triumph at the Chopin Competition reaffirms the outstanding talent of Asians in the arts. We must strive for equal opportunities and representation in all fields, ensuring that Asian achievements receive full recognition and dismantling the stereotype of Asians as "perpetual foreigners."
