Events

Details



From November 23 to 24, 2024, the 2024 AWDPI Asian Women Development Forum was successfully held online. This year's forum centered on "Orange the World—Building a Future Without Violence" and "A New Era of Shaping Female Identity," engaging in in-depth dialogue with government agencies, academic institutions, nonprofit organizations, experts, scholars, opinion leaders, and concerned individuals worldwide who are committed to the development of Asian women.


Over the two-day live forum, 54 guests "gathered together" through academic presentations, roundtable discussions, paper reviews, and a debate competition across four segments, addressing the barriers and challenges Asian women face in empowerment and development. From multidimensional and forward-looking perspectives, nearly a thousand participants "took the stage" together to exchange ideas and share insights on issues concerning Asian women and women at large in areas such as gender-based violence, employment, entrepreneurship, political empowerment, technological advancement, climate change, and film and art.



One

Academic Presentations: Focusing on Women's Issues,

Sharing In-Depth Achievements


The academic presentation segment, launched on the morning of December 23, focused on domestic violence, Asian laborers, and political and economic empowerment of Asian women, inviting eight speakers to share their research findings.



Li Yuling

As a representative of the organizer, Ms. Li Yuling, Director of the Asian Women Development Plan International (AWDPI) and Founding & Managing Partner of Han Sheng Law Firm, introduced AWDPI's work in advancing the development and empowerment of Asian women, and expressed deep gratitude to all attendees and participants. Ms. Li emphasized that the AWDPI Asian Women Forum is not only a platform for sharing experiences and exchanging ideas, but also a crucial opportunity for advocacy and driving change. She expressed hope to join hands with like-minded individuals across sectors to foster a gender-friendly environment, enabling women to step onto broader stages.



Zheng Anni


Ms. Zheng Anni, Head of Human Resources at the Asian Women Development Plan International (AWDPI), introduced to the attendees the organization's overall operational model, as well as the inspiring stories of volunteers—small lights that continuously bring fresh energy to the organization and grow alongside it.


Zhang Xiaoshan


Ms. Zhang Xiaoshan, Secretary-General of the Chengdu Foundation for the Welfare of the Disabled and also a Director of the Asian Women Development Plan International (AWDPI), analyzed, from the perspective of China's excellent traditional culture, its positive significance and role in shaping modern female identity.


Wang Yanping


Ms. Wang Yanping, Director of the Asian Women Development Plan International (AWDPI), renowned expert on domestic violence, and senior social work practitioner, began her speech by referencing the 1995 Fourth World Conference on Women (Beijing Women's Conference), reflecting on the journey of combating domestic violence from today's vantage point.


Attorney Renren


As a frontline legal professional, renowned marriage and family law attorney and founder of the self-media brand "Renren Bu Xucixing," Attorney Renren delivered a keynote speech on psychological violence. Combining professional theory with real-life cases, she provided a detailed analysis of why women struggle to escape abusive environments, and how servility and Stockholm syndrome become obstacles preventing women from breaking free from violence.






Wang Lin


Ms. Wang Lin, China Director at BSR and expert in labor issues within China's supply chains, has long focused on women workers. Drawing from research and personal experience, she shared that in the context of a globalized economy, empowering and supporting women across relevant supply chains has become a direction society must discuss, advance, and innovate..


Xu Hui


Dr. Xu Hui, Sociology PhD from the University of Jena, Germany, and Founder of the China-Germany Center for Skills and Social Development, spoke on the issue of Asian women laborers. He It elaborated on the survival challenges faced by Asian women overseas in terms of language, market competitiveness, work environment, salary and benefits, and analyzed the underlying logic leading to these difficulties. In his view, while narratives of high-achieving women are increasingly common, the voices of grassroots women must not be ignored.


Zhang Ning


Ms. Zhang Ning, Founder and CEO of Flatfee Xiaofei Network and Founding Partner of the U.S. law firm Cross-border Counselor LLP, shared her observations on economic empowerment of Asian women in the U.S. workplace based on years of professional experience and research. She stated: In terms of dominant discourse power, Asian women still tend to occupy a disadvantaged position.






Sun Yi


Ms. Sun Yi, Director of the Asian Women Development Plan International (AWDPI), the first Asian Pacific Islander Vice Chair of the League of Women Voters of Pennsylvania, and the first female immigrant Director of the Pennsylvania Governor's Asian Pacific American Affairs Commission, presented on the development, opportunities, difficulties, and challenges in Asian women's political rights. Ms. Sun noted that Asian women face awkward situations in education, employment, daily life, and political participation. Nevertheless, in collective struggles by Asian women, many Chinese women have emerged as visible leaders—successfully securing voting rights and inspiring today's women with courage and the right to speak, becoming role models and exemplars for Chinese women.




Two

Roundtable Discussions: Gathering Expert Wisdom,

Exploring How Asian Women Can Break Through on the Global Stage

The roundtable discussion segment covered eight major themes: intangible cultural heritage (ICH) transmission, women's platforms, artificial intelligence, film and art, women's entrepreneurship, climate change, accessibility construction, and rural development, bringing together experts from various fields to jointly explore challenges and opportunities for Asian women in these areas.



01

[Roundtable Discussion] From Local Culture to the Global Stage: Women Empowering the Transmission and Dissemination of Intangible Cultural Heritage


Jing Qiuhong

Liu Xiaoyu

Pan Miaoyan

Wan Jianghong


In the "From Local Culture to the Global Stage: Women Empowering the Transmission and Dissemination of Intangible Cultural Heritage" roundtable discussion, the four panelists focused on the inheritance and innovation of intangible cultural heritage (ICH), discussing under today's context how women can drive the sustainable development of ICH through creativity and leadership, and how to attract more young women to join in the protection and promotion of ICH,, helping traditional culture step from local roots onto the world stage and collectively shaping cultural diversity.


Moderated by Pan Miaoyan, Founder of the Oral History Project for ICH Inheritors and Forbes FGA International Leading Talent, the discussion featured Jing Qiuhong, the fourth-generation inheritor of Xuzhou Cao's Incense Pouch and national inheritor of the national intangible cultural heritage Xuzhou Incense Pouch; Liu Xiaoyu, Professor at the Academy of Fine Arts, Jingdezhen Ceramic University, and inheritor of Jingdezhen traditional colored-glaze porcelain and Jizhou kiln ceramic firing techniques; and Wan Jianghong, inheritor of the 300-year-old ethnic ICH brand Jiang's Ancient Tea & Nanbian Tea production techniques and National Model Pioneer in ICH Innovation.

Duan Fang

Moderated by Duan Fang, Founder of Yue Ta Women and "Most Beautiful Mentor" of China Media Group, the discussion on "Women's Platforms Under a Global Vision: Empowering the Next Generation of Female Leaders" featured Qiu Dongying, Co-Founder of Lean In China and short-video IP incubator & brand consultant; Wang Tiantian, Chairwoman of Shanghui International Education Group and Head of the Women's Education Channel at China National Radio; and Yin Yang, Founder of Yangguang Mama Migu Hui, bestselling author of *Mom's Magic Book: Awakening Children's Inner Drive* and *Excelling Together with Your Child*.


Together, the four panelists explored how women's platforms, under globalization, can advocate mission-driven values and unlock the potential of female leadership, collectively building a diverse, inclusive, and globally sustainable ecosystem for women's empowerment. Each shared valuable insights from their experience on how platforms support women, specific practices in empowering women's growth, reflections and journeys during entrepreneurial stages, and achievements and lessons learned..

[Roundtable Discussion] Women's Roles in the Age of AI Transformation: Technological Innovation and International Collaboration


Tan Lin

Li Baoqiong


Shi Lan

Wu Lingwei


We are now at the forefront of the AI technological revolution, where every step is critical. Amid this powerful tide of change, how can we adapt and respond? As women in emerging industries, what paths will we lead?


In the "Women's Roles in the Age of AI Transformation: Technological Innovation and International Collaboration" roundtable discussion, the four panelists conducted a comprehensive and multidimensional discussion on the AI technology sector from four angles: women's career development, organizational transformation, pathways for international collaboration, and expectations and advice for young women. They analyzed the challenges and opportunities women may face in this field and proposed actionable strategies and solutions, offering valuable experience and insights for those who follow.


This session was moderated by Tan Lin, Founding Director and Chief Futurist of the PHBS Center for Innovation and Entrepreneurship at Peking University and Vice President of the 2024 Peking University Alumni Entrepreneurship Association. Panelists included Li Baoqiong, General Manager of Micang Think Tank and Founder of Putao Zhaoche & Meipu Zhixing; Shi Lan, Founder and CEO of Café Tech and Board Member of MIT Sloan School of Management; and Wu Lingwei, Chairwoman of Yilai Technology, Founder and Chief Advisor of AA Accelerator Group.

[Roundtable Discussion] Women's Narratives Connecting the World: Diversity and Cultural Integration in Film and Art


Tang Xuedan

Chen Xiaowen


Gao Yuer

Qian Haiying


"Women's Narratives Connecting the World: Diversity and Cultural Integration in Film and Art" The roundtable discussion was moderated by Tang Xuedan, founder and artistic director of CiLENS Berlin Chinese Film Festival, co-founder of Siying Film Education, and film curator. The panelists included Chen Xiaowen, Associate Professor and PhD supervisor at De Montfort University, founder and director of the UK-China Film Cooperation Research and Development Center; Gao Yu'er, mainland Chinese actress and public welfare ambassador of Huayang Lianzhong Public Foundation; and Qian Haiying, founder of Banff China and pioneer and leader in China's outdoor film industry.


Drawing from their respective perspectives and experiences, the four panelists engaged in a practical discussion on a series of issues concerning women creators in film and television arts. They explored the ways of expressing women's narratives and the role of diverse value narratives in cross-cultural dialogue, possible breakthroughs and innovations in women's storytelling, and the kinds of female narrative expressions they hope to see. All four panelists emphasized that Chinese culture is a unique code guiding Chinese film and television, and also plays a role in promoting the development of traditional culture. Listening to the changing rhythms of the times and emphasizing diverse expressions, they aim to convey Chinese women's stories and strength to a broader world.


05

[Roundtable Discussion] Women's Leadership and the International Entrepreneurial Ecosystem: Opportunities and Challenges


Lu Xi

Luo Yuan


Yang Zhen

Zhang Shengnan


The roundtable discussion titled "Women's Leadership and the International Entrepreneurial Ecosystem: Opportunities and Challenges" delved deeply into the advantages women possess and the challenges they face within the entrepreneurial ecosystem.


Women leaders, with their outstanding cohesion, connectivity, coordination and communication skills, as well as efficient utilization of social resources, have become a significant force in the field of entrepreneurship. However, women leaders also face multiple challenges, including heavy maternal responsibilities, cultural and environmental biases, and overly modest self-perception. The panelists encouraged women not to limit themselves, but to bravely try, continuously learn, and improve self-awareness, while calling on society to pay greater attention to the development of women leaders.


This dialogue was moderated by Lu Xi, founder of Xishiqu and outstanding young talent of the G20 Young Entrepreneurs' Alliance, with panelists including Luo Yuan, Deputy Dean of the College of Innovation and Entrepreneurship at Guangxi Normal University and national-level course ideological and political education master teacher recognized by the Ministry of Education; Yang Zhen, Chair of Haiyun Women's Entrepreneurship Association and Secretary General of Shanghai Changzhi Chamber of Commerce; and Zhang Shengnan, Product Operations Lead at BrainCo, founder of the Harvard Graduate School of Education Brain Science Association, and founder of the International Society for Neuroscience and Education.

The panelists also expressed the hope that women could have greater participation in top-level decision-making, encouraged women to speak up bravely, and looked forward to the creation of more platforms for women to voice their opinions, so that women's power and contributions can be seen by more people.

They also called on society to provide more support and attention to stimulate women's enthusiasm for returning to rural entrepreneurship..



Three

Debate Performance Match

"Does 'Women First' Help Gender Equality?"


In this debate, the debaters engaged in an in-depth discussion on the topic: " In today's context, does 'Women First' help gender equality? " The affirmative team consisted of Chen Jiajun, cooperation and development advisor at AWDPI Asian Women Development Plan International and co-founder of Future Light Family Happiness Office, and member of the Guangzhou Yuexiu District Committee of the Chinese People's Political Consultative Conference; Su Xizhi, co-founder of the Youth Sustainable Development Debate Conference and Global Shaper at the World Economic Forum Davos; and Zeng Zixiang, Best Debater of the Chinese Debate World Cup Invitational Tournament for Elite Universities and brand marketing consultant at National University of Singapore Enterprise Development. The opposing team included Chang Aizi, PhD in Communication and Media Studies from Monash University and Best Debater of the Chinese Cup Debate Competition; Chen Yuqian, former captain and coach of Sun Yat-sen University Debate Team and former content training mentor for iQiyi's variety show *I Can I BB*; and Zhang Xinxin, council member of Shaanxi Women's Theory, Marriage and Family Research Association and social gender education and entrepreneurship mentor for Shaanxi universities.



Chen Jiajun

Su Xizhi

Zeng Zixiang


The affirmative side argued that 'Women First' is a manifestation of gender equality, helps address discrimination faced by women, and promotes gender equity. The affirmative emphasized that true equality should not ignore gender differences and the current disadvantaged status of women, but rather promote the implementation of women-first measures based on an understanding of women's real-life situations.

V.S.




Chang Aizi

Chen Yuqian

Zhang Xinxin


The opposing side argued that 'Women First' policies may reinforce gender stereotypes, and hinder the genuine realization of gender equality. They emphasized that many slogans and movements initiated spontaneously by women have already effectively addressed gender inequality, making it unnecessary to rely on the 'Women First' concept, which may imply female dependency.



In this debate, both teams shared the ultimate goal of achieving gender equality, and engaged in intense discussion over the paths to achieve it. The vigorous exchange between the debaters not only demonstrated their profound thinking on gender equality but also provided the audience with opportunities for deeper reflection on the topic.




Four

Paper Review

Building a learning platform for youth engaged in gender issues


This forum specially included a paper review session, aiming to provide more opportunities for sharing academic research outcomes and to inspire public enthusiasm for participating in gender-related research.


Pei Yuxin

Wang Yong


Wang Chenyao

Zhao Fang


The AWDPI forum's paper review session was hosted by Qian Siyu and attracted 16 submissions from both domestic and international participants. After rigorous discussion and evaluation, four outstanding papers were selected for review.


The session invited four renowned scholars as reviewers, covering the fields of sociology, economics, journalism and communication, and social work: Professor Pei Yuxin, Associate Professor at the School of Sociology and Anthropology, Sun Yat-sen University, and Fulbright Scholar; Professor Wang Yong, Academic Vice Dean at the Institute of New Structural Economics, Peking University, and Secretary-General of the New Structural Economics Research Alliance; Professor Wang Chenyao, Professor at the School of Journalism and Communication, Nanjing University, and Director of the Journalism Innovation Lab and the Global Public Communication Research Center; and Professor Zhao Fang, Chair of the Department of Social Work, School of Social Development and Public Policy, Fudan University, and Director of the Fudan-Daphne Child Protection Research Center.


The four papers respectively explored from conducted research and analysis on four topics: a qualitative study of young Filipino female workers in Denmark, the historical context, current status, and future directions of time-use issues under China's gender inequality, a study of gender discourse hegemony in the public sphere from a feminist perspective, and the limitations and future development directions of female imagery in brand advertising, and the paper authors received constructive feedback and encouragement from four guest commentators.




Five

Conclusion


This year's forum reached new heights in scale, format, topics, and academic atmosphere, bringing together experts and scholars from various fields who delivered insightful presentations and shared valuable experiences, deeply exploring the challenges and opportunities women face across multiple domains in modern society, and collectively witnessing an intellectual feast.



The closing of the forum is not the end.



In the future, AWDPI Asian Women Development Plan International will work closely with individuals, institutions, organizations, enterprises, and government departments that care about the development and empowerment of Asian women, continuously advancing the cause of gender equality and women's empowerment, and jointly striving for a more equal and inclusive future.
