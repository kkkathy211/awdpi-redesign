Online exhibition "Her Story: Between Light and Shadow"

In Her Light and Shadow: Stories Seen and Heard


Sometimes, we think we have already seen a person.


We see her photo, profession, resume, identity, achievements, and the various labels the world has given her.


She is an artist.

She is a doctor.

She is a scholar.

She is an entrepreneur.

She is a mother.

She is an Asian woman.


But—


When we say these words, do we truly know her? Perhaps not. Because a person's story has never existed only in the part that is seen.


The migrations never written into a resume, the hesitations born from crossing cultures, the confusions about family, identity, growth, and belonging, as well as unspoken dreams, fears, and struggles…


Often hide where the light does not reach.

Hidden in the shadows.


Beyond being "seen," what else is there?

In the context of contemporary Western society, Asian women seem increasingly "visible."

They appear in media, social platforms, universities, corporations, art institutions, and public spaces, creating achievements in various fields.


Yet visibility does not equal understanding.

We can quickly learn a person's name, profession, and identity, but we may never know what kind of life journey she has experienced.

We can see her standing under the spotlight, but we do not know how she got there.

We can appreciate a painting, but do not know what unheard voices lie behind this portrait of life.


Light is how we see her.

Shadow is the life story that has yet to be heard.


"Her Story: Between Light and Shadow" was born from this very reflection.


Interactive online photography project



This is an interactive online photography project hosted by AWDPI, co-organized by Viva la Vida, and strongly supported by Edinburgh Festival Fringe Chinese Culture Week, presented on the Cultural Week's event platform.


We invite Asian women from diverse cultural backgrounds, professions, and life stages to share their personal stories through portraits, voices, and words.


They come from fields such as technology, academia, finance, fashion, healthcare, and the arts.

They have entirely different life trajectories:

Some were born in one country and raised in another;

Some left their familiar hometowns to rebuild their lives in unfamiliar cities;

Some continuously break barriers in their professional fields;

Some are still searching for their identity;

Some have already found answers;

And some are still seeking.


We do not attempt to define them with a single narrative.

Because "Asian women" has never been a single story, but a composition of countless different voices.

From light to shadow; from seeing to listening

Light brings a life into our view.

And the shadow reminds us:

She has more stories yet to tell.


Every soul is an unfinished portrait of life.


We draw light and shadow, record voices,

not to define who she is,

but to open, within this interwoven space of light and shadow,

a dialogue about "life."


When portraits shed their labels and voices pierce through silence,

the faint glimmers of survival, love, and struggle

intertwine into a broader landscape of life through mutual resonance.


Therefore, "Her Story: Between Light and Shadow" hopes to create not an opposition between "light" and "shadow," but a bridge connecting the two.

Let us begin with seeing, move toward understanding, and ultimately arrive at true listening.


A photograph takes only a few seconds.

But understanding a life may require a voice recording, or perhaps a genuine conversation.

When we see her portrait, hear her own voice, and leave our own resonance in this space, only then do we begin to move from "viewing" to "listening."

Chinese Culture Week (CCW) is an international cultural and arts platform based in Edinburgh, held during the Edinburgh Festival Fringe. It aims to connect artists from China, those with Chinese cultural backgrounds, and from various global regions with international audiences through theatre, dance, music, exhibitions, traditional crafts, workshops, and cultural exchange activities.


The official organizing body of CCW is Intercultural Connections Initiative (ICI), a Community Interest Company (CIC) registered in Scotland, primarily focused on cultural exchange, artistic innovation, and education. CCW is officially positioned as a platform connecting artists, cultural institutions, and audiences from China, the UK, and around the world.


Organization introduction



AWDPI (Asian Women Development Plan International) is an international nonprofit organization dedicated to eliminating discrimination and gender-based violence against Asian women living overseas, and promoting their empowerment and development. The organization evolved from Avoice (Overseas Chinese Women Protection Project), founded in 2020. AWDPI advocates for attention to the challenges faced by Asian women overseas, provides them with sustained protection and support, and helps transform them from a vulnerable group into a vital force driving social progress.


In this project, AWDPI is responsible for the planning and execution of the offline exhibition, as well as the portrait photography and voice archive collection of 16 participating Asian women.

Viva la Vida (VIVA) is a global cross-cultural arts brand operating in over 60 countries, dedicated to using art to record life stories, promote cross-cultural understanding, and advance social good. We invite people from diverse cultural backgrounds to answer "What is your life?" through a piece of artwork, thereby recording diverse life stories and fostering cross-cultural understanding. Over its eight years of operation, VIVA has partnered with organizations such as the United Nations, Coca-Cola, and Tencent to host over 400 events, collecting more than 20,000 life portraits, transforming individual expressions into bridges for cultural dialogue.
