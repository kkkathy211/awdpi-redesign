November 25 is the United Nations' International Day for the Elimination of Violence against Women, marking the start of the "16 Days of Activism" campaign, which runs annually from this day until December 10. The 16 Days of Activism aims to encourage people worldwide to take active steps to eliminate gender-based violence.


To date, violence against women and girls remains one of the most widespread human rights violations in the world. According to United Nations statistics, approximately 736 million women globally—nearly one in three—have experienced gender-based violence at least once in their lives, including violence occurring in workplaces and online spaces.


This year, Asian Women Development Plan International (AWDPI) will host five online workshops during the 16 Days of Activism and relaunch the "Mobile Mailbox" initiative, joining people across countries who care about gender issues to express support for survivors of domestic violence. In the United Kingdom and Belgium, we will organize offline exhibitions against gender-based violence; in the United States, we will conduct research and interviews on the living conditions of minority ethnic caregivers.

Did you know? Globally, girls account for less than one-third of students in STEM-related fields, and women have received fewer than 4% of all Nobel Prizes. As we continue advancing toward a digital future, the digital divide may also place women at greater risk of being left behind.


Many girls in underdeveloped countries and regions face challenges in accessing quality science and technology education. How can we better encourage girls to study STEM in ways that suit local conditions? How can we inspire girls to pay attention to community issues around them and bravely propose their own solutions? What kind of educational content can enable sustainable, long-term development in underdeveloped regions? We look forward to exploring innovative pathways for STEM education for girls in underdeveloped regions together with you!
