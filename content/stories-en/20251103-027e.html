The second part of the 2025 Spring Recruitment is finally here to meet you all! Click here to read the first part.



Do you still remember the seed of Ta Power we sowed in spring? Though seemingly small, it has been persistently growing toward the sunlight, through the cracks in conservative social structures and outdated beliefs.


AWDPI As the soil safeguarding this strength, we are once again searching for changemakers this year—and once more, we have heard echoes back. People from diverse cultural backgrounds and life experiences have, once again, used words as letters of faith, offering Ta their heartfelt answers.


Among them are those who wish to uplift others through action, those determined to turn ideals into practice to dismantle barriers in reality, and those who simply want to embrace another soul in need of belonging, in their own unique way.


We asked: "Why do you want to join AWDPI?"

We also asked: "What path have you walked, and what stories have you lived through?"


In the 2025 Spring Recruitment, the new members offered Ta their responses—thousands of voices, thousands of strengths. Just like the over one thousand applications last year, some drew near because they had suffered, took action because they understood, and joined because they believed.


Today, these voices continue to sprout. Those once silent, struggling, defiant, and resolute stories are now blooming from lines of application texts.


Below are Ta their stories—



⎣Because I've been through it, I must resist⎤


From the past to the present, from domestic to overseas, they have constantly reflected—on difference and individuality, on empowerment and cross-cultural identity.


"Joining AWDPI is to help women in crisis, and to move one stone away from a world swept by conservatism."


ZCX

United States National Office

Member, US Fundraising Division

Although I was fortunate to grow up in an open-minded family, where my parents gave me great freedom and space for choice, the stories of the previous generation in my family have profoundly influenced me. My great-grandparents had nine children, but only three uncles had the opportunity to attend university. Under the poverty-stricken social conditions of that time, my great-aunts had no access to education and could only remain in rural villages, farming, marrying, bearing children, and devoting their lives to their families. They had no right to education, nor freedom to choose the direction of their lives.


Member, Project Quality Department – Project Design Division

LLY

I have briefly lived in the UK, the US, and Singapore, gaining some understanding of local cultures and the strength of women there. Women who already face challenges surviving in their home countries encounter even more difficult obstacles overseas. Moreover, certain forms of discrimination and violence against women are, in fact, global. Sometimes laws and regulations can serve as tools to protect ourselves, but moral loopholes are often irreparable. After experiencing other civilizations, some Asian women develop more diverse aspirations and build richer identities, yet the violations of their rights often become even more complex.


JZ

Communications Department – Publicity Division, Xiaohongshu Operations

Many Asian women are actually quite shy. Some women who suffer misfortune or bullying overseas dare not speak up or challenge injustice in society. In a sense, they are vulnerable and helpless. For many people, the stereotype of Asian women is that they are weak and easy to bully. This personality trait and stereotype further exacerbate the overseas challenges faced by Asian women.


Australia National Office

Australia Partnership Development Department

Member, Australia Partnership Development Division

ZWQ 

I am a social work student currently studying in Australia. As my gender awareness has gradually increased, I've become increasingly aware of gender inequality around me, and I've constantly reflected on how, as an individual, I can better participate and take action. Since last year, I began long-term volunteer work with an NGO. Through this experience, I've felt the joy of being driven by passion and mission, and of working alongside like-minded partners. At the same time, as I progress through different stages of life, I continue to reflect on how we can further advance gender equality within different structures and environments.


SX

Human Resources Department – Performance Team

Performance Evaluation Member

 By chance, I borrowed a book from a friend—Professor Ueno Chizuko's *Misogyny*. After reading it, I was deeply shocked; the oppression of women is everywhere in this world. From that moment, I began actively seeking knowledge about women's rights. Later, I was fortunate to come across the work *My Brilliant Friend*. One passage described a female character suffering domestic violence but unable to escape her marriage due to social norms. I felt intense anger, and a stronger desire to change something through my own efforts.


Australia National Office

Australia Partnership Development Department

Member, Australia Finance Division

DYC 

As someone who has spent five years studying and living in an English-speaking environment, I often pay attention to the academic and living conditions of Asian communities overseas. I have always opposed any form of racist propaganda and maintain zero tolerance for discriminatory behaviors in daily life. For Asian women, knowing how to protect themselves overseas is extremely important.


HYR

Advocacy and Influence Department

Member, Advocacy and Influence Department (Shanghai)

That summer at 18, I was organizing *Folded Voices: Interviews with Gen-Z Asian Women* in a café in a Shanghai alleyway. From the voice recorder came a student's choked sobs in Berlin: "When they compliment my English, their eyes are measuring my Eastern genes." I remembered myself in high school, wearing Hanfu to the graduation ball, only to be asked, "Are you cosplaying?" Age is never a barrier to understanding suffering; youth is precisely the sensitive period for perceiving injustice—perhaps this is exactly the voice AWDPI needs: untrained sharpness, and sincere belief in the possibility of change.


⎣Where care lies, action follows.⎤

They began with "I want" and "I hope," throwing stones at the world with all their strength, making change possible.


For them, and for us.


WRQ

Australia National Office

Australia Partnership Development Department

Member, Australia Partnership Development Division

As an Asian woman who has lived overseas for a long time, I deeply understand the difficulties of overseas life, and I want to help more sisters.


Communications Department – Publicity Division – Xiaohongshu Operations

ZYR

When I saw AWDPI advocating for anti-discrimination legislation in Brussels, exposing gender pay gaps in New York, and protecting the nighttime safety of Asian girls in London… I realized this is not just an international call, but a cross-cultural, intergenerational awakening. I hope to become part of it, contributing my voice, skills, and love to this "women's empowerment network" spanning Europe, the Americas, and the Asia-Pacific region.


HXY

Support and Assistance Department – Case Coordination Division Member

In my past internship experiences, I had the opportunity to meet many female help-seekers and listen to their stories. However, as an intern, I was often only able to provide them with a non-judgmental space full of positive regard during brief interactions, unable to accompany them throughout the entire process of support and rebuilding. This limitation left me with deep regret and made me even more eager to join a platform capable of providing sustained support.


In a recent internship, a mentor's team had, ten years prior, established an integrated support system combining social workers, psychologists, and other professionals, successfully helping a client reintegrate into society. However, the project was later discontinued, failing to sustain its original support framework. This experience made me deeply aware of the effectiveness and importance of combining individual psychological support with systemic social assistance. Therefore, AWDPI's multidimensional collaborative model is precisely the field of practice I aspire to.


Advocacy and Influence Department

Member, Advocacy and Influence Department (Shanghai)

HYR 

AWDPI does not need perfect warriors, but gardeners willing to plant the first dandelion in Glasgow's drizzle—I apply to become a sower in this garden, bringing Gen-Z's media sensibility, idealism unquenched by reality, and the most precious trait of "inability to stay silent." Because real change has always allowed a nineteen-year-old girl to believe: when she writes poetry for Brussels' twilight, her verses will eventually become punctuation marks in policy documents.


ZCX 

United States National Office

Member, US Fundraising Division

It was only when I began to be frequently labeled in class, internships, and daily life that I realized this identity is both a challenge and a source of strength. My first academic paper at school was about the negative impact of the "cooling-off period for divorce" on domestic violence survivors. This paper was not merely an academic exercise; for me, it was a profound process of self-identification and value reflection. I gradually realized that what I want is not just to "pay attention" to women's issues, but to become someone who can "act" and "change" reality, even if just a little.


For five years, AWDPI has, on the foundation of "Participation, Inclusion, Empowerment," documented the firm journeys of Asian women overseas moving from hardship to self-identification, and recorded the brilliance and radiance of "her" shining moments.


The warm reflections and awakening power in application forms will not remain on paper forever. Their voices will slowly turn into action, and their actions will eventually converge into the gentle light illuminating our path forward.



On AWDPI's journey, may each of us experience days full of challenges yet profoundly fulfilling.
