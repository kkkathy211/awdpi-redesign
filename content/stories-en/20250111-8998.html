2024 United Nations Human Rights Council Forum



Date: 31 October – 1 November 2024

Venue: Room XX, Palais des Nations, Geneva


2024 United Nations Social Forum Focused on "Financing for Development and Humanitarian Development" , in accordance with United Nations Human Rights Council Resolution 53/20, explored the critical role of development financing in advancing humanitarian efforts. Currently, post-pandemic economic recovery and ongoing global crises have left developing countries facing severe resource and financial constraints, these challenges significantly hinder the realization of sustainable development and human rights..


Discussions at the forum centered on how to close the financing gap between developed and developing countries , preventing the long-term entrenchment of the sustainable development divide. Financing for development is key to global well-being and achieving the Sustainable Development Goals, especially in ensuring that all countries, particularly developing ones, can participate in and benefit from global development.


The Addis Ababa Action Agenda set clear goals for development financing, but implementation has fallen short of expectations. This year's forum laid the groundwork for the Fourth International Conference on Financing for Development to be held in Spain in 2025, and promoted dialogue among governments, international organizations, civil society, and the private sector, especially in advancing reforms of the international financial architecture, seeking solutions.

Member Introduction and Forum Engagement

Human Rights



Callum Robert Antonio Luciani, AWDPI UK member, a post-2000s individual of dual British and Italian heritage, currently pursuing a degree in Environmental Science at the University of the West of England. He is committed to advancing sustainable development, with a particular focus on environmental protection, and cares deeply about the rights and well-being of women and children in developing countries.



Over the two-day forum, Callum represented the AWDPI organization and delivered in-depth speeches on two topics: "Domestic Resource Mobilization" and "Private enterprises and finance, domestic and international". Additionally, during the Q&A session, he raised a question regarding the impact of digital commercialization on low-income women, demonstrating his profound insights and concern in these areas.

Statement on Topic One: "Domestic Resource Mobilization"

Human Rights



Original Text ——


Thank you ! 


Today I would like to discuss a crucial issue: the intersection of child rights and domestic funding. Children are the future of our societies, yet millions are denied their natural need to a dual parent household. It is imperative that countries mobilize resources effectively to address these pressing needs.


The education system in the west is not perfect but it is not where the core educational problems of children in these countries come from, these problems stem from broken families and with almost a quarter of children living in the USA without a father in the home , it's no wonder depression and suicide rates have spiked in recent times. Even more damning however is the fact that in America 64% of all black children grow up in single parent households,  unfortunately this strongly correlates with negative outcomes in the child's life and therefore the current and future economy.

 

This issue can be tackled by offering a multifaceted initiative that includes financial incentives and support resources . My suggestion is that couples who marry and complete a premarital education program receive a one-time financial grant , followed by additional bonuses for staying married for 5, 10, and 15 years. The program should also feature matched savings accounts for children's education, providing financial support for families that save. Tax incentives could include joint filing benefits and a family tax credit for eligible dual-parent households. Homeownership incentives, such as lower down payments for qualifying families, promote stable living environments. The expected outcomes include increased marriage rates, improved child well-being, enhanced economic stability, and stronger community ties.



Translation ——


Today, I will discuss a crucial topic: the intersection of children's rights and domestic funding. Children are the future of our society, yet millions of children are deprived of the basic right to grow up in a two-parent household. Therefore, countries must efficiently mobilize resources to address this urgent need.


While Western education systems have their shortcomings, these are not the root cause of children's educational challenges in these countries. In the United States, nearly one in four children grows up in a fatherless home, a phenomenon closely linked to the sharp rise in depression and suicide rates in recent years. More seriously, 64% of Black children in the U.S. live in single-parent households, a situation strongly associated with negative life outcomes and profound impacts on current and future economic conditions.


To address this issue, we can adopt a multi-pronged approach, including providing financial incentives and supportive resources. My proposal is that governments offer a one-time financial grant to couples who marry and complete pre-marital education courses, followed by bonus payments at five, ten, and fifteen years of marriage. Furthermore, the plan should include matched savings accounts for children's education, encouraging families to save for their children's future, and provide financial support to eligible families. Tax incentives could include joint-filing tax benefits and family tax credits for qualifying two-parent families. Housing incentives, such as reduced down payment requirements, would help promote stable living environments for families. Expected outcomes include higher marriage rates, improved child well-being, enhanced economic stability, and strengthened community ties.



03



Statement on Topic Two: "Private Enterprises and Finance, Domestic and International"

Human Rights



Original Text ——


Today I would like to discuss the potential of Microbial fuel cells, which use microorganisms to convert organic waste into electricity.


These fuel cells have dual benefits: they generate clean energy and manage waste which makes them especially appealing for industries seeking sustainable operations. Private businesses can leverage MFCs to reduce energy costs and carbon footprints. This directly improves their bottom line while enhancing environmental, social and ESG performance which is a key consideration for investors in the modern world.


The integration of microbial fuel cell technology into private business models can catalyse a shift towards circular economies , where waste is not merely discarded but reimagined as a resource. For instance, companies in the food and beverage sector can use MFCs to process organic waste from production, turning a liability into a revenue-generating asset. This creates new investment opportunities, particularly for venture capital and private equity firms looking to fund innovative solutions that align with sustainable practices.


On an international scale, the adoption of MFCs can foster partnerships between businesses in developed and developing regions . Wealthier nations can invest in MFC technology in emerging markets, helping to solve waste management challenges while creating a new revenue stream. This not only enhances the financial viability of local businesses but also drives sustainable development, aligning with global goals like the UN Sustainable Development Goals.


To conclude, the business case for microbial fuel cells is clear. They offer a pathway to reduce costs, meet regulatory demands, and attract investment. Embracing microbial fuel cells is not just a smart financial decision; it's a commitment to a more sustainable and equitable future for all.





Translation ——


I would like to explore the immense potential of microbial fuel cells (MFCs), an innovative technology that uses microorganisms to convert organic waste into electricity. Microbial fuel cells offer a dual advantage: they not only generate clean energy but also effectively manage waste, making them particularly attractive to businesses seeking sustainable operations. Private enterprises can leverage this technology to reduce energy costs, lower carbon footprints, improve financial performance, and enhance environmental, social, and governance (ESG) performance—factors increasingly critical in modern investment decisions.


Integrating microbial fuel cell technology into business models can drive the transition toward a circular economy, in which waste is no longer a discarded burden but a valuable resource. For example, companies in the food and beverage industry can use microbial fuel cells to treat organic waste generated during production, transforming what would otherwise be a liability into a profitable asset. This transformation creates new opportunities for investors, especially venture capital and private equity firms seeking to support innovative solutions aligned with sustainable development practices.


At the international level, the promotion of microbial fuel cells can foster collaboration between developed regions and developing countries. Wealthier nations can invest microbial fuel cell technology in emerging markets, helping to solve waste management issues while creating new sources of income for these markets. In this way, the financial viability of local enterprises is enhanced, and sustainable development is advanced, aligning with global initiatives such as the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals.

 

In summary, the commercial value of microbial fuel cells is evident. They offer an effective pathway to reduce costs, meet regulatory requirements, and attract investment. Adopting microbial fuel cells is not only a sound financial decision but also a commitment to building a more sustainable and equitable future for all humanity.

Q&A Session

Human Rights



Q: 

AWDPI Question ——


How can we address the impact of digital commercialization on low-income women?


:A

Representative Response ——


In my previous speech, I overlooked the perspective of women's protection. In fact, digital commercialization can effectively safeguard women's rights in the following ways.


First, digital commercial entities should actively develop new occupations suitable for women, a major advantage of digital enterprises over traditional ones. In the digital economy, women are no longer constrained by gender, pregnancy, or physical strength. For example, emerging industries such as online retail, internet healthcare, and live streaming provide new employment platforms and opportunities for women, breaking down gender inequality barriers in traditional industries.


Second, governments should strengthen digital skills training for women, especially rural women in remote and underdeveloped areas. ⟧72⟧ Specialized digital economy entrepreneurship training programs targeting women can be established to help them improve their digital skills. Digital enterprises can also leverage their strengths to provide training resources and support, helping women better integrate into the digital economy and enhance their employability. . Specialized digital economy entrepreneurship training programs for women could be established to help them improve their digital skills. Digital enterprises can also leverage their strengths to provide training resources and support for women, helping them better integrate into the digital economy and enhance their employability.


Finally, digital enterprises should strive to develop artificial intelligence applications free from gender bias. This means ensuring that data used in AI development is gender-sensitive and avoiding unintentional gender discrimination in algorithm design. This not only promotes gender equality but also helps women gain more opportunities in the digital age.




At this forum, AWDPI engaged in productive exchanges and learning with other humanitarian-related non-governmental organizations. Through these interactions, we conducted a multidimensional review of development financing strategies and proposed concrete recommendations on how to address the development gap through international cooperation.


However, this was not merely a theoretical discussion, but a call to action. It inspires each of us—governments, the private sector, and civil society alike—to take practical and feasible measures to ensure our development path is inclusive, sustainable, and benefits every corner of the globe and every person in need. Our efforts are not only for today's prosperity, but also to lay a more equitable and sustainable development path for future generations..
