After you left China, have you ever taken a fresh look at your own body?


"Pale, young, thin" was once the ideal female image instilled in many people from childhood. But when you are overseas, facing a different set of beauty standards, you might find that here, "sexiness," "curves," and "confidence" are preferred—leading you to wonder whether you are "too thin" or "not sexy enough."


Women's bodies seem perpetually measured by some standard, constantly redefined across different cultures.


As Chinese women move overseas, experiences concerning their bodies, beauty ideals, and being looked at often become more complex.


As a nonprofit organization consistently focusing on the development and living conditions of Chinese women overseas, AWDPI hopes to bring these experiences and reflections into dialogue with you.



May 30, we have invited Meng Tong, PhD in Sociology of Education from Durham University, to join us in an online conversation about bodies, aesthetics, and intimate relationships, collectively unpacking the cultural and structural forces behind these seemingly "personal" confusions.




About the guest




Meng Tong, PhD in Sociology of Education from Durham University, researches digital intimacy, Chinese international students, and intercultural communication. Her doctoral research focused on Chinese female international students' perceptions and experiences using dating apps in the UK. Her academic work has been published in Sociological Research Online, Chinese Journal of Communication, and Feminist Media Studies.



We will discuss




  • How is the "pale, young, thin" beauty ideal linked to traits like submissiveness and infantilized femininity? What exactly is being disciplined?

  • What role does globalization play behind the differences between Eastern and Western beauty standards?

  • Does "conforming to Western beauty ideals" truly mean freedom? Or is it merely jumping from one form of discipline into another?

  • Why are Chinese female international students more inclined not to date Chinese men? Does being desired by foreign men equate to being respected?

  • As a Chinese female scholar, how can one find her place within Western academic spaces?
