1- Prologue


The curtain falls, the dialogue begins

After the stage lights dim in London and Edinburgh, the echoes about women's conditions do not fade. Between the narratives of "independent women" and "intimate relationships," we seem constantly pushed toward a certain choice—one that has never been merely about personal will. It is entangled with tensions among family expectations, cultural experiences, economic realities, and identity, a complexity often stripped from the very structures that shape it, reduced instead into simplified labels that judge women's experiences. Yet collective reflection on how women make choices within these structures remains insufficient, and Asian women within Western discourse frequently face underrepresentation and narrative voids.


As part of AWDPI's empowerment initiative, this event seeks to respond to that absence. We invite you to step into the script reading workshop of *The Dream of Being a Madame*to reposition the concept of "choice" within its structural context through concrete scenarios of abstract dilemmas; through dialogue and listening, women are no longer mere objects of observation, but become subjects narrating their own experiences. We hope to continuously reconstruct the expressive space and debates around "independence" and "relationships" for Asian women within cross-cultural contexts, to portray more complex, authentic, and multi-dimensional portraits of Asian women, and to foster empathy, resonance, and connection through intellectual exchange.


2- Main Body


Work Analysis & Artist Profile & Core Themes

*The Dream of Being a Madame* is a play by playwright Qi Wang focusing on contemporary women's identity choices. Since its premiere in London, the work has been staged at multiple theatres and art platforms including Etcetera Theatre, Edinburgh Fringe Festival (theSpace on the Mile), and Omnibus Theatre, continuously drawing attention within the UK's fringe and small-theatre drama scene. The piece has received positive reviews from several media outlets and multiple four-star ratings, showcasing a distinct authorial voice and strong stage potential.


As an Asian woman artist active in the British theatre scene, Qi Wang Qi Wang brings sharp observations of women's lived realities, consistently documenting the silenced voices of women. She is not only a writer of stories, but also an observer of women's fates, deconstructing societal labels such as "alpha woman" and "love-obsessed" and placing them into a real-life laboratory for comparison.


Lairs, a hedonist who lives freely and "for love"


May, a restrained realist who upholds independence and self-reliance


As they confront personal destinies, intimate relationships, independence versus dependence, responsibility versus self-fulfilment,


they navigate between their emotional desires and social realities, reflecting and colliding with each other,


constantly opposing and pulling apart, yet pausing for one another, reaching out a helping hand,


composing a narrative of intense ideological conflict and contemporary female dilemmas. Even as the story reaches its irreversible finale,


the work offers no definitive answers, but instead invites audiences to reflect within the conflict, confronting "problems that cannot be easily solved."


Their dialogue is also an artistic experiment on "identity, responsibility, and relationships." Through vignettes of Asian women's survival in "a foreign land," the play constructs a thicket of imagery: Is a woman's body and will her own safe haven, or a toolkit of social structures?


Through script excerpts, we will jointly deconstruct the following deep-seated conflicts:


  • Love vs. Survival: Unpacking the real costs and survival instincts behind emotional choices, exploring how women are shaped under societal gaze

  • Independence vs. Dependence: Peeling back the romantic veil of love to reveal hidden power dynamics; what possibilities exist for women's "free choice" under structural constraints?

  • Female Friendship: Examining the complex support, psychological projection, and emotional boundaries between women

  • Responsibility and Identity in a Cross-Cultural Context: Amid pressures from family, marriage, overseas survival, and cultural compression, how do Asian women seek authentic love and selfhood, and how do they redefine what it means to be a "successful woman"?


3- Event Details


An immersive experiment of "identity shift"

This is not a one-way traditional lecture or reading, but an interactive online script read-through × deep critical thinking workshop. We invite guests to interpret unspoken subtexts, pause at every explosive moment, and feel the script's breath and struggle through voicing lines ourselves, breaking the "fourth wall" between "observer" and "character."


Here, the stage is not predetermined, and women's emotions are no longer elusive abstract concepts. We invite you to let your thoughts flow freely, allowing the script to extend, intertwine, and resonate with reality. Through diverse analyses of emotional relationships and artistic exploration, we aim to achieve the most honest self-examination of "how we choose our lives": women who often unconsciously fall into society's preset templates must re-understand those concrete life junctures, so that every decision tomorrow is grounded in inner clarity.

4- Conclusion


From "her" to "us"

This is not just a heart-to-heart between "two girls," but a story about every woman seeking love and selfhood in the cracks. The characters May or Lairs are not distant fictions—they may be right beside us, and their struggles may lurk in some moment of your or my life. As AWDPI, we hope to invite you into these public discussions on gender issues. Perhaps we cannot find answers in a single conversation, but we can jointly ask questions, re-understand, and deeply connect. The significance of discussion will not end the moment the script or event concludes.


On 28 April we hope art becomes a bridge, connection continues to grow, and those unspoken experiences slowly emerge and cease to be isolated through telling, listening, and responding. We look forward to you joining us to "awaken," "experiment," and "reclaim" your "scripted life," and to tell those "theys" who are just like "us": How would you rewrite their endings, and how would you author your own narrative sovereignty?
