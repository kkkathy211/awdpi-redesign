How does AI amplify violence against women, and what can laws, platforms, and each of us do about it? Why can an ordinary photo posted on WeChat Moments, LinkedIn, or a school website be turned into a "nude" within seconds using AI? Why do the fear, shame, and sense of losing control feel so real, even when the images are fake?


Earlier this year, X platform's built-in AI chatbot Grok was widely used by users to "undress" real women's photos with one click. According to estimates by the nonprofit Center for Countering Digital Hate (CCDH), Grok generated over 3 million sexualized images in just 11 days. Additionally, a study cited by UN Women shows that 98%90% of deepfake videos online 99%target women.

But forged images are only the most visible part. Extortion using fake nudes, tracking and monitoring partners through smart devices, using deepfakes and online harassment to drive female journalists and public figures out of public discourse—AI did not invent gender-based violence, but it has made it easier to carry out and harder to hold accountable.


Thursday, November 19, Sharon, Head of Advocacy and Impact at AWDPI's U.S. National Office and Research and Policy Team Lead at Stanford University's Public Interest Technology Lab, will lead a public in-person workshop at Stanford University's Haas Center for Public Service, titled

"AI-Enabled Gender-Based Violence and Intervention Strategies."

"AI-Facilitated Gender-Based Violence and Strategies for Intervention"


This workshop will begin with a real-life case, mapping out the forms of AI-enabled gender-based violence and who is harmed, and tracing the "supply chain" of a deepfake image from creation to dissemination to identify intervention points for legal systems, tech companies, and communities.

Discussion questions will include, but are not limited to:

01

When AI tools are used to commit gender-based violence, who is responsible—the person entering the command, the company developing and launching the AI tool, or the app stores and payment platforms profiting from it? Where should intervention begin?

02

If AI-enabled violence is pushing some women out of public life, what should a "survivor-centered" response look like, and who should be involved in designing it?
