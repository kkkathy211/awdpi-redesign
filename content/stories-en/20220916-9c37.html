 Introduction:


Save the Children (UK) Beijing Representative Office held a five-day "Youth Innovation and Entrepreneurship Lab" offline in Chengdu from August 22 to August 26, 2022.

The "Youth Innovation and Entrepreneurship Lab" is dedicated to providing young people who use business approaches to promote social development with a sustainable, participatory growth platform, aiming to enhance youth awareness of green development, support their transition from school to society, and help them succeed in future green jobs, bringing lasting and inspiring change.

Five members of Avoice registered as a team and deeply participated in the Youth Innovation and Entrepreneurship Lab's offline workshop. Through guest sharing sessions, the Avoice team gained in-depth understanding of the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs), built foundational knowledge of innovation, entrepreneurship, and the green economy, and learned essential professional skills for the entrepreneurial process. Going through five stages of social innovation—"identifying a social issue to address," "selecting a creative solution," "prototyping a product/service," "testing and refining," and "pitching the idea"—with support from Save the Children facilitators and Avoice advisors and members, the Avoice team explored real market needs and pain points, designing business solutions to better serve their target group.


The Avoice team focused on overseas Chinese individuals, investigating the challenges and root causes they face in employment, and promoting decent work opportunities for them. In particular, the Avoice team paid special attention to women who have suffered domestic violence and gender-based violence, offering them sustained employment support so that while escaping violence, they can also achieve economic independence and security.


At the end of the event, the Youth Innovation and Entrepreneurship Lab held a pitch competition and invited relevant professional guests as judges. The Avoice team won first prize in the pitch competition, earning praise from judges and other teams for their compassionate vision, creative plan, well-developed website, thoughtful service design, and confident, poised presentation.



Through this event, Avoice members grew through the exchange of ideas, built friendships through collaboration and communication, learned from each other's strengths in daily interactions, and discovered their own unique qualities. More importantly, this experience strengthened Avoice members' shared belief—creating a safe and equal environment for survival and development, and building together a women's community rooted in love and equality.




Offline Team Reflections


Yimar

Avoice Project Founder


I'm so glad to have had this opportunity to come to Chengdu with several Avoice teammates to participate in the Youth Innovation and Entrepreneurship Lab's offline training and business competition.

Most of Avoice's work usually takes place online, so this was a rare chance for us to spend several days meeting face-to-face and engaging in deep exchanges.

Our Avoice teammates are truly outstanding and reliable—working together and fighting side by side was such a joyful experience~

During this time, we also met in person with Xiao Shuan, one of Avoice China's board members and Secretary-General of the Chengdu Disabled Persons' Welfare Foundation, and enjoyed a Sichuan hotpot together (*≧▽≦) 


In this event, in response to Save the Children's requirements, we focused primarily on UN Sustainable Development Goal (SDG) 8 "Decent Work and Economic Growth," designing a business proposal whose underlying social mission was to address employment challenges faced by vulnerable overseas Chinese women.

Why did we want to do this? In fact, among the over 200 domestic violence cases Avoice has supported, we've seen that some women suffering from domestic violence also face economic and employment difficulties. However, it's hard to effectively help them solve these problems through purely charitable means alone, which has left us feeling somewhat powerless. So I thought that one day, when the right opportunity came, I would want to use business-driven, sustainable forces to drive improvement and solutions for this social issue.

This opportunity came just in time. After a week of research and planning, our team completed a relevant business proposal. On the final day of the competition, our proposal and pitch were fortunately recognized by all five judges, and we won first place!


Thank you to the Save the Children team for your thoughtful arrangements and patient guidance, thank you to the judges for your recognition, support, and sincere suggestions, and I'm also delighted to have met so many wonderful and lovely new friends in beautiful Chengdu.

I will never forget, during these beautiful years of youth, in the beautiful land of abundance—Chengdu—this wonderful time spent learning and growing together, joyfully tasting delicious food, pulling all-nighters to finalize our proposal, and shining brightly together for our shared ideals!



Guo Ruonan

Avoice

Member, Coordination and Management Division


During the five days of the Youth Innovation and Entrepreneurship Lab hosted by the international organization Save the Children, I heard many stories and met many people. Our encounters allowed us to feel more of the beauty in one another. We also accomplished many things I never would have imagined at the beginning. The talents, abilities, and insights each of Yimar, Xiao Yi, Song Song, and Sanjin possess amazed me. Conversing with them felt like "broadening my horizons"—something I found truly remarkable. I'd also like to thank facilitator Qianjie, who took time to talk with me individually when I was feeling low, listened to my thoughts, and made me genuinely experience Save the Children's humanistic care (smile).


In the team, my main responsibility was simulating our website, which caused me to "crash" for two days. I never imagined that as a humanities student, I'd end up doing technical work like web design. I now affectionately call the final product "my son." When people asked me a very Avoice question—"Why a son, not a daughter?"—I immediately replied: "Because daughters wouldn't stress me out this much! (Not really)"


I'd like to share some fun moments:


One of my daily rituals was asking Xiao Yi, "What are we eating tonight?" So Xiao Yi dutifully led us to great food spots. One day when she had to go home early, she still made sure to tell us in advance what we should eat that night. Another daily ritual was "sticking close" with Song Song—she'd always bump heads with me and say, "Let's stick!" Then there was hanging out with Yimar, gossiping and laughing like silly fools; and deep conversations with Sanjin about our life experiences, which sparked many resonances... On the final night, it started raining in Chengdu—long-awaited rain after a dry spell. At 2 a.m., we went downstairs for nucleic acid testing, running barefoot through the streets in slippers, dodging raindrops while laughing hysterically.


After leaving Chengdu, sitting on the northbound train, I reflected: what exactly did these five days give me? I think the meaning lies in us sitting together in a skewer restaurant chatting about everything under the sun, staying up until 5 a.m. working on our proposal, all picking up Sanjin's catchphrase "abstract," and in these brief, fragmented moments, maintaining our ability to share, learn, and be joyful. We are connected by our similarities, and we grow through our differences.



Song Zirui

Avoice

Member, Community Management Division


I've always thought "fate" is magical. Because of shared beliefs, we joined Avoice; because of the event hosted by Save the Children, we came from different places to gather in Chengdu; because we happened to be assigned to Group Three, we met Sister Zhang Qian and Teacher Haiqiang, spending five unforgettable days together; and all the other staff and teammates—we were all sincere, wonderful, warm, and lovely people in this event.


I know I'll never forget some small, fragmented moments—they made up the beautiful memories of these five days.


The large sheet of paper with our Group Three team name and everyone's names, listing our shared characteristics and unique traits. On it, I saw unique, shining individuals, and also the bonds that connected us.


The castle built from Lego bricks of different colors—I've always seen it as a metaphor for our collaboration. We come from different places, study different disciplines, and have different experiences, interests, and strengths. Our differences are like Lego bricks of various shapes, colors, and functions—complementary or mutually enhancing. When realizing our shared vision, we used our individual "bricks" to support one another, ultimately completing our joint creation.


The lunch meals that were always so abundant we couldn't finish them, the spicy hotpots and skewers, Yan Taipo's cold noodle guokui, vegetarian dishes from Wenshu Temple's vegetarian restaurant, hand-rubbed ice jelly sold by roadside vendors, late-night ChaBaiDao takeout deliveries... Each dish not only delighted our taste buds but also recorded our time together. How did we grow close? I think it was when we shared a pack of biscuits, asked "What are we eating tonight?", gossiped about things we'd heard, talked about our life choices and future plans, and pulled all-nighters together striving for one goal.


I've always remembered a quote by Huang Yongyu: "Whenever you feel the pain of parting upon first meeting someone or entering a new environment, you must have already fallen in love with it." At the end of the event, my emotions were mixed—joy from winning first prize, and sadness as everyone had to say goodbye. The five days had passed like a dream, flashing by in an instant. How many opportunities in life allow you to meet friends from diverse backgrounds, exchange ideas equally, openly, and inclusively, collaborate with a reliable team who shares your beliefs, and use your talents and creativity to gradually bring ideas to life? I cherished every moment with everyone, and sincerely hope we'll meet again—sending big hugs to each friend. Love you all!



Yang Xin

Member, Avoice Communications Division


During the five-day Youth Innovation Lab hosted by the international organization Save the Children, I traveled from a small town in Jiangsu-Zhejiang to Sichuan, where the weather remained scorching hot. At the time, I had visited five or six medium-to-high-risk areas, and soon started sneezing and coughing—I even thought I might have caught COVID. But everyone was so understanding and supportive. Staff from Save the Children kept helping me find solutions, and my Avoice teammates stayed connected online, checking in on me regularly. I truly love you all!


I gained a lot during these few days. Although I've had multiple fieldwork experiences as a documentary worker, this life experience was different from my usual solo investigations. The teamwork this time truly moved me. Everyone had their role, and we pulled all-nighters together. Even though I kept saying, "I hate business competitions—I'll never do this again," deep down, I'm really looking forward to continuing to brainstorm new ideas and solutions with all of you.


After we parted ways, I found myself stuck in a small city in western Sichuan, and spent many moments wondering: is it experience or simply the passage of time that makes us grow? I suddenly recalled reading Melinda Gates' book The Moment of Lift recently, and one sentence particularly resonated with me: "Supporting feminism means believing that women have the right to speak up, to unlock their potential, and that men and women should work together to eliminate the barriers and biases that still hold women back!" I'm deeply grateful to Avoice and Save the Children for giving me the chance to experience and create some entirely new moments.
