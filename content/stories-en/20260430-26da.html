Why does something that seems like "love" make us feel increasingly uneasy? Why do we clearly sense that something is "off" in a relationship, yet still can't walk away?


Control, belittlement, emotional coldness, isolation—these harms, which may not always leave physical "traces," often appear in our lives under the guise of "love," hidden within the closest relationships we have.


Following the first lecture in April, AWDPI continues to partner with the nonprofit organization PCE Club to present the second online public welfare lecture:


"Between Love and Safety: Family 'Violence' (II)—Psychological Support, Identifying 'Dangerous Relationships,' and Healing Intergenerational Trauma [Continued]"



PCE Club (Parents, Children, and Education Club) is a nonprofit educational organization established in 2003, dedicated to emotional intelligence parenting and mental health education, providing a platform for learning and exchange for Chinese families in North America, having served over 3,000 families to date.


This lecture will incorporate real-life cases and be co-led by a family lawyer and a psychotherapist, guiding participants to recognize "dangerous relationships," understand violence, manipulation, and intergenerational trauma within families and intimate relationships, and safeguard safety within love.


Special guest—screenwriter and director of the TV dramas "Don't Talk to Strangers" and "Dangerous Relationships"—Xue Xiaolu will join the discussion. Xue Xiaolu will explore with the audience how "violence, manipulation, and harm" are not just plotlines on television, but are being enacted every day in real life and within families.


Let the storyline reflect reality; let us understand the stories, and also see ourselves and our families, making clear-eyed judgments between love and safety.
