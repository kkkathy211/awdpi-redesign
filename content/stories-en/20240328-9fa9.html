 NO.2 

[France Chapter]Are you or someone you care about experiencing domestic violence?


1. [Legal Definition]

  • According to the French Penal Code and Civil Code, domestic violence refers to violent acts against a spouse or partner, regardless of whether the couple is married, cohabiting, or separated. Violent acts may include psychological, physical, economic, or sexual violence.


2. [Current situation of domestic violence in France]

  • According to statistics from Statista, in 2020, a total of 213,000 women in France suffered physical or sexual violence from a current or former spouse.

  • In addition, in 2021, the number of femicide cases increased by 20% compared to the previous year, with 122 women killed by their current or former partners.


        Source:

  • https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/section_lc/LEGITEXT000006070719/LEGISCTA000006181751/2018-08-06/#LEGISCTA000006181751 

  • https://www.aa.com.tr/en/europe/france-struggling-to-tackle-violence-against-women/2748376 

  • https://www.statista.com/statistics/782264/women-victims-physical-violence-domestic-violence-france/ 


3. [Common types]

(1) Physical violence

  • Inflicting physical abuse on a spouse, such as persistent low-level violence, frequently slapping, hitting, grabbing, pulling, shoving, beating, or confining a partner.

(2) Psychological violence (cold violence)

  • A form of invisible psychological abuse characterized by indifference, contempt, neglect, and emotional distancing; showing apathy toward the partner, minimizing verbal communication, and refusing to perform household duties.

(3) Economic violence

  • Forcibly controlling the victim's money and property, depriving the victim of financial resources and medical access, severely affecting the victim's quality of life, and recklessly spending shared funds for personal enjoyment.

(4) Sexual violence

  • Forcing the other person into any form of sexual act, attempting sexual acts, making offensive sexual suggestions or sexual harassment, or engaging in sexual transactions.



 NO.3 

[France Chapter]How to seek legal protection?


1. [Emergency measures]

1.1 Emergency hotline numbers:

  • Police emergency number: 17

  • French national domestic violence helpline: 3919


1.2 Preserving evidence

  • Medical certificates, testimonies, complaint records, accident reports, photos, videos, screenshots, or copies of written communications (letters, emails, text messages, etc.), or recordings of verbal communications (voicemails), especially in cases of psychological violence. Testimonies from relatives are also one of the main forms of evidence in domestic violence cases, whether physical or psychological.

  • However, in civil proceedings, evidence must be obtained lawfully and fairly. Recordings made without the abuser's knowledge may be deemed inadmissible. Each testimony must be dated and signed, handwritten, and accompanied by a photocopy of the submitter's identification. In civil cases, children's testimonies are generally not accepted. However, they may be admitted in criminal proceedings. There is no minimum age for minor children to testify. The judge assesses the child's capacity to discern within the context of family violence and may base decisions on the child's testimony.


2. [Civil protection orders]

2.1 Introduction

  • According to French Ordinance No. 2010-769 of 9 July 2010 on violence specifically targeting women, domestic violence, and its impact on children: victims may obtain a civil protection order, an emergency civil measure available to victims of violence within a partner relationship.


2.2 Eligibility conditions

  • Eligible individuals:

    • Civil protection orders apply to various partner relationships, including married couples, couples bound by a civil solidarity pact (PACS), or couples cohabiting as common-law partners. Under the Grenelle Law, eligibility for civil protection orders has been extended to former spouses, former PACS partners, and non-cohabiting partners.

  • Conditions:

    • A civil protection order may be applied for when the applicant is suffering from domestic violence. The violence may be physical, psychological, or sexual, and may have occurred during or after the relationship. Even if the victim has not yet filed a criminal complaint, a judge may issue a civil protection order upon application.


2.3 Application procedure

  • The applicant files a request with the family court judge (juge aux affaires familiales). The plaintiff must provide evidence demonstrating the violence suffered (e.g., medical certificates) and the danger faced by the victim. This evidence will be presented and debated before the judge, who must within six days decide whether to grant the protection order.


2.4 Legal consequences

  • Any person subject to one or more obligations or prohibitions specified in a protection order issued under Article 515-9 or 515-13 of the French Civil Code who fails to comply shall be liable to two years' imprisonment and a fine of 15,000 euros.

  • Under a protection order issued under Article 515-9 of the French Civil Code, any person required to make payments or allowances who fails to notify the creditor of a change of address within one month shall be liable to six months' imprisonment and a fine of 7,500 euros.


2.5 Legal basis

3. [Shelter]

3.1 Introduction

  • Contact the police:

    • Police stations can help locate emergency accommodation for individuals experiencing domestic violence. To apply for this service, you may go to any nearby police station ("commissariat de police") or gendarmerie ("brigade de gendarmerie"). Police will try to find accommodation for the victim on the same day, including shelters exclusively for women.

  • Contact "Samusocial":

    • This organization manages the national support and advice hotline 115 for homeless individuals. They will make every effort to provide emergency shelter for victims, including hotel rooms or shelters at night, helping victims stay as far away from the abuser as possible. However, there may be waiting times to access shelter services, therefore contacting the police is recommended as the first option.

  • Applying for social housing in France:

    • If your financial resources are limited, you can apply to rent social housing, known as "logement social," also referred to as "HLM." HLM refers to rent-controlled housing, which is usually cheaper than private housing. Victims of domestic abuse are placed on a priority list for housing applications.


3.2 Eligibility Conditions

To apply for social housing, you must:

  • Have the right to reside in France for more than three months.

  • Not exceed the maximum allowable income. The exact amount depends on the type and location of the housing, the number of people to be accommodated, and varies by French department.

  • If a judge has issued you a specific type of alert, known as "Téléphone Grave Danger" (https://www.womenforwomenfrance.org/zh/our-resources/safety-planning-and-protection-orders/protection-orders/asking-a-judge-to-prevent-an-ex-partner-from-approaching-you) "Téléphone Grave Danger" (Severe Danger Phone), and you apply for social housing, the housing can be automatically assigned to you. You can contact the organization responsible for managing the "Téléphone Grave Danger," which will help you learn more about the social housing application process.

  • If you were living with your partner and left the home due to domestic violence, you are considered a priority applicant for social housing (according to the "Code de la construction et de l'habitation", art. L-441.1). In your application, you must provide an official document from a family court judge, known as a "juge aux affaires familiales (JAF)," which includes evidence of the violence suffered. Based on your application, the court may issue:

    • An interim ruling establishing emergency measures, such as authorization to live separately and with your children (if any).

    • Or the issuance of a protection order, known as an "ordonnance de protection" (https://www.womenforwomenfrance.org/zh/our-resources/safety-planning-and-protection-orders/protection-orders/asking-a-judge-to-prevent-an-ex-partner-from-approaching-you) .

    • "ordonnance de protection." However, in practice, some organizations managing social housing ("bailleurs sociaux") also accept police complaints. In such cases, you only need to provide proof that you have taken this step (a "récépissé de plainte"), and your access to social housing should be faster than usual. However, waiting times vary depending on the department where you reside.      


3.3 Application Procedure

(1) Police Station:

  • If you are unsure whether you can communicate accurately in French, you can show them this letter to assist communication.

    • Download link: https://www.womenforwomenfrance.org/zh/our-resources/police-and-justice/letter-to-present-to-the-police

  • You will need to answer questions about the abuse you have experienced to prove that your home is no longer a safe place.

  • You do not need to file a formal complaint (https://www.womenforwomenfrance.org/zh/nos-ressources/police-et-justice/reporting-domestic-abuse-to-the-french-justice-system) to receive emergency accommodation.

  • You may also ask the police to accompany you to retrieve your belongings. Depending on the urgency of the situation the police must handle, this may take more or less time.

(2) Samusocial:

  • Call the 24/7 free hotline 115

  • If you indicate that you cannot communicate accurately in French, an interpreter will be arranged for you.

  • It is recommended to call between 11 a.m. and 3 p.m.

  • If no one answers your call, you may try multiple times.

(3) Social Housing:

Your application must include the following documents:

  • Photos of identification documents, such as both sides of your ID card or passport

  • If you are not a citizen of an EU member state, provide a photo of your residence permit or "titre de séjour"

  • Documents proving your financial resources, such as pay slips and tax statements

  • If you have suffered domestic abuse, provide documents proving the abuse:

    • If possible, provide a ruling issued by a family court judge, known as a "juge aux affaires familiales"

    • Otherwise, provide a document showing that you have filed a complaint, known as a "récépissé de plainte."

(4) Online Application:

Most social housing applications are submitted online, following these steps:

  • Visit the website http://www.demande-logement-social.gouv.fr/ and complete the form.

  • Upload the required documents, ensuring your application is as complete as possible.

  • After registration, someone will verify your photocopies of identification and your application.

  • Once your application is verified, you will receive a unique registration number, known as a "numéro unique d'enregistrement," within five working days of submission. With this number, you can track the progress of your case online.

(5) In-Person Application:

You must complete this form (https://www.formulaires.service-public.fr/gf/cerfa_14069.do) and submit it along with all required documents.

You can find a list of available local offices in your city or town at https://www.demande-logement-social.gouv.fr/offresParCommune.afficher:

  • Click on "Connaître les offres de logements sociaux sur une commune"

  • Enter your postal code

  • At the bottom of the page, select "Consulter la liste des guichets."

(7) Getting Help

If you are unsure how to complete the application, you can make an appointment with a professional (https://www.womenforwomenfrance.org/zh/our-resources/money-accommodation-independence/accommodation/applying-for-social-and-affordable-housing-in-france#find_support) who can help you fill out the form.


4. [Free Legal Aid]

4.1 Introduction

  • If you do not have enough money to hire a lawyer or pay legal fees, you can request that the government cover these costs, known as legal aid or "aide juridictionnelle." In certain cases, primarily based on your income, "L'aide juridictionnelle" can cover part or all of your legal expenses. This allows you to hire a lawyer or legal professional, such as a "huissier de justice."

  • You can apply for "l'aide juridictionnelle" before or during legal proceedings, and you may choose a lawyer who accepts cases supported by "l'aide juridictionnelle," or request that a lawyer be assigned to you when you submit your application.


Legal Counseling:

  • "Point-justice" centers bring together various organizations that can provide legal advice based on your situation, and sometimes also assist with administrative procedures.

    • Free service

    • These centers go by many names: "Maison de Justice et du Droit (MJD)," "Point d'accès au droit (PAD)," "Relais d'accès au droit (RAD)," "Antenne de justice (AJ)," or "France services (FS)."

    • You can find a "point-justice" near you by:

      • Using the online directory (http://www.annuaires.justice.gouv.fr/annuaires-12162/les-point-justice-34055.html) to search

      • Calling 3039 from mainland France or +33 9 70 82 31 90 from overseas. They will ask for your postal code and refer you to a "point-justice." Services are primarily in French.

  • "Centres d'Information sur les Droits des Femmes et des Familles (CIDFF)" provide assistance to the public in many areas, especially women, such as: legal rights, health, job searching, training, entrepreneurship, and even parenting.

    • Free service

    • They will inform you of your rights and the steps you need to take. Some centers can help you complete various procedures and paperwork.

    • Available languages: primarily French

    • Contact: For contact details of "CIDFF" in your region, see the directory (https://fncidff.info/les-cidff/cidff-a-votre-service) .


4.2 Eligibility Conditions

In most cases, you can apply for "l'aide juridictionnelle" if:

  • You are currently involved in or about to initiate legal proceedings

  • You meet the financial eligibility criteria: your income and assets must not exceed a certain amount, (https://www.service-public.fr/particuliers/vosdroits/F18074)

    • based on reference tax income and the value of movable and immovable property below a certain threshold. You can use the following online service to simulate the amount of legal aid: Estimate the amount of legal aid (https://www.service-public.fr/particuliers/vosdroits/R59382)



  • You have the right to reside in France, are a French or European national, or habitually reside in France (excluding short-term stays)

  • You do not have insurance covering all your legal expenses. If unsure, check your various insurance policies, such as home insurance.

However, exceptions exist:

  • If you do not meet the residency or financial eligibility criteria, you may still automatically qualify for "l'aide juridictionnelle" in extremely serious circumstances, for example, if:

    • You were a victim of a serious crime such as rape

    • You have applied for a protection order ("ordonnance de protection")

    • You have received a document called "Obligation de quitter le territoire français (OQTF)" requiring you to leave the country.

  • You may also qualify for "l'aide juridictionnelle" if you are in a situation deemed sufficiently serious by law, known as a "situation digne d'intérêt." It is recommended to contact an organization offering free services that specifically supports victims of domestic violence and/or foreigners in legal matters. They can assist you with this application.


4.3 Application Procedure

  • You must use the cerfa form n°16146 * 03. You can fill it out online, download it, or obtain it at a town hall or court. (Apply for legal aid: https://www.service-public.fr/particuliers/vosdroits/R1444)

  •   Legal aid can be applied for before or during legal proceedings. You may also apply for legal aid after the proceedings end, for example, to enforce a court judgment. If your legal protection insurance does not cover trial costs, you must attach a certificate of non-coverage. If your policy covers part of the costs, you must include details of the covered expenses. (Certificate of non-coverage by insurer: https://www.service-public.fr/particuliers/vosdroits/R39717)

  • You can receive help filling out the form at a Maison de la Justice et du Droit (or Point d'accès au droit) near you.

    • Local support services for victims of violence, known as "associations"

      • These services are free. The services offered by each "association" vary. They can provide advice and sometimes assist with various procedures and paperwork.

      • Available languages: primarily French.

      • In the directory, (https://arretonslesviolences.gouv.fr/associations-de-lutte-contre-les-violences-sexistes-et-sexuelles/associations) select your French department to find a list of "associations" near you that specialize in helping victims of violence.

  • The legal aid office responsible for processing your application depends on the court that must hear the case. For district courts or appellate courts:

    • You must submit your application to the legal aid office responsible for your place of residence.

    • If you do not have a fixed address, you may submit your application to the legal aid office responsible for the institution that issued your certificate of domicile choice.

    • The legal aid office is responsible for deciding on applications for legal aid in cases submitted to first and second instance courts. Examples: judicial courts, administrative courts, appellate courts, administrative appellate courts.

      • You can find the office responsible for your residence in the annex of the decree establishing the list and jurisdiction of legal aid offices, dated December 7, 2020:

      • If you do not have a stable residence, you can undergo a domicile procedure before submitting your documents. (https://www.service-public.fr/particuliers/vosdroits/F17317) .

  • France does not provide legal aid for cases before foreign courts. However, if you are a French resident from countries such as Quebec, Tunisia, Morocco, or Algeria, you may receive legal aid under the same conditions as nationals of those countries. You can make a request according to the applicable procedures in the relevant country.

  • If your dispute is being heard by a court in another country European Union: Austria, Belgium, Bulgaria, Croatia, Cyprus, Czech Republic, Denmark, Estonia, Finland, Germany, France, Greece, Hungary, Ireland, Italy, Lithuania, Latvia, Luxembourg, Malta, Netherlands, Poland, Portugal, Romania, Slovakia, Slovenia, Spain, Sweden (as of January 1, 2021, excluding Denmark and the UK), the relevant state may provide you with assistance, but only in civil and commercial matters. Assistance will then be provided according to the current procedures of the respective country.

  • In this case, you must use a specific form (https://e-justice.europa.eu/content_legal_aid_forms-157-fr.do) and send it to the French Ministry of Justice, which will forward your request to the relevant country.

5. [Holding the perpetrator criminally responsible]

5.1 Crimes the perpetrator may be charged with

  • Sexual harassment: Viewing a person's private parts by any means, without their consent or knowledge, when the person has concealed their private parts due to their clothing or presence in a closed space.
    (Article 226-3-1 of the French Penal Code, see https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/article_lc/LEGIARTI000037288087/2018-08-06)

  • Physical violence: Violence intentionally or unintentionally inflicted on another person. For example, waiting for a spouse, direct ascendant or descendant, or any other person usually residing in the home of the person mentioned in the first paragraph, with the purpose (supported by one or more significant facts) of using or threatening to use a weapon to commit violence. (Articles 222-7 to 222-15-1 of the French Penal Code, see https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/article_lc/LEGIARTI000006417608;
    https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/article_lc/LEGIARTI000043540886)

  • Breach of a protection order: Any person subject to one or more obligations or prohibitions under a protection order issued under Article 515-9 or 515-13 of the Civil Code, who fails to comply with these obligations or prohibitions, shall be liable to two years' imprisonment and a fine of 15,000 euros. (Articles 227-4-2 to 227-4-3 of the French Penal Code, see https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/article_lc/LEGIARTI000038312972)

  • Rape: Any act of sexual penetration or any oral or genital act committed on another person or by the perpetrator through violence, coercion, threat, or surprise constitutes rape. (Articles 222-23 and 222-24 of the French Penal Code, see https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/article_lc/LEGIARTI000043409305; https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/article_lc/LEGIARTI000043409300)

  • Spousal harassment : Repeated acts intended or having the effect of deteriorating the living conditions of a spouse or cohabiting partner, resulting in harm to their physical or mental health. The same penalties apply to offenses committed by a former spouse or partner of the victim, or by a former partner bound by a civil solidarity pact with the victim. (Article 222-33-2-1 of the French Penal Code, see https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/article_lc/LEGIARTI000042193490)


5.2 How to file a report

(1) Reporting to the police, through four methods:

  1. By phone: dial the free number 17.

  2. By text message: send a message in French to 114, indicating your exact address.

  3. Online: Chat service (https://www.service-public.fr/cmi) . This service is available in multiple languages.

  4. In person: visit any police station, known as a "commissariat de police" or "brigade de gendarmerie". You can find the nearest station at https://www.interieur.gouv.fr/Contact/Contacter-une-brigade-de-gendarmerie-ou-un-commissariat-de-police. If you do not speak French, they are required to provide you with an interpreter, which may take some time.

    • Any police station or gendarmerie has a legal obligation to take a statement, preferably through a formal complaint, or alternatively through a report of incident (main-courante or procès-verbal de renseignement judiciaire).

      • A formal complaint is the act by which a person brings to the attention of the public prosecutor, a police department, or a gendarmerie a criminal offense of which they believe themselves to be the victim.

      • A report of incident is a simple statement that can be made to any national police station or gendarmerie, with the reported facts recorded in a register or online form. In principle, submitting such a report does not trigger any investigation or judicial follow-up, and serves only to create a written record.

(2) Call the national free helpline for victims of violence at 3919:

  • During the call, an advisor will listen and guide you to the appropriate support services.

  • Available languages: French.

    • The following languages may sometimes be available: English, Arabic, Spanish, Turkish, Mandarin, Chinese, Kurdish, Czech, Polish, Hebrew, Persian, Soninke, Creole, Kinyarwanda, Kirundi, and Swahili.

    • Unfortunately, these languages are not regularly available and no fixed schedule is provided.

  • Contact: Call 3919 at any time. The call will not appear on your phone bill.

  • Deaf, hard-of-hearing, aphasic, or individuals with speech or language impairments can click the phone icon in the bottom right corner of the website www.solidaritefemmes.org to access the services they need.

6. [Divorce]

6.1 Types of divorce

  • Divorce by mutual consent

  • Judicial divorce

    • Including divorce by mutual agreement, divorce based on fault, and divorce due to the breakdown of the marriage.


6.2 Grounds/conditions for divorce

According to Article 233 of the French Civil Code, spouses may jointly request divorce when their marriage has broken down, regardless of the facts causing the breakdown.


6.3 Divorce procedures

 (1) Judicial divorce by mutual agreement

  • Article 230 of the French Civil Code: When both spouses jointly apply for divorce, they are not required to disclose the reasons publicly; they only need to submit a draft agreement for judicial approval, and the judge will determine the consequences of the divorce.

    • The application may be filed by each party's respective lawyer, or by a single lawyer jointly chosen by both parties, with mutual consent.

    • Divorce by mutual consent may not be requested during the first six months of marriage.

  • Article 231 of the French Civil Code: After reviewing the application, the judge shall convene the lawyers or the parties themselves for a hearing.

    • If both spouses maintain their desire to divorce, the judge will inform them that their request must be renewed after a three-month reflection period.

    • If the request is not renewed within six months after the end of the reflection period, the joint application becomes invalid.

  • Article 232 of the French Civil Code: If the judge is convinced that both spouses genuinely and freely consent to the divorce, the judge shall grant the divorce. The judge shall simultaneously determine how the agreement resolves the consequences of the divorce.

    • The judge may refuse to approve the agreement and deny the divorce if the judge finds that the agreement fails to safeguard the interests of the children or of one of the spouses.

  • Articles 233 to 234 of the French Civil Code: If both spouses agree to divorce but cannot reach an agreement on matters such as property, they may bring the case to court without stating reasons for the divorce, and the judge will rule directly. However, once divorce by mutual consent is granted, it cannot be reversed or appealed.

  • Articles 237 to 238 of the French Civil Code: If spouses have been separated for more than one year, either party may request divorce on the grounds of separation exceeding one year. The judge will grant the divorce upon proof of separation lasting over one year. Additionally, the disappearance of one spouse falls under this category.

    • It should be noted that one spouse may file a petition during the separation period, but legal proceedings for divorce can only begin after the one-year separation period has expired.

(2) Divorce based on fault

  • Articles 242, 244, and 245 of the French Civil Code: One spouse may request divorce based on events attributable to the other spouse that constitute a breach or repeated breaches of marital duties and obligations. A spouse who, due to fault or serious violation of legal duties and obligations, makes married life intolerable may be sued for divorce by the other spouse, who may also claim damages.


6.4 Child custody

  • Article 373 of the French Civil Code

    • A parent may lose or be temporarily deprived of parental authority in the following circumstances: if they are unable, absent, distant, or for any other reason unable to express their will; if they have been sentenced for one of the various grounds of abandonment of the family. If sentenced for one of the various grounds of abandonment of the family, they must have resumed their duties for at least six months. If a judgment of full or partial dismissal is issued against them, their rights shall be declared forfeited.

  • Article 374 of the French Civil Code

    • If the parent-child relationship is established with only one of the parents, that parent shall exercise parental authority alone.

    • If the parent-child relationship with both parents is established in a manner other than provided in Article 372, parental authority shall be exercised by the mother.

    • Parental authority may be exercised jointly if both parents make a joint declaration before the chief clerk of the court of first instance. In cases of joint exercise of parental authority, the parent with whom the child does not usually reside shall contribute to the child's maintenance and education according to the respective capacities of the parents.

    • In any case, the family court may, upon request by the father, mother, or public prosecutor, modify the conditions for exercising parental authority over a biological child. The court may decide that the authority be exercised by one of the parents or jointly by both. In such cases, the judge will designate the child's habitual residence.

    • The family court may grant supervisory authority to a parent who does not hold parental authority.

  • Article 374-1 of the French Civil Code

    • The court ruling on the establishment of natural parentage may decide to temporarily entrust the child to a third party, who will be responsible for requesting the organization of guardianship.

  • Article 374-2 of the French Civil Code

    • In all cases provided for in this section, guardianship may be established even in the absence of property to manage, and shall be organized according to the rules set out in Chapter X.


6.5 Child support

Even after separation, one parent may be required to pay a certain amount to the other. This is called the allowance for child maintenance and education, or "contribution à l'entretien et à l'éducation de l'enfant," also known as "pension alimentaire."


This is particularly the case when:

  • The children primarily reside with one parent, i.e., their "résidence habituelle"

  • One of the parents has a lower financial situation.

How to obtain it

  • The "contribution à l'entretien et à l'éducation de l'enfant," also known as "pension alimentaire," is determined when establishing child custody arrangements or "modalités d'exercice de l'autorité parentale."

Amount of financial support

  • The amount of "contribution à l'entretien et à l'éducation de l'enfant" largely depends on the financial situation of each parent. It is determined based on:

    • Your income and expenses; the other parent's income and expenses; the needs of the children.

  • The amount may differ significantly from what you might receive in your home country. To estimate the amount of financial support you may receive, you can run a simulation (https://www.service-public.fr/simulateur/calcul/pension-alimentaire) .

  • The "contribution to the child's maintenance and education" is usually paid until the child becomes financially independent, even after they reach the legal age of adulthood (which is typically 18 in France), unless a judge rules otherwise.

Payment of financial support

  • The "pension alimentaire" can take several forms of payment:

    • The amount can be reviewed at any time if the parents' circumstances change. In such cases, an application can be submitted to the "Juge aux affaires familiales" (Family Affairs Judge) through your lawyer.

    • In most cases, it is a total monthly payment.

    • In certain situations, other forms may be used, such as direct payment of the child's expenses.

  • The "Juge aux affaires familiales" usually specifies in the judgment the date from which the financial support payments begin, along with the child custody arrangements, known as "modalités d'exercice de l'autorité parentale" (methods of exercising parental authority).

  • Since March 2022, "pension alimentaire" payments ordered by a judge are automatically handled by the national agency responsible for child support, "l'Agence de recouvrement et d'intermédiation des pensions alimentaires (Aripa)", unless both parents explicitly do not wish this. The process is as follows:

    • The judge sends their decision directly to "Aripa".

    • The agency collects the amount each month from the parent paying the maintenance allowance ("débiteur") and then transfers the funds to the parent receiving the maintenance allowance ("créancier").

If financial support is not paid

  • If your former partner fails to pay the required maintenance allowance, the following remedies are available:

    • In the case of divorce, the judge may require one spouse to provide financial support to the other during the divorce proceedings, in the form of a maintenance allowance, or "pension alimentaire au titre du devoir de secours" (https://www.womenforwomenfrance.org/zh/nos-ressources/separation-divorce-garde-denfants/separation-et-divorce/receiving-financial-contributions-from-your-spouse-during-divorce-proceedings) .

    • If the parents are separated, the judge may require one parent to pay a certain amount to the other, namely "contribution à l'entretien et à l'éducation des enfants" (https://www.womenforwomenfrance.org/zh/our-resources/separation-divorce-child-custody/child-custody/receiving-a-financial-contribution-from-the-other-parent-to-contribute-to-child-related-expenses) maintenance and education expenses, also known as the maintenance allowance or "pension alimentaire".

  • If the payment of the maintenance allowance is handled by "Aripa":

    • If a payment is missed, "Aripa" will initiate recovery proceedings against the "débiteur" starting from the first month of default.

    • While awaiting recovery of the arrears, "Aripa" will pay you the financial support in the form of a "allocation de soutien familial" (family support allowance).

6.6 Spousal support allowance

  • Article 270 of the French Civil Code: Divorce ends the duty of support between spouses.

    • One spouse may be required to pay the other an allowance to compensate as fairly as possible for the disparity in living conditions caused by the breakdown of the marriage. This benefit is a one-time payment, taking the form of a capital sum determined by the judge.

    • However, if fairness so requires, the judge may refuse to grant this benefit, either by considering the criteria set out in Article 271, or by taking into account the specific circumstances of the breakdown, declaring the divorce entirely unfavorable to the spouse requesting the benefit.

  • Article 271 of the French Civil Code: The compensatory allowance is determined based on the needs of the spouse receiving it and the resources of the other spouse, taking into account the situation at the time of divorce and its foreseeable evolution in the near future.

    • To this end, the judge shall particularly consider:

      • The duration of the marriage

      • The age and health of both spouses

      • Their professional qualifications and circumstances

      • The impact of one spouse's professional choices during the marriage on child-rearing, the time still required for it, or choices made to favor the other spouse's career at the expense of their own

      • The estimated or foreseeable assets of both spouses after the settlement of the marital property regime, including assets and income

      • Their existing and foreseeable rights

      • Their respective situations regarding pensions, and, as far as possible, an estimate of the reduction in pension rights the spouse receiving the compensatory allowance may suffer due to the circumstances described in paragraph six.

  • Article 274 of the French Civil Code: The judge shall decide the method of execution of the capital compensatory allowance in one of the following forms.

    (1) Payment of a lump sum, provided the divorce meets the guarantee conditions set out in Article 277

    (2) Temporary or lifelong use, attribution of the residence or usufruct, with mandatory transfer to the creditor upon the judgment's effectiveness. However, for property acquired by the debtor spouse through inheritance or gift, the debtor spouse's consent is required for the transfer of ownership.

 NO.4 

[France Chapter]How do domestic violence and divorce affect your visa?


Individuals who married a French citizen and came to France or obtained French residency due to the marriage may retain their residency rights if they cease living with their spouse due to domestic violence or polygamous status.

  • Two conditions must be met:

    1. You came to France or obtained French residency through a civil marriage with a French national, i.e., as a "conjoint de Français".

    2. After arriving in France, you experienced domestic violence or discovered you were in a polygamous relationship.

  • When applying, you must provide evidence of domestic violence or polygamous status. The local French administrative authority, the "préfecture", responsible for reviewing residence permit or "titre de séjour" applications, will assess the evidence and decide whether to approve or reject your application.


  • Legal basis:

    Code of Entry and Residence of Foreigners and of the Right of Asylum, see https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/texte_lc/LEGITEXT000006070158?init=true&page=1&query=CESEDA&searchField=ALL&tab_selection=all
