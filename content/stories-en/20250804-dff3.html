Under the shadow of gender-based violence, we continue to raise our voices,

not to shout loudly or perform for show,

but to ensure that women who have lost their voices can finally be heard.


This year marks the fifth anniversary of AWDPI (Asian Women Development Plan International). To commemorate this milestone, we have curated a reading and viewing list that explores issues of gender relations, gender systems, and sexual violence. These works either reveal the roots of gender-based violence, document survivors' courage in resistance, demonstrate the power of women's awakening, or offer incisive analyses of structural inequality.


Together, these classic works from both Eastern and Western contexts form a spiritual path that helps us understand, reflect upon, and attempt to challenge existing systems. Let us begin with these profoundly moving works, hear the silence, see the cries, and find a way forward.



This issue recommends 4 books: The Vegetarian, The Color Purple, The Institution of Marriage, Walking Toward Spring: A Female Lawyer's Case Notes


and 4 film and television works: Prima Facie, She Said: Moments in Women's Lives, Black Box Diary, The Survivors: Inside the US Gymnastics Scandal


Recommended Books

BOOK RECOMMENDATION


1

The Vegetarian


Silent resistance within an East Asian cultural context,

a woman's "self-destructive" awakening in a suffocating environment.



Author: Han Kang

Translator: Hu Jiaotong

Year of publication: 2007

Douban link: https://book.douban.com/subject/35534519/


Recommended by: Ayuan

Difficulty level: ★★☆☆☆

Recommendation




The author vividly and directly depicts verbal and physical violence against women in East Asian families, as well as a form of resistance women may adopt under such cultural pressures: nonviolent escape. The deep empathy and mutual support among women stand in sharp contrast to the indifference shown by society and men toward male-perpetrated violence.


 Recommended by: Jade

Difficulty level:★★★☆☆

Recommendation




The Vegetarian tells the story of a woman who, amid a life filled with endurance, discipline, and obedience, suddenly discovers her true self—though this self is not accepted by society. Yeong-hye is not merely a vegetarian; she becomes "the vegetative" itself. Becoming "vegetative" is her brave and passionate choice. Is death the worst fate? No—never having truly lived is. Yeong-hye envies wild grass by the roadside, yet she must also become a mother bird sheltering her young. She longs to become a tree in the mountains, surviving solely on photosynthesis, silently rooting deep into the earth and stretching her branches upward. In this sense, Yeong-hye achieves self-realization. But the truth is cruel—as perhaps free will itself may never truly exist.  


2

The Color Purple


Women stripped of subjectivity gradually become independent individuals who can love, feel anger, and dare to resist.



Author: Alice Walker

Translator: Tao Jie

Year of publication: 1982

Douban link: https://book.douban.com/subject/36181310/


Recommended by: Yuni Bobo

Difficulty level:: ★★★☆☆

Recommendation




Exceptionally profound and powerful writing, filled with sincere and tender emotion. Rather than simply describing Celie's awakening, it is more accurate to call it her rebirth as an independent subject. When she first realizes her equal existence alongside others, when she first sees the power of resistance, when she is first drawn to Shug's charisma—she becomes genuine, passionate, unafraid to love and hate. Discussions of grand themes such as gender relations, patriarchy, female same-sex bonds, racial discrimination, and religious belief are woven into a series of letters, simple yet deeply moving. Even more valuable is the author's portrayal of men's transformation under patriarchy, making the dismantling of patriarchy not merely a struggle for women alone. I am deeply grateful that the author gave Celie and Nettie such a warm and touching ending—it brought me to tears. It has been a long time since a book moved me to cry.


3

The Institution of Marriage


How does the traditional marriage system provide fertile ground for the operation of gender-based violence?

Understanding the logic deeply embedded within social structures

is a crucial step toward resisting violence and seeking change.



Author: Fei Xiaotong

Year of publication: Originally compiled in 1946, reissued in 2022

Douban link: https://book.douban.com/subject/30258045/


Recommended by: Dudu

Difficulty level: ★★★★★

Recommendation




This work reveals the marriage system as a structural root of systemic gender-based violence: its established principle of "patrilineal succession" objectifies women as reproductive tools and carriers of labor, the theory of "bilateral parenting" masks women's subordinate status within the family, and the function of "family alliance" institutionalizes customs such as bride price and patrilocal residence, stripping women of autonomy. The book clarifies how traditional marriage, through a triad of violent logics—economic dependence, bodily control, and cultural discipline—provides legitimacy for domestic violence, reproductive oppression, and sexual control, exposing gender-based violence as an institutional cost inevitably generated to maintain "social succession." This analytical framework offers a key theoretical tool for deconstructing the power structures behind violence.


4

" Walking Toward Spring: A Female Lawyer's Case Notes "


Only when theoretical critique translates into concrete action and warm support can we help individuals escape isolation and move toward "springtime blossoms."



Author: Li Ying

Year of publication: 2023

Douban link: https://book.douban.com/subject/36423782/


Recommended by: ZY

Difficulty level:★★☆☆☆

Recommendation




Written from the perspective of a professional lawyer with over two decades of experience in protecting women's and children's rights and providing legal aid to domestic violence survivors, this book uses real cases to illustrate existing legal challenges in safeguarding women's and children's rights. Drawing from personal experiences, unforgettable cases, and reflections on legal practice, the author documents her thoughts on women's struggles and the current state of women's rights. She founded an assistance center, hoping to create a respectful and supportive community atmosphere that helps women rebuild confidence in life. Through storytelling, the book guides readers facing hardship to form new, solid emotional connections, helping them realize they are not isolated islands. As the author says, "Nearly 20 years have passed in a flash—this has been the most fulfilling period of my life. The years of struggle are ultimately reflected in a series of numbers, appearing simple and concrete, but only I know the hardship, complexity, depth, and lasting impact behind them! These are their stories, and mine as well."


Recommended Films and Documentaries

MOIVE RECOMMENDATION

1

Prima Facie (film)


The film sharply questions legal rules built upon male perspectives and experiences, delivering such powerful emotional impact that it forces viewers to reflect: how can the law more fairly "see" and "listen" to survivors of sexual violence?



Director: Justin Martin

Year of release: 2022

Douban link: https://movie.douban.com/subject/35861791/


Recommended by: uu

Difficulty level:★★★☆☆

Recommendation




This nearly two-hour solo performance showcases the protagonist Tessa's re-examination of the fairness and effectiveness of the legal system after experiencing sexual violence. The film offers a meaningful critique of the male-dominated legal system. Under male-centered social norms, many legal provisions related to sexual violence intentionally or unintentionally "overlook" women. Perhaps Tessa's case never receives a clear, satisfying resolution—but the significance of her outcry lies in inspiring every viewer to keep shouting, until the blind spots in gender perspectives are filled with new strokes of our own making.


2

She Said: Moments in Women's Lives (TV series)

In a concise episodic format,

it demonstrates how investigative journalism can break collective silence,

revealing the astonishing power of collective voice.



Directors: Vanessa Caswill, Lachlan Sharpe, Vicki Featherstone

Writers: E.V. Crowe, Rachel De-lahay, Tanika Gupta, Ife Ajenifuja, Trish Cooke, Charlene James, Vicky Jones, Abi Morgan

Year of release: 2018

Douban link: https://movie.douban.com/subject/30244576/


Recommended by: Dudu

Difficulty level: ★★★☆☆

Recommendation




An excellent case study on the power of investigative journalism, breaking systemic silence, and the strength of collective voice.


3

Black Box Diary (documentary)


Director Ito Shiori fearlessly shares her own experience, using documentary film to reveal the judicial challenges and social pressures she endured, forcibly opening the "black box," exposing invisible violence and injustice to public view.



Director: Ito Shiori

Writer: Ito Shiori

Lead: Ito Shiori

Year of release: 2024

Douban link: https://movie.douban.com/subject/36675987/



Recommended by: Jade

Difficulty level: ★★☆☆☆

Recommendation




Documentation becomes a weapon for seeking justice. The so-called "imperfect victim" chooses no longer to remain invisible—she struggles to break free from constraints, forcing everyone to see what she has endured.


4

The Survivors: Inside the US Gymnastics Scandal (documentary)


It not only traces a sports scandal that shocked the world,

but also deeply documents how survivors rise from trauma,

reclaim their voices, and embrace love and life once again.


Directors: Bonni Cohen, Jon Shenk

Year of release: 2020

Douban link: https://movie.douban.com/subject/34994827/



Recommended by: Xue Jialu

Difficulty level: ★☆☆☆☆

Recommendation




The most unforgettable moment: "We are not victims, but survivors. We have voice now." Sexual assault may temporarily rob survivors of their ability to love, but many of them struggle to recover and reclaim their lives.


From literary fiction to academic theory, from legal practice to documentary film, this curated list reflects the complexity of gender issues and the diversity of resistance. These works record wounds, but also document awakening, solidarity, and unyielding resistance.

 

Five years is both a milestone in AWDPI's development and a new starting point for action. We hope these works will not only inspire reflection and insight, but also ignite our passion to continuously pay attention, speak out, and take action, thus advancing a future with greater gender equality.

 

Thank you to every recommender for sharing—let us together continue, through reading and viewing, this journey concerning dignity and justice. of the journey.
