Gender issues must be grounded in reality to reveal possibilities, and every case handled, every project advanced, and every person seeking help supported relies on the hard work and dedication of volunteers. Some choose to join the team so others won't endure the same pain; others take action to live out their beliefs by participating in the organization. Although people join AWDPI for different reasons, they all come with a shared vision of helping others, bringing warmth and care to those in need.

 

Greatness can be seen in the smallest details, and it is often in these subtle moments that we find the most moving acts. They use their efforts and attentiveness to clear away the shadows and frost in the lives of those who need help. They grow alongside the organization, achieving mutual growth, lighting the way forward with the torch in their hearts. Reporting Department: Chen Zihan


Chen Zihan is a senior at Soochow University. Since her freshman year, she has wanted to engage in work related to social public welfare. She joined AWDPI because of the uniqueness of the group it serves. In her view, in the unstable post-pandemic environment, overseas Chinese women are more likely to face difficulties, and being able to help them is a meaningful endeavor.


Zihan grew up in Jiangsu, and previously had little concept of gender issues. It wasn't until her freshman year, when she traveled to the border region between Yunnan and Myanmar, that this rural experience first exposed her to the living conditions of marginalized women. She began to develop a gender perspective and truly started paying attention to women's issues. Gradually, Zihan realized these rigid and invisible structural contradictions actually exist around us, and have even occurred and existed in her own mother's life. Therefore, she wants to examine underlying social problems by continuously exploring the issue of women's protection.


Zihan works in the Reporting Department, where her main tasks include interviewing people seeking help or inviting scholars engaged in gender studies and department heads to share their insights. Participating in these conversations has inspired Zihan greatly and strengthened her confidence in her chosen path. For her, interviewing and compiling the stories of those seeking help is highly challenging, because interview articles must not be leading, and reporters often need to carefully manage the angle.


Zihan chose AWDPI partly because she wants to use her own efforts to help overseas Chinese women and reduce the occurrence of violence; on the other hand, Zihan may live abroad in the future and will herself become an overseas Chinese woman. She thinks that when she encounters difficulties overseas, AWDPI will give her a sense of support, rather than leaving her feeling alone, which fills her with confidence and conviction about her future life.


Zihan believes that a single spark can start a prairie fire. Public welfare should also become part of life, and everyone can contribute their strength to maintain a fair environment.



Reporting Department: Fan Shanma

Fan Shanma, a member of the Reporting Department, is currently a senior at Zhejiang University. She first encountered AWDPI during the summer of her sophomore year and immediately signed up upon seeing the recruitment link.


Shanma first strongly felt the power of words when she read Chai Jing's book *Seeing* in middle school. One chapter featured interviews with women who had been sentenced for unintentional homicide after resisting domestic violence from their husbands—this was also the origin of her aspiration toward journalism.


As she grew older, Shanma's most frequent involvement at AWDPI has been in reporting and editing. She has interviewed and compiled articles together with colleagues from the Collaboration and Expansion Department, managed book review columns, and written educational content on specific topics. Since joining, Shanma considers her greatest achievement to be interviewing outstanding women from different fields, exchanging thoughts with them about women's movements both domestically and internationally, hearing about their real-life actions and experiences, and ultimately turning these into articles that inspire more like-minded friends.


For the future, Shanma hopes that women's protection organizations can continue to grow stronger. At the same time, she looks forward to greater progress in gender equality, a reduction in gender discrimination and violence—she hopes society can become more inclusive, fair, and respectful of every individual. She firmly believes that the passion and courage of volunteers will ultimately bring about change.



Empowerment Support Department: Fang Ziyi

Fang Ziyi is currently studying sociology at the London School of Economics. The term "gender discrimination" is not unfamiliar in her life—relatives at home have long suffered domestic violence but received no support when choosing to divorce; in high school, no matter how excellent the female students were, teachers would only praise the boys… These experiences led Ziyi to join AWDPI, as she wants to take action to reduce injustice in society.


Ziyi is currently a member of the Empowerment Support Department, primarily responsible for managing AWDPI's large online communities. Her daily tasks include regularly posting updates in groups, sharing event information, and maintaining community engagement. At the same time, they also provide resources to women in need, such as applying for government assistance abroad or finding overseas organizations that can offer direct help.


When she first joined the organization, Ziyi was still in university and had little social experience. Facing women with different life circumstances seeking help, she often felt at a loss about how to communicate and respond effectively. Based on respect, she tried to understand them. Through gradually empathizing with these women, Ziyi came to realize the depth of some women's difficulties and their sense of isolation, gaining a deeper understanding of gender issues and society. Ziyi has encountered women who reached out late at night to share their emotions. While she is glad to offer support, over time she also realized that maintaining appropriate boundaries is important for volunteers.


Ziyi believes the first significance of AWDPI is that the very existence of such an organization makes those seeking help feel they are not alone. From a sociological perspective, the second significance of NGOs is that they are important forces driving social change. Organizations represent certain social consensus; when more people get involved, help and influence grow together. By conveying demands, they can truly promote social reform or bring about policy changes.


Ziyi feels that in recent years, the theoretical foundation of women's issues in society has not been critical enough. She hopes the future environment can become more feminist; she especially hopes to expand influence so that more women can have opportunities to voice their demands and participate in decision-making.


Ziyi says that volunteer work at AWDPI is deeply empowering—feminist awareness becomes more profound through practical action, thus better advancing her mission and goals. "Be practical, be careful," "grow through reflection," "know what you want" are the suggestions Ziyi offers to future volunteers, and also her own summary of professional growth.


As an early member, Ziyi has also witnessed the organization's development. Now, she sees more members joining, and the overall structure, rules, and regulations becoming more standardized, comprehensive, and professional. She is genuinely delighted.



Psychological Support Department: Qiu Siyu

Qiu Siyu is one of the heads of the Psychological Support Department and currently works in psychological counseling, family education, and family therapy. In her junior year, she hoped to learn more about psychology and also wanted to use her knowledge to help more people. Because AWDPI has a psychological support department, Siyu chose to join.

 

At AWDPI, Siyu is responsible for external liaison work for the Psychological Support Department, mainly negotiating collaborations with external organizations and recruiting new members and counselors. At the same time, she also provides support for internal department activities, such as planning events. In addition, the external liaison team has another important task: matching those seeking help with psychological counselors. From building the initial structure to its later growth and development, including feedback from case handling, consulting teachers are needed to provide professional advice—on this, Siyu has offered AWDPI tremendous help.

 

Siyu finds every frontline work experience extremely valuable. A case she encountered while studying in the UK left a deep impression. The person seeking help was in France, and right after pandemic restrictions were lifted, her husband claimed it was unsafe outside and forbade her from leaving the house—a particularly terrifying situation. If the husband cannot find a job and the woman overseas lacks sufficient economic ability, she may have to follow him in constant wandering. From many such cases, Siyu has realized that societal support for overseas Chinese women is indeed limited and difficult to reach everyone, making the support provided by AWDPI indispensable.

 

During this time, Siyu has felt the potential and diversity brought by women's initiatives. And indeed, some recipients have said that the existence of such an organization gives them hope. Siyu feels the organization is growing with increasing strength and professionalism, which holds great significance for society.

 

Siyu hopes that in the future, more people in need will discover AWDPI, and that the organization can offer even more services and support that people desire. As a volunteer, she also hopes everyone is willing to become a beam of light, illuminating the path ahead for more people, and also willing to become their own light—walking side by side and passing strength forward.



U.S. National Office: Zheng Hao


After graduation, Zheng Hao worked as a product manager in New York. She believes that women's rights are still in an unequal state, and women continue to be objectified by society as a whole. She once thought gender equality had already been achieved, but reality often falls short—especially when she witnessed her parents' troubled relationship and her father's long-term domestic violence. That's when she decided she wanted to create change. As a woman herself, Zheng Hao hopes the status and treatment of women in her generation and beyond will improve. Eager to take concrete action, she applied to join AWDPI.

 

Applying her expertise and resources in the AI field, Zheng Hao participated in the production of a video displayed on screens in New York's Times Square, contributing to scriptwriting and solving technical issues. She also took part in outreach efforts for a public education lecture on domestic violence inspired by the Google murder case, helping spread AWDPI's mission and perspectives. When she saw the video she helped create displayed in Times Square, Zheng Hao felt a strong sense of accomplishment and deep meaning in her work. Additionally, through AWDPI, she has met a group of wonderful friends who support each other as they move forward.

 

In Zheng Hao's view, AWDPI first provides psychological comfort to those seeking help, letting them know there is an organization that supports and stands with them. At the same time, the very existence of the organization emphasizes and advances the conversation around gender equality.

 

Zheng Hao believes that one key point for women's protection organizations to understand is that only when women gain real power of speech can many things be changed. The most fundamental goal is to help women gain more power and financial resources, enabling them to recognize the importance of self-independence. Only when more women achieve better social status can the actual rights and interests of women as a whole be elevated.

 

Zheng Hao hopes that every volunteer joining women's protection organizations will stay true to their original intentions, remembering why they came, and valuing every small step and every bit of progress they contribute.



Dong Zhi, Rescue and Support Department

During the pandemic, the isolated and helpless situations of some female friends studying and working overseas around her made Dong Zhi (Bonnie) deeply aware of the need for such related support. After learning about AWDPI, which is dedicated to protecting overseas Chinese women, from founder Yimar by chance, Dong Zhi decisively signed up and joined the organization.

 

Currently, Dong Zhi manages case work at AWDPI and also serves as a review advisor for the writing and editing team. Through interactions with clients who have experienced various hardships, Dong Zhi has been deeply inspired by their shining resilience, determination, and optimism. One case particularly stood out: a client she worked with eventually chose to join the volunteer team, passing strength on to other women in similar situations.

 

Through her experiences with friends and clients, Dong Zhi has come to see that lack of social support is a common challenge faced by Chinese women overseas. Due to language barriers, resources such as psychological, legal, and medical services that may be easily accessible to locals often remain distant and out of reach for them. Therefore, she believes the social value of AWDPI and other women's protection organizations is immeasurable—they not only provide essential support and protection to women in need, but also help raise awareness of women's rights. Through the efforts of every volunteer, more women can receive practical assistance and emotional support.

 

To those who want to join AWDPI and other women's protection organizations, Dong Zhi says, do not underestimate your own power—every individual's contribution can bring change. She hopes more people will join this cause and work together to advance women's rights and safety.



Guo Siwei, Psychological Support Department



Guo Siwei, a student in the Counseling Psychology program at the University of Edinburgh, has had opportunities in her studies to work with many domestic violence survivors. This has given her a deep understanding that, beyond necessary legal and emergency aid, rebuilding psychological stability is equally crucial for survivors. By chance, Guo Siwei saw AWDPI's recruitment post in a senior schoolmate's social media feed. Around the same time, she was deeply affected by news reports about overseas Chinese women suffering domestic violence during the pandemic with no way to seek help. Motivated by this, she decided to join the organization, hoping to contribute her professional knowledge.

 

Currently, Guo Siwei serves as the academic team leader in the Psychological Support Department, primarily responsible for organizing academic resources, designing and producing psychological courses, and advancing internal academic exchanges. She previously served in the service team, providing emotional support and temporary assistance to help clients manage stress and trauma. During that time, she remotely provided psychological support to a female survivor. Through this process, she truly felt how her support helped the woman rediscover inner strength and rebuild order in her life.

 

In Guo Siwei's view, AWDPI's assistance goes beyond direct support and resources—it also challenges and transforms societal gender inequality and gender-based violence through advocacy and education. AWDPI does not aim to "grant" power to women, but rather helps them discover their own inner strength and provides environmental support to nurture it. Guo Siwei also hopes that in the future, women's protection organizations like AWDPI can further expand their influence, reaching more regions and groups. She hopes they can spread the message of gender equality and women's rights through more channels, promoting broader social awareness.

 

Guo Siwei believes that for volunteers involved in women's protection work, the ability to remain sensitive and compassionate is just as essential as having strong professional skills. She also believes that volunteers who join will surely meet like-minded friends in AWDPI, growing and supporting each other together.



Huang Wei, Rescue and Support Department

Huang Wei majored in German at Nanjing University for her undergraduate studies and later pursued graduate studies in Frankfurt, Germany. She is now about to begin working in the internet industry. During her undergraduate exchange in Germany, she came across Avoice's recruitment announcement online. Given her location and capabilities, Huang Wei felt she could contribute to rescue efforts. With this in mind, she applied to join the organization.


In the early days of joining, Huang Wei participated in various tasks, including building rescue networks and working with community teams. Later, she became a member of the German team in the Language Support Department. In 2021, Huang Wei officially became the head of the Language Support Department. The work of AWDPI's Language Support Department goes beyond direct language assistance, such as translating professional documents. It also includes cross-lingual outreach efforts—translating and sharing daily articles or videos—to help as many people as possible understand the nature and serious harm of domestic violence, and to inform them how to seek help if they experience it. In addition, Huang Wei and her team members sometimes handle urgent tasks, such as helping female survivors make emergency calls to the police.


For Huang Wei, AWDPI has shown her how women's protection organizations continuously expand and improve their rescue efforts. From basic help, protection, and support, to assisting women in achieving greater economic independence, AWDPI is striving to transform rescue work from isolated interventions into a comprehensive, multi-faceted support system—from immediate relief to long-term development.

Li Qi, Human Resources Department

Note: Interviewee Li Qi prefers to be referred to with the pronoun "ta." The following text uses "ta."


Li Qi's connection with AWDPI began last year. During a gap period, Li Qi encountered many feminist ideas but felt regretful about lacking a channel to help improve the situation. Later, ta saw a senior schoolmate share AWDPI's recruitment post on social media. Holding onto the belief of "becoming part of a collective force," Li Qi decided to join AWDPI.

 

Currently, Li Qi mainly serves as a recruitment officer and also reviews content for AWDPI's official WeChat public account. For Li Qi, working at AWDPI has been a "free exploration." Freed from the constraints of societal hierarchies and evaluations, Li Qi has completed countless challenging and passionate tasks over the past year at AWDPI, such as optimizing the entire volunteer recruitment system.

 

"Social phenomena produce social outcomes; when this force matures, even small steps forward may drive larger-scale shifts in mindset." Li Qi wrote in the application. For ta, every effort made as a volunteer at AWDPI is like a beam of light—though small, it is already enough for those who see it. It is the down-to-earth, cumulative efforts of volunteers that form the continuously flowing river of AWDPI.

 

Ta hopes that in the future, the organization can receive more support, secure more collaborations, and expand the scope and scale of its activities; stay true to its original mission, and make more impactful contributions toward advancing gender equality! To those who want to join but are still hesitating, Li Qi says, please let go of utopian fantasies, and boldly take even the smallest action. To the current team members, Li Qi wants to say, even if you choose to leave in the future as you enter different life stages, please remember this unforgettable experience and remember that you once fought for such an ideal—may your ideals always remain alive.



Reporting Department: Tang Xinran

Tang Xinran majored in psychology during her undergraduate studies. After obtaining a master's degree in law, she chose to pursue a second master's degree in psychological counseling. During her senior year, deeply moved by the portrayal of domestic violence survivors' "silent screams" in the program *News Investigation*, she developed a desire to help women affected by domestic violence. After seeing AWDPI's recruitment post on WeChat, Tang Xinran decided to join the organization.

 

Currently, as a member of the Reporting Department, Tang Xinran is primarily responsible for conducting interviews and writing professional articles. The department follows a rigorous writing and review process—only after going through stages such as topic selection, drafting, and review does an article have the chance to be published. This journey, filled with both fun and challenges, has brought Tang Xinran not only a sense of achievement but also skill development. Learning how to write content suitable for the platform and how to avoid the trap of "writing for self-satisfaction" have been her greatest takeaways.

 

Limited by insufficient systemic knowledge, Tang Xinran in the past often felt "sorrow for their misfortune and anger at their lack of resistance" toward women around her experiencing domestic violence. However, after gaining more knowledge, the complex dilemmas faced by abused women became more fully visible to Tang Xinran: they may develop learned helplessness, be trapped by enormous sunk costs, or feel lost and uncertain about the future. In Tang Xinran's view, this is precisely the significance of women's protection organizations like AWDPI: to promote a deeper understanding of feminism. As a volunteer in the Reporting Department, Tang Xinran hopes these fact-based, investigative articles can help more people, including volunteers, move beyond assumptions and serve as a "bridge" across the "void" between government agencies, nonprofit organizations, and vulnerable groups, helping women in need connect with governments, NGOs, courts, lawyers, and all possible sources of support.

 

As a member of the Reporting Department, she hopes these articles can reach more readers, We also look forward to more friends joining AWDPI's writing and reporting team! And as a woman, Tang Xinran hopes women can move beyond empty slogans, focus on taking real action, strive to improve themselves, and reach higher places.


Stay sharp, keep watching, continue caring, and help more women transcend the "social fate" imposed upon them before they even realize their identity as women—this is a narrative co-written by AWDPI's members. On this path that may have no end but only continuity, they are joining hands with fellow travelers, taking bold strides forward.
