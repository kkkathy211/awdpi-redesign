White Male Google Employee Sues Asian Female Executive, Sparking Online Debate

(1) Incident Summary

On January 30, U.S. website Business Insider reported a highly controversial case of alleged workplace sexual harassment and racial discrimination. In this case, Ryan O'Rourke, a white male employee who had worked at Google for 15 years, accused his Asian female supervisor, Tiffany Miller, of sexually harassing him in 2019 and, after he rejected her advances, retaliating against him professionally by abusing her position. Although the details of the case remain unclear, the "identity politics" involved have sparked widespread debate. Due to long-standing discrimination against women, ethnic minorities, and other marginalized groups in Western societies, these groups have historically emphasized their identity labels to unite and advocate for fair treatment—a concept scholars term "identity politics." However, in recent years, an increasing number of people have observed that these once-marginalized groups, having gained more attention and preferential treatment, are in fact no longer "marginalized," while white men—often assumed to be the "dominant group"—frequently face guilt-by-association in public discourse and find themselves isolated and defenseless. For example, O'Rourke claimed Google "favors ethnic minorities, women, and sexual minorities," and stated, "If the genders were reversed, my sexual harassment claim would be taken more seriously"—a reality that objectively exists in American society. As a result, media outlets with different audiences reported the story with contrasting tones: The Washington Post and Business Insider, which target higher-income, more "liberal" audiences, gave more space and prominence to perspectives favorable to Tiffany Miller. In contrast, outlets like the New York Post, which cater to lower-income, more conservative white male readers, used language more sympathetic to O'Rourke. Thus, Differing psychological needs stemming from various positions lead to biased discourse, making justice difficult to seek amid emotionally charged prejudiced arguments. (Information sourced from Global Times report)



(2) Commentary Perspective

1. Beware of Identity Politics Leading to Labeling and Discrimination Against Women

From comments supporting O'Rourke, it is evident that a segment of society resents "identity politics." Favoring ethnic minorities, women, and sexual minorities does not truly achieve gender equality for women; instead, it traps women in another form of disadvantage—labeled and shamed. Only by genuinely recognizing women's value, believing in their capabilities, and providing equal opportunities for advancement and self-fulfillment for both men and women, can we truly respect women. In the news, Google admitted that if the genders were reversed, this case would have received different levels of attention. Without clear facts, such handling not only damages Tiffany's personal reputation but also deepens discrimination against women, reinforcing the stereotype of women as "weak." What women need is equality, not condescending pity or charity.


2. Beware of Asian Fetish

Asian fetish is distorted affection, rooted in racial discrimination, posing risks and humiliation for Asian women and undermining personal dignity. Some white men are not interested in forming rational, equal relationships with Asian women, but instead indulge in a deformed fetishization of Asian women. This stems from racist stereotypes that portray Asian women as "submissive, obedient, easy." Asian women are often socialized to be kind, gentle, cute, and considerate. These white men may appear to admire Asian culture, but in reality, they impose their imagined notions onto Asian women to satisfy their own sense of superiority. The report mentions that O'Rourke emphasized the appeal of Tiffany's Asian identity. Without knowing the full facts, this statement alone seems to suggest a hint of the perverted sexual interest associated with Asian fetish.


3. Public Opinion Cannot Reveal Truth—Only Rigorous and Fair Investigation Procedures Can

The news sparked heated online debate. On the comment section of the UK's Daily Mail, when one netizen suggested, "Perhaps the truth is completely the opposite," others immediately attacked him: "How shameful! Why do you assume a man's words are not credible?" Some argued, "Only a fool would think men aren't the primary perpetrators of sexual harassment," while neutral voices claimed, "If society weren't so hypocritical, people would understand that both men and women, whites and minorities, can be perpetrators." The viral public discourse has placed both Tiffany and O'Rourke at the center of intense scrutiny—facing overwhelming online support or violent attacks. While public discussion is meaningful, the fundamental principle must be grounded in facts. A fair and rigorous investigation procedure should be initiated promptly to reveal the truth.

Chinese Business Owner Robbed at Knife Point, Nearly Slit Throat


According to a February 9 report by Qiaobao News, a Chinese-owned mobile phone repair shop located under the Williamsburg Bridge in New York's Lower East Side was robbed on the afternoon of February 2, with the young Chinese shop owner nearly having his throat slit.


It is reported that four masked men entered the store one after another, pretending to browse products. Two of them suddenly rushed behind the counter and pulled out knives, threatening the owner to hand over money. One suspect pressed a knife to the owner's throat, then took his wallet, keys, and phone. A third suspect used a hammer to smash a glass display case and stole several phones inside.


In an interview, owner Mr. Li said he lost over $12,000. Despite the trauma, he said he must continue running the shop to make a living.


On February 8, police released images of three suspects, appearing to be two Hispanic and one African American men. The suspects remain at large, and police are urging the public to come forward with information.

Chinese-American Girl Sues U.S. Adoptive Parents Alleging Forced Labor and Racism


According to NBC News on February 3, a 19-year-old Chinese-American girl filed lawsuits in New Hampshire against her American adoptive parents, local police and government, and the U.S. adoption agency.


Olivia Atkocaitis, 19, alleged in the lawsuit filed on Monday that her parents, Denise and Thomas Atkocaitis, prevented her from attending public school and kept her confined in the basement for long periods. The lawsuit also states they forced her to perform intense physical labor, physically assaulted her, and subjected her to racial discrimination and other forms of abuse for nearly 14 years.


In addition, the lawsuit claims that the girl attempted to escape multiple times throughout her childhood, but each time was reprimanded by local police and returned to the dangerous home. Therefore, in addition to accusing her adoptive parents of racism, the lawsuit also alleges racial discrimination by local and state authorities.


As of now, the state is reviewing the submitted documents.

Seattle King County Man Charged with Hate Crime for Stabbing Stranger


According to the Seattle Times on February 10, a 29-year-old man, Jimmie Patrick, and his 40-year-old girlfriend, Crystal Gibson, have been charged with first-degree assault and hate crime.

They are accused of stabbing a passerby in Seattle's University District. Jimmie Patrick reportedly identified himself as a skinhead and made anti-Asian remarks.


The victim told officers that he was waiting in a van parked in an alley near Northeast 45th Street and University Way Northeast for a friend when two suspects entered the alley. At the time, Gibson rammed a handcart into the van, while Patrick angrily demanded the man move the vehicle so they could pass. His language was filled with profanity, insults, and anti-Asian sentiment.


Since the van's keys were in the possession of the man's friend, he had no way to start or move the vehicle. Later, Patrick threw stones at him from a construction site, and the man threw stones back. Subsequently, Patrick grabbed the man by the neck, while Gibson seized him around the waist, pinning his arms to his sides. Patrick stabbed the man in the chest with a folding knife, and Gibson held the man down while Patrick fled the scene.


The victim was immediately taken to a local hospital and has since been discharged. Both suspects were arrested on the same day.

Chinese siblings violently robbed on their way home


According to a February 20 repost by the WeChat public account "Huaren Xunxi Wang," a violent robbery targeting Chinese compatriots recently occurred in the city of Florence, Italy. On the evening of the incident at around 10 p.m., four masked men ambushed a pair of Chinese siblings on their way home from work, blocking their path and carrying out a violent robbery, during which one of the assailants was armed with a knife.


During the struggle, both siblings sustained injuries of varying severity, with multiple wounds across their bodies and swollen eyes. The assailants stole 1,800 euros in cash, a Rolex watch, and a backpack from the two before fleeing the scene by car. The injured Chinese individuals were taken to the hospital for treatment.


Police arrived at the scene and conducted an investigation. Preliminary analysis suggests this may have been a premeditated crime. The assailants had reportedly conducted surveillance and followed the two Chinese individuals before setting up an ambush on their usual route home.

Two Chinese individuals in Spain kidnapped at gunpoint by compatriots


According to a February 10 repost by the WeChat public account "Huaren Zixun Chuanmei," a shocking armed kidnapping and ransom case occurred in Barcelona, Spain. Two Chinese individuals were kidnapped by three other Chinese nationals and held for a ransom of 370,000 euros. Fortunately, thanks to timely police intervention, the two victims were rescued unharmed.


Police received a report stating that two Chinese individuals (one man and one woman) had been kidnapped. After verifying the credibility of the call, law enforcement quickly rushed to the scene. At around 5:10 p.m. that day, Catalonia's regional police force, Mossos d'Esquadra, suddenly surrounded a building at Amigó Street 39 in the Sarrià-Sant Gervasi district of Barcelona. A large number of officers swarmed the area, sealing off the entire street and prohibiting anyone from entering or leaving.


Around 8 p.m., police began negotiations with the three Chinese kidnappers inside the residence. At the last moment, police launched an assault, breaking down the door under the cover of two smoke grenades, arresting the three Chinese kidnappers, and successfully rescuing the two Chinese hostages.

Campaign against racism and discriminatory bias in digital platforms and services


Ahead of World Day of Social Justice and UNESCO's World Conference on "The Internet for Trust," Racismo MX and UNESCO Mexico launched a campaign titled "Revoluciona ElAlgorimo" on February 20 in Mexico City, calling for changes to algorithms. The campaign began with the screening of a documentary video titled "Revolution and Algorithms" at the Digital Culture Center in Mexico City.


The documentary "Revolution and Algorithms" shows how different groups and individuals with diverse identities continue to be stigmatized by the ways they are represented in spaces and platforms used daily—such as internet search engines—hindering the elimination of bias and inequality.


The campaign was launched amid growing public concern over recent advances in artificial intelligence (AI), including machine learning and deep learning, sparking discussions about employment prospects, copyright, and impacts on criminal behavior. However, critical issues remain unresolved, such as misinformation, racism, discrimination, and hate speech in everyday technology use, communication platforms, and simplified AI applications, requiring interdisciplinary and cross-sectoral collaboration.

Afghan women call on international community to support education for girls and women


"Today, Afghan girls who want to exercise their fundamental human right to education have two options. If she is lucky, she goes abroad or underground. She becomes a refugee, or effectively a criminal within Afghanistan. We are talking about a 12-year-old child."


These are the powerful words of Shabana Rasikh, founder of the Afghan Leadership Academy, one of three Afghan women who spoke at a United Nations event in New York commemorating this year's International Day of Education. UNESCO has dedicated this day to Afghan girls and women deprived of their right to education and has consistently called for the immediate lifting of bans restricting their access to schooling.


Ms. Rasikh's institution—the Afghan Leadership Academy—was a boarding school for girls operating in Kabul from 2006 to 2021. It was forced to relocate its entire school community to Rwanda, where it currently operates. "The reality is," said Ms. Rasikh, "a woman without access to education is a woman without agency. She is a woman without economic or personal independence. A woman who cannot see her own hand, completely dependent on others to meet her basic needs."


Since September 2021, the return of all Afghan girls over the age of 12 to school has been indefinitely postponed, leaving 1.1 million girls and young women without access to formal education. In December 2022, women's access to university education was suspended indefinitely, affecting over 100,000 female students enrolled in public and private higher education institutions.


Ms. Rasikh called on the international community to invest in ensuring high-quality education for Afghan women refugees in their host countries.

Views of Black Americans on gender equality and gender roles in society and family


According to data released in February this year by the U.S. polling organization Pew Research Center, Black Americans largely support gender equality and are critical of the lack of progress in American society. About seven in ten Black adults, regardless of their religious affiliation, say opposing gender discrimination or discrimination against women is essential to their faith or moral beliefs (71%). Many Black Americans view their support for gender equality as a core part of their religious or moral values.


A very high proportion of Black Americans believe that in families where both parents are present, parenting responsibilities should be shared equally by both parents (86%). This view is held across most demographic subgroups of Black adults, with almost no differences between them. While a majority of Black adults believe men and women should equally share financial responsibilities in the household, their religious congregations tend to emphasize this role more for men than for women.


These survey findings come from two Pew Research Center surveys of U.S. adults conducted in 2019 and 2020.

Lenovo ranks on 2023 Bloomberg Gender-Equality Index for fourth consecutive year


Lenovo has been included in the 2023 Bloomberg Gender-Equality Index (GEI) for the fourth consecutive year. The Bloomberg Gender-Equality Index framework tracks progress toward women's empowerment and equality in the workplace across 484 companies in 11 industries, 45 countries, and regions worldwide.


Among the five key pillars used to evaluate the companies measured by the index, Lenovo scored particularly high in building an inclusive culture, anti-sexual harassment policies, and leadership and talent pipelines.

Calvin Crosslin, Vice President of Human Resources at Lenovo Group, Chief Diversity Officer at Lenovo Group, and Chair of the Lenovo Foundation, said: "Lenovo Group has been on a journey toward inclusion, building a culture where everyone belongs—while it's an honor to be recognized again on the Bloomberg Gender-Equality Index, our work is not done."


Lenovo Group has over 82,000 employees worldwide and operates in more than 180 markets, with women making up 36% of its workforce. The company is working toward its commitment to increase the proportion of women in its executive leadership team from 21% in 2020 to 27% by 2025.

Reopened Manchester Museum gives voice to South Asian diaspora


After 18 months of closure and a £15 million redevelopment, the Manchester Museum reopened to the public on Saturday, February 18.


The two-storey extension houses a set of new exhibition spaces, including the Lee Kui Hung Chinese Culture Gallery and the South Asia Gallery, developed in collaboration with the British Museum. According to museum director Esme Ward: "For a long time, museums have told rather one-dimensional stories, whether from the perspective of collectors—typically white and male—or from the institution itself." "But now it's time to consider many other perspectives." Everyday objects such as ceramics, clothing, and CDs are displayed alongside sculptures and taxidermied animals. The stories span from South Asia and China to the British diaspora; visitors can learn about forgotten women of the Mughal Empire through ancient coins and jewellery, as well as through British Asian sound systems in 21st-century London.


The South Asia Gallery presents global history through a regional lens. Photo: Christopher Thomond/The Guardian

Singaporean athlete represents Singapore at the Hangzhou Asian Games for the second time


Lucas Ng, a Singaporean short track speed skater, was the first Singaporean to compete in the Asian Winter Games in 2011. As a member of Singapore's roller skating team, he is set to participate in the Asian Games to be held in Hangzhou from September 23 to October 8 this year.


The 34-year-old, who has over a decade of professional experience and won one gold and two silver skating medals across two Southeast Asian Games appearances, will join the Singapore roller skating team for the Asian Games in Hangzhou from September 23 to October 8. The four-member team will compete in the men's 3000-meter relay.——This marks the first time Singapore has sent roller skating representatives to the Asian Games. Ng was the first Singaporean to participate in the 2011 Asian Winter Games, competing in the men's 500m and 1000m short track speed skating events.


Ng said, "I hope our qualification for the Asian Games will be a good beginning, and people will see the potential for growth in this sport."
