Column Introduction:


In September, the alarm bell rang once again on gender equality: 33% of female university students in England experienced campus sexual harassment, a woman's suspected murder in Argentina may have been livestreamed, and Disney settled a gender pay discrimination case for $43.25 million—fairness and safety remain core demands for women.


Meanwhile, incidents targeting Asians have continued: a London-based Asian women's football team was subjected to racist and anti-Chinese abuse during training, "Kill Asians" graffiti appeared near Japantown in San Francisco, and an 86-year-old Korean American man in Los Angeles died after being attacked by vicious dogs, all of which have continuously impacted the sense of security within Asian communities.


Yet hope is also growing—on September 16, Hmong-American city councilor Mai Vang announced her candidacy for the U.S. House of Representatives, bringing new momentum to Asian representation and women's political participation.

Gender Equality


News


UK universities report

one-third of female students experience sexual harassment during their studies,

nearly one-fifth suffer sexual assault or sexual violence


On September 25, 2025, the UK higher education regulator OfS released a survey of over 50,000 final-year undergraduate students: 33% of female students reported experiencing sexual harassment during their time at university (24% overall; women nearly three times more likely than men, 33% vs. 12%); 19% of female students reported experiencing sexual assault or sexual violence (7% of male students). Nearly 40% of harassment incidents occurred off-campus but were often linked to university-affiliated individuals; only 13% of respondents reported the incident to their institution in the past year, and among those who did, only 47% rated the response as "good," while 39% rated it "poor."


Survey data shows: younger students, LGB individuals, and students with disabilities face higher risks; students aged 31 or older are more likely to report perpetrators as faculty or staff. Unions and regulators have labeled this a "national scandal," urging universities to immediately implement institution-wide accountability and governance reforms.

News


Argentina: suspected "live-streamed" murder of women sparks nationwide protests, placing gender-based violence governance on the agenda


In Buenos Aires, Argentina, protests erupted on September 28, as thousands took to the streets, protesting the torture and murder of two 20-year-old women and a 15-year-old girl, allegedly livestreamed; the three were abducted on September 19 and later found buried in a residential yard. Police say the perpetrators were a drug-related gang and have arrested several suspects (including a fifth accomplice), while the suspected ringleader remains at large. On-site banners directly referenced "narco-femicide," and victims' families cried out, "Women must be better protected."


Reports indicate the alleged livestream occurred on a private account visible to only 45 people, though the platform denied it took place on its site. The protest has placed extreme violence against women on the public agenda, demanding immediate government action on prevention, law enforcement, victim support, platform collaboration, and evidence preservation mechanisms.


Source:

Regencia, Ted. "Thousands Protest Livestreamed Murder of 2 Women, Young Girl in Argentina." Al Jazeera , 28 Sept. 2025, www.aljazeera.com/news/2025/9/28/thousands-protest-livestreamed-murder-of-2-women-young-girl-in-argentina.



News


Disney gender pay discrimination case:

court finally approves $43.25 million settlement,

and mandates three years of external pay equity audits


On September 16, 2025, the Los Angeles Superior Court in California, USA, issued final approval in Rasmussen et al. v. The Walt Disney Company: Disney will pay $43.25 million to settle a class-action lawsuit over gender-based pay discrimination. Plaintiffs alleged the company systematically underpaid female employees, overlooked them for promotions, and assigned unpaid overtime; Disney denied any wrongdoing. In addition to monetary compensation, the settlement requires Disney to hire an external labor economist to conduct pay equity analyses for certain positions over the next three years and implement "prospective relief" through improved job classification and benchmarking.


The case received preliminary approval in May; amid tightening pay transparency regulations, it is seen as a compliance model for large employers, with its non-monetary provisions expected to benefit current and future employees.


Source:

Christ, Ginger. "Court Approves $43M Settlement in Disney Gender Pay Discrimination Case." HR Dive , 16 Sept. 2025, www.hrdive.com/news/court-approves-43m-settlement-in-disney-gender-pay-discrimination-case/760245.

Anti-Asian Violence


News


East Asian women's grassroots football team

subjected to sustained verbal abuse and threats during a park match,

police file case as hate crime


During a scheduled training session at Barnard Park in Islington, London, the Chinese women's team Screamer Alliance WFC was verbally abused with anti-Chinese slurs for about an hour by around twenty teenagers, who also threw footballs at them and forcibly occupied their reserved field. After local media reported the incident on September 5, the Metropolitan Police recorded it as a hate crime, and Islington Council increased security for future training sessions.


Team founder Ricky Lin said, "I don't want players to feel they must flee," expressing concern that trauma could undermine participation confidence. Players Ouyang sisters noted that as the only women on the field and visibly "non-local," they were repeatedly verbally attacked and disrupted. The report also mentioned that the team had previously been forced to relocate twice due to similar harassment (from Rosemary Gardens in De Beauvoir and Castlehaven in Camden Town), and another women's and non-binary team, Riot FC, also stopped training after being abused in the same park.

News


Rottweiler attack results in death of 86-year-old Korean American elder


On the morning of September 6, 2025, in Norwalk, Los Angeles County, California, an 86-year-old Korean American man was suddenly attacked by two Rottweilers that had escaped their owner's supervision while he was walking near 166th Street and Greystone Avenue. He suffered severe bites, including arterial bleeding in his left arm. Bystanders intervened and administered first aid before he was rushed to hospital for emergency surgery, but he died on September 7. The Los Angeles County Sheriff's Department reported the incident occurred around 6:45 a.m.; investigator Steve De Jong confirmed the victim was a Korean American man but withheld his name due to the ongoing investigation. The dog owner claimed not to know how the dogs escaped. Both Rottweilers were subsequently isolated by the Los Angeles County Animal Care and Control (LACACC).


While this case was not a human-to-human hate attack, it sounded an alarm for community public safety and the protection of elderly Asian pedestrians, highlighting the need for local authorities to strengthen preventive governance in dangerous dog management, leash enforcement, and age-friendly walking environments.

News


"Kill Asians" graffiti appears near Japantown, triggering community shock


On September 6, 2025, near the sidewalk adjacent to Raymond Kimbell Playground Park at the intersection of Fillmore and Japantown (Japantown) in San Francisco, USA, black spray-painted messages containing anti-Asian slurs and explicitly stating "Chinese should die" were discovered, shocking passersby who reported it to media. City park patrol staff later removed the graffiti, but the San Francisco Police Department initially had no direct report linked to it. Interviewee Katie Lee said, "Japantown is right here, and I'm Asian too—seeing these words is deeply upsetting." Some residents also recalled hearing threats referencing WWII's "Pearl Harbor" in Japantown recently.


The report also notes, although hate crimes against Asians in San Francisco have declined since the 2021 peak, public safety anxieties persist, especially among the elderly. While this incident involved only verbal and graffiti-based hate expression, its public nature and implication of lethal violence have amplified unease within East Asian communities, underscoring the need for cities to implement faster graffiti removal and evidence collection procedures, routine patrols, accessible reporting channels, and community collaboration and anti-hate education to curb "normalized xenophobic discourse."


Source:

Park, Brooke. "Racist Graffiti near S.F.'s Japantown Calls for Death of Asian People." San Francisco Chronicle, 5 Sept. 2025, https://www.sfchronicle.com/bayarea/article/racist-graffiti-japantown-21033619.php. Accessed 5 Oct. 2025.



News


Taiwanese woman attacked at night by four teenage girls after getting off a vehicle,

suspected hate motive, ROC representative office in UK intervenes


On the evening of September 20, 2025, on Collier Street in Islington, London, a 25-year-old Taiwanese woman (surnamed Chen) was surrounded and attacked by four teenage girls after exiting a vehicle. On September 24, the Metropolitan Police (MET) stated they are investigating it as a suspected racially motivated assault and urged witnesses to call 101.


Police did not respond immediately; they contacted the victim about three hours later and scheduled a statement (originally set for the morning of September 25). Personnel from the Taipei Representative Office in the UK have provided assistance; as of the report, no arrests have been made. The case highlights the exposure risks faced by East Asian women at night during public transit and street transfer points, and reflects the need for improved response timeliness in evidence collection and victim support for hate-related cases.

Hmong-American councilor Mai Vang announces run for U.S. Congress, centering women's and immigrant issues


In Sacramento, USA, on September 16, Hmong-American female city councilor Mai Vang announced the launch of her campaign, challenging Doris Matsui, the incumbent U.S. Congresswoman who has held office for nearly two decades, for California's 7th Congressional District.


Vang's campaign is grounded in "grassroots mobilization and refusal of corporate PAC funding," emphasizing issues affecting immigrants and working-class families, highlighting the possibility of Asian American women advancing from local governance experience into federal politics; this move is also seen as a direct challenge to patriarchal political norms long dominated by established power networks, enhancing the visibility of women in public leadership and their ability to shape policy agendas.

News


National Book Award longlist announced:

Korean American author Susan Choi,

Chinese-language writer Yiyun Li among finalists, East Asian presence stands out


On September 12, the National Book Foundation announced the longlists (10 titles per category) for its five award categories. The most anticipated fiction entries include Angela Flournoy's *The Wilderness* and Korean American author Susan Choi's *Flashlight*.


The top five finalists will be announced on October 7, and the awards ceremony will take place in Manhattan on November 19, where honorary awards will be presented to George Saunders and Roxane Gay. This year, East Asian representation stands out: Yiyun Li is shortlisted for nonfiction, Han Kang for translated literature, and Chinese American poet Esther Lin is nominated in the poetry category.

Emmys sidelines: AAPI spotlight:

Bowen Yang has become the

most nominated Asian male actor in history, calling for "breaking categories."


Comedian Bowen Yang received his fourth Primetime Emmy nomination for his performance on Saturday Night Live (SNL), making him the most nominated Asian male performer in Emmy history.


At the "One House Toast to the Emmys" event hosted by Gold House, he said he was the only AAPI nominee in any performance category this year, a fact that left him feeling "complex"; last year, Shōgun made history with Anna Sawai and Hiroyuki Sanada winning Best Actress and Best Actor, yet the 2025 acting nominations marked the worst diversity in the past five years. Bing Chen, co-founder of Gold House, reminded the audience: "When one person wins, it opens doors for many more." Bowen Yang also expressed gratitude for Gold House's long-term support and offered the message "We should not be easily categorized (defy categorization)"— a moment that is both a milestone and a reminder that the industry must do more to improve representation in nominations and awards.
