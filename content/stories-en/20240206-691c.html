PUA, a "game for the brave"



1. Assessment Test for Manipulative Relationships


If you are currently in a manipulative relationship, try assessing the severity of the manipulation. Freedom can not only improve the quality of your relationship but also help you end it. Try to envision the possibilities that freedom could bring, which will offer you a fresh perspective and help you decide whether to stay in this relationship, attempt to change it, or bring it to a definitive end.

If you answered "Yes" to one or more questions in the risk assessment, you may be in danger within this relationship, please seek help from a lawyer or the police to protect your safety.


In general, the difference between emotional manipulation and healthy relationships can be summarized in three points: encouragement, fear, and punishment.


2. Continue or End—The Moment of Decision


No matter what decision you make, try to think independently:

1

What worries you most if you stay in the relationship?

2

What worries you most if you end the relationship?

3

What can you do, and what can you change, to achieve the outcome you desire?


Ending a relationship is extremely painful, but over time, the pain will gradually fade. You don't have to remain in a relationship that restricts you or requires constant caution. If you've already tried to save the relationship without success, it might be time to end it and walk away.


The manipulator may repeatedly undermine your self-esteem, making you believe that no one else will ever love you, but someone who truly loves you will try to support your growth, not drag you down.

A. Continuing the Current Relationship 

The following suggestions can help you learn to stand your ground and live freely and safely within the current relationship:


  • Maintain connections with others outside the intimate relationship

  • Participate in social activities

  • Seek help from professionals

  • Clarify your financial situation

  • Develop habits that get you out of the house regularly

  • Back up important contacts and documents

  • Make a list of your goals

  • Make a list of your boundaries—your boundaries are yours to define

  • Regularly assess your progress toward your goals

  • Try to find legal counsel regarding finances and child custody

  • Encourage the manipulator to participate in violence intervention programs

  • Encourage the manipulator to seek medication or psychological therapy


Even if the manipulator genuinely intends to change and stop manipulative behaviors, they might simply shift strategies and continue manipulating you in other ways, remember that changing a person is extremely difficult—don't expect the manipulator to change easily.














B. Ending the Current Relationship 

What support can you seek:

  • Anti-domestic violence organizations

  • Therapy or counseling (If your counselor cannot help you, find one who is a better fit)

  • Medical assistance

  • Seek help from the police or a lawyer


What preparations do you need to make:

  • Protect your property

  • Protect your children

After the manipulator leaves, women may feel they will never recover. At such times, seeking help from others is crucial. May you have the strength to envision the freedom you could gain, may you live in a safe place, and may you have companions by your side during this difficult journey.















3. Healing the Wounds


You have your own pace and the right to your own feelings. Some things can never be forgiven, but believe this: even without forgiveness, full healing is possible.


Find healing methods that work for you:

  • Re-engage in things the manipulator forbade you from doing but that you wanted to do

  • Rebuild relationships with family and friends the manipulator tried to separate you from

  • Spend time with people who support you

  • Organize your finances

  • Seek psychological counseling and therapy

  • Participate in social activities

  • Exercise regularly

  • Focus on yourself

    — How am I feeling right now? What do I want to do now?

  • Completely cut off contact with the manipulator


4. Helping Others


If someone around you is experiencing a similar situation of manipulation, you can try to offer help:


  • Let her know you care about her

  • Ask her if she needs help

  • Don't tell her what to do

  • Listen patiently and allow her to express her feelings


After violence occurs, many abusers are arrested but quickly released, returning to the victim with even greater anger—the consequences are unimaginable. When necessary, apply to the police for a protection order and seek official protection.


Finally, confronting a manipulator is a "game for the brave," but that doesn't mean you have to be the one who loses—believe in yourself, stay strong, seek support and help promptly, stop easily trusting manipulators whose nature is unlikely to change, fully immerse yourself in this game, and play your best hand, win the match—you can definitely do it.


In this "game" of PUA, you will be the ultimate winner and completely end this cruel game.
