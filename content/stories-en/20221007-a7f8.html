Scotland begins implementing

the free provision of menstrual hygiene products for all women

Image source: unsplash


On August 15, Scotland, UK began enforcing a new law requiring all educational and public institutions to provide free menstrual hygiene products to women. This makes Scotland the first region in the world to pass legislation ensuring "period freedom" for women. Monica Lennon, a Scottish Labour Party MP who championed the bill, said its implementation aims to prevent women from being unable to afford essential hygiene products due to poverty. Amid rising costs of living, the enforcement of this regulation is "a ray of hope."

Iranian woman dies in custody after being arrested for violating hijab law

Sparking outrage and protests among Iranian women


Iranian woman Mahsa Amini died in prison after being arrested for allegedly violating the hijab law, triggering mass protest marches across Iran. In recent months, Iranian police have intensified enforcement against women wearing so-called "loose hijabs" or failing to fully cover their bodies, including physical harassment and arrests. Protesters have responded by burning hijabs, demanding authorities stop cracking down on and harassing women who do not comply with hijab requirements, and calling for a thorough investigation into the true cause of Amini's death in custody. Video footage shows female protesters removing and burning their headscarves while chanting slogans such as "Justice, freedom, no to hijab," clashing with police and resulting in the deaths of three protesters.

Bomb package discovered on Boston campus

Detonated near Northeastern University's Center for Research on Gender and Sexuality

A staff member was injured in the arm when opening a package, and police later found a second unexploded device after securing the area. The explosion occurred at Northeastern University's Creative Writing and Women's, Gender, and Sexuality Studies Center. However, current information does not confirm any connection between the bombing and gender-related issues.

UK welcomes its third female prime minister in history

Image source: Liz Truss's Twitter account


On September 5 local time, current Foreign Secretary Liz Truss defeated former Chancellor Rishi Sunak to become the new leader of the UK's ruling Conservative Party. Truss's election as the new Prime Minister sets a powerful example for women's advancement in politics. She becomes the third female prime minister in UK history and the youngest ever. Truss previously served as Secretary of State for Justice and Lord Chancellor in 2016, becoming the UK's first female Lord Chancellor. In 2021, she became the UK's second female Foreign Secretary, earning praise within the Conservative Party for her role in securing trade agreements with multiple countries post-Brexit while serving as International Trade Secretary.

News


Married Asian couple shot dead by deputy sheriff


According to reports, on September 7 local time, Devin Williams Jr., a deputy with the Alameda County Sheriff's Office in Dublin, California, stormed into a house in the area and shot dead a married Asian couple—57-year-old Benison Tran and his 42-year-old wife Maria Tran, who had immigrated to the United States from Vietnam in 2004. Benison was a retired civil engineer and Maria a nurse. Police arrived and declared them dead at the scene.



At the time of the killings, six people were inside the house, including a child and a relative from out of town. No one else in the house was injured. A law enforcement source said the incident appeared to be an execution-style killing. Devin Williams Jr.'s motive may be linked to a personal relationship dispute, but the exact motive remains under investigation.


After the crime, Devin Williams Jr. fled to the city of Corning, approximately 160 miles (258 km) south of the crime scene, where he called Dublin police to surrender.

Home invasion robbery results in death of Chinese citizen


According to a notice posted on September 7 by the Chinese Embassy in Zimbabwe, on the evening of September 1 local time, a Chinese citizen residing in the Bulawayo mining area was violently attacked and robbed by several intruders at home. After the assault, the victim felt physically unharmed and did not seek medical attention. However, on the morning of September 3, he was found unconscious at home and died on September 4 despite emergency treatment.


This violent criminal case targeting a Chinese citizen has drawn high attention from the Chinese Embassy in Zimbabwe. The embassy has already raised the issue with the Zimbabwean government, demanding local authorities and police conduct a thorough investigation and apprehend the perpetrators as soon as possible.

Campaign signs of multiple Chinese candidates in Auckland

maliciously vandalized


According to a September 6 report by Radio New Zealand (RNZ), campaign signs of multiple Chinese candidates in Auckland have been maliciously damaged.


Paul Young, a councilor running for re-election this year, said his face was cut out or painted over with white paint on more than 20 billboards. This is Young's eighth time participating in local elections. While his signs have been damaged before, he said this year's vandalism is the worst so far. He said, "It's really bad for young East Asians or minority candidates who want to step forward and contribute to our community and Tāmaki Makaurau… This racism is unacceptable."



Robert Hu, a mayoral candidate in Auckland, is one of three East Asian candidates whose faces were painted over with white paint on campaign boards along Somerville Road. Hu has reported the incident to police and the Human Rights Commission, saying, "Are they saying our faces aren't white enough? Are they saying we must be white to qualify for election?"


Human Rights Commissioner Meng Foon described these acts as "despicable" racist behavior, and the Commission is investigating the matter.

University lecturer admits guilt in murder of Asian wife


According to the Daily Mail, Adam Brown, a senior lecturer in digital media at Deakin University in Victoria, Australia, was charged by police with murdering his Asian wife, Chen Cheng, at their home in northern Croydon on the evening of April 30. On Tuesday, September 13, 2022, Adam Brown admitted to the murder charge and pleaded guilty in court.


Neighbors of Chen Cheng said she attempted to escape from her husband but failed. One neighbor said: "She was screaming 'Help! Help! Help!' and then her voice just completely stopped." With help from another neighbor, the two approached the house to investigate. Paramedics and police later arrived and found Chen Cheng's bloodied body behind the front door, where she was pronounced dead at the scene. Forensic experts and detectives removed bags of blood-stained evidence from the two-story townhouse.



The specific motive for Brown's crime remains unknown. Chen was regarded by neighbors as a "lovely neighbor" who went out of her way to help others; Brown was a senior lecturer in media and communication with a PhD. The couple married in China in 2017 and had two young children. Chen's death shocked Australia, with people spontaneously placing flowers outside her home on Patrick Avenue to honor her memory.

Asians become targets of hate crimes



According to the New York Daily News on September 6, a Queens man has been charged with a hate crime, assault, and aggravated harassment for unprovoked attacks on the head of an Asian man. Police said that on July 12, 41-year-old Rudy Coslopez randomly targeted a 22-year-old Asian victim on Steinway St. near 34th Avenue in Astoria. Before the attack, Coslopez hurled vicious anti-Asian slurs at the man and knocked out his teeth.


According to the latest data from the New York Police Department, 427 people have become victims of hate crimes so far this year. This represents an 18.9% increase compared to the same period last year, when only 359 hate crimes were recorded.

Caught in the Crossfire: The Fears of Chinese-American Scientists


On September 23, the "Asian American Scholar Forum" released a nationwide survey report. According to the report, since the launch of the China Initiative under the Trump administration in 2018, Chinese-American scientists have become more inclined to leave the United States and less motivated to apply for federal grants due to potential pressure from federal investigations. By analyzing institutional affiliation data from over 2.3 million scientific papers, the report's authors found that the number of Chinese-origin scientists returning to China from the U.S. has been steadily increasing. Additionally, they conducted a survey of Chinese-American scientists holding tenured positions at U.S. universities (n=1,300), which revealed that widespread fear and anxiety have led them to consider leaving the U.S. and/or ceasing to apply for federal grants.

South Asian Women Unite

Opposing Leicester's "Senseless Violence"


According to Devdiscourse on September 25, following a cricket match between India and Pakistan, a group of South Asian women leaders in Leicester gathered to push back against what they described as "senseless violence" by Hindu and Muslim men on their city streets. Local media reported that they gathered outside the city hall in central Leicester, where Indian-origin councillor Rita Patel read a joint statement. "As Asian women from Leicester, we call on the people of this city to stand united against the senseless violence that has torn our communities apart over the past week. We condemn those who carry out hate and violence in our city—you will not succeed in dividing us," the statement read.


In response to the recent violence, police have arrested 47 people and are gradually filing charges. Some of the worst clashes over the weekend were described by local police as "serious disorder," with social media videos showing groups of men throwing glass bottles and religious symbols being attacked.


The Indian High Commission in London strongly condemned the violence against the Indian community, and Foreign Minister S. Jaishankar raised security concerns during a meeting with UK Foreign Secretary James Cleverly at the United Nations General Assembly in New York.

Tucker Carlson: Fox News Host Says

The British Brought Civilization to India


According to BBC on September 14, an American television host sparked outrage among Indians by suggesting that the British civilized India. Tucker Carlson of Fox News claimed that India had not produced any architectural wonders after British rule ended. The host made the remarks during a program about the death of Queen Elizabeth II. His comments were criticized by politicians, commentators, and social media users as racist and ignorant. The remarks, coming shortly after the Queen's death, reignited a sensitive debate about imperial colonial history.


In recent days, prominent writers and scholars from around the world, including India, have criticized the British monarchy, saying it has yet to acknowledge the humiliations and brutality of the empire.


Carlson attempted to rebut these criticisms on last week's show, claiming the British Empire was "not just about racial slaughter." "Strong nations dominate weak ones. That trend hasn't changed," he said in a tweet that later went viral on Twitter. The host added that, unlike the United States, the British "took their colonial responsibilities seriously" and ruled the world with a decency "unmatched by any empire in human history." He said: "When the British left India, they left behind an entire civilization—language, legal systems, schools, churches, and public buildings—all of which are still in use today."

Squid Game actor Lee Jung-jae

Makes History at the Emmys


Photo source: Associated Press


According to the Daily Star on September 13, Netflix's global hit series "Squid Game" made history at the Emmys, winning a total of six awards. In the process, Lee Jung-jae became the first Asian actor to win the Outstanding Lead Actor in a Drama Series award.


Lee Jung-jae is the fourth Asian actor to win an Emmy. He was nominated alongside popular actors such as Bob Odenkirk (Better Call Saul), Jason Bateman (Ozark), Adam Scott (Severance), Brian Cox, and Jeremy Strong (Succession). Upon accepting the award, Lee chose to speak in both English and Korean. "Thank you to the Television Academy, thank you to Netflix, thank you to the director, thank you for creatively bringing the real issues we all face onto the screen with such a powerful script. Thank you, Squid Game team. Thank you to everyone watching in Korea," the actor said in an interview.


"Squid Game" director Hwang Dong-hyuk also took home the award for Outstanding Directing for a Drama Series. The director became emotional after receiving the award. "People keep telling me I've made history, but I don't think I've made history—because you opened the door for Squid Game, inviting us here to the Emmys tonight. I have to say, we've all made history together. I truly hope Squid Game won't be the last non-English series to come to the Emmys, and I hope this won't be my last Emmy. I will be back with Season 2," Hwang said in his speech.

Bristol's New Festival

Creating a "Safe Space" for Asians

Photo source: BBC NEWS


According to BBC on September 7, a new arts and cultural festival has been created with the hope of providing a "safe space" for East and Southeast Asian (ESEA) communities. The "Moon Festival" in Bristol will showcase a range of creative works by East Asian-origin artists across the city. The one-day event will feature a lineup of musicians, visual artists, and dancers, along with food and crafts.


"I hope this will bring more Asian representation to the event scene in Bristol," said festival founder Monica Wat. Ms. Wat was inspired to launch the festival after first arriving in Bristol in September 2021. Now that she has made many friends and built connections with creative and community organizations across the city, she hopes to give others the same opportunity. Serena Appleby, owner of Kring Kringz, will be running a food stall at the event. "I'm proud to be part of this festival, especially as a Filipino mixed-race person raised in the UK," said Ms. Appleby. "I've never been surrounded by so many other Asians before, and it feels good to be accepted, to know there are others like me, with similar stories, feelings, and experiences."
