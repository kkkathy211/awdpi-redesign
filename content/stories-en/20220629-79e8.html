What is Roe v. Wade?

What aspects of abortion rights did the U.S. Supreme Court actually overturn?


On June 24, 2022, the U.S. Supreme Court officially announced, by a 6-3 vote, the overturning of American women's constitutional right to abortion, a right that had been protected for nearly half a century.


Roe v. Wade was a landmark ruling that legalized abortion. Since 1973, it had guaranteed, at the national legal level, pregnant women's right to legal abortion to the greatest extent possible.


The overturning of Roe v. Wade means that U.S. states now have independent legislative power to determine laws regarding women's abortion rights. Women's legal right to abortion is no longer protected by the Constitution or federal law.

What does the overturning of the abortion ruling mean?

What consequences will it bring? 


1) The legality of abortion and related laws will be decided at the state level.


Thirteen U.S. states have already passed "trigger laws," meaning that once Roe v. Wade was overturned, these states would automatically enact abortion bans. According to data from the Guttmacher Institute, a research organization studying reproductive rights, an estimated 26 states will pass laws further restricting women's abortion rights.


These states include Arkansas, Idaho, Kentucky, Louisiana, Mississippi, Missouri, North Dakota, Oklahoma, South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas, Utah, and Wyoming. Democratic-led states such as New York, California, and Connecticut are increasing support for women's abortion rights. Connecticut has already passed a law protecting abortion service providers. In the future, states dominated by the Democratic Party are expected to become sanctuaries for women seeking abortions, leading to a significant influx of such women. Of course, this will further deepen the political divide in the United States.


2) Restricting abortion does not stop people from seeking it—it only makes abortion more dangerous.


According to analysis by The New York Times, due to the overturning of Roe v. Wade, approximately half of American women of reproductive age will lose access to affordable, low-risk abortion procedures. In the states imposing these strict abortion restrictions, harsh penalties will be imposed on any institution, individual, or even drivers who assist women in obtaining abortions. In Texas, even before Roe v. Wade was overturned, a law rewarding the reporting of abortion assistance had already been passed. The environment for women to access legal and safe abortion will only worsen in the future.




3) Women in vulnerable situations (domestic violence, abuse, sexual violence, etc.) will face even greater threats to their personal safety.


Women who become pregnant due to sexual violence:

After the overturning of Roe v. Wade, in many conservative states, even if women become pregnant due to domestic violence, rape, or sexual assault, abortion will be strictly prohibited once a certain gestational limit is exceeded. Even in some states where abortion is permitted for pregnancies resulting from sexual assault, medical resources for safe abortion are extremely scarce.


More than half of the states are reducing abortion-related medical resources. For example, Missouri is closing clinics that provide safe abortion services on a large scale, and Mississippi has restricted abortion rights only to women who have experienced rape. Some states do not even allow sexual violence survivors to choose abortion. This will increase the likelihood that women in vulnerable situations resort to illegal methods with higher mortality rates (such as taking drugs, unconventional methods, hooks, etc.) to terminate pregnancies. For example, some women use coat hangers as makeshift clamps and forceps, inserting them into the uterus to self-induce abortion, causing severe physical harm or even death…


Women experiencing domestic violence:

For women who suffer from contraceptive coercion (a form of marital rape) and become pregnant as a result, this poses an even greater threat to their safety. Pregnancies resulting from domestic violence (marital sexual violence) may not qualify for abortion permission as readily as those resulting from rape or incest.


Although most conservative states allow abortion for women who have been raped, proving the existence of marital rape itself is extremely difficult. Women experiencing domestic violence endure immense physical and psychological trauma during forced pregnancies, making them more likely to resort to the dangerous abortion methods mentioned above in order to terminate their pregnancies.

4) Minority women, especially those facing economic hardship and limited social resources, will face greater survival challenges.


Facing intersectional discrimination:

According to the theory of intersectional discrimination developed by feminist scholars (such as Deborah King, Patricia Hill Collins, Kimberlé Crenshaw, Cherrie Moraga, Gloria Anzaldúa, and Nira Yuval-Davis) in the 1970s–1980s, minority women face dual discrimination based on both race and gender, and therefore must inevitably confront greater challenges when abortion rights are stripped away.


For minority women who are already economically disadvantaged, being forced to carry pregnancies to term and raise children will require them to pay even higher costs (such as sharp income reductions, deteriorating physical and mental health, etc.), further deepening their economic, physical, and psychological hardships.


Bearing higher abortion costs:

According to projections by the Guttmacher Institute, a research organization studying reproductive rights, after the overturning of Roe v. Wade, women living in the aforementioned states (and future states likely to impose stricter restrictions) will face greater challenges related to abortion. To obtain abortions, they will have to travel longer distances, face increasingly unsafe conditions, and bear significantly higher costs.


Even in states with relatively large minority populations like New York and California, the nationwide sudden shortage of abortion resources will affect the legal abortion rights of women of all ethnicities, including Asian women.



3

Why is it being overturned now?


The stark contrast between "the majority of Americans supporting broad legalization of abortion rights" and "the Supreme Court overturning abortion rights" highlights a sharp divide. The long-planned anti-abortion movement by conservatives stands in strong opposition to public opinion. According to surveys by U.S. polling think tanks Pew Research Center and Gallup, over half of the public opposes overturning abortion rights. On this issue, American public opinion is deeply divided: anti-abortion supporters (Pro-Life) are mainly concentrated in conservative states with strong Republican bases; abortion rights supporters (Pro-Choice) are primarily in liberal states with strong Democratic support.


1) From a legal perspective, the 1973 Roe v. Wade ruling, based on the right to privacy, had a relatively weak foundation.


The original legal argument was primarily derived from the "Due Process Clause" of the Fourteenth Amendment, which inferred a "right to privacy," and through this right to privacy "created" the right to abortion, rather than directly guaranteeing women's abortion rights from a broader perspective of women's rights, thus laying the groundwork for its eventual overturning. The late Justice Ruth Bader Ginsburg had previously commented on this vulnerability.


2) Various forces have long been preparing to overturn Roe v. Wade, and women's abortion rights have become a political bargaining tool.


This overturning was not sudden. Since the 1980s, various groups (religious figures, conservatives, etc.) have attempted, based on their own interests and beliefs, to restrict women's abortion rights. What began as a case ensuring women's access to safe abortion has evolved into a tool for political parties to seek identity, draw boundaries, and mobilize voters. For example, Reagan's rise to power in the 1980s relied heavily on strong opposition to abortion rights to attract conservative voters. The three conservative Supreme Court justices appointed during the Trump era also played a key role in this overturning (Trump's legacy). It was precisely Trump's appointments that brought the number of conservative justices on the current Supreme Court to six, laying a crucial foundation for this decision.

Why is protecting women's reproductive rights so important?


1) Violating abortion rights is also a violation of women's reproductive rights, autonomy, and right to survival.


Abortion rights are a crucial component of reproductive rights. Violating abortion rights is equivalent to violating women's control over their own bodies. Restricting abortion access squeezes women's basic living space and autonomy.


UN Secretary-General spokesperson Stéphane Dujarric stated that the United Nations has repeatedly reaffirmed that reproductive rights are an essential part of women's rights, a principle of human rights protection, and are reflected to varying degrees in the laws of most countries.


UN High Commissioner for Human Rights Michelle Bachelet pointed out that the principle of access to safe, legal, and effective abortion services is deeply rooted in international human rights law and is a core safeguard enabling women to exercise autonomy over their bodies and lives without discrimination, violence, and coercion. In the past 25 years, more than 50 countries that previously had restrictive laws have liberalized their abortion legislation. However, the U.S. Supreme Court's ruling is causing the United States to deviate from this progressive trend.


The United Nations Population Fund, in its "State of World Population 2022" report, pointed out that nearly half of all pregnancies worldwide are unintended, and more than 60% of these may end in abortion. But shockingly, globally, 45% of abortions are unsafe and constitute a major cause of maternal mortality. If abortion is subjected to further restrictions, unsafe abortions will increase around the world, reversing progress already made in abortion rights and having wide-ranging negative impacts on women's rights and choices.


2) Safeguarding women's right to abortion is crucial for socioeconomic development and stability in public health.


Ensuring the legality and safety of abortion significantly reduced crime rates in the United States during the 1990s. According to research conducted in 2001 and 2021 by University of Chicago professor Steven Levitt and Stanford Law School professor John Donohue on the impact of legal abortion rights on crime rates, one of the main reasons for the decline in U.S. crime rates over the past two decades has been the widespread legality of abortion. After the 1970s, the number of unwanted children sharply decreased, thereby reducing the population born into adverse environments and lacking parenting support who might otherwise turn to criminal paths.


Restrictions on women's abortion rights will further deepen the wealth gap. The journals Science and Nature, based on previously published research, have issued warnings about the future: in a society that restricts women's abortion rights and reproductive rights, women's productivity and their contributions to economic development will be greatly diminished. According to the well-known Turnaway Study, most women unable to access abortion services are forced to carry pregnancies to term, and the majority do not relinquish child-rearing responsibilities—this plunges already vulnerable women and their families into deeper economic hardship.


Widespread restrictions on abortion rights will exacerbate the mental health crisis among the American public. The president of the American Psychological Association stated that this ruling completely ignored previous scientific research on the relationship between restricting abortion rights and citizens' mental health, and such actions will increase the prevalence of anxiety and depression among the public.

What can we do?


The overturning of Roe v. Wade has triggered strong condemnation from American civil society and the international community. Currently, major U.S. corporations including Apple, Microsoft, Tesla, Meta (formerly Facebook), and Disney have all introduced measures to reimburse employees for abortion-related expenses. As individuals, what can we do?


1) Learn and promote accurate knowledge about contraception


Some unintended pregnancies can be avoided through proper contraceptive methods (e.g., condoms, subdermal implants, hormonal IUDs, contraceptive injections, contraceptive patches, vaginal rings, regular/emergency contraceptives, male sterilization, etc.). In many states where abortion access has been severely restricted, we can learn about and promote accurate contraceptive knowledge and methods to reduce unintended pregnancies at the source.


Planned Parenthood It is the largest organization in the United States dedicated to promoting correct contraception; you can access the most authoritative contraceptive knowledge and resources by visiting their website.


2) Learn about channels that provide abortion support, share them with those in need, and widely promote them.


According to Guttmacher Institute a 2020 study, over half of abortions in the United States are performed through medication rather than surgery, and last year the FDA fully lifted restrictions on mailing abortion pills.


Meanwhile, many organizations are working in this area—we can share this information with more people:


Resources for managing unintended pregnancy:

  • Planned Parenthood: Provides access to abortion medication. After the FDA removed restrictions on online purchase of early abortion pills in 2021, Planned Parenthood launched corresponding services at the same time.

  • INeedAnA.com: Allows you to search for the nearest abortion clinic; if clinic assistance is needed, you can also check estimated wait times and other requirements.

  • Miscarriage and Abortion Hotline : Offers counseling services via text message and phone calls during miscarriage or abortion.


Legal aid for women's reproductive rights:

  • If/When/How: Lawyering for Reproductive Justice Provides legal aid and legal defense funds to assist individuals entangled in the criminal justice system due to pregnancy.

  • Center for Reproductive Rights: Offers legal help and support to protect reproductive rights. Through its project "What if Roe loses?", it outlines the legal challenges people may face and provides solutions to assist those in need.


3) Support local and national reproductive rights protection organizations and donate when possible


Abortion medical support organizations

  • Donate to local abortion clinics (https://keepourclinics.org/) and national organizations supporting women's reproductive rights in the U.S., such as the National Abortion Federation Fund (https://abortionfunds.org/), to help women under strict abortion regulations and ensure the physical and mental safety of women needing abortions, as well as their needs in raising children.

  • Abortion Care Network It is an independent clinic in the United States providing abortion medical support, currently raising donations to keep the clinic operational.


Comprehensive organizations:

  • Planned Parenthood: The largest reproductive rights advocacy and civil rights organization in the United States, operating 50 branches nationwide, providing medical and educational services in reproductive rights, and offering contraception education and services.


Advocacy and education organizations:

  • Guttmacher Institute: A think tank dedicated specifically to research on women's reproductive rights, committed to public education and professional research in this field.

  • We Testify: Focuses on spreading reproductive rights knowledge and education among racial minorities and LGBTQ+ communities.


Support and rescue organizations:

  • the National Latina Institute for Reproductive Justice: A national Latinx reproductive rights protection organization and also the largest minority reproductive rights organization in the United States.

  • Afiya Center : A reproductive health organization preventing at-risk women from HIV infection https://www.theafiyacenter.org/about-us
