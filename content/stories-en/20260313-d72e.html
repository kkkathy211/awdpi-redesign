The Gambia's Supreme Court accepts case:

FGM ban faces legal challenge


In January 2026, The Gambia's Supreme Court officially accepted a case seeking to overturn the ban on female genital mutilation (FGM). Religious leaders and lawmakers bringing the lawsuit argue that the ban infringes on religious and cultural freedoms, while opponents emphasize that FGM is fundamentally a severe form of gender-based violence that causes irreversible harm to women's health. Approximately 70% of women in The Gambia have undergone this traditional practice, many during early childhood. Although the country passed a law banning FGM in 2015, the first conviction under the law did not occur until 2023. This case has reignited international debate over the boundary between cultural tradition and women's rights, and is seen by many women's organizations as a significant signal of global pushback against women's rights.

Taiwan Human Rights Commission releases gender equality assessment report: institutional gaps remain in women's political participation and judicial protection


On January 29, 2026, Taiwan's National Human Rights Commission released an independent assessment report on the implementation of the Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination against Women (CEDAW). The report notes that while progress has been made in gender equality legislation, significant shortcomings remain in political participation, handling of gender-based violence, and work-family balance. Data shows that the proportion of women in Taiwan's cabinet has long failed to reach one-third, and at the local level, numerous constituencies have never elected a female representative. The report also highlights low prosecution rates for sexual assault cases under the current criminal justice system, with approximately 95% of cases failing to enter judicial proceedings due to insufficient evidence. The report argues that increasing women's political representation is not only a matter of rights, but also influences the priorities of public policy and the allocation of social resources.

February

World Bank's 2026 report newly released:

China's score improves steadily


At the end of February, the World Bank officially released the *Women, Business and the Law 2026* report, covering 190 economies and 10 key topics, with data current as of October 1, 2025.


The report reveals a clear imbalance across economies between legal equality, the supporting policies needed to implement laws, and actual enforcement—legal equality remains a long and difficult journey for every woman globally.


From a macroeconomic perspective, the report cites calculations by a joint research team from Yale University and the World Bank: discriminatory laws, weak enforcement, and lack of services that disadvantage women can cost a country 15% to 20% of its economic output. In specific figures, China scored 70 out of 100 on the legal framework index; the average for high-income OECD countries was 87.93, while the Middle East and North Africa region scored the lowest at 43.24. Notably, the East Asia and Pacific region shows the largest internal disparity, with a gap of 73.27 points between the highest- and lowest-scoring economies—far exceeding the 30.23-point gap in Europe and Central Asia—indicating that inequality in the region is not evenly distributed but highly polarized.


Source:

The World Bank. (2026). Women, Buisness and the Law 2026. https://wbl.worldbank.org/en/wbl

January

Study highlights anti-Asian hate incidents,

affecting America's international image in Asia


In January 2026, a study by the London School of Economics found that anti-Asian hate crimes in the United States significantly affect how Asian publics perceive the U.S. The research team conducted survey experiments with over 15,000 people across nine Asian countries, including China, Japan, South Korea, the Philippines, and Singapore. Results showed that when respondents were exposed to information about anti-Asian hate crimes in the U.S., negative views toward the U.S. and Americans increased by an average of 10 to 12 percentage points. Many Asian publics feel an emotional connection to Asian Americans through a sense of "pan-Asian identity," so when violence against Asians is reported internationally, it is often interpreted as hostility toward the entire Asian group. The study also found that while subsequent U.S. legislative responses can somewhat mitigate negative perceptions, they still fail to fully offset the damage to national image caused by hate crime incidents. These findings suggest that racial violence not only harms targeted communities but also damages a nation's image in diplomatic terms. The study calls on governments to adopt clearer policies to address hate crimes and reduce their social and international repercussions.


Source:

Rhee, Inbok, et al. "Anti-Asian Hate Crimes in the US Damage America's Reputation across Asia." LSE USAPP Blog, 19 Jan. 2026, https://blogs.lse.ac.uk/usappblog/2026/01/19/anti-asian-hate-crimes-in-the-us-damage-americas-reputation-across-asia/.

February

Three hate attacks targeting East Asians occur in Toronto within two weeks; two underage suspects arrested


On the evening of February 20, three suspected hate crime attacks occurred in downtown Toronto, Canada, with three East Asian victims unprovokedly kicked from behind, subjected to racial slurs, and struck on the head. After the Toronto Police Hate Crime Unit launched an investigation, a 14-year-old and a 12-year-old female suspect were arrested on February 23 and 25, respectively. The two face a total of five charges, and under Canada's Youth Criminal Justice Act, their identities are not disclosed. Police stated that all three attacks are being treated as "anti-East Asian hate crimes," and expressed concern that more victims may exist.

January

Oscar nominations announced: Asian filmmakers and creators gain attention across multiple categories


In January 2026, the 98th Academy Awards announced its list of nominees, with multiple filmmakers and works of Asian background entering competition for major awards. Among them, Chinese director Chloe Zhao received several key nominations for her film *Hamnet*, while the animated film *K-Pop Demon Hunters*, featuring Korean-origin creators, gained award attention for its original song *Golden*. Additionally, in technical and behind-the-scenes categories, several Asian film professionals received nominations, including Japanese teams in makeup and hairstyling as well as visual effects. The strong Asian presence in the 2026 Oscar nominations is seen by some commentators as a sign of gradual shifts in the global cultural industry, and suggests that Asian stories are increasingly entering mainstream international narratives.

February

TikTok "becoming Chinese" trend:

Gen Z reshapes perceptions of China through social media


In February, the British Broadcasting Corporation (BBC) reported that a trend of "becoming Chinese" (Chinamaxxing / Becoming Chinese) has emerged on TikTok and Instagram, with large numbers of young people in Europe and North America imitating Chinese lifestyle habits (such as drinking hot water and practicing Baduanjin), and related videos surpassing 20 million views. Analysts suggest this phenomenon reflects Western Gen Z's disillusionment with traditional political narratives and the erosion of U.S. narrative dominance. Through direct exchanges on platforms like Xiaohongshu, many foreigners are beginning to view and understand real-life China on equal terms—a spontaneous cultural identification that represents an active grassroots dissemination of Asian and Chinese voices.


On February 6, Chinese Foreign Ministry spokesperson Lin Jian said at a press conference: "We are glad to see more and more foreign friends willing and able to experience China's development and changes, and the everyday lives of ordinary Chinese people." According to the Foreign Ministry, in 2025, the number of inbound and outbound trips by foreigners exceeded 82 million, a year-on-year increase of 26.4%, with visa-free entries rising nearly 50% year-on-year.
