Image source: The Good Fight (U.S. TV series)


The Good Fight continues the elite legal drama genre, but focuses on women's struggles in the workplace and in real life. The plot closely follows current events, exploring a series of political and legal issues concerning women's legal rights. The experiences of the female characters realistically reflect the challenges women face in the workplace. Yet, as the title suggests, all the women in the series are strong and unyielding, daring to fight against injustice. The show also attempts to discuss the difficulties women face in the new era, such as sexual harassment cases in the metaverse, online gender-based violence, and the definition of transgender rights. As the world changes rapidly, women's rights issues have become increasingly complex.

Background and Introduction

Recently, many social incidents harming women have been heartbreaking, and public discourse often points to individual evil. As the world shifts toward conservatism, women's rights are increasingly vulnerable to extremist ideologies. Traditional social norms and extremist religious beliefs obstruct women's access to reproductive freedom. Discussions about women's reproductive rights have deviated from the protection of life to misogyny, triggering global protest movements. The path to protecting women's rights remains long and arduous.


However, despite the difficult journey ahead, there are always people speaking up for women's rights.


Like Diane in The Good Fight, or real-life groups of women who have driven the introduction and revision of women's rights legislation.


We, the younger generation, were once passive beneficiaries of the struggle for equality. Now, the baton of the equality movement has been passed to us. We can become the "Diane" who firmly defends women's rights in court, or the "Deng Yingchao" who advances women's rights in the political arena.

"How To Become Yourself" Online Workshop: Life Portrait Review


On November 18 , VIVA will once again join hands with Avoice to host the "Born to Rebel, I Define the Rules" Online Workshop on Women's Legal Rights, warmly inviting partners who care about women's vitality, feminism, and women's rights protection to join us in exploring the future path of women in public affairs. Avoice has long been committed to protecting the rights and development of overseas Chinese women, with extensive legal experience and project expertise.


VIVA looks forward to collaborating with Avoice, transcending text and language, breaking through expressive limitations, and using artistic creation to gather diverse ideas, sparking deep reflections on feminist issues.


This online workshop is honored to invite two guests, Ms. Zhao Tiansong and Ms. Zhang Yuyan, who are engaged in research and work in the fields of social public service and women's rights protection. Ms. Zhao Tiansong is the Chairperson of Hangzhou Yijiarin Social Work Service Center, China, and has long been dedicated to social public service. She has conducted multiple online training sessions for Avoice on volunteer support for anti-domestic violence rescue. Meanwhile, Ms. Zhang Yuyan is currently pursuing her bachelor's degree, but also serves as the head of the Singapore team at the Legal Academic Division of the Avoice Overseas Chinese Women Protection Project, providing legal expertise support to Avoice.

In addition to guest presentations, this workshop will introduce a new "Painting Life" Relay Co-Creation segment, where participants will have the opportunity to transform their insights and inspirations about the journey of women's rights gained from this event into artistic elements through drawing, using brushstrokes to "paint" life and "paint" women's power; this is also an opportunity to showcase one's creativity, express oneself, and engage in mutual exchange with guests and peers.

Who We Are | Partner Organizations


Viva la Vida is an international organization with influence in over 50 countries, inspiring young people to reflect on life and understand themselves, using art as a medium to better perceive life and build meaningful emotional connections with others. To date, VIVA has become a globally influential cross-cultural IP, with partners including the United Nations, G20 YEA, APEC, Facebook, Toutiao, Alibaba, and China Unicom.


A long-term volunteer project primarily supporting overseas Chinese women. It aims to use youth-driven public service to provide timely and comprehensive assistance to overseas Chinese women suffering from domestic violence, and through partnerships with overseas Chinese organizations, women's aid agencies, academic institutions, and government departments, increase public awareness of the living difficulties faced by overseas Chinese women, creating a safe and equal environment for survival and development for those facing domestic violence.
