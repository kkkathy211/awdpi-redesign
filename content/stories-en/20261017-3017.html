About MIT AWDA



MIT Asian Women Development Association (MIT AWDA)


AWDPI | Asian Women Development Plan International


MIT Asian Women Development Association, MIT AWDA It is a newly established official student organization at MIT, focused on the development and support of Asian women, while open to students of all ethnicities, genders, and backgrounds. We aim to build an inclusive and open student community, enabling more people who identify with this mission to participate and collectively advance projects and issues related to women's development.


The work of MIT AWDA extends beyond event organization. We will operate across areas including community support and resource navigation, women in technology, innovation and entrepreneurship, career development and mentorship programs, education and curriculum, research and policy, lectures and public events, on- and off-campus collaboration, and media outreach. Specific activities include helping students connect with professional support resources within MIT, compiling opportunities in innovation, entrepreneurship, and career development, running mentorship and women in tech initiatives, and conducting research and content production on issues such as Asian women, gender equality, and women in STEM.


MIT AWDA is currently forming its inaugural team. The current departments represent our primary focus areas at this stage, but will not limit future growth. We welcome members to propose new projects and topics based on their interests and expertise, and to help develop new focus areas, project teams, or departments as appropriate.

Recruitment Information



Recruitment is not limited to MIT students and scholars; applicants from other universities in the Greater Boston area are welcome. Some positions support remote participation, and we also welcome partners from outside the area who can commit consistently. In cases of comparable qualifications and team needs, preference will be given to applicants who can stably participate in in-person activities in the Greater Boston area.



Role Structure | Departmental Positions


Each functional department at MIT AWDA will ideally have 1 to 2 Chairs, and will recruit several Fellows.


 Chairs  Coordinators responsible for overall department management, including developing work plans, advancing key projects, coordinating team assignments, tracking progress, and communicating and collaborating with the Executive Board and other departments.


 Fellows  Members participate in specific departmental tasks and projects, and may lead different initiatives or workstreams based on personal interest, experience, and skills. We encourage Fellows to proactively propose new project ideas and participate in the full process from conception to execution.



Flexible Structure | Flexible Department Setup


The current departmental structure reflects MIT AWDA's main focus areas at this stage. We will adjust departmental setups and staffing based on actual recruitment outcomes, team capacity, and project needs.


Members who wish to pursue new projects or topics beyond the current departmental mandates are encouraged to propose them. For ideas that are well-defined, actionable, and aligned with MIT AWDA's overall mission, we may support the creation of new project directions, working groups, or even new functional departments as appropriate.



Term | Duration of Commitment


The term for all Chairs and Fellows is at least one year.


For core leadership roles, candidates who can commit to longer terms, maintain consistent involvement, and contribute to the organization's sustained development will be given priority, all else being equal.

Community Support and Resources Department

Provides peer support, resource navigation, and relevant information to MIT students, helping those in need access professional support resources both on and off campus, while also conducting resource education on topics such as gender-based violence prevention, wellbeing, and student safety.


Main Responsibilities:

  • MIT AWDA provides peer support, resource information, and resource navigation, but does not offer psychotherapy, medical advice, legal opinions, case investigations, or Title IX determinations.


Specific Requirements:

  • Interest in Student Support, Gender Equality, Wellbeing, or Community Service.

  • Strong listening, communication, and empathy skills.

  • Ability to respect personal boundaries, privacy, and individual autonomy.

  • Strong sense of responsibility regarding sensitive information, and willingness to undergo relevant training.

  • Prior experience in Peer Support, Advocacy, or Community Service is preferred.


Support Boundaries:

MIT AWDA provides peer support, resource information, and resource navigation, but does not offer psychotherapy, medical advice, legal opinions, case investigations, or Title IX determinations.

Innovation and Entrepreneurship Department

Responsible for MIT AWDA's initiatives related to women in technology, innovation, and entrepreneurship, helping students understand MIT's innovation and entrepreneurship ecosystem and available resources, while providing learning, networking, and project showcase opportunities, with special support for women entrepreneurs.


Main Responsibilities:

  • Organize thematic content and experience-sharing sessions on practical entrepreneurial topics such as idea validation, user needs, early-stage products, founding teams, and fundraising.

  • Invite founders, investors, product leaders, and tech industry professionals to share innovation and entrepreneurship experiences.

  • Compile and explain resources such as MIT Sandbox, Martin Trust Center, delta v, startup competitions, funding opportunities, and relevant courses, including their target stages and application processes.

  • Provide students with existing tech, innovation, or entrepreneurial projects opportunities to present, exchange, and receive feedback.

  • Launch special projects on topics such as women in tech, AI, FemTech, women's health, technology for social good, and female entrepreneurship.

  • Collaborate with the Career Development and Mentorship Department to connect entrepreneurial mentors.

  • Work with the Partnerships and External Relations Department to connect startups, VCs, companies, and entrepreneurial institutions.


Specific Requirements:

  • Interest in technology, innovation, entrepreneurship, AI, or women in tech.

  • Willingness to proactively learn about MIT's innovation and entrepreneurship ecosystem.

  • Strong skills in information gathering, organization, communication, and project execution.

  • Prior experience in startups, technology, product development, VC, or entrepreneurship is preferred.

Main Responsibilities:

  • Develop content around themes such as Asian women, leadership, technology, entrepreneurship, gender equality, global development, and social impact.

  • Manage speaker invitations, communications, scheduling, and follow-ups.

  • Prepare agendas, discussion questions, moderator guides, and speaker briefings.

  • Handle event moderation, speaker reception, and on-site coordination.

  • Build and maintain a speaker database to accumulate resources for future events.

  • Collaborate with other departments and on- or off-campus organizations to plan, promote, and execute events.


Specific Requirements:

  • Interest in public events, women's issues, international affairs, technology, business, or social impact.

  • Strong communication, research, and organizational coordination skills.

  • Willingness to proactively reach out to speakers and follow up consistently.

  • Detail-oriented, capable of managing scheduling and on-site logistics.

  • Prior experience in event planning, moderation, or speaker outreach is preferred.

Career Development and Mentorship Department

Responsible for MIT AWDA's career development, mentorship, and professional networking programs, providing members with career resources, mentorship support, and access to professional networks.


Main Responsibilities:

  • Design and operate the MIT AWDA mentorship program.

  • Invite alumni, professionals, founders, researchers, and industry leaders to serve as mentors.

  • Match mentors and mentees based on career interests and development needs, and follow up on feedback from both parties.

  • Conduct mentorship activities including one-on-one mentoring, small group mentoring, and office hours.

  • Organize career development programs on resume writing, interviewing, networking, personal branding, and career strategy.

  • Host career conversations focused on fields such as STEM, business, policy, entrepreneurship, academia, and nonprofit.

  • Continuously compile internships, fellowships, research opportunities, leadership programs, conferences, and other development opportunities.

  • Build and maintain the MIT AWDA mentor network and professional network.


Specific requirements:

  • Interest in career development, mentorship, or professional networking.

  • Willingness to proactively reach out to mentors, alumni, and professionals.

  • Strong communication, interpersonal coordination, and relationship-building skills.

  • Ability to design programs based on members' actual needs.

  • Prior experience in mentorship, career services, networking, or recruiting is preferred.

Education and Curriculum Department

Responsible for MIT AWDA's curriculum development, teaching, and educational programs, including on-campus initiatives at MIT and public education efforts targeting broader groups of women and girls, such as participation in MIT's official ESP Splash program.


Main responsibilities:

  • Develop courses on leadership, AI, technology, gender equality, and social impact.

  • Design syllabi, lesson plans, slides, worksheets, and other teaching materials.

  • Participate in course research, lesson preparation, teaching, and instructor coordination.

  • Engage in on-campus educational programs such as MIT ESP Splash.

  • Implement AI, digital literacy, technology, and STEM education programs for girls.

  • Adapt course content and teaching methods according to age, region, and learning background.

  • Establish the MIT AWDA curriculum library to preserve and continuously improve course materials.

  • Assess course effectiveness through feedback, surveys, or evaluations.

  • Collaborate with schools, NGOs, educational institutions, and youth organizations to deliver educational programs.

  • Scale successful courses to additional campuses, communities, or partner organizations.


Specific requirements:

  • Interest in education, teaching, AI, STEM, women's development, or youth education.

  • Willingness to participate in curriculum design, material creation, teaching, and feedback collection.

  • Strong expression, communication, and content organization skills.

  • Ability to translate complex content into teaching materials suitable for target students.

  • Prior experience in teaching, curriculum development, or youth programs is preferred.

Specific requirements:
