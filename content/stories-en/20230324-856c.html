The winter of 2022 has passed, and the breath of spring is already spreading through the city's streets and alleys. Yet, some people are still living in the winter of their lives, enduring the aftershocks of survival. Harm does not end the moment the incident occurs—it continues on, like a tireless maggot, still gnawing at the survivor's body and soul.

Ms. L (pseudonym) found us during the darkest winter of her life. She choked up several times during the interview, having suffered severe physical and psychological trauma, and was diagnosed with mild depression requiring antidepressant medication. Avoice provided her with legal aid, and after legal assistance, she won her lawsuit. She hopes to share her story to warn and inspire other women.


If this incident had not occurred, 28-year-old Ms. L would still be holding pure hopes for love, still full of expectations for the future on her smooth life path. So, what exactly did she experience during this short relationship that made her feel it was a stain, a deception, a destruction?


1

Ms. L's moment of nightmare

"

 Ms. L was born into a happy, middle-class family, well-protected by her family, with a smooth academic and career path. After earning her master's degree, she successfully joined a public institution, known for her sincerity, kindness, simplicity, and honesty. She met Mr. S (pseudonym) on a social media platform. He was a graduate of a prestigious university, outgoing, talkative, and witty, leaving a good impression on her. After meeting Mr. S, she suddenly found much more joy in life, and their feelings grew through constant communication. Soon, Mr. S took the initiative to confess his love, even kneeling down to propose. His deeply affectionate demeanor moved her, and Ms. L, who had already fallen for him at first sight, agreed. It seemed like a match made in heaven—sweet and happy. But she never imagined that behind this façade, a nightmare was brewing.


"Sometimes he would disappear for several days, then suddenly reappear and contact me frantically."


The relationship increasingly felt wrong: Mr. S used a fake account to announce their relationship; during the so-called passionate phase, he often vanished for days without explanation… Ms. L had a vague feeling that a third person was involved. This intuition was soon confirmed—during a trip, she accidentally discovered that his phone contained a large number of flirtatious messages with many different women.


"The things they said to each other were exactly the same as what he said to me!"


Ms. L decisively broke up with him. He knelt down to beg her to stay, once again playing the role of the devoted lover. Still emotionally attached, Ms. L softened at heart, "I admit I still had a sliver of hope for him." And so, they reconciled—but this was the true beginning of the nightmare that Ms. L would later deeply regret.


After reconciling, Mr. S continued to act passionately in love with Ms. L, while secretly recording private videos of her and using them as leverage, threatening to post them online. Ms. L completely broke down, "I have never met such an evil person before." Her trust collapsed, overwhelming shame, and online language violence such as "victim-blaming" nearly robbed her of the courage to go on living.


Image source: freepik


Although the path to women's rights protection is full of hardship and long obstacles, fortunately, there are still those willing to help her move toward the light. After receiving legal aid and winning her lawsuit, she reached out to Avoice, hoping to share her experience so other women can avoid the risk of being secretly filmed, avoid emotional manipulation, and attain happy lives.


2

Take up legal weapons

Defend your own rights

"

This is only Ms. L's story—yet some evils continue unseen. According to authoritative reports, approximately one in three women worldwide (30%) have experienced physical and/or sexual violence, mostly at the hands of intimate partners. According to a report by Hong Kong Wen Wei Po, the Women's Affairs Committee of the Hong Kong Democratic Alliance for the Betterment and Progress of Hong Kong conducted a survey on issues related to secret filming, and the results showed that over 10% of respondents had either been secretly filmed or witnessed someone else being secretly filmed.


Image source: freepik


Ms. Zhu Xiaodan, lawyer from the Avoice Legal Aid Department, who provided legal assistance to Ms. L, stated: "On the basis of greatly enriched material life, people today increasingly value and care about personal freedom, dignity, and worth, hoping their privacy, personal information, and other fundamental rights can be effectively protected. In light of this, the Civil Code specifically dedicates an independent section to personality rights, detailing their content and protection, thus establishing a relatively comprehensive system for protecting personality rights, more fully and effectively safeguarding our personal dignity. Articles 990–991 of the Civil Code clearly stipulate: 'Personality rights are rights enjoyed by civil subjects, including the right to life, bodily integrity, health, name, title, portrait, reputation, honor, and privacy.' 'The personality rights of civil subjects are protected by law; no organization or individual may infringe upon them.'


Romantic relationships are a continuous process of mutual understanding and adjustment. If conflicts and contradictions arise during this process, making it impossible to continue together, this is a normal part of life. However,


Dating is a continuous process of getting to know and adjusting to one another. It is common in life for conflicts and disagreements to arise during this process, making it impossible for both parties to continue moving forward together. However, In romantic relationships, men should not use advantages in gender, education, or other areas to emotionally bully, verbally insult, or physically threaten women, or even humiliate them in public—such as secretly filming private videos and threatening to release them online. Even if no physical harm is caused, such acts still constitute illegal violations of personality rights.


Women in romantic relationships, if their personality rights are violated, should


clearly recognize the violation at the earliest moment, not fear various pressures, and take up legal weapons—using both civil litigation and police reporting—to defend their legitimate rights and interests, a behavior worthy of praise and one that many women should learn from. Therefore, if one experiences a violation of personality rights, one can file a civil lawsuit to hold the perpetrator accountable for eliminating negative impacts, restoring reputation, issuing apologies, or even paying compensation for mental distress. At the same time, one can report the incident to the police, leading to administrative penalties for the offender, or criminal liability in serious cases. Image source: freepik


Women should not be taken as natural victims; they must have the courage to break prejudices and end silence. In this process, while protecting their own bodies, they should also try to collect solid evidence—including witness and physical evidence—dare to confide in or seek help from trusted people, and dare to report to the police and relevant authorities.


Those who have been violated should not remain silent. Our society should have more rights defenders and fewer silent lambs, and certainly should not have indifferent bystanders. Guard against wolves in sheep's clothing

In 2020, BBC journalist Stacey Dooley's documentary *Spycam Sex Criminals* (Sexual Secret Filming in South Korea) revealed that some secret filmers are individuals of very high social status, such as doctors, judges, professors, and bankers. Regarding how to prevent being secretly filmed, based on years of frontline work experience and insights,


Lawyer Zhu Chuan, former chief inspector and judge, offered his advice: First, when women go out, they should inform friends or family about their current location, whom they will meet, where and when they will go, what activities they will engage in, how long they plan to stay, when they expect to leave, the names and contact information of companions, and ticket or vehicle license plate numbers… Additionally, never consume food or drinks that have been left unattended.


Second, be cautious of locations prone to secret filming incidents: such as bars or KTVs with shady crowds, restrooms, hotels with lax ID registration, etc.


Third, pay attention to items that may contain hidden cameras: such as toilet tanks, bathtubs, tissue boxes, electrical outlets, spy shoes, makeup mirrors, sunglasses, camera-equipped cups, routers, pinhole cameras in restrooms, cartoon keychains, lighters, hooks, smoke detectors, sports shoes, drink cans, shower gels, toothbrushes, and pens. While staying away from home, avoid discussing personal private information such as phone numbers, workplaces, and home addresses. If suspects obtain victims' personal information through hidden cameras, it may lead to other criminal cases.

Fourth, if victims discover items that might be hidden cameras, they should quickly take their mobile phone, identification documents, and essential belongings and leave the location or room, heading to a nearby public area with more people, such as a lobby, roadside, or supermarket, then call the police. After the police arrive, the victim can return to the site where the suspected hidden camera was found, point out the suspicious item to the officers, and describe the situation and issues they encountered. After filing the report, victims should change their accommodation or room to prevent possible retaliation by the suspect.


Fifth, if victims are extorted or blackmailed due to being secretly filmed, they should try to record the phone calls or take screenshots of communication records, then immediately report the incident to the police. Remember: do not pay any money. Once payment is made, the suspect will regard the victim as a target for further financial exploitation.


Sixth, anti-spying devices such as infrared detectors and thermal imaging cameras can be purchased online.


Seventh, in the face of violation, say no bravely.

Recognizing Gender-Based Violence

Proactively Seeking Help

"

The L woman incident is a case of gender-based violence; then, how should gender-based violence be defined, and how should it be addressed? Grace, head of the Psychological Support Division, explains gender-based violence from a psychological perspective and offers guidance on how to respond if one experiences such violence.


Image source: freepik


● What is gender-based violence?

There are many forms of violence in intimate relationships, including persistent harassment and stalking, deprivation of freedom, psychological and emotional abuse, sexual violence and physical abuse, economic control, and verbal threats.

Gaslighting refers to psychological control, which includes not only verbal belittlement and degradation but also emotional blackmail, making the individual feel extremely inadequate and suffer from extremely low self-esteem. Economic control means the abuser restricts and controls the victim's use of financial resources, requiring even small expenditures to be reported. Meanwhile, threat of abuse is a more covert form, where the abuser may say, "If you don't do as I say, I will threaten the lives of you, your family, or your pets." This, too, is a form of violence.

All these forms of violence often share one commonality: the perpetrator attempts to control the victim through various means, reflecting an unequal power relationship. Victims are not facing a single issue, but a series of complex, systemic problems, It is necessary to recognize the structural dilemmas within, rather than unilaterally attributing them to individual traits.


● How to determine if you are experiencing gender-based violence?

You experience emotional confusion in the relationship, and these feelings are often ignored, suppressed, blamed, or denied by the other party, leading to a sense of distorted reality or thoughts like "I shouldn't feel this way."

You frequently experience emotions such as self-blame, low self-esteem, guilt, depression, powerlessness/helplessness, and even when suffering violence and isolation, you still attribute the fault to yourself.

Anyone can experience gender-based violence. If you suspect you are in such a situation, trust your feelings and remember: it is not your fault. Moreover, no matter what emotional or physical reactions you have (confusion, anger, fear, unease, anxiety, guilt, shame), they are all normal. First, recognize and identify your discomfort and the pain caused by the relationship, then seek external and internal resources and proactively ask for help.


Image source: freepik


● How to prevent gender-based violence?

Trust your feelings and identify discomfort in relationships;

You are not alone—you can receive support;

Your experiences can be understood by others; try speaking up;

You have many supportive resources available to seek help.


● How to find resources for self-rescue?

External resources: public social resources, communities with similar experiences, trusted family and friends, psychological counseling or other professional help, and, if necessary, legal action.

Internal resources: relaxation techniques such as mindfulness and meditation, self-healing through exercise, drawing, crafting, or writing emotional journals, recognizing and listening to your inner needs, believing in your psychological resilience, and practicing self-affirmation and self-support.

Surviving the Aftermath, Walking Forward Together

"

The moment of violation is like a nightmare for survivors—the world collapses, long nights are filled with loneliness, and life feels unbearable. Even after time passes, the wounds continue to resurface, forcing survivors to repeatedly endure alone the fear and shame inflicted upon them, draining their courage to rebuild life over and over again. Psychological trauma often lasts for years, and the shattered self requires a long time to reconstruct. But fortunately, the key to rebirth remains in your hands—you are not alone.


Image source: freepik


Public welfare organizations like Avoice and other social institutions are working hard to provide greater support and assistance to women who have suffered misfortune. Perhaps this women's struggle is not entirely smooth, but even the smallest light can gather into a blazing sun. The harsh winter will eventually pass, and the light of spring is already on its way. Your light will travel far with you.


In addition, Avoice will soon launch a series of psychology courses focused on self-healing, specifically designed for survivors of relationship violence, to help participants understand their own reactions and confusions through trauma education and emotional awareness, and actively discover internal and external resources.
