October 31, 2026, the Boston–Silicon Valley AI Film Festival was successfully held at the MIT Stata Center (MIT Computer Science and Artificial Intelligence Laboratory). As an official event of the 2026 Boston Tough Tech Week, the festival brought together filmmakers, artificial intelligence experts, tech entrepreneurs, and academics to exchange ideas on AI-powered filmmaking, technological innovation, and the future of the film industry.


This year's festival was co-organized by Boston International Media Consulting, Starry Sky Culture USA, and North America Entertainment, in collaboration with the 2026 Boston Tough Tech Week, Asian Women Development Plan International (AWDPI), and MIT student group Asian Women Development Association (AWDA), aiming to promote interdisciplinary integration between artificial intelligence and cinematic art.



From Silicon Valley to Boston


The Silicon Valley AI Film Festival originated in Silicon Valley, the global hub of technological innovation. In January 2026, the inaugural Silicon Valley AI Film Festival successfully collected over 2,000 AI-generated film entries from around the world, showcasing innovative applications of artificial intelligence in film storytelling, visual generation, and film production.


This Boston–Silicon Valley AI Film Festival leveraged MIT's and the region's rich technological and academic resources to connect film creation with artificial intelligence, venture investment, and the creative industries, building a cross-disciplinary exchange platform for a new generation of creators.

Distinguished Guests Gather to Discuss the Future of AI Cinema


The festival was hosted by Shanshan Cao, who moderated the opening remarks, award presentations, panel discussions, and on-site exchanges, creating a dialogue platform for guests from the fields of film, technology, and academia.


At the opening of the event, Alda Witherspoon, Commissioner of the Massachusetts Cultural Council, delivered a keynote address and presented a Massachusetts State Commendation Certificate to the festival, accepted by festival chair Gary Yu.




The event also featured Priya Samant, Co-Founder and Chief Business Officer of CloserLook.health, as a special guest, and hosted two industry roundtable discussions bringing together professionals from film, technology, academia, and business.




Panel 1 | The Human Craft: Human Artistry—Co-Creating with AI



The first panel was moderated by Tommy Tang, Director of Bioinformatics at AstraZeneca, and featured Woody Yuan, Founder and Artistic Director of Zero Theater; Nicholas Manley, filmmaker and AI media art educator at Harvard Extension School; Odis Spencer Jr., SAG-AFTRA actor; and Alice Zhou, neuroscientist, Founder and Director of BRAVE Lab.


The panelists discussed human creativity, artistic expression, and human-AI collaboration in the age of artificial intelligence from perspectives including filmmaking, performing arts, AI technology, and neuroscience, exploring how technological advances can open new possibilities for cinematic storytelling.



Panel 2 | The New Industry: The Emerging Industry—Markets, Capital, and the Future



The second panel was moderated by Cristina Yao, Founder of CS Global Edge, and featured Margaret Wallace, Associate Professor at Boston University; David A. Hall, Chief Revenue Officer at Hill Research; Xinyue (Moon) Zhang, Chief Creative Officer at Golden Infinity Inc.; and Natalie Ip, Systems Lead at Google.


The discussion focused on the commercial potential of AI-generated film content, emerging technological applications, and future market opportunities from the perspectives of academic research, technological application, business innovation, and industrial development, examining how artificial intelligence is transforming the production processes and business ecosystems of the film industry.



Outstanding Works, Diverse Creativity


The 2026 Boston–Silicon Valley AI Film Festival awarded Best Film, Grand Prix by regional category, Jury Prize, and multiple specialized awards to honor works demonstrating innovation in AI-powered filmmaking, narrative expression, and artistic design.


🏆 Best Film

"Earlier"


This film was independently created by young filmmaker Yu Kuai, currently a third-year master's student in Game and Interactive Design at the China Academy of Art. He served as director, screenwriter, producer, editor, and visual designer for this film.


The film tells the story of Cheng Weixi, a young lab director who, after the sudden death of her lover, attempts to return to the moment before the tragedy through a time experiment, exploring the possibility of changing fate amid love, regret, and time loops.



🏅 Grand Prix by Regional Category

"Seven Fleeting Days"



This film was produced and released by StarStream Culture Technology Co., Ltd. (Shanghai), co-released by YuGuang Tongchen (Hangzhou) Culture & Technology Co., Ltd.; Chi (Phoenix) Liu served as director, screenwriter, and executive producer, with Mimi Liu and Lingyun Xu as producers.


The film follows Lia, a single mother, as she faces physical trauma and long-buried memories during a seven-day recovery after a severe boating accident in Bali, ultimately moving toward self-reconciliation.



🎖️ Jury Prize

The Origin of Splendor (Jinxiu Yuanqi)



This film was produced by Woodfilm (Beijing) Culture Media Co., Ltd., with Fiona Feng (Fangfang Feng) as project lead and lead producer, Zhe Xiao, Xincheng Zhao, and Zimeng Cui as producers and co-producers, and Sicheng Wang as AI director.


The film follows modern hanfu designer Su Jinxin, who accidentally travels back in time to ancient China and, through exploring silk weaving, traditional dyeing, embroidery, and gold-inlay techniques, rediscovers the meaning of Chinese clothing culture and the legacy of craftsmanship.

A Resounding Success, Co-Creating the Future



The Boston–Silicon Valley AI Film Festival not only showcased the diverse achievements of AI-powered filmmaking but also provided filmmakers, technologists, investors, and young creators with opportunities for exchange and collaboration.

As a partner of this event, BIMC actively promoted connections among Boston's technological, cultural, and creative resources, supporting cross-sector dialogue between artificial intelligence and the film industry.


From Silicon Valley to Boston, the Silicon Valley AI Film Festival continues to expand its international network. As generative AI transforms the way films are made, the festival will continue to provide a platform for creators worldwide to explore the ever-evolving possibilities of cinematic art in the age of artificial intelligence.
