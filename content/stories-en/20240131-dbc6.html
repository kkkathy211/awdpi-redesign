2023 International Gender Equality Prize awarded to the Afghan Women Skills Development Center in recognition of its outstanding international contributions to women's empowerment


On December 11, 2023, the International Gender Equality Prize was officially awarded at a grand ceremony in Tampere, Finland, to the Afghan Women Skills Development Center, honoring its exceptional international contributions in the field of gender equality. Founded in 1999 by Mary Akrami and headquartered in Kabul, the center is dedicated to advancing and defending the rights of Afghan women, supporting their skill development and economic independence through training and financial assistance. The organization operates in eight provinces across Afghanistan and collaborates with the United Nations High Commissioner for Refugees (UNHCR), primarily aiming to protect women and families at risk. The center also provides humanitarian aid, including food, hygiene supplies, and cash, to meet women's basic needs.


At the award ceremony, Finnish Prime Minister Petteri Orpo presented the 300,000-euro prize to Mahbouba Seraj, representative of the Afghan Women Skills Development Center. American news anchor Richard Lui hosted the event, during which representatives from countries including Ukraine, Afghanistan, and Rwanda discussed gender equality in conflict zones. Writer and war correspondent Janine di Giovanni delivered the keynote speech.


The International Gender Equality Prize is awarded by Finland's Ministry of Social Affairs and Health. Tampere has been a partner and host city of the award ceremony since 2017. The prize is awarded biennially, with nominations reviewed by an independent international jury, aiming to recognize outstanding organizations advancing gender equality worldwide.

COP28 Gender Equality Day: UN climate conference focuses on just transition and climate financing, calling for global action


On December 4, a series of events were held at the COP28 UN Climate Change Conference for Gender Equality Day, aiming to advance gender-responsive climate action with a focus on just transition and financing. Ina Parvanova, Director of Communication and Engagement at the UN Climate Change Secretariat, stated, "Just transition has the potential to drive ambitious climate action."


The COP28 President launched the Partnership for Gender-Responsive Just Transition and Climate Action, endorsed by 68 Parties, including commitments on finance, data, and equal opportunities. Ina Parvanova emphasized, "Just transition and advancing gender equality are inherently linked," and important gender dimensions must be considered.


A fair and inclusive transition can promote gender equality and economic empowerment by providing women with equal employment opportunities. However, improving women's participation in and shaping of the green economy requires more inclusive decision-making, incorporating voices from feminists, youth, Indigenous peoples, and other groups.


Other Gender Day events at COP28 included technical dialogues on financing for gender-responsive just transition and climate action. The role of data in advancing gender-responsive climate action received significant attention. The Global Conference on Gender and Environment Data called for action to strengthen the collection and use of gender and environmental data, advancing global gender-equal climate and environmental initiatives. Calls to action included ethically transparent data generation, accelerating data use, funding the production of gender and environment data, ensuring inclusive data governance, and building multi-stakeholder partnerships.

Chinese student in the U.S. safely rescued after four days of disappearance,

police expose "virtual kidnapping" telecom fraud


At 3:30 a.m. on December 28, 17-year-old Chinese international student Zhuang Kai (name transliterated) went missing from his host family in Riverdale. After receiving photos of Zhuang and ransom demands, his parents contacted his American high school, which then notified his host family. Following the host family's report, Riverdale police issued a "missing person alert" on December 29 and launched a search in collaboration with the FBI and the Chinese Embassy in the U.S.


On the evening of December 31, the search made a breakthrough. Police used a search drone to locate Zhuang in a tent in the Brigham City canyon area of Utah, where he was found safe. The tent contained only limited food, water, and several mobile phones, with no heating equipment. Police suspect the phones were tools for the "virtual kidnapping." Authorities said Zhuang was "very cold and very scared." "On the way back to the police station, he said he just wanted to call his family to make sure they were safe, and asked police for a hot cheeseburger," said officials. The investigation revealed that someone had lured Zhuang to run away and manipulated him into believing his family was under threat, leading his family to pay an $80,000 ransom.


Zhuang's case is a classic example of "virtual kidnapping" telecom fraud, a type of scam that has become increasingly frequent and particularly targets international exchange students. Police urge the public to remain vigilant and ensure student safety.

Former president of the South Africa Qilu Association dies after being attacked,

reigniting concerns over the safety of overseas Chinese in South Africa


Fan Qing, former president of the South Africa Qilu Association, was shot by bandits in Johannesburg on the evening of December 13, 2023. He passed away at 3 a.m. on December 14 at the age of 58. Founded in 1999, the association is one of the earliest overseas Chinese organizations in South Africa following the establishment of diplomatic relations between China and South Africa. Fan Qing's death marks another tragic case after the 2020 murder of Zhong Zhiwei, then president of the same association, and his wife. The association has long been committed to safeguarding the rights of Shandong-origin overseas Chinese in South Africa and promoting exchanges between China and South Africa, becoming a significant civil force.


The 2020 murder of President Zhong and his wife shocked the Chinese community in South Africa and drew widespread attention and strong condemnation. Overseas Chinese spontaneously organized activities and submitted open letters to the South African government, demanding a thorough investigation and criminal accountability for the perpetrators. The tragic death of Fan Qing has once again sparked public concern.


With the arrival of South Africa's Christmas and New Year holiday season, the Chinese Embassy in South Africa issued a notice on December 5, reminding overseas Chinese to strengthen safety precautions. The embassy emphasized that personal safety must be the top priority during this critical period, urging overseas Chinese to enhance safety awareness, guard against all types of crime, and immediately report emergencies to local police, consulates, or the local Chinese community-police cooperation center for assistance.

An Asian restaurant owner in Oakland, California, was subjected to

armed robbery, narrowly escapes after a dramatic vehicle chase


A shocking and terrifying robbery recently occurred in Oakland, California. In the early morning of December 15, an Asian-owned Chinese restaurant owner became the target of armed robbers. According to the victim, he parked his vehicle in front of his restaurant in the morning, preparing to start the day's work, when he suddenly noticed several suspicious cars nearby and attempted to drive away. However, his vehicle was surrounded by a Dodge, a Cadillac, and a Jeep. Although the owner managed to break through the blockade, the pursuit did not stop. At around 6:30 a.m. on the 2800 block of Lakeshore Avenue, one of the vehicles struck his car, causing it to overturn outside a canopy near Lakeshore Avenue. The robbers seized the opportunity to steal the owner's belongings. Fortunately, the victim was not shot. The suspects then quickly fled the scene.


A local resident witnessed the entire incident and called 911. Surveillance footage later reconstructed this terrifying scene. It was reported that when the owner attempted to escape, the suspects had also surrounded another male driver, forcing him to open his car door so they could quickly search the vehicle before hastily leaving.


The incident has raised concerns among residents. An Asian business owner operating in the area said such crimes have become too common. Residents emphasized that not all Asian individuals carry large amounts of cash and believe such crimes may be based on false assumptions. They are calling on authorities to take stronger measures to ensure community safety.

President Biden issued a statement

on the 80th anniversary of the repeal of the Chinese Exclusion Act


The U.S. Senate and House of Representatives passed a historic resolution by unanimous vote on October 11, 2011, and June 18, 2012, respectively, formally apologizing for the historical Chinese Exclusion Act through legislative action. Mr. Xue Haipeng, then chairman of the National Council of Chinese Americans and current president of United Chinese American (UCA), was the initiator and key driving force behind this "apology" campaign. He has long been committed to urging the White House to issue an official statement on the Chinese Exclusion Act. Congresswoman Judy Chu, currently the only Chinese American member of the U.S. House of Representatives, was the primary sponsor of the resolution in the House.


In March 2023, UCA President Xue Haipeng engaged in discussions with the White House, hoping the President would issue a Presidential Proclamation during Asian American and Pacific Islander Heritage Month in May. At that time, the White House politely declined, citing a heavy schedule and timing conflicts related to AAPI Month events, but later agreed to consider releasing the statement on December 17. In the following months, UCA engaged in multiple discussions with the White House and the Congressional Asian Pacific American Caucus (CAPAC), seeking consensus. On December 1, UCA submitted an open letter signed by over 330 Asian American and Chinese American organizations and nearly 3,000 individuals, urging the White House to issue the historic statement on or before December 17.


At 9:37 p.m. on December 16, Erika Moritsugu, the White House's senior official for Asian American and Pacific Islander affairs, emailed UCA President Xue Haipeng, informing him in advance that the White House would release an announcement on the Chinese Exclusion Act the next day. At 8:03 a.m. on December 17, a White House official for Asian American and Pacific Islander affairs sent another email confirming to UCA that the Presidential Proclamation had been officially released, and attached the text of the proclamation.


Image source: United Chinese American


UCA President Xue Haipeng stated: "Although the White House did not issue a formal apology for the Chinese Exclusion Act passed by Congress in 1882, this is the first official condemnation of the Chinese Exclusion Act by the White House in 141 years, from its enactment in 1882 to today, marking a significant step forward in the historic efforts of Chinese American and Asian American communities to achieve equal treatment."

Asian Americans Advancing Justice files appeal over alleged anti-Asian discrimination in U.S. government no-fly list


Asian Americans Advancing Justice – AAJC, together with the law firm Willkie Farr & Gallagher LLP, has challenged the U.S. government's no-fly list, accusing it of Islamophobia and discrimination against Asian Americans, particularly South Asians and Muslims. Under their leadership, a total of 24 Asian American organizations, civil rights, and human rights groups signed the "Advancing Justice – AAJC's amicus brief."


The case involves U.S. citizen Yonas Fikre, who opposes his placement on the "no-fly list," viewing it as an example of government abuse of power. Leaked documents revealed that over 98% of individuals on the list are Muslim. AAJC stated that the United States has a history of exclusion based on race and religion, and this ruling is crucial for the Muslim and South Asian communities' fight against travel discrimination. The government's process for placing individuals on the no-fly list is often opaque, and the case has raised public concern due to its discriminatory impact on Muslim communities.


The case is currently being heard by the U.S. Supreme Court. Willkie Farr & Gallagher LLP stated that due to the lack of transparency in the operation of the no-fly list and the government's discriminatory practices against Muslim and South Asian communities, Mr. Fikre has even stronger grounds to continue protesting his placement on the no-fly list.
