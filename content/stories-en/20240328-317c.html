 NO.2 

[Germany Chapter]Are you or someone you care about experiencing domestic violence?


1. [Legal Definition]

  • Domestic violence is not explicitly defined in German law. According to the website of the German Federal Ministry for Family Affairs, Senior Citizens, Women and Youth, domestic violence refers to physical and/or psychological acts of violence between individuals who live or have lived in the same household, such as within marriage, civil partnerships, or intimate relationships.


2. [Current situation of domestic violence in Germany]

  • According to the annual criminal statistics assessment on intimate partner violence (Kriminalstatistische Auswertung zur Partnerschaftsgewalt) by the Federal Criminal Police Office (Bundeskriminalamt), in 2020, 148,031 people in Germany were victims of intimate partner violence (141,792 in 2019). Because many incidents are not reported to the police, the actual number of affected individuals is much higher. Although men are also victims of intimate partner violence, police statistics show that four out of five victims in domestic violence cases are women. The 2014 study "Violence against Women in Relationships" ("Gewalt gegen Frauen in Paarbeziehungen") found that women from middle to high educational and social backgrounds are also victims, and domestic violence does not end as women grow older.


3. [Common types]

  • Domestic violence includes physical violence, such as beating, confinement, and cutting off food supply; psychological violence, such as humiliation, harassment, or threats; sexual violence, such as sexually suggestive harassment, unwanted physical touching, or forcing someone into sexual acts; and economic violence, such as preventing access to joint bank accounts or maintaining sole control over household income.



 NO.3 

[Germany Chapter]How to seek legal protection?


1. [Seeking help]

1.1 Call the police

  • If you are in immediate danger, call 110 to report it. The police will usually ask for your name, address, and the nature of the problem, and officers will be dispatched to protect you (and your children). Staff at the police emergency call center usually speak only German. If you need language assistance, call the helpline of the organization "Gewalt gegen Frauen" ("Violence against Women"). Their staff are available 24/7, speak multiple languages—including English, Arabic, French, Persian/Dari, Kurdish (Kurmanji), and Turkish—and can assist you. You can reach them at 08000 116 016.

  • You may also visit a police station in person to file a complaint. If you need accompaniment, you may bring a friend or a lawyer. You may also bring an interpreter with you, or request one from the police. After experiencing domestic violence, it is best to have a doctor examine and document your injuries as soon as possible to serve as evidence for your claims. Including photos with your complaint can greatly assist in handling your case.


1.2 Shelter and refuge

  • There are specialized centers that anonymously and free of charge examine and document victims' injuries. You can call 116006 to contact staff from the victim support association WEISSER RING, who will refer you to a contact point in your area. Association staff are available daily from 7:00 a.m. to 10:00 p.m., speak German and English, and offer free, anonymous advice.

  • If you do not wish to file a complaint yet, have questions, or simply need someone to talk to, contact a counseling center. The helpline "Gewalt gegen Frauen" ("Violence against Women") offers anonymous and free advice 24 hours a day in multiple languages. You can reach their staff at 08000 116 016. On www.frauen-gegen-gewalt.de and http://frauenhauskoordinierung.de/, you can also search for counseling centers that speak your language and are located in your area.

  • If you no longer feel safe at home or in your current accommodation, you may go to a "women's shelter." Women's shelters are safe places where women who have experienced violence (and their children) can find refuge. Victims can stay there until their situation is resolved. The addresses of these shelters are not publicly accessible, meaning no one can find you there. However, the number of available women's shelters is limited, and sometimes there may be no vacancies—but staff will always try to find a solution for you. You can find women's shelters in your area online or ask the "Gewalt gegen Frauen" helpline to recommend one. Helpline staff speak multiple languages and are available 24/7. Call 08000 116 016 to reach them.


1.3 Lawyers

  • You can apply to family court for a "protection order" (see below). The court may prohibit the perpetrator from entering the shared apartment for an extended period (up to 6–12 months). This can give you space to manage your affairs and possibly find a new residence. Such orders may also prohibit the perpetrator from approaching you or contacting you. You can apply for a protection order in person at the appropriate family court or hire a lawyer to apply on your behalf. You can find the responsible family court at http://justiz.de/ and professional lawyers at https://anwaltauskunft.de/.

  • At the victim support association WEISSER RING, survivors of violence can receive a voucher for a free initial legal consultation with a lawyer. The association's staff speak German and English and are available for free, anonymous advice daily from 7:00 a.m. to 10:00 p.m. at 116006. With the help of counseling centers, you may also apply for a "victim's lawyer" ("Opferanwalt") in court. If the court approves your application, you will not have to pay legal fees. For more information and assistance on this, search for counseling centers in your area.


1.4 How children can seek help

  • When children are forced to witness or experience domestic violence, they suffer greatly. They feel helpless, yet often try in vain to help their parents. Many children are severely traumatized by domestic violence and need immediate support.

    • Children and adolescents can call the "Nummer gegen Kummer" helpline at 0800 111 0333 from Monday to Saturday (2:00 p.m.–8:00 p.m.). The call is free and can be anonymous upon request. Children can also call 0800 111 0111 or 0800 111 0222 to speak with staff from "Telefonseelsorge," who offer free, anonymous support 24/7. At www.youth-life-line.de, children and adolescents can also seek advice online.

    • Additionally, you may contact your local youth migration service or the responsible youth welfare office at any time. Staff at youth migration services speak multiple languages.


2. [Applying for a "distance order"]

2.1 Introduction

  • Victims may apply for a civil protection order—"distance order"—under the Act on Civil Law Protection against Violence and Harassment, which can require the abuser to:

    • not enter the home (exclusion order)

    • stay a certain distance away from the home

    • stay away from other specified places frequently visited by the victim, such as the workplace or school

    • not contact the victim, including via telecommunications (e.g., phone, text, or internet)

    • not attempt to meet the victim

  • According to Article 1 of the Act on Civil Law Protection against Violence and Harassment, upon application by a person who has suffered harm, threats, or harassment, the court may order the abuser to stay away from the home and from specific places frequently visited by the victim. For child victims, the same applies under Article 1666 of the Civil Code.

  • The Act on Civil Law Protection against Violence and Harassment does not apply if the victim or threatened person was under the parental custody, guardianship, or supervision of the perpetrator at the time of the incident. Instead, child protection laws apply. Under Articles 1666 and 1666a of the Civil Code, family court must take necessary protective measures to prevent danger to the child's best interests. These include the measures listed above.

  • Orders issued under Articles 1666 and 1666a of the Civil Code apply not only to perpetrators with parental custody but also to third-party abusers (e.g., the mother's boyfriend).


2.2 Applicants

  • A "victim or threatened person" may apply for a protection order under the Civil Law Protection from Violence Act. Parents or legal representatives must apply on behalf of children under 18.

  • There is no formal requirement under Section 1666 of the Civil Code to apply for a civil protection order; if legal prerequisites are met, the court may issue such an order at its discretion. The other parent, youth welfare office, the child, or a third party (e.g., a teacher or neighbor) may informally prompt court intervention by reporting the case to the court.


2.3 Preserving Evidence

  • In interim injunction proceedings, the victim must provide evidence of unlawful conduct through a statutory declaration and, if possible, medical documentation. In main proceedings, it is helpful to provide witness statements, police reports, photographs of injuries or property damage, text messages, and personal records detailing the incident.


2.4 Duration of Protection

  • Protection orders are typically issued for a limited period (e.g., six months), depending on the circumstances, but may be extended or issued without time limits. However, restrictions apply if the residence is not solely owned or rented by the victim. For example, if the perpetrator is ordered to stay away from a residence rented solely or jointly by the perpetrator and the victim, the order cannot exceed six months, though it may be extended by another six months if the domestic violence victim cannot find alternative accommodation.


3. [Free Legal Aid]

3.1 Introduction

  • In Germany, there is a distinction between advisory legal aid (Beratungshilfe) and litigation legal aid (Prozesskostenhilfe).

    • Advisory legal aid: According to the Act on Legal Advice and Representation for Low-Income Citizens (Gesetz über Rechtsberatung und Vertretung für Bürger mit geringem Einkommen (Beratungshilfegesetz)), advisory legal aid provides legal counseling and out-of-court representation assistance to those in need.

    • Litigation legal aid: Is a public scheme established by the government to assist those in need in obtaining legal representation by a lawyer during legal proceedings.


3.2 Application Procedure

  • Advisory legal aid (Beratungshilfe): Under Sections 1 and 3 of the Act on Legal Advice and Representation for Low-Income Citizens (Gesetz über Rechtsberatung und Vertretung für Bürger mit geringem Einkommen (Beratungshilfegesetz)), individuals who cannot afford necessary legal costs due to financial hardship, have no other reasonable means of assistance, and are not deliberately abusing the advisory aid system may apply to the local district court for out-of-court advisory aid (either orally or in writing). Advisory aid is provided by lawyers and legal advisors who are members of a bar association.

  • Under Section 3: Applicants must submit a declaration of personal financial circumstances, particularly proof regarding marital status, occupation, assets, income, and liabilities; and must confirm that they have not previously applied for advisory aid for the same case, or that any prior application has already been decided.

  • Litigation legal aid (Prozesskostenhilfe): Applicants may apply to the court during the initiation of legal proceedings and must also submit a declaration of their financial circumstances.

4. [Criminal Prosecution of the Perpetrator]

4.1 Offenses the Perpetrator May Be Charged With

  • Sexual assault/rape: Under Section 177(1) of the German Criminal Code (Strafgesetzbuch), anyone who engages in sexual acts against another person's clear will, causes the other person to engage in sexual acts, or orders the other person to perform sexual acts with a third party or tolerate such acts by a third party, shall be sentenced to imprisonment from six months to five years.

  • Sexual harassment: Under Section 184 of the German Criminal Code (Strafgesetzbuch), anyone who physically touches another person in an obscene manner and thereby harasses them shall be sentenced to imprisonment for up to two years or a monetary fine. In particularly serious cases, the prison term shall be from three months to five years. The offense is generally considered particularly serious if committed by several persons together.

  • Insult: Under Section 185 of the German Criminal Code (Strafgesetzbuch), anyone who commits an insult in public, at a meeting, through dissemination of content (Section 11, Paragraph 3), or by means of physical violence, may be sentenced to imprisonment of up to one year or a fine.

  • Assault: Under Section 223 of the German Criminal Code (Strafgesetzbuch), anyone who inflicts bodily harm or impairs another person's health shall be sentenced to imprisonment for up to five years or a fine. Attempted assault is also punishable.

  • Serious bodily harm : Under Section 226 of the German Criminal Code (Strafgesetzbuch): If bodily harm results in the loss of vision in one or both eyes, hearing, speech, or reproductive capacity, loss or permanent inability to use a major limb, permanent severe disfigurement, debilitation, paralysis, or mental illness or disability, the offender shall be sentenced to imprisonment from one to ten years. If the perpetrator intentionally or deliberately caused one of these consequences, the sentence shall be no less than three years.

  • Stalking : Under Section 238 of the German Criminal Code (Strafgesetzbuch): Anyone who, without permission, repeatedly stalks another person in a manner likely to seriously impair their way of life shall be sentenced to imprisonment for up to three years or a fine. Stalking includes: visiting the vicinity of the person, attempting to contact the person via telecommunications or other communication means or through third parties, misusing the person's personal data, ordering goods or services on their behalf, inducing third parties to contact them, or threatening to harm the person, a relative, or another close person's life, physical safety, health, or freedom.


4.2 How to Report

  • If you are in immediate danger, call 110 to report it. The police will usually ask for your name, address, and the nature of the problem, and officers will be dispatched to protect you (and your children). Staff at the police emergency call center usually speak only German. If you do not speak German, call the "Gewalt gegen Frauen" ("Violence Against Women") organization's help center. Their staff are available around the clock and speak multiple languages, including English, Arabic, French, Persian/Dari, Kurdish (Kurmanji), and Turkish. You can reach their team at 08000 116 016.

  • You may also go to a police station in person to file a complaint and hand over any clothing or other items that may carry traces of the perpetrator so that evidence can be collected. If you need support, you may bring a friend or a lawyer with you. You may also bring an interpreter or ask the police to provide one. After experiencing domestic violence, it is best to have a doctor examine and document your injuries as soon as possible to support your claim. Including photographs with your complaint can greatly assist in processing your case.

  • The police will first collect your personal information, then gather and record your account of the incident or your observations. You will be required to sign to confirm the information.

  • You will be asked to decide whether to file criminal charges. Many domestic violence-related offenses can only be prosecuted upon such a complaint. If you are unsure when filing the complaint, you will be given time to make a decision.

  • If you wish to pursue criminal charges, the police will notify the responsible domestic violence coordination and counseling center. Staff from the coordination and counseling center, women's and children's shelters, and other social counseling centers will provide you with professional personal counseling and guidance on ways to escape the situation. If necessary, staff will also accompany you to court and assist you in applying for a court protection order.

  • After you file a criminal complaint, the police station handling your case will provide you with a file reference number. Be sure not to lose this number, as you will need it to obtain any information about the progress of your case.

5. [Divorce]

5.1 Legal Grounds for Divorce

  • Under Sections 1564–1567 of the German Civil Code (Bürgerliches Gesetzbuch): A spouse may apply to the court for divorce if the couple has lived apart for at least one year and both agree to the divorce; or if they have not lived apart for one year but continuing the marriage would cause the applicant unreasonable hardship.

    • If the couple has lived apart for three years, the marriage is considered irretrievably broken.

    • However, under Section 1568 of the German Civil Code (Bürgerliches Gesetzbuch), if due to special circumstances maintaining the marriage is deemed essential for the best interests of minor children, or if due to exceptional circumstances divorce would impose severe hardship on the defendant who opposes it—even considering the applicant's interests—then the marriage should not be dissolved, even if the marriage has failed.

  • For more information, refer to Sections 1564–1587 of the German Civil Code (Bürgerliches Gesetzbuch), see https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bgb/index.html 


5.2 Child Custody/Child Support

  • Under Section 1570 of the German Civil Code (Bürgerliches Gesetzbuch): A divorced spouse may claim maintenance from the other spouse for at least three years after the birth of a common child. The duration of the maintenance claim may be extended if fairness so requires, taking into account the child's needs and available childcare options.

    • The duration of the maintenance entitlement may be extended if fairness so requires, considering childcare and employment arrangements during the marriage and the duration of the marriage.

  • Under Section 1612(a)(b) of the German Civil Code (Bürgerliches Gesetzbuch): Minor children may claim maintenance from the parent with whom they do not live, calculated as a certain proportion of the parent's minimum subsistence level. The minimum subsistence level depends on the tax-free minimum standard of living for minors and must meet the child's financial needs.

  • For more information, refer to Sections 1589–1711 of the German Civil Code (Bürgerliches Gesetzbuch), see https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bgb/index.html 


5.3 Spousal Maintenance

  • Under Sections 1571–1578 of the German Civil Code (Bürgerliches Gesetzbuch): A divorced spouse may claim maintenance from the other spouse due to old age, physical inability to work, illness, or inability to find suitable employment after divorce. The court will allocate and decide on maintenance based on principles of fairness and need.


  • For more information, refer to Sections 1569–1586b of the German Civil Code (Bürgerliches Gesetzbuch), see https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bgb/index.html 


 NO.4 

[Germany Chapter]How do domestic violence and divorce affect your visa?


  • Generally, a person's opportunity to obtain citizenship, residency, or asylum rights in Germany will not be negatively affected by approaching or seeking help from German state authorities to obtain protection from domestic violence. This is true regardless of whether the spouse is German, a permanent resident of Germany, or neither.

  • However, if the spouse holds residence rights in Germany solely derived from marriage, divorce from the spouse may lead to problems with residency rights. Under specific conditions, residence rights may still continue for a certain period even after divorce. For example, if the couple has legally cohabited for at least three years during the marriage (as stipulated in Section 31(1)(1) of the German Residence Act (Aufenthaltsgesetz)), the spouse has an independent right to reside in Germany for one year after the divorce. Where necessary, the three-year cohabitation requirement may be waived to avoid exceptional hardship. In cases of domestic violence, such hardship is explicitly recognized (§ 31 Abs. 2 Satz 2, 2nd half-sentence, AufenthG).

  • During this one-year period of independent residence status, you may receive social assistance (Sozialhilfe) or unemployment benefit II (Arbeitslosengeld II). After this one-year residence period expires, you may apply for an extension of your residence permit. To obtain the extension, you may need to prove that you have sufficient income to support yourself and your children (if applicable). The amount you need to earn and the type of state support you may continue to receive depend on the number of people you are responsible for. You may also need to meet other conditions, such as being enrolled in a health insurance plan. Therefore, consult your local immigration authority promptly to learn the specific requirements for extending your residence permit.
