Introduction:


On November 25, 2023, the world united in response to the International Day for the Elimination of Violence against Women, (International Day for the Elimination of Violence against Women), among which anti-gender-based violence marches in France and Italy became focal points, demonstrating urgent global concern for women's rights. In France, millions gathered on major city streets, holding banners and chanting, "Women of the world, unite!" In this massive march, families of domestic violence victims led the procession carrying photos of their deceased loved ones, calling out: "We don't want to keep counting the dead anymore." French President Emmanuel Macron voiced support via social media, pledging to extend hotline service hours, establish a digital support platform, and simplify reporting procedures—responding deeply to women's demands.


In Italy, the murder of Giulia Cecchettin by her ex-boyfriend on November 22 sparked nationwide outrage and mourning. In a public speech, Italian President Sergio Mattarella expressed his vision for human society—one inspired by civilizational standards, intolerant of violence against women. This speech triggered profound reflection on the systemic oppression and cultural indifference women face under patriarchal structures.


On this day, the shared pain of France and Italy resonated globally, becoming a significant example in the worldwide struggle for women's equality and rights. This was not merely a protest against isolated incidents of gender-based violence, but a deeper inquiry into women's status and conditions within contemporary social structures, and a profound critique of traditional patriarchal ideology. The marches in France and Italy became society's collective cry for gender equality, inspiring shared reflection and action toward a more just and safe future.

The European Union releases a joint mid-term report on the implementation of the Gender Action Plan III (GAP III), extending the Gender Action Plan's timeline to 2027


Three years after the launch of the EU Gender Action Plan III (GAP III) for 2021–2025, the Joint Mid-Term Report on the Implementation of the EU Gender Action Plan was published on November 21, 2023. The joint mid-term report highlights the policy framework's key achievements and commitments in advancing gender equality and empowering women and girls, reviewing the EU's support and outcomes in promoting gender equality among partner countries and the public, including measures to strengthen protection of women and girls from gender-based violence. To further deepen this impact, the EU has extended the Gender Action Plan's timeline from 2025 to 2027, supporting the progress toward gender equality.


On this, Josep Borrell, High Representative of the EU for Foreign Affairs and Security Policy/Vice-President of the European Commission, stated, "This extension will help increase initiatives and funding for women's and girls' empowerment, serving as a key driver of change." EU Commissioner for International Partnerships Jutta Urpilainen said, "Our goal is to maintain our political engagement and the level and effectiveness of funding for gender equality. Extending implementation to the end of 2027 aligns the timeline with the EU's multiannual financial framework."


The Gender Action Plan makes a significant contribution to the EU's achievement of the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs), is considered a priority across all EU external policies and actions, and serves as a global focus for policy and action. The EU will continue to strengthen gender mainstreaming, increase gender-specific actions and funding, and ensure targeted policy development and results monitoring through gender analysis, data collection, and process reviews.

News


Countries worldwide respond to the International Day for the Elimination of Violence against Women, with leaders from France and Italy pledging action to stop violence against women


On November 25, in response to the International Day for the Elimination of Violence against Women, women's rights organizations and labor unions in France held marches nationwide, strongly demanding that the government allocate more resources to better protect victims of gender-based violence. Thousands of marchers gathered on main streets in major cities including Paris, Lyon, Toulouse, Lille, and Strasbourg, holding banners and chanting, "Women of the world, unite!" The Paris march was led by families of women killed by domestic violence, who carried photos of their loved ones with signs reading: "We don't want to keep counting the dead anymore." As of now, France has recorded 121 femicides this year. The march organizers called on the government to establish 15,000 dedicated housing units as emergency shelters for women experiencing domestic violence.


On the day of the march, French President Emmanuel Macron posted a video statement on social media: "Violence against women is not inevitable." "We must stop this, and we are working to do so." He announced further actions, including extending service hours for domestic violence helplines, establishing a digital support platform, simplifying reporting procedures, increasing the number of dedicated investigators, deploying "serious danger phones" and "emergency danger bracelets," and setting up emergency accommodation sites.


Besides France, large-scale anti-domestic violence marches held across Italy on November 25 also drew significant attention. On November 11, Giulia Cecchettin, a 22-year-old Italian female student at the University of Padua, was brutally murdered by her ex-boyfriend Filippo Turetta. A week later, police discovered Giulia's body, with at least 20 stab wounds to her head and neck. Subsequently, Filippo was arrested near Leipzig, Germany. The murder sparked widespread public outrage, with thousands participating in protests in Rome, Milan, Messina, and other Italian cities, and the Tuscany region declared a regional day of mourning. That day's protests included marches, runs, walks, flash mobs, sit-ins, and free gynecological check-ups, aiming to raise awareness of gender-based violence and patriarchy. On the 25th, Giulia's father, Gino Cecchettin, delivered a speech at the University of Padua, commemorating the International Day for the Elimination of Violence against Women and all victims of violence against women.


On the day of the events, Italian President Sergio Mattarella stated, "The tragic news of Giulia Cecchettin's death has shaken the conscience of the nation. This is a symbolic event, but only the latest in a series." "A human society inspired by standards of civilization cannot accept, cannot tolerate the relentless violence, even murder, against women." Italian Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni also expressed grief and anger over Giulia's murder, stating that the government had increased funding specifically for national anti-violence centers and women's shelters, and planned to introduce stricter new regulations to combat gender-based violence. She pledged to launch new educational initiatives in schools to combat Italy's widespread culture of misogynistic violence. According to data from the Italian Ministry of the Interior, 106 women have been murdered in Italy this year—equivalent to nearly one woman killed every three days—with 55 killed by current or former partners.

News


Australian Universities Accord signs new charter to prevent campus sexual violence,

proposing measures to address sexual harassment and assault in universities


On November 17, 2023, Universities Australia, representing 39 Australian universities, signed the Charter on Sexual Harm, proposing measures to address sexual harassment and assault in universities. These include: publicly reporting the number of sexual harm cases received by institutions each year; clearly informing the public about offices and procedures for reporting sexual violence; strengthening, developing, and enforcing policies against gender-based violence, with sanctions for any violations; and providing support to all individuals reporting sexual harm and to survivors of sexual harm. One week later, state, territory, and federal education ministers met to discuss responses to the crisis of sexual violence in universities, while also considering the appointment of a national student ombudsman.


Professor David Lloyd, Chair of Universities Australia, stated, signatories to the charter are committed to addressing sexual violence as a major societal issue because the number of sexual violence incidents in local communities remains unacceptably high. Professor David Lloyd said: "Sexual harm is abhorrent and has no place in our society, which is why we are committed to taking stronger action." Catriona Jackson, Chief Executive of Universities Australia, said universities are working every day to support individuals and ensure their safety, but more needs to be done. Ms. Jackson said: "We are not standing by in the face of this major social issue, but we also need other organizations to join us. We call on all sectors—government, schools, businesses, workplaces, and the media—to commit to this charter so that we can work together to improve our society. "

News


Armed robbery in New York, suspects impersonate delivery workers

Steal $70,000 worth of belongings from two ethnic Chinese individuals


At around 9:30 p.m. on November 8, a robbery occurred at a residence on 43rd Avenue, Crescent Street, Long Island City, Queens, New York City. Two suspects impersonated delivery workers and used a firearm to rob two ethnic Chinese individuals of belongings worth $70,000, including cash and a Rolex watch.


The victims were a 22-year-old woman and a 25-year-old man. After the suspects lied about being delivery workers and rang the doorbell, they forced their way in the moment the victims opened the door, brandished a gun, taped the victims' mouths, arms, and legs, restrained them, and quickly ransacked the house before fleeing on foot. Fortunately, aside from property loss, both victims were unharmed.


The two suspects are still at large. Police released photos of the suspects on November 15 after investigation. According to the latest CompStat report, the 108th Precinct has reported 230 robberies in 2023 so far, an increase of 61 compared to 169 during the same period last year, representing a 36.1% rise.

News


A Chinese woman in Chiba Prefecture, Japan,

beaten to death by two men, suspects still at large


Around 3 a.m. on November 9, a 33-year-old Chinese woman was beaten in the face by two young men outside a residence in Matsudo City, Chiba Prefecture, and died after being rushed to hospital. The two perpetrators are currently on the run.


Just after 3 a.m., police received a call from a witness in Matsudo City who reported: "I heard a woman screaming; she was being beaten by two men." When police arrived, they found the woman lying unconscious on the ground with severe head and facial injuries. She was taken to hospital but died from hemorrhagic shock. Surveillance footage from the scene shows the two suspects attacked the woman as she returned home and parked her car, striking her violently. According to nearby residents, the woman had two young children, and the Chinese family had moved to the area about five years ago. Investigators said the suspects fled westward after the attack, and a large number of police officers launched a search along the banks of the Edo River.


The incident has sparked widespread discussion among Japanese netizens, who have expressed deep concern about violent crime and personal safety. One user said: "I am a resident of Chiba Prefecture, and I am worried that violent crimes are increasing recently. Due to worsening security, people are deeply concerned about Japan's future. I pray the perpetrators are caught soon." Another said: "Two men beating a woman is extremely cruel." A third commented: "I feel that events once unimaginable in Japan are becoming more frequent. I think soon we won't be able to leave home without self-defense tools."

Asian American groups send letter to Congress,

protesting reauthorization of controversial surveillance law

——Section 702 of the Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Act


Asian Americans Against Warrantless Surveillance (Asian Americans Against Warrantless Surveillance) is a coalition led by Asian Americans Advancing Justice, Asian American Scholar Forum, Chinese for Affirmative Action, and Stop AAPI Hate.


On November 14, the coalition joined over 50 other Asian American and allied organizations in sending a letter to Congress protesting the reauthorization of Section 702 of the Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Act of 1978 Amendments Act of 2008 without comprehensive reform, as it can be used for racial profiling of communities of color and there are concerns it has been used to surveil Asian Americans. In the letter, they urged congressional lawmakers to reject short-term extensions of the law to prevent U.S. intelligence agencies from accessing communications data of non-Americans without a warrant. Gisela Perez Kusakawa, Executive Director of the Asian American Scholar Forum, said government surveillance agencies need proper checks and balances to prevent racial profiling.


Asian American groups are calling for independent legislative review of Section 702 to allow for public debate and amendments. They support the Government Surveillance Reform Act of 2023, which requires warrants for government purchases of private data. In their letter, these Asian American groups emphasized the impact of this surveillance law on Asian communities, such as leading to mistaken targeting, unjust surveillance, and serious harm to their careers, livelihoods, and reputations.

News


Members of the Congressional Asian Pacific American Caucus

urge Biden to take urgent action to reduce visa backlog


On November 15, members of the Congressional Asian Pacific American Caucus sent a letter to President Biden urging urgent measures to address the growing visa backlog, which is severely affecting the Asian American community.


The letter emphasized that immigrants from Asia currently make up more than one-third of the 4 million people waiting in the family immigration backlog, highlighting that the U.S. immigration system is failing to meet demand. Lawmakers led by California Representative Judy Chu and Hawaii Democratic Senator Mazie Hirono urged President Biden to take immediate action, including reallocating unused visa numbers from the past 20 years, especially in family- and employment-based categories. They also called for expanded use of parole for family reunification, allowing more family members awaiting approval to enter the United States.


The letter noted that the majority of Asian Americans are immigrants, and Asian immigrants primarily rely on family reunification visas to come to the U.S. In fiscal year 2022, only 165,642 of the 226,000 available family preference visas were issued. This reflects decades-long structural problems in the U.S. immigration system. The lawmakers stated: "These common-sense reforms will allow long-separated families to reunite, help alleviate ongoing labor shortages, and support individuals pursuing a better life in the United States."
