#Iran Hijab Incident: Materials and Commentary

(1) The Iran Hijab Incident

1. Event Review


On September 13, 2022, Mahsa Amini, a resident of Saqqez city in Iran's western Kurdistan province, was arrested by the "morality police" at a metro station in Tehran while visiting relatives with her brother, for wearing her hijab too loosely, which was deemed non-compliant with dress code regulations. She was then taken to a "re-education center" for women who violated dress rules. About an hour later, when her brother arrived at the detention center to pick her up, he was told she had collapsed and was rushed to hospital. On the 16th, police announced Amini had been declared brain dead. Iranian authorities claimed Amini died of "sudden heart failure." However, Amini's family insisted she had no history of heart disease and that she had visible injuries when admitted to the hospital, suspecting her death resulted from police brutality. A photo circulating online, purportedly showing Amini's brain CT scan, indicated skull fractures and brain hemorrhage. The incident drew widespread attention from media, international organizations, and public figures, triggering bloody protests around the world for over three weeks.

3. Public Opinion

" "Security forces should not use unnecessary or inappropriate force." "

—UN Secretary-General António Guterres

Source: UN Secretary-General António Guterres has been closely following developments, expressing deep concern over reports of the Iranian security forces' "ongoing violent crackdown on hijab protests." Guterres told Iranian President Raisi that human rights, including freedom of expression, peaceful assembly, and association, must be respected. "Security forces should not use unnecessary or inappropriate force, and all parties should exercise maximum restraint to prevent further escalation." He also emphasized the need for an independent body to conduct a swift, fair, and effective investigation into Amini's death.

 

"Authorities must stop targeting, harassing, and detaining women who do not comply with hijab requirements, and should abolish all discriminatory laws mandating hijab."

—Nada Al-Nashif, Acting UN High Commissioner for Human Rights

Source: Nada Al-Nashif, Acting UN High Commissioner for Human Rights, expressed profound shock. She said, "The tragic death of Mahsa Amini and the allegations of torture and abuse she suffered must be swiftly, impartially, and effectively investigated by an independent competent authority, with particular attention to ensuring Amini's family has access to justice and the truth." Al-Nashif noted that Iran's laws mandating women to wear veils remain deeply concerning. In Iran, women appearing in public without a hijab face imprisonment. In recent months, "morality police" have intensified street patrols, verbally and physically harassing women wearing so-called "loose hijabs," even arresting them. The Office of the UN High Commissioner for Human Rights has received numerous verified videos of violence against women, including slapping, beating with batons, and throwing them into police vehicles. Al-Nashif stressed that authorities must stop targeting, harassing, and detaining women who fail to comply with hijab rules, and called for the abolition of all discriminatory laws enforcing mandatory hijab. She condemned reports of unnecessary or excessive use of force by authorities against protesters and urged Iran, as a state party to the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights, to respect people's rights to peaceful freedom of expression, assembly, and association.

 

"Call on Iranian authorities to conduct an independent, impartial, and prompt investigation into Amini's death, release the findings, and hold all perpetrators accountable." —Joint Statement by UN Independent Human Rights Experts

Source: Several UN independent human rights experts issued a joint statement strongly condemning violence against women and Iran's denial of human dignity through its mandatory hijab policy. The statement calls on Iranian authorities to conduct an independent, impartial, and prompt investigation into Amini's death, release the findings, and hold all perpetrators accountable.

 

"UN Women stands with Iranian women, supporting their legitimate demands to protest injustice, free from retaliation, and to freely exercise their bodily autonomy."

—UN Women

Source: UN Women issued a statement on Amini's death, stating that the death of any young person, including women, is an immeasurable tragedy. The circumstances surrounding these events require special attention. The agency noted that while the exact cause and circumstances of Amini's death remain unclear, it is certain that her detention and treatment violated fundamental human rights. This incident also highlights the abuse suffered by women and girls worldwide.

 

"In Iran, there has long been a pattern of impunity for human rights violations, with security forces repeatedly accused of using lethal force resulting in protester deaths."

—Ravina Shamdasani, Spokesperson for the Office of the UN High Commissioner for Human Rights

Source: In response to Iran's internet shutdown during the protests, UN Human Rights Office spokesperson Liz Throssell stated that some leaders had made statements discrediting protesters and used clearly unnecessary and excessive force against them. She emphasized that security forces must never use firearms indiscriminately to disperse gatherings. Firearms may only be used when there is an immediate threat to life or serious injury. She called on Iranian authorities to ensure people's rights are exercised through due process and to release all those arbitrarily deprived of their freedom. She further stated that disruptions to communication services severely affect people's ability to exchange information, conduct economic activities, and access public services, undermining many human rights, especially freedom of expression, and urged authorities to fully restore internet access. She also pointed out that in Iran, there has long been a pattern of impunity for human rights violations, with security forces repeatedly accused of using lethal force resulting in protester deaths, particularly in November 2019, July 2021, and May 2022. The Office of the High Commissioner for Human Rights therefore called on Iranian authorities, as a state party to the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights, to fully respect the rights to freedom of opinion, freedom of expression, peaceful assembly, and association.

 

"Iran deliberately using lethal force to suppress women-led protests"

— Amnesty International, London-based human rights organization

Source: London-based human rights organization Amnesty International stated that the events documented a "widespread pattern of torture" and sexual assault. In a statement released a week earlier, the organization said Iran is deliberately using lethal force to suppress women-led protests. It said it had obtained leaked documents dated September 21 sent to military commanders across provinces, ordering them to "severely confront" protesters. Another leaked document showed that commanders in Mazandaran Province ordered troops to "mercilessly strike any unrest by rioters and counter-revolutionaries, even resulting in death."

(2) Commentary Perspective


It is no coincidence that this incident has caused a global uproar. The growing strength of women worldwide, combined with ongoing protests triggered by Iran's newly enforced "hijab law," has made the Amini hijab incident a direct flashpoint of conflict.

Evidence

Swipe up or down to read

In Iran, the headscarf is no small matter. After the 1979 Islamic Revolution, Iran implemented mandatory dress codes requiring all women to wear headscarves and loose clothing to cover their bodies in public. Iran has a special "morality police" (Gasht-e Ershad), whose duty is to crack down on and punish violations of dress codes and other prohibited behaviors. After President Raisi came to power, he further tightened the "headscarf law." In July and August this year, Raisi signed two decrees requiring women not only to cover their hair with headscarves, but also to cover their necks and shoulders, and imposing penalties on women who post photos without headscarves on social media, including deprivation of certain social rights; female government employees who violate the rules may be dismissed. The implementation of the new decrees sparked public discontent, leading to small-scale protests starting in July that lasted for several weeks, eventually converging with the Amini incident. Last Thursday, a planned interview between CNN senior female journalist Christiane Amanpour and Iranian officials fell through over the headscarf issue. She posted on Twitter that President Raisi had canceled the interview after she refused to wear a headscarf. For a long time, the headscarf issue has been used as a weapon by politicians and men; to some extent, even the term "Iranian women" itself has become a political tool. Therefore, rather than being a question of "yes or no," the headscarf issue is more accurately a question of "can or cannot." In their struggle to transform from pawns into players, Iranian women yearn to escape the original sin of their identity and cast off the politically imposed burdens forced upon them.


Protests surrounding the Iranian headscarf incident have intensified, and the government's indiscriminate use of violence against demonstrators has sparked widespread public outrage across multiple regions, turning the movement from an initial human rights defense into a military-civilian conflict.

According to the Iran-based Justice and Human Rights Organization, demonstrations in support of the movement simultaneously took place in 159 cities worldwide—from Oakland to New York, from Seoul to Zurich—demonstrating global public dissatisfaction with the Iranian headscarf incident and the Iranian government's repressive measures. Iran's intelligence agency revealed that "nine foreigners" from countries including France, Germany, Italy, the Netherlands, and Poland were arrested either "at the scene of the unrest or behind the scenes," along with 256 members of "illegal opposition" groups. The Oslo-based Iran Human Rights organization reported that at least 92 people have been killed in the crackdown, a number that continues to rise.


External forces and neighboring countries have taken advantage of the situation to instigate conflicts and attacks, using the Iranian headscarf incident as a "natural smokescreen" exploited by illegal groups and extremist organizations, further destabilizing Iran and its surrounding regions.

Rishi Sunak elected leader of UK Conservative Party, making Britain's first Asian-origin Prime Minister


On the 24th local time, the leadership election of the UK's ruling Conservative Party concluded. Former Chancellor of the Exchequer Rishi Sunak, as the sole candidate, automatically became the new leader of the Conservative Party and will succeed Elizabeth Truss, who resigned after only 45 days in office, to become the UK Prime Minister. This will also mark the first time in British history that an Asian-origin individual becomes Prime Minister.


Sunak was born in Southampton, UK, in 1980, became a Conservative Party MP in 2015, and was appointed UK Chancellor of the Exchequer on February 13, 2020. On July 5, 2022, Sunak resigned from the government, citing a loss of confidence in then-Prime Minister Boris Johnson's leadership.

Chinese American Hollywood actress Anna May Wong becomes the first Asian face on a U.S. coin


The U.S. Mint announced the release of 25-cent coins bearing the image of Chinese American actress Anna May Wong starting October 23, making Wong the first Chinese American woman to be featured on U.S. currency. Anna May Wong was the first Chinese American Hollywood actress and the first Asian American actress to gain international recognition. Her career spanned 60 films, many of which were from the silent film era.


In 1960, she received a star on the Hollywood Walk of Fame, becoming the first Chinese actress to be honored with a star in Hollywood. From 2022 to 2025, the U.S. Mint's American Women Quarters Program will honor five pioneering women in American history each year. This year's fifth coin features Anna May Wong's portrait. The U.S. Mint expects to produce over 300 million Wong quarters at its facilities in Philadelphia and Denver. Mint Director Ventris C. Gibson described Wong as "a trailblazing advocate who opened doors for Asian American performers in Hollywood, championing greater representation and more multidimensional roles."

Columbia University Chinese American female PhD candidate Lily Ling, the first female music director of the Broadway hit Hamilton, shares her advice for success


Lily Ling is a current doctoral student in the Music and Music Education program at Teachers College, Columbia University. She is the first Asian to conduct Hamilton on Broadway and the first woman to music-direct the show. The Chinese Canadian music director shared that she has been "lucky enough" to turn her passion into her career—a path many people never get to experience.


She is an education advocate and a mentor with the organizations MAESTRA and MUSE, dedicated to providing visibility, advocacy, and support resources for marginalized and underrepresented populations in the musical theater space. Outreach to immigrants, in particular, resonates deeply with her. Ling mentors students from her homeland, China, using her cultural translation skills and fluency in Cantonese and Mandarin to share her advice and experiences. "I feel incredibly fortunate to have such a specific and distinct perspective compared to most people in my industry," she said. "I entered this industry nearly a decade before any social or political upheaval or support... As a woman and an Asian, I feel a responsibility to advocate for change. How do we rebuild the system?" She explained that although she is very introverted, she wants to "share my voice with those who are willing to listen."

Lin's house may have also been occupied by drug dealers, as clear evidence of alcohol, methamphetamine, and other narcotics use was found inside. Now, Lin faces high bills and cleaning costs far beyond her limited financial means, forcing her to live in shelters for an extended period.

Scotland may simplify process for transgender people to change birth certificates, no longer requiring psychiatric diagnosis

The Scottish Parliament passed, by a vote of five to two, measures supporting the simplification of the process for transgender people to change their birth certificates, potentially removing the requirement for a psychiatric diagnosis to change legal gender in the future. It also supports lowering the age at which people can apply for a Gender Recognition Certificate (GRC) from 18 to 16, aligning with Scotland's broader laws on legal capacity, and reducing the required period of living permanently in one's acquired gender from two years to three months. The new rules have triggered protests from demonstrators, including J.K. Rowling, who raised concerns about the safety of single-sex spaces. However, the Scottish government stated that the bill will neither alter nor diminish women's rights, nor will it change or expand the rights of transgender people.
