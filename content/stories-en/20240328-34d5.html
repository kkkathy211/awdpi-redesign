 NO.2 

[Netherlands Chapter]Are you or someone you care about experiencing domestic violence?


1. [Legal definition]

  • According to the Dutch government website: domestic violence refers to violent acts committed by someone within the victim's domestic circle. This includes partners and former partners, immediate family members, other relatives, and family friends. The term "domestic violence" is used when there is a close relationship between the perpetrator and the victim, often involving a power imbalance where the victim is dependent on the perpetrator. Forms of domestic violence can include physical abuse, sexual abuse, or psychological abuse.

    (Government of the Netherlands, https://www.government.nl/topics/domestic-violence/what-is-domestic-violence)


2. [Current situation of domestic violence in the Netherlands]

  • Every year in the Netherlands, 200,000 to 300,000 people suffer from severe or repeated domestic violence.

    (Government of the Netherlands, https://www.government.nl/topics/domestic-violence)


3. [Common types]

Domestic violence typically manifests in the following four forms:

(1) Child abuse;

  • According to a 2010 study by Leiden University and the Netherlands Organization for Applied Scientific Research (TNO), approximately 119,000 children in the Netherlands suffer physical, emotional, or psychological abuse each year.

(2) Elder abuse;

  • Including: physical and psychological violence, neglect or failure to care for the elderly, sexual abuse, and financial exploitation such as unauthorized financial interventions against the will of the elderly.

(3) Honor-based violence, such as honor killings, female genital mutilation (forced removal of female genital organs), and forced marriage;

  • psychological or physical violence inflicted on victims to defend the honor of a family, clan, or ethnic group.

(4) Various forms of abuse between intimate partners or former intimate partners, including psychological abuse and harassment (e.g., stalking, verbal harassment).

  • Physical violence: slapping, hitting, kicking, beating, and other physical assaults;

  • Sexual violence: forced sexual intercourse or other forms of sexual coercion;

  • Psychological (emotional) abuse: insults, humiliation, threats, intimidation, deliberate harm, threats to take away children, etc.;

  • Controlling and dominating behaviors: cutting off the victim's contact with family and friends, monitoring their words and actions, restricting access to financial resources, interfering with the victim's employment, and limiting the victim's access to education or medical services.


(Government of the Netherlands, https://www.government.nl/topics/domestic-violence/what-is-domestic-violence)

1.2 Preserving evidence

  • Call logs, text messages, email correspondence and attachments, messages from communication apps such as WhatsApp/WeChat/Line, social media messages, photo/audio/video records, medical records, official documents and notices from the municipality/police/court, and previous records of seeking help.

  • Please, while ensuring your own safety, make copies of this information and upload them to a cloud storage service accessible only by you (e.g., Google Drive/Baidu Wangpan, etc.).



2. [Restraining orders]

2.1 Introduction

        Domestic exclusion order

  • According to Article 2(1) and Article 9 of the Temporary Restraining Order Act (Wet tijdelijk huisverbod) 2008, the mayor or prosecutor may issue a ten-day restraining order against a person posing a potential threat. If there is a risk of danger or the serious situation continues, the order may, with court approval, be extended for up to four additional weeks..

  • In certain emergencies, if a written temporary restraining order cannot be issued, the order may be issued orally. (Article 2(7) of the above Act)

    (Wet tijdelijk huisverbod 2008, https://wetten.overheid.nl/BWBR0035362/2016-08-01)


2.2 Eligibility criteria

  • Restraining orders may be issued to victims who have suffered repeated harm, such as stalking or intimate partner violence; they may also be issued to victims of a single incident, such as rape survivors.


2.3 Application procedure

  • Applicants can initiate the restraining order application by reporting to the police. After the report, the police will decide whether to notify the assistant public prosecutor, who will then decide whether to initiate the temporary restraining order procedure. Applicants are also required to complete a risk assessment form (RIHG) at the location where the domestic violence was reported, to determine whether the temporary restraining order will ultimately be implemented.


2.4 Legal consequences

  • According to Article 11(1) of the Temporary Restraining Order Act (Wet tijdelijk huisverbod) 2008, anyone who violates the terms of a restraining order may face up to two years in prison or a Category IV fine.


2.5 Legal basis

  • Temporary Restraining Order Act (Wet tijdelijk huisverbod) 2008, https://wetten.overheid.nl/BWBR0035362/2016-08-01 

  • Marieke Remmelink, The use of a Temporary Restratining Order in the Netherlands, p6 https://essay.utwente.nl/64448/1/Scriptie%20M.%20Remmelink%20openbaar.pdf 

  • Dutch government website, https://www.government.nl/topics/domestic-violence/domestic-exclusion-order-and-sentencing-for-domestic-violence 


3. [Shelter]

3.1 Introduction

  • When you leave your home, regardless of whether it is due to domestic violence, whether you have the ability to live independently, or whether you have already received assistance, you may apply to the municipality for shelter accommodation. The municipal committee will promptly announce the review results and develop support measures upon receiving your application.

  • You may also apply to the municipality for a personal budget to receive life services and assistance from third parties.


3.2 Eligibility criteria

  • Protected housing: When applicants are unable to maintain their lives through their own efforts or with help from others in their social network due to psychological or psychosocial issues.

  • Shelter: As long as you have left your original residence, regardless of whether it is due to domestic violence, whether you have the ability to live independently, or whether you have already received assistance, you may apply to the municipality for shelter assistance.


3.3 Application procedure

  • After receiving the application, the municipality will initiate an investigation within six weeks. Before the investigation begins, applicants may inform the municipality about the type of assistance they hope to receive.

    • The investigation includes: the applicant's needs, personal circumstances, and preferences; whether shelter can be provided and whether the applicant's current situation can be improved; Whether it is possible to rely on people around the applicant to meet the applicant's needs; Whether it is necessary to find informal caregivers; Whether social activities can be used to enhance the applicant's independence; The possibility of coordinating services with health insurers, public health, and welfare under the Health Insurance Act.

  • If the applicant wishes, the municipality can provide a personal budget allowing the applicant to obtain assistance from third parties, such as help with housing.


3.4 Legal Basis

  • Article 1, paragraph 2, sub c, and Article 2, paragraph 3 of the Dutch Social Support Act 2015 (Social Support Act); (https://wetten.overheid.nl/BWBR0035362/2022-07-01/0)


4. [Free Legal Aid]

4.1 Introduction

  • Free legal aid refers to free professional legal advice and assistance available when you face rights violations and unlawful acts.


4.2 Eligibility Conditions

  • Victims Support Netherlands provides emotional support, criminal proceedings assistance, and other services.

    • This organization requires signing an agreement with you before providing services; if you fail to comply with the agreement or conflicts of interest arise during the process, the organization may discontinue services.

    • For criminal proceedings assistance, you must contact the organization at least five days before the hearing to allow sufficient preparation time.


4.3 Application Procedure

  • When you need help, you can call 0900-0101 at any time or contact them via email[email protected]to reach out; the organization will provide you with a safe place to receive support.


4.4 Legal Basis

  • See the Victims Support Netherlands official website: https://www.slachtofferhulp.nl/ 


5. [Criminal Prosecution of the Perpetrator]

5.1 Introduction

  • Under the Dutch Criminal Code, criminal liability related to domestic violence can be categorized into physical violence, psychological violence, and sexual violence.


5.2 Possible Charges Against the Perpetrator

  • Assault (Articles 301–301)

    Intentionally harming another person's physical health is equivalent to abuse and constitutes assault, punishable by up to three years' imprisonment or a Category IV fine. If serious bodily harm results, the penalty is up to four years' imprisonment or a Category IV fine. However, mere intent to assault without actual harm is not punishable. Premeditated assault carries the same penalty as regular assault. However, if premeditated assault causes serious bodily harm, the penalty is up to six years' imprisonment or a fine.

  • Intentional Infliction of Serious Bodily Harm (Articles 302–303)

    Intentionally causing serious bodily harm to another person constitutes the crime of serious assault, punishable by up to eight years' imprisonment or a Category V fine. Premeditated serious assault is punishable by up to twelve years' imprisonment or a Category V fine.

  • Negligent Infliction of Serious Bodily Harm (Article 308)

    If serious bodily injury results, or temporary illness or injury causing work absence occurs, the penalty is up to one year's imprisonment or a Category IV fine. If the act was committed in a moment of passion, the penalty is up to two years' imprisonment or a Category IV fine.

  • Stalking (Article 285b)

    Illegally and intentionally invading another person's privacy with the intent to force them to act, refrain from acting, or tolerate an action, or to instill fear in them, constitutes stalking if the circumstances are serious, punishable by up to three years' imprisonment or a Category IV fine.

  • Coercion (Article 284)

    Threatening someone with violence, intimidation, or defamation to force them to act, refrain from acting, or tolerate an action constitutes coercion, punishable by up to two years' imprisonment or a Category IV fine.

  • Threatening Violence (Article 285)

    Threatening to publicly use violence against a person and/or property causing serious harm constitutes the crime of threat, punishable by up to two years' imprisonment or a Category IV fine.

  • Rape (Articles 242–245)

    Anyone who uses violence or threats to force another person into sexual acts commits rape, punishable by up to twelve years' imprisonment or a Category V fine (Article 242). If the perpetrator knows the victim is unconscious or unable to resist, the penalty is up to eight years' imprisonment or a Category V fine (Article 243). If the victim is under 12 years old, the penalty is twelve years' imprisonment or a Category V fine (Article 244). If the victim is at least 12 but under 16 years old, the penalty is up to eight years' imprisonment or a Category V fine (Article 245).

  • Sexual Assault (Articles 246–247)

    Forcing someone into indecent acts is punishable by up to eight years' imprisonment or a Category V fine (Article 246). If the victim is unconscious or unable to resist, or is under 16 years old, the penalty is up to six years' imprisonment or a Category IV fine.


    Legal Basis:

    Dutch Criminal Code (Wetboek van Strafrecht) https://wetten.overheid.nl/BWBR0001854/2021-07-01 


5.3 Reporting the Crime

(1) Calling the Police or Visiting a Police Station

  • If you are in immediate danger due to violence and need urgent help, call 112; the police will intervene immediately. If the situation is not urgent, you can call the general emergency number 0900-8844.

  • Alternatively, you can visit a police station at any time and inform the officers about the violence you have suffered. If you choose to file a formal complaint, the police will ask you to provide evidence (e.g., photos of injuries, medical records, or witness statements). You may show the police copies of evidence proving you have suffered domestic violence, while keeping the originals safe.

6. [Divorce]

6.1 Types of Divorce

  • Three options: full divorce (divorce), legal separation (still married but living apart), and dissolution of marriage after legal separation.

  • The procedures for all three options are the same.


6.2 Grounds/Conditions for Divorce

  • According to Articles 151 and 154 of Book 1 of the Dutch Civil Code (Burgerlijk Wetboek Boek 1), divorce can be requested from the district court when one or both parties believe the marriage has permanently broken down. (See https://wetten.overheid.nl/BWBR0002656/2022-10-01/#Boek1_Titeldeel9)

6.3 Divorce Procedure

(1) If applying for divorce jointly with your partner:

  • A lawyer submits a "joint divorce petition" to the court, along with the divorce agreement, a BRP extract, and a copy of the marriage certificate.

    • If there are children, birth certificates of minors, a parenting plan, and agreements on splitting child support and education costs must also be submitted.

  • No court hearing is required; the judge issues a ruling promptly, and you will receive the divorce judgment. You may also sign a waiver agreement to forgo the right to appeal, allowing you to register a new marriage at the municipality.

  • You must register the divorce at the municipal office where the marriage was registered within six months of receiving the divorce judgment. Completion of registration signifies legal and formal divorce.

(2) If applying unilaterally:

  • Filing the application: The applicant hires a lawyer to file the divorce petition with the court; the other party will receive a copy of the petition.

  • Submission of a defense: The non-applicant may submit a defense statement and hire a lawyer; alternatively, they may use a lawyer to submit a written declaration stating they will not file a defense and will leave the decision to the court.

    • The defense must be submitted within six weeks of receiving the petition copy. If more time is needed, a lawyer can request an extension. If you reside abroad or your address is unknown, you have three months to submit a defense. If you do not respond, the judge will rule on the divorce request, and you will receive the court judgment within a few weeks.

  • Property division agreement: If no prenuptial agreement exists, you may engage a civil-law notary to prepare an asset list and reach a property division agreement with the help of a lawyer or notary. If no agreement is reached, the court will decide on property division. If you wish to handle property division later or in a special manner, you may initiate a separate legal procedure.

  • Court hearing and mediation: In court, both parties have the opportunity to present oral explanations, and divorce hearings are held in private. You may also reach an agreement through mediation; the court will assess whether your case is suitable for mediation.

    • If there are children, the judge will decide whether professionals such as the Child Protection Board or a family guardian should participate in the hearing.

    • If the applicant intends to withdraw the petition, the proceedings end immediately.

  • Appeal: Either party may, through a lawyer, file an appeal with the appellate court within three months of receiving the judgment.

  • Registering the divorce judgment: After receiving the divorce judgment, you must register the divorce at the municipal office where the marriage was registered within six months. Completion of registration signifies legal and formal divorce.

    The divorce process typically takes between six weeks and over a year, depending on how quickly the spouses reach agreement.

    Legal Basis: https://www.rechtspraak.nl/Onderwerpen/Ech 


6.4 Child Custody

  • Both parties must negotiate a range of issues concerning the children. If agreement cannot be reached on specific matters, the judge will make a ruling. (See: https://www.government.nl/topics/divorce-separation-and-ending-a-civil-partnership/arrangements-concerning-the-children/parental-responsibility-for-children-after-split-up)


6.5 Child Support

  • At divorce, both parties must reach a child support agreement, with payments required until the child turns 21. The agreed amount will be reviewed by the court. If the court deems the amount too low, it will adjust it. If the parties cannot agree on the amount, the court will decide, and this decision must be included in the parenting plan within the divorce proceedings. (See: https://www.government.nl/topics/divorce-separation-and-ending-a-civil-partnership/arrangements-concerning-the-children)


6.6 Partner Maintenance

  • If your income is insufficient to support your independent living after divorce, your former partner has a legal obligation to provide financial support. You may negotiate the amount and duration of maintenance with your former partner, with assistance from a lawyer, mediator, or civil-law notary. If no agreement is reached, you may apply for a court ruling. The maintenance amount is updated annually; each November, the government adjusts the amount based on changes in average income, effective from January 1 of the following year. (See: https://www.rechtspraak.nl/Onderwerpen/Alimentatie; https://www.government.nl/topics/divorce-separation-and-ending-a-civil-partnership/agreements-about-maintenance/what-is-partner-maintenance)



 NO.4 

[The Netherlands Section]How do domestic violence and divorce affect your visa?


Under specific conditions, the Immigration and Naturalization Service (Immigratie- en Naturalisatiedienst [IND]) may approve and issue you an independent residence permit:


  • If you have held a residence permit linked to your partner for at least five years: Generally, you are required to pass an integration exam to obtain an independent residence permit. However, as an exception, if you have ended the relationship due to being a victim of domestic violence, you may directly apply for a permanent humanitarian residence permit.

  • If you have held a residence permit linked to your partner for less than five years: If you can prove the existence of domestic violence, you may apply for an independent residence permit (permanent humanitarian residence permit). You will need to provide documents from the police or prosecution authorities, including: a police report regarding the domestic violence; a statement you made to the police about the domestic violence; and an official statement from the public prosecutor or police confirming that a case has been opened. Documents from support services must also be submitted, including: a statement from staff at the shelter or other accommodation where you are staying; medical records from your family doctor or hospital; and statements from other support agencies.

  • If you do not hold a residence permit in the Netherlands: If you can prove the existence of domestic violence and demonstrate that you are unable to escape the abuse in your country of origin, you may apply for a residence permit in the Netherlands (a temporary humanitarian permit). If, after one year, the threat of domestic violence persists and there are other compelling humanitarian grounds, you may apply for a long-term humanitarian permit.


    Legal basis:

    https://ind.nl/en/residence-permits/other-residence-permits/residence-permit-honour-based-or-domestic-violence#the-residence-permit
