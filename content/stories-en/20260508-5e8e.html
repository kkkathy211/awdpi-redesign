[This episode]


It's May, but we haven't finished talking about the news from March yet.


In this episode, we temporarily step away from the roses and arts of Women's Day, and turn our attention to two sets of real data—


"First, the current state of women's protection within the global justice system"

In March 2026, UN Women issued a warning: Women globally hold only 64% of the legal rights men have.

64%. In more than half of all countries, rape laws still do not legally require lack of consent as a defining element. In nearly three-quarters of countries, the law still permits forced marriage of girls. These are not history—they are happening today.


"Second, the real cost of the gender gap in the workplace"

The latest data from the International Labour Organization shows: For every dollar earned by men globally, women earn just 51 cents. In low-income countries, it's only 33 cents. And the "motherhood penalty" has left a clearly visible mark on the career paths of countless women.


We don't want to only report the bad news. There's also one piece of data in the report worth remembering: since 1970, family law reforms have enabled over 600 million women worldwide to gain new economic opportunities. When laws change, lives truly change.


These gaps are not natural laws—they are man-made. And because they are man-made, they can be rebuilt.

The name "Zhiyin" carries three meanings.


"Zhi" (weaving) comes from the traditional Chinese narrative of gender division of labor—"men plow, women weave." We want to acknowledge this history, and also grow new possibilities from it.


"Zhi" also means to weave: our strength is interlaced thread by thread, and every voice is a strand.


"Zhiyin" sounds like the Chinese term "zhiyin" (soulmate)—we hope to hear your voice, and also become your zhiyin.




[About AWDPI]




Zhiyin etVoice is the official podcast of AWDPI.


AWDPI (Asian Women Development Plan International) is an international nonprofit organization dedicated to eliminating discrimination and gender-based violence against Asian women living overseas, and promoting their empowerment and development, so that Asian women abroad can gain greater opportunities in social, economic, cultural, and educational fields, and transform from a marginalized group into a vital force driving social progress.


We operate in multiple countries and regions across Europe, North America, and the Asia-Pacific, and have established National Offices in the UK, Belgium, the USA, Thailand, and Australia. We have built a broad network of partnerships with various sectors, including United Nations system organizations, relevant government departments in different countries, international organizations, multinational corporations, and academic institutions.
