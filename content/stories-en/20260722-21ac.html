Speaking up is often where leadership begins.


For many young women, learning to express their thoughts, building confidence, engaging in public dialogue, and transforming personal experiences into action are crucial steps in their growth. Leadership does not only exist in formal positions or titles; it also appears in a courageous speech, a meaningful conversation, an act of attention to community issues, and the effort to turn ideas into action.


July 25, 9:00 AM (Eastern Time) 9:00 PM, July 25, Beijing Time, AWDPI (Asian Women Development Plan International) will invite Josephine Worlali Adatsi, a gender advocate, youth leadership practitioner, and award-winning public speaker from Ghana, to share insights on how young women can build confidence, speak bravely, engage in public affairs, and create positive change.




Guest Introduction




Josephine Worlali Adatsi She is a gender advocate, youth leadership practitioner, and award-winning public speaker from Ghana. She has long been dedicated to empowering youth, with a particular focus on how women and girls can build confidence, enhance leadership skills, and create positive change within their communities.


She is a Fellow of the UNFPA Ghana Youth Leadership (YoLe) Programme and currently serves as the Women Commissioner Representative for Africa at the Africa Union Students Platform. She is also the founder of the Josephine Worlali Adatsi Initiative, which has already impacted over 300 young women and students through leadership, confidence-building, and public speaking programs.


Her work centers on youth leadership, gender equality, communication and expression, and civic engagement, and she has delivered speeches at numerous conferences and youth summits. She is currently pursuing a Master of Arts in Youth Leadership and Development at the University of Ghana.



We will discuss together



Josephine will draw on her experience in youth leadership, public expression, gender equality advocacy, and community action to explore with us:


How young women can build confidence and speak up courageously;

The relationship between communication, public speaking, and leadership;

How women can move from personal growth to public participation;

How to transform individual voices into community action and social change;

How to create more supportive environments for young women's growth and leadership.

Significance of the Event



Often, young women do not lack ideas.


They simply learn caution too early,

become too accustomed to observing before speaking;

are too often reminded to be "appropriate,"

but rarely encouraged to "make an impact."


When we talk about leadership, we often think of positions, titles, power, and outcomes.


But for many young women, leadership may first begin in subtler ways:


daring to voice a true thought in a discussion,

daring to ask one more question about an issue they care about,

daring to turn personal experiences into public expression,

daring to believe their own voice can become the starting point of change.


This dialogue aims to discuss not just how to become "a better communicator."


But how to build confidence through expression,

recognize one's own power through action,

understand leadership through connection with others,

and gradually find their own place in schools, workplaces, and communities.


We hope this online lecture will help more young women realize:


leadership can begin with one expression,

with one act of participation,

with one choice to step forward.


AWDPI hopes that through this conversation, more young women will recognize the value of their voices, and be encouraged to transform confidence, expression, and care into real action, creating gentle yet firm change within their own communities.
