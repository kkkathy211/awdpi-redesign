"International Day for the Elimination of Violence against Women"

Event Recap

In 1999, the United Nations General Assembly passed a resolution designating November 25 each year as the "International Day for the Elimination of Violence against Women," in memory of the Mirabal sisters, who were assassinated by the Trujillo dictatorship. The original intention was to eliminate domestic violence against women. Twenty-two years after the establishment of this day, however, the shadow of domestic violence has yet to dissipate. It remains hidden within intimate relationships, hovering over the daily lives of countless overseas Chinese women—and has even intensified with the arrival of the COVID-19 pandemic.


As a young nonprofit force dedicated to providing timely protection and assistance to overseas Chinese women facing the threat of domestic violence, the Avoice Overseas Chinese Women Protection Project responded to the United Nations' call and joined this year's November 25 campaign for the "International Day for the Elimination of Violence against Women."

Using our official account as a platform,

we raised our voices,

and received echoes in return.



Public Welfare Classroom

Starting from the "International Day for the Elimination of Violence against Women," we published a series of educational articles.

We confronted the current reality of overseas Chinese women trapped in domestic violence—presenting both stark facts and figures, as well as decades of global outcry and action across continents.



Addressing common challenges faced by overseas Chinese women in situations of domestic violence, we explored pathways forward together.

Beginning with identification and definition, we examined how to break through the darkness and escape violence.



Starting with answering the question "Why can't she leave?", we focused on the hearts and minds of women in darkness, which deserve to shine.



Grounded in the current pandemic situation, we looked ahead to the future together.





Focusing on the dark corners, gathering light,

we passed on kinship across the seas.


Mobile Mailbox

#Hear Her,Heal Her#


We have set up a mobile mailbox, both online and offline, to collect greeting cards filled with love and send them to female compatriots far away in foreign countries.



For every overseas Chinese woman suffering from domestic violence, both her inner world and her future deserve to be bathed in bright sunshine.

Campus and Street Interviews


The fight against domestic violence is never a battle that overseas Chinese women face alone—it is a campaign requiring the participation of the entire society.

We hope you can believe that kindness always outweighs cruelty, and that there are always fellow compatriots waiting for you in the light, ready to walk beside you.


Expert Interview

We interviewed Ms. Li Ying, a veteran lawyer in anti-domestic violence work, who shared case studies and guidance on evidence collection, offering professional advice to help victims seek assistance and protect their legal rights.

Amidst the unpredictability of the pandemic, our protection remains steadfast.

The series of events for this year's "International Day for the Elimination of Violence against Women" has now concluded. Yet awareness-raising and advocacy are only the starting call to eliminate domestic violence—our journey continues.

The "Overseas Chinese Women Protection Project" initiated by Avoice is dedicated to helping overseas Chinese women suffering from domestic violence and improving the living conditions of vulnerable groups of women overseas, building a bridge between women in need and various resources, becoming their solid support and warm shelter..

We emphasize self-empowerment for the target group of women, while also providing multi-dimensional support, including language, psychological, legal, medical, shelter, referral channels, education, economic aid, storytelling and amplifying voices, and community support—expanding the breadth of assistance while creating a virtuous cycle of mutual support.
