To commemorate the 30th anniversary of the Beijing Platform for Action, the National Women's Council of Ireland (NWC) and the Irish Consortium on Gender-Based Violence (ICGBV) hosted an event during the 2025 Commission on the Status of Women (CSW) meetings in New York, titled "Survivors Leading Change" highlighting the critical role of survivors' voices and experiences in designing effective services, systems, and laws to respond to, seek redress for, and address violence against women and girls.


Co-hosted by the Irish Department of Foreign Affairs, the event took place on March 20 from 10:00–11:30 a.m. local time at the Permanent Mission of Ireland to the United Nations (885 Second Avenue, 21st Floor, New York), corresponding to 2:00 p.m. in Ireland.


Despite progress since 1995, misogyny and violence against women and girls remain deeply entrenched in societies, with gender equality regressing in many countries. In Ireland, more than 52%  one in three women will experience sexual violence in their lifetime. Globally, one in three women has experienced physical or sexual violence by an intimate partner or sexual violence by a non-partner. Far-right movements are increasingly threatening democracy and women's rights globally, including in Ireland.



This event brought together perspectives from member states, NGOs, and survivor-led organizations, focusing on survivor-led and survivor-centered approaches in combating domestic, sexual, and gender-based violence.


Justice systems and survivor needs


"The current justice system in Ireland does not effectively support survivors. We urgently need substantial reforms, drawing from survivor communities and international best practices. We must build structures that genuinely engage with survivors and recognize them as leaders and experts by virtue of their lived experience.


These structures must incorporate survivors' real-life experiences at every stage and support them throughout their journey toward justice and healing. We need to believe women when they speak out and ensure they receive appropriate support. Everyone who interacts with victims must receive trauma-informed training, and victims must have access to free legal advice and support. The Irish government has committed in its Third National Strategy on Domestic, Sexual and Gender-Based Violence to listening seriously to survivors' voices, but now this commitment must be translated into concrete action to deliver better outcomes for survivors and society as a whole."


From "survivor-centered" to "survivor-led"


Research presented by ICGBV emphasized the shift from "survivor-centered" to "survivor-led" responsibility, placing survivors as leaders and drivers in decision-making and change processes.


However, funding for gender-based violence prevention and response is currently facing severe contraction. In 2024, only 1.3% of humanitarian funding was specifically allocated to gender-based violence prevention (OCHA FTS data), and between 2016 and 2022, less than 1% of development aid was dedicated to this area (OECD data). Therefore, ensuring sustainable and flexible funding for this life-saving intervention is more critical than ever.


We welcome commitments made by member states at the 69th session of the Commission on the Status of Women (CSW69) to eliminate all forms of violence against women and girls, and to recognize the significant resources and funding required to achieve this goal.


Placing survivors at the heart of change


ICGBV Coordinator Róisín Gallagher said ahead of CSW 2025:

" Meaningful and lasting change to address gender-based violence can only happen when survivors are placed at the center and their lived experiences are recognized as expertise. In Ukraine, survivors have played a pivotal role for the first time during an ongoing conflict, shaping policy and legal frameworks that have secured reparations and legal protection for nearly 400 individuals affected by conflict-related sexual violence. This demonstrates that only through survivors' perspectives can we truly understand the full extent of their suffering, and only they can lead society toward fundamental transformation. "
