Facts about immigrant women and domestic violence


Like all women, immigrant women face a high risk of domestic violence, and due to their immigration status, they often find it even more difficult to escape. Immigrant women are constrained by immigration laws, language barriers, social isolation, and lack of financial resources, often trapping them in abusive relationships.


Although recent federal legislation has created safer pathways for some immigrant women experiencing domestic violence to obtain immigration status, violence remains a significant and pressing issue for immigrant women—and for women across the United States.


01

A recent study in New York City found that 51% foreign-born women were victims in 55% of intimate partner homicides, while 45% of victims were U.S.-born.

02

In one study, 48% 60% of Latina women reported that their partners' violent behaviors had increased after they immigrated to the United States.

03

A survey of Korean immigrant women found that 60%39% of respondents had experienced physical abuse by their husbands.

04

The rate of married immigrant women who have experienced physical or sexual abuse is 59.5%53.4%, higher than that of unmarried immigrant women (49.8%).



Abusers frequently use their partner's immigration status as a tool of control. In such cases, the abuser typically controls their partner's immigration status to force her to remain in the relationship.


Immigrant women are often more likely to be physically abused than U.S. citizens because the cultures they come from may have higher tolerance for domestic violence, or because they have less access to legal and social services compared to U.S. citizens. Additionally, both immigrant abusers and victims may believe that the U.S. legal system's penalties and protections do not apply to them.



Immigrant women who are abused and attempt to flee may struggle to access bilingual shelters or obtain financial assistance or food. Moreover, they are less likely to receive help from qualified interpreters when reporting to courts, police, or 911 operators, or even when seeking information about their rights and the U.S. legal system.
