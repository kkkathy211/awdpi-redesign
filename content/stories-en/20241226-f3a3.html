November 23–24, 2024,

on the eve of the 16 Days of Activism against Gender-Based Violence,

the 2024 AWDPI Asian Women Development Plan International Forum

was successfully held online.


This forum was organized by AWDPI (Asian Women Development Plan International), an organization dedicated to raising awareness of the survival challenges faced by Asian women overseas and continuously supporting Asian women in accessing opportunities for comprehensive development.


At the forum, AWDPI shared an overview of its work with partners who care about Asian women, introducing from a micro perspective the organization's structure and volunteer divisions. "The same moon shines over distant snows; mountains and seas journey together under miles of light." With strong support from partners long committed to the development of Asian women, AWDPI was able to host a series of rich activities during this forum, including keynote speeches, thematic dialogues, paper reviews, and debate competitions. Centered on issues such as eliminating gender-based violence, overseas labor, women's career development, women in AI and technology, and women's platforms, the forum brought together 54 prominent leaders from the public welfare, academic, and professional sectors to jointly explore new pathways for women's growth and amplify voices for gender equality. This forum established a deep exchange and dialogue platform reaching over a thousand participants, attracting more than ten thousand viewers who actively participated, collectively discussing the empowerment and development of Asian women and generating extensive and far-reaching impact.


Opening Remarks


Opening Remarks Speaker: Li Yuling


Ms. Li Yuling, Director of AWDPI (Asian Women Development Plan International) and Founding and Managing Partner of Han Sheng Law Firm, delivered a powerful opening speech that inaugurated the forum.


First, Ms. Li, speaking as a representative of the organizing body, expressed deep gratitude to the attending guests and participants. She also introduced the thematic focuses of previous AWDPI Asian Women Development Plan International Forums, and clarified the close relationship between nonprofit organizations and social development processes, explaining how AWDPI leverages its unique characteristics to exert influence and create social value.


Additionally, Ms. Li provided a brief overview and concrete analysis of the current environment within multiple contexts of gender issues, emphasizing particularly the importance and urgency of combating gender-based violence, calling for increased attention to the issue and advocating for reduced likelihood of violence through prevention, policy formulation, and implementation. She issued an active appeal, hoping that more organizations and individuals would join efforts to promote gender equality and improve responses to gender-based violence.


AWDPI likewise hopes that people will place greater value on women's role in advancing the sustainable development agenda, and is therefore exploring pathways for the development of overseas Chinese women. The organization continuously re-examines women's current circumstances through issues such as economy, technology, environmental protection, and influence, constantly innovating new directions to promote equality.


Ms. Li pointed out that the AWDPI Asian Women Development Plan International Forum is not only a platform for sharing experiences and exchanging ideas, but also a significant opportunity to advocate and drive change. AWDPI hopes to join hands with like-minded individuals across sectors to contribute to a gender-friendly environment, enabling women to step onto broader stages. Finally, she sincerely wished the forum a complete success.

Ta Power — Entering the World of Volunteers, Quiet Yet Radiant: Listening to Their Stories with AWDPI

Zheng Anni, Head of Human Resources at AWDPI (Asian Women Development Plan International) and a finance MBA graduate from China Europe International Business School, provided a detailed introduction to AWDPI's overall organizational structure, outlining its development trajectory and evolutionary changes.

Through vivid stories of volunteers, Zheng Anni illustrated the communication network built to oppose gender-based violence. In assistance cases, many individuals face situations where they have nowhere to turn for help. In a sense, AWDPI is not only a nonprofit organization capable of providing professional legal aid, but also a source of courage and support that empowers individuals to claim their rights, offering the strongest companionship as they endure hardships abroad. Keynote Speech:

How China's Excellent Culture Supports the Shaping of Modern Women's Identities


Keynote Speaker: Zhang Xiaoran


Ms. Zhang Xiaoran, Director of AWDPI (Asian Women Development Plan International) and Secretary-General of the Chengdu Disabled Persons Welfare Foundation, analyzed from the perspective of China's excellent traditional culture, explaining its positive significance and role in shaping modern women's identities.


In today's era, with relatively equal access to education and employment opportunities, expectations—both societal and personal—regarding women's roles are gradually transforming. An increasing number of women now have broader career choices. Against this backdrop, their self-development inevitably faces multiple dilemmas and pressures. At such a time, traditional Chinese culture may offer some new perspectives. Ms. Zhang cited the examples of Hua Mulan and Li Qingzhao to illustrate how qualities such as resilience, intelligence, and righteousness found in traditional culture hold strong contemporary relevance for shaping modern women's spirits. The positive aspects of traditional culture can provide women with support, promote their cognitive and personal growth, and help them define their life direction and assume social responsibilities. "Reading classics to understand the world; reviewing texts to reflect on oneself." Chinese culture, with its long history, continues to nourish modern society through the wisdom of Confucianism, Buddhism, and Daoism, as well as folk customs, offering unique and diverse pathways for individual development. It enables people to clearly understand themselves and become aware of their inner world, rather than seeking validation externally—providing an Eastern perspective to address modern anxieties and challenges. As the global promotion of sustainable development goals continues, Ms. Zhang mentioned in her speech that today's women are living in a relatively favorable era. National policies and laws are increasingly emphasizing gender equity. Compared to the past, explicit trade-offs between women's careers and family life have significantly decreased, and social systems now offer corresponding support. People are beginning to realize that women's development is intertwined with the development of all aspects of society. Ms. Zhang believes that, with the support of China's excellent traditional culture, more and more women will shine brightly, and in turn, they will also contribute to the evolution of traditional culture itself.

Keynote Speech:

From the 1995 World Conference on Women to Today: How Far Have We Come in Combating Domestic Violence? Building and Practicing Support Systems for Survivors


Keynote Speaker: Wang Yanping


Ms. Wang Yanping, Director of the Asian Women Development Plan International, renowned expert on domestic violence, and senior social worker, began her speech by reflecting on the World Conference on Women, looking back on the journey against domestic violence from today's vantage point.


The World Conference on Women undoubtedly advanced the issue of combating domestic violence. Ms. Wang used anti-domestic violence work in Shaanxi Province as a starting point, sharing her years of professional experience through personal stories. In her social work practice, Ms. Wang and her colleagues experimented with frameworks from various disciplines, but ultimately decided to ground their work in social work theory and methodology. After years of effort, the Anti-Domestic Violence Law was officially implemented, providing legal basis and legitimacy for nonprofit organizations and social workers. Ms. Wang outlined the multiple obstacles encountered in handling such cases, discussing how nonprofit organizations relate to and form an experiential system through engagement at the societal level (building a supportive environment), the individual level (quality education and awareness), and the legislative level (protective support). She emphasized that gender awareness must be the key lever in addressing domestic violence. Many social attitudes are underlying causes of domestic violence, and public authorities often refrain from investigating or prosecuting perpetrators due to entrenched local customs. At the same time, victims may face social pressure, trapping them within abusive marriages. Therefore, combating domestic violence must be a multi-directional, comprehensive, and interdepartmental effort. The most urgent priorities are stopping violence and responding promptly to calls for help. Equally important is strengthening education against violence and the severity of punishment, while eliminating outdated beliefs. Both are indispensable and essential components of maintaining a supportive environment.

Keynote Speech:

The Invisible Mask of Domestic Violence: Seeing the Truth of Violence and Achieving Self-Redemption, Rejecting Tamed Love


Keynote Speaker: Lawyer Renren


As a frontline legal professional, renowned marriage and family lawyer Ren Ren, founder of the self-media brand "Ren Ren Bu Xu Ci Xing," delivered a keynote speech on the topic of psychological violence.


Combining professional theory with real-life cases, she carefully analyzed why women find it difficult to escape abusive environments, and how servility and Stockholm syndrome become barriers preventing women from breaking free from violence. In Attorney Ren's view, psychological violence and emotional manipulation are characterized by concealment, longevity, and far-reaching impact. Abusers use moral coercion to gradually undermine the victim's sense of self-worth, trapping them in a vortex of violence from which it is hard to escape. Some victims may even develop deep, symbiotic emotional ties with their abusers out of a desperate need for social connection. Attorney Ren pointed out in her speech that abusers succeed because people are heavily constrained and disciplined by societal gender norms. Patriarchy imposes a heavy burden of otherness on women, making it difficult for them to break free from the prescribed image, and even harder to develop identification with or trust in themselves or other women. As a result, completely disentangling from a painful intimate relationship is extremely difficult. And when they attempt to escape, society may once again leave them isolated and helpless. Society should provide women with a supportive living environment, freeing them from daily constraints and giving them sufficient space to grow. But until such a women-friendly society truly arrives, the way forward for women lies in their own awakening—building subjectivity, bravely speaking out against injustice, and living their lives brightly and authentically.

Keynote Speech:

Challenges and Empowerment of Asian Women Workers


Keynote Speaker: Wang Lin


Wang Lin, China Director at BSR and expert in labor issues within China's supply chains, has long focused on women workers. In the context of economic globalization, how to empower women and provide them with adequate support within relevant supply chains has become a direction that society must discuss, promote, and innovate.


Ms. Wang shared her research and experience. She pointed out that women in the lower and middle tiers still largely work in manufacturing and service industries. Long-term mobility prevents their rights from being sufficiently protected, and due to the difficulties of migrant work, they must constantly balance career and family responsibilities, such as breastfeeding. She also noted that women's work brings crucial impacts to families and even entire communities. Long-standing gender-unfriendly environments make it difficult for these female workers to defend their rights. The situation is even more difficult for Asian women working abroad, who face not only structural discrimination based on gender and race, but also barriers in language and technology, especially for lower- and middle-tier women. Nonprofits or enterprises can first begin by respecting them, engaging in sufficient communication, providing a relatively supportive environment, and offering opportunities to showcase their potential—thus stimulating their internal motivation. Ms. Wang specifically mentioned that while training female workers, efforts should also be made to involve male employees in dialogue and mutual exchange. She shared her project experiences in this area. In Ms. Wang's view, the long-standing sense of unworthiness imposes a heavy psychological burden on these women workers, so external support is needed to help them rebuild—from personal financial management to life goals and beliefs. More importantly, we must see and understand the underlying logic of empowering women.

Keynote Speech:

From Garment Factories to Nail Salons: Livelihood Transitions of Asian Women Workers in North America


Keynote Speaker: Xu Hui


Dr. Xu Hui, a sociology PhD from the University of Jena, Germany, and founder of the China-Germany Center for Skills and Social Development, spoke on the issue of Asian women workers. Having worked in overseas communities, Mr. Xu encountered many Asian women whose work transitions sparked his interest.


He began by language, market competitiveness, working environment, salary and benefits, and other aspects He outlined the general trajectory of labor movements and later touched upon various issues such as ethnic fragmentation and internal structural discrimination, offering a brief analysis of their underlying logic. Because Chinese women workers are often at the bottom of the social hierarchy, they are frequently bullied not only by other ethnic groups but also oppressed by other Asian communities, with wage theft and physical punishment being common tactics. In his research, Mr. Xu observed that in industries such as nail salons or factories, due to long-term working conditions, Asian women are more prone to illness, and their children also face health risks. Facing loss of voice and rights violations, they often lack the capacity or time to resist, and some workers are even unaware of the invisible discrimination embedded in the system. In his view, while narratives of successful high-achieving women are increasingly common today, he believes grassroots voices must not be ignored. These issues must be addressed and answered by someone. He hopes his research can offer new perspectives on the study of Asian women workers and help more people see and understand the grassroots struggles of Asian women overseas. Keynote Speech:

Economic Empowerment of Asian Women

Keynote Speaker: Zhang Ning


Zhang Ning, founder and CEO of Flatfee Xiaofei Network and founding partner of the U.S. law firm Cross-border Counselor LLP, has worked abroad for many years. In her speech, she shared her observations on the economic empowerment of Asian women in the U.S. workplace.


According to the latest data, Asian women have surpassed white men in income for the first time. However, Ms. Zhang sees the reality:


this is merely due to the high average income among Asians; in terms of voice and influence, Asian women still face a disadvantaged position. In many companies, Asian women are often limited to staff-level roles rather than leadership positions. Even when qualified, most can only rise to mid-level management. Ms. Zhang presented a statistic: female-led venture capital (VC) firms make up only 5.6% of all VC firms in the U.S., and within that 5.6%, 66% are led by white women. Asian women, therefore, occupy only 0.8% of VC leadership roles. This shows that Asian women often work several times harder than white men, yet the so-called higher income is merely a minimal compensation for their excessive labor. After years in the workplace, she realized that women's own mindset is a crucial factor. Asian women should abandon the attitude of passively maintaining relationships and bravely speak up for their rights. Only when people genuinely change their thinking and improve the environment from within can Asian women achieve true empowerment. Keynote Speech:



08

Political Empowerment of Asian Women

Keynote Speaker: Sun Yi


Ms. Sun Yi, board member of AWDPI Asian Women Development Plan International, the first Asian Pacific Islander Vice Chair of the Pennsylvania League of Women Voters, and former Director of the Office of Asian Pacific American Affairs under the Pennsylvania Governor, delivered a speech on political rights for Asian women.


She briefly summarized the status and development of Asian communities, while also listing the opportunities, difficulties, and challenges they face. In the U.S., the Asian population is growing rapidly, but during community interactions, the multilingual and multicultural nature creates communication barriers. Smaller Asian ethnic groups often lack detailed classification and are lumped into broad categories. Women in the U.S. gained voting rights relatively late, and Asian women even later. Only through the relentless efforts of countless women did Asian women secure this fundamental right. Ms. Sun cited pioneers such as Mary Choy and Binghua Li, whose stories reflect the real conditions of Asian women. To some extent, due to historical factors, Asian communities do not receive the same treatment as Black communities, and some situations are even worse. Asians often face awkward situations—unable to enroll in school or find jobs—due to a lack of suitable community access points, and women face even greater hardship. Ms. Sun analyzed that Indian and Japanese communities have higher levels of political and democratic participation. Yet in collective struggles by Asian women, many Chinese women can also be seen—women who not only successfully gained voting rights but also brought courage and the right to speak to today's women, becoming role models and exemplars for Chinese women.

International Perspectives on Rural Development: Women's Practices and Impact


This dialogue was moderated by Yang Ling, Deputy Secretary-General of Xi'an Rural Development Public Welfare Foundation and initiator of the "Her Power in the Countryside" project, and featured guests Shi Yan, Co-Chair of Urgenci (International Network for Sustainable Agriculture and Food Systems) and recipient of the 15th "Cobb Award for the Common Good," the highest honor in the fields of ecological philosophy and ecological civilization; Sun Junli, Chair of Laiyang Ifuli Ecological Planting Professional Cooperative and deputy to the Yantai Municipal People's Congress; and Xu Fei, founder of Lufei Premium, Hexiangxi Agriculture, and Fengyingtang Bio, and council member of Xiamen University Young Entrepreneurs Alumni Association.

Yang Ling

Shi Yan


Sun Junli

Xu Fei


This forum focused on the important role of women in rural revitalization and the potential of China's rural economy and culture in the international market. In rural industries, women make up a high proportion and play a significant role. They demonstrate high efficiency and strong execution capabilities in practical operations, which is important for improving household income and promoting social equity. In addition, women possess qualities such as diligence, perseverance, and strong approachability, and their unique perspectives often attract attention and support. Afterwards, the guests discussed the internationalization of rural economic and cultural development. From a communication perspective, they emphasized the need to explore the essence of China's agricultural culture and showcase the unique values of Chinese farmers. From a product perspective, they discussed how rural revitalization projects can effectively connect with international markets, proposing measures such as showcasing high-quality samples, ensuring efficient fulfillment, adopting advanced packaging technologies, and international promotion to build foreign customers' confidence. They also explored integrating intangible cultural heritage with modern markets, selling agricultural creative products through e-commerce platforms and agents to expand rural revitalization projects into international markets. Xu Fei also shared specific strategies, including understanding market demand, selecting suitable products, focusing on infrastructure development, return on investment, and innovation. Finally, the guests advised women who wish to return to rural areas for entrepreneurship to choose industries based on their own conditions and to sincerely communicate with experienced individuals. At the same time, they called on society to provide more support and attention to inspire women's enthusiasm for returning to rural areas to start businesses..





(3) Debate Performance




In this debate performance, the debaters engaged in an in-depth discussion on "In today's context, does 'women first' contribute to gender equality?" . The affirmative team consisted of Chen Jiajun, cooperation and development advisor at AWDPI Asian Women Development Plan International and co-founder of Future Light Family Happiness Office, and member of the Guangzhou Yuexiu District Committee of the Chinese People's Political Consultative Conference; Su Xizhi, co-founder of the Youth Sustainable Development Debate Conference and Global Shaper at the World Economic Forum Davos; and Zeng Zixiang, Best Debater of the Chinese Debate World Cup Invitational Tournament and Master's graduate in Enterprise Development from National University of Singapore. The opposing team included Chang Aizi, PhD in Communication and Media Studies from Monash University and Best Debater of the Zhonghua Cup Debate Competition; Chen Yuqian, former captain and coach of Sun Yat-sen University Debate Team and former content training mentor for iQiyi's variety show *I Can I BB*; and Zhang Xinxin, council member of Shaanxi Women's Theory, Marriage and Family Research Association and social gender education and entrepreneurship mentor for Shaanxi universities.


Affirmative Team


Chen Jiajun

Su Xizhi

Zeng Zixiang


The affirmative side argued that "women first" is an expression of gender equality , helping to address discrimination faced by women and promote gender equality. The affirmative side emphasizes that female priority is a compensatory measure targeting structural gender inequality, proposing that integrating the value of women first into equity policies can directly acknowledge and resolve the discrimination women face. They argue that women-first policies aim to meet core demands of women and serve as compensatory measures to narrow the gender gap and elevate women's status. The affirmative side emphasizes, true equality should not ignore gender differences or the current vulnerabilities of women as a disadvantaged group, but should instead promote the implementation of women-first measures based on a genuine understanding of women's real-life circumstances.



The opposing side


Chang Aizi

Chen Yuqian

Zhang Xinxin


argues that women-first policies may reinforce gender stereotypes and hinder the true realization of gender equality. They point out that women-first approaches only treat symptoms, not root causes, easily creating new inequalities and misunderstandings, and may be misused, leading to gender antagonism and skepticism toward the broader concept of gender equity. The opposition questions whether the "women first" concept proposed by the affirmative side might be misunderstood as merely a slogan, overlooking its practical application in real life. They emphasize that many slogans and movements initiated spontaneously by women have already effectively addressed gender inequality, making it unnecessary to rely on a "women first" notion that may imply female dependency. The opposition also argues that true women-first action should be reflected in concrete practices and policy implementation, such as equal pay for equal work and workplace protections for women, rather than remaining merely symbolic or rhetorical.


In this debate, both teams shared the ultimate goal of achieving gender equality, engaging in intense discussion over the paths to reach it. The vigorous exchange between the debaters not only demonstrated their deep reflection on gender equality but also provided the audience with opportunities for further critical thinking on the topic.





(4) Academic Paper Review Session




This forum featured a special academic paper review session, designed to provide more opportunities for sharing research findings and to inspire public engagement in gender-related research. Hosted by Qian Siyu, the AWDPI forum's paper review session attracted 16 submissions from both domestic and international scholars. After rigorous discussion and evaluation, four outstanding papers were selected for review. The session invited four renowned scholars as reviewers, representing the fields of sociology, economics, journalism and communication, and social work.


Pei Yuxin

Wang Yong


Wang Chenyao

Zhao Fang




1



The first presented paper was "Taking disaster social capital aboard A qualitative study young female migrant workers in Denmark" a qualitative study on young Filipina female workers in Denmark. Using qualitative methods, the study analyzed the decision-making processes of young Filipina women migrating to Denmark, finding that they developed unique forms of social capital after experiencing natural disasters. The research emphasized the importance of "bonding" social capital in collective action and internal trust during disasters, as well as the problems that may arise from a lack of "bridging" social capital. Professor Zhao Fang, Chair of the Department of Social Work at the School of Social Development and Public Policy, Fudan University, and Director of the Fudan-Dongfen Research Center for Child Protection, provided the first review. She praised the originality of the topic while pointing out issues and offering suggestions regarding conceptual definitions, capital transformation, research methodology, and future research directions. Professor Pei Yuxin, Associate Professor at the School of Sociology and Anthropology, Sun Yat-sen University, and a Fulbright Scholar, raised several suggestions and questions in her review, focusing on research methodology, transparency in the research process, the application of grounded theory, and the process of identifying theoretical frameworks and specific research approaches.



2



The second presented paper was "A Review of Gender Inequality in Time Use in China: Historical Context, Current Challenges, and Future" a literature review exploring the history, current status, and future directions of time use under the context of gender inequality in China. This paper examined the relationship between unequal time use and behavioral inequality, pointing out that women's time investment in unpaid labor limits their economic participation. By analyzing data from China's Time Use Survey, the study revealed the heavy burden of household labor borne by women and discussed the impact of reform and opening-up on gender roles and time allocation. The research also proposed future directions, emphasizing the need to consider the combined influence of political, cultural, and economic conditions.


Professor Wang Yong, Academic Vice Dean of the Institute of New Structural Economics at Peking University and Secretary-General of the Research Alliance for New Structural Economics, shared his views from an economic perspective, affirming the importance of studying issues specific to China's national conditions and stressing the need to build theoretical frameworks based on China's realities rather than simply adapting Western theories. He analyzed how technological advancement affects gender comparative advantages, emphasizing that research on gender inequality must consider social burdens, cultural factors, and differences in gender roles across life stages, and discussed the potential applications of time-use data in studying socioeconomic issues such as gender equality, household production, and population aging. Professor Pei Yuxin again affirmed the significance of the topic, further exploring the distribution of gender roles in parenting and family life, while noting that after incorporating a sociological perspective, researchers need to further identify theoretical frameworks and specific research methods.



3



The fourth presented paper was "A Study on the Phenomenon of Gender Discursive Hegemony in the Public Sphere from a Feminist Perspective", which explored the phenomenon of gender discursive hegemony and analyzed how the internet and new media blur the boundaries between public and private spheres, enabling a pluralistic public sphere from a feminist perspective, and discussed issues such as wage gaps, lack of political representation, and gender stereotypes, analyzing the impact of gender discursive hegemony on women's status and calling for continued advancement of gender equality. In her review, Professor Zhao Fang highly praised the paper's structure and logical expression, but pointed out the lack of empirical research and clear methodology, insufficient exploration of the impact of new technologies on gender hegemony, and underdeveloped discussion on gender constructivism and gender collaboration. She recommended that the author conduct more empirical research in the future.


Professor Wang Chenyao, Professor at the School of Journalism and Communication, Nanjing University, and Director of the Journalism Innovation Lab and the Global Public Communication Research Center, noted in her discussion that although the paper explored a feminist perspective, it fell short in structure and theoretical contribution. She suggested that future research pay closer attention to how news media reflect and shape gender roles and social phenomena, encouraging young scholars to deeply explore academic paths that integrate practical experience with theoretical analysis.



4



The fourth paper presented is "Identity Imagination and Identification: A Study of Ritual Communication in Cosmetic Brands' Advertisements in the Era of 'She-Economy'" which attempts to approach from a female perspective, exploring the semiotic interaction between women's images and advertising, using case analysis and multimodal discourse analysis to examine advertising strategies during women's holidays and the limitations and future development of women's representations in brand advertising. Professor Wang Yong supplemented the brand advertising research from an economic perspective. Professor Wang Chenyang commended the paper's clear logic and suggested that the theoretical perspective should be more precise, with a "slimming down" of methods and theories to focus on more specific and relevant theoretical frameworks—for example, when applying semiotic theory, the analysis could be approached through metaphor. He also expressed hope that the author would conduct deeper research in this area in the future.




Conclusion




This year's forum reached new heights in scale, format, topics, and academic atmosphere, bringing together experts and scholars from various fields who delivered insightful presentations and shared valuable experiences, deeply exploring the challenges and opportunities women face in multiple domains of modern society, and collectively witnessing an intellectual feast.


We believe that in the process of building a violence-free future, every woman possesses immense potential. The power of "her" is rising and will undoubtedly shape a new era in response to the call of the times.


The closing of the forum is not the end. In the future, AWDPI Asian Women Development Plan International will work closely with individuals, institutions, organizations, enterprises, and government departments that care about the development and empowerment of Asian women, continuously advancing the cause of gender equality and women's empowerment, and jointly striving to build a more equal and inclusive future.
