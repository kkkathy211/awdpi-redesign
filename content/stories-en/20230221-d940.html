One

Personal Profile



Yang Qun alumnus of Queens' College, University of Cambridge, founded the UK-based biotech company Biorbyt in 2010, formerly served as Executive Director of the Centre for Women's Leadership at Cambridge Judge Business School, and invited several outstanding Chinese women to speak at Cambridge Judge Business School. She co-organized the Women's Sustainable Development Conference with the China Women's Development Foundation and Cambridge Judge Business School.


As a mother of two and a successful female entrepreneur, she has lived and worked in the UK for 20 years and deeply understands that Chinese women in the UK need channels to speak up and increase their social influence. In 2020, she initiated the proposal to establish the UK Chinese Women's Coalition and was elected as its first Chairperson.


This interview focuses on the challenges faced by Chinese women in overseas work and life, ranging from how to respond to workplace discrimination and leverage technology, to how women can identify and deal with domestic violence, maintain their sense of self within family life, and finally, Dr. Yang offers suggestions on the development direction and methods for overseas Chinese women's organizations.


Two

Workplace Discrimination: How to Respond?


Reflecting on her own career experiences in the UK, Dr. Yang Qun sighed, "Chinese women really have to work many times harder, competing under unfair conditions."


When overseas Chinese women face "stereotypes" or varying degrees of "discrimination" in the workplace, Dr. Yang believes they should first think independently, avoid conflict in certain situations as much as possible, and minimize harm to themselves. At the same time, they should unite, communicate more, so that stereotypes can be challenged. When necessary, they should seek legal means to protect their rights.


Dr. Yang suggests that Chinese women can respond to workplace discrimination in the following ways:


1. Reject the victim mentality and avoid assuming upfront that one is being discriminated against. The other party may not yet realize that their words or actions constitute discrimination; sometimes the unfairness we perceive may stem from various subjective and objective factors.


2.  Communicate with employers or supervisors to find solutions and reduce discrimination against women through various means.. For example, working mothers can reduce their burden through job sharing or applying for remote work, without having to choose between family and career.


3. Join collective organizations to drive change through group strength. It is extremely difficult to combat prejudice as an individual; if everyone works toward the same goal and speaks up together, collective power can bring about greater change.


As a senior figure who has lived and worked in the UK for 20 years, Dr. Yang encourages overseas Chinese women to step beyond the limitations of the Chinese community and maintain an open mindset to integrate into local circles:


1. Shed the "outsider" mentality and integrate into local life as early and as well as possible. A primary issue for many Chinese women is a lack of belonging—they subconsciously identify themselves as outsiders. With this mindset, it is difficult to truly integrate into local life.


2. Build confidence in language expression and learn to understand and respect local history and culture.. Even if one lacks confidence in English proficiency, one should try to express oneself in English as a sign of respect. Understanding British culture and history enables more common ground with locals.


3. Avoid being confined by one's original lifestyle; try different ways of living, eating habits, and more.. Once one adopts an open mindset and steps out of the comfort zone of one's original culture, one will discover that life offers many more diverse choices.


Three

Future Technology: What Possibilities Exist?


As founder of the UK biotech company Biorbyt, Dr. Yang Qun believes the power brought by technology offers women more possibilities.


She says: "In primitive society, it was extremely difficult for women to go out hunting and bring back a lion or tiger, which led to the division of labor where men worked outside and women inside the home. But in modern society, with technology, women at least do not have a disadvantage. In fields like computing, women may have greater focus and patience than men, so I encourage more women to try and understand modern technology, because technology can empower them."


At the same time, Dr. Yang particularly points out, the tech industry can also protect women's rights through specific approaches.


1. During scientific research, especially in product development stages, companies or technology developers should ensure the completeness of female samples. Taking drug development as an example, human trials often use male subjects rather than female ones, so data on how medications affect women is sometimes lacking. If female samples are inherently missing from the data, decisions may disadvantage women.


2. Tech companies can pay more attention to whether data collection has flaws and whether it includes diverse user groups. For instance, in drug development, due to concerns that experiments may affect women's fertility, post-menopausal women are often chosen as subjects. While this is not intentional discrimination, if tech companies pay more attention to whether data collection covers all user groups, it would also be a way of protecting women's rights.


Four

Domestic Violence: How to Escape?


Newly arrived and unfamiliar with the environment, overseas Chinese women often lack someone to confide in or emotionally release their feelings. Their avenues for choosing partners are relatively limited, and marriages are often entered into hastily, with insufficient understanding of their partners. These factors make it difficult for Chinese women to promptly recognize problems in marriage and family relationships, or to know how to seek help once problems are identified. In addition, cultural differences and conflicting values between spouses can easily complicate simple issues and even escalate to extreme violence.


The forms of domestic violence that overseas women may experience are diverse—not only physical violence, but also emotional coldness, economic control and lack of support, or even more hidden forms of violence, making it difficult for some women to realize they are already in an unhealthy relationship.


In the painful reality that "domestic violence is either zero times or countless times," how can overseas Chinese women, who are often isolated and vulnerable, determine whether they need to seek help and break free from their predicament? Dr. Yang offers us some guidance:


1. Detach yourself and analyze the situation from a "third-person perspective"


If experiencing domestic violence, Dr. Yang hopes women can "try to detach themselves and view the entire situation from a third-person perspective, setting rules and boundaries for the other person. If, from this third-person view, the other person has personality flaws, drug or alcohol problems, then stop making excuses for them."


2. Make assessments based on the situation and seek organizational help when necessary


When overseas women suffer severe domestic violence, their top priority should be to seek help from the police or organizations to obtain more information and support. At the same time, they should assess the situation—if serious physical harm has already occurred, they must not continue to endure or wait, but must seek help from relevant groups and organizations.


3. Strive for both economic and emotional independence, and learn to leverage external support


Overseas Chinese women are more susceptible to various external constraints, such as visa factors, economic factors, psychological factors, and emotional factors. Striving to maintain economic and psychological independence, rather than being in a state of complete dependence on the other party, can better help oneself break free from the predicament. One can also seek more external support to understand which organizations can help oneself break free from the predicament.


Additionally, if unfortunately facing the threat of domestic violence, it is recommended to try the following steps:


1. Join organizations like the UK Chinese Women's Coalition to gain more information by connecting with other Chinese women;


2. If no similar support groups are available nearby, visit websites or local women's organizations to gather information;


3. If domestic violence has indeed occurred, immediately contact the police or relevant organizations to report and understand one's situation.


Five

Women's Alliances: Mutual Support and Development


Dr. Yang's idea of dedicating herself to Chinese women's causes originated in 2019 and was realized in 2020 during the pandemic. In 2020, many Chinese women and families were isolating at home, often worrying about "what to do if I get sick" or "how to seek help if infected with COVID-19." To meet the mutual aid needs of the Chinese community, Dr. Yang initiated the UK Chinese Women's Coalition.


Contact and Join: UK Chinese Women's Coalition (UKCWC)


In its early days, the main activities of the UK Chinese Women's Coalition involved online video chats where members shared daily life updates and provided emotional support during the pandemic. The internal connections within the coalition are strong, offering many members opportunities for information exchange, making friends, and securing internships, while also helping newcomers to the UK better integrate into local life. Regarding the issue of domestic violence that Chinese women may face, Dr. Yang frankly admits, while the UK Chinese Women's Coalition is not an expert in anti-domestic violence, it can serve as a "platform for sharing information and resources."


Besides the UK Chinese Women's Coalition, there are other Chinese women's organizations and communities in the UK that protect the rights of Chinese women. Mutual support and connection among these organizations can maximize their impact. So, how can connections among Chinese women's organizations be strengthened?


Dr. Yang proposes three ideas:


1. Each organization should clearly define its own focus, as no single organization can cover all areas comprehensively.


2. Organizations should recognize their own shortcomings and the limitations of their capabilities. After recognizing their own limitations in professional capacity, organizations should guide those seeking help to other institutions that may be able to provide appropriate support.


3. Increase the organization's visibility to better reach the groups in need of assistance.. Many people in need often remain silent; they may quietly follow educational content and lectures, and only come forward for help when they truly need it.



Finally, for Chinese women living overseas, Dr. Yang Qun hopes that everyone, in both life and work, can develop a deeper understanding of cultural differences between China and other countries, actively integrate into the diverse overseas cultural environment, and bravely communicate and seek solutions when facing unfair treatment or discrimination.


As for overseas Chinese women who may be or are already experiencing domestic violence, Dr. Yang Qun emphasizes that domestic violence often develops gradually, making awareness and understanding of it especially important. She recommends that everyone regularly attend educational lectures and proactively seek relevant information and necessary assistance through established organizations.
