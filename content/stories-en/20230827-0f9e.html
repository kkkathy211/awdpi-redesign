I. AWDPI Annual Forum


AWDPI (Asian Women Development Plan International) was initiated in early 2020 at the onset of the global COVID-19 pandemic. It is a growing international nonprofit organization dedicated to eliminating discrimination and gender-based violence against Asian women living overseas, and promoting their empowerment and development. AWDPI has established a broad and effective network to combat domestic violence for Chinese women overseas facing abuse, and promotes societal attention toward Asian women by building extensive collaborations with United Nations agencies, government departments, international organizations, multinational corporations, and academic institutions. Among its key initiatives are the AWDPI Asian Women International Development Forum and the Avoice Overseas Chinese Women Protection Project (Against Violence to Overseas Chinese Women Program). After more than three years of development, these projects have expanded to East Asia, Europe, North America, and Australia.

To summarize our efforts over the past three years and better plan for future development, AWDPI plans to host its second annual forum (Global Women's Innovation Summit & the 2nd AWDPI Asian Women Development Forum), expanding from the first forum into three thematic sub-forums and one paper review session. This announcement is for the[Paper Review]call for research paper and study submissions..

For more detailed information about the AWDPI Asian Women International Development Forum, please follow our official WeChat account—we will gradually introduce our exciting forum agenda and our distinguished keynote speakers!


II. Call for Papers and Research Submissions


1. Purpose of the Paper Review Session

  • To showcase AWDPI's academic standards and capabilities

  • To provide AWDPI members with opportunities to participate in academic activities

  • To build connections with more scholars in related fields

2. Paper Review Process

  • The AWDPI Asian Women International Development Forum will allocate 2–2.5 hours specifically for the review and presentation of relevant thematic papers.

  • We will select 4–6 high-quality submissions from the entries, each of which will be presented by the author in an 8-minute presentation, followed by approximately 10 minutes of feedback and suggestions from expert reviewers.

  • If we receive a sufficient number of submissions, we will also select several additional high-quality papers for online poster presentations. Participants will be able to "move between" different virtual meeting rooms to view presenters' academic posters, listen to their explanations, and engage in discussions.

3. Eligibility for Submission

 This call for papers is open to:

  • Current active AWDPI members

  • AWDPI database members and former members

  • Friends introduced by AWDPI members who express interest in participating

  • Members of the public and students from all backgrounds who have strong interest or ideas related to the forum themes

4. Paper Submission Themes

Any topic related to fields AWDPI is involved in or plans to enter, including but not limited to:

  • Anti-gender-based violence movements

  • Protection of Asian women overseas

  • Violence-free digital spaces initiative

  • Women's leadership

  • Women and health

  • Women and intimate relationships

  • Women's political and workplace empowerment

  • Overseas Chinese women workers

No disciplinary background restrictions; we especially encourage interdisciplinary research submissions, such as:

  • Technology + Philanthropy (e.g., how women's websites and forums support and currently shape public sphere construction)

  • Policy + Women's Health (e.g., women's health from medical and social security perspectives)

  • Workplace + Education (e.g., cultivating workplace competence among overseas Chinese women)

  • Law + Psychology (e.g., psychological trauma in children exposed to domestic violence and its correlation with criminal behavior in adulthood)

Paper themes and content should align with AWDPI's vision of "creating a world of love and equality for women" and its values of "participation, inclusion, and empowerment."

5. Paper Reviewers

We will invite guest experts from AWDPI's expert database and well-known scholars in the field of women's rights to conduct paper reviews.

6. Paper and Research Selection Criteria

  • Submissions in Chinese or English are accepted; for other languages, please contact the Academic Department separately:[email protected]

  • Clear purpose, distinct theme, sufficient evidence, logical structure, and concise language

  • Must follow standard academic format, including title, author name, abstract, keywords, main text, and references

  • Recommended length: no more than 15,000 Chinese characters or 8,000 English words

  • Papers must be original; plagiarism is prohibited, and no infringement of others' legal rights is allowed

  • Papers that have already been publicly published will not be accepted

  • This forum encourages authors with strong interest or ideas about the themes—especially those who have not previously participated in formal academic forums—to actively submit. We welcome various types of submissions, including experimental studies, literature reviews, systematic reviews, and meta-analyses, to promote academic exchange and vitality. We also welcome students without complete research results to submit and share high-quality research proposals, ongoing studies, term papers, or short essays.

01 Selected Paper

"Women's Online Activism under New Media Empowerment: A Case Study of a Sanitary Pad Mutual Aid Initiative at a Chongqing University"

Author: Tan Shuyue

Presenter: Tan Shuyue

Abstract   In the information age, feminist discourse and action in new media have become a significant direction in media and gender studies. This paper takes the sanitary pad mutual aid box initiative at a university in Chongqing as a case study. Within the framework of new media empowerment theory, and using online observation and in-depth interviews, it records and analyzes the formation and mobilization capacity of feminist online organizational actions. The study finds that new media empowers women at the levels of information, expression, and action, providing favorable space and negotiable pathways for women to invert dominant discourses and claim their voices. Women's extensive use of new media in this process also provides material and models for studying Chinese feminist movements. However, it is still necessary to note that due to insufficient overall societal awareness of equality—including among participants—and the influence of China's unique historical and cultural context, Chinese digital feminist movements still face many problems and challenges in organizational structure, self-empowerment awareness, and goal selection.


Excerpt from Reviewer Comments   The paper contains rich details, demonstrating the characteristics of online organizations through solid online observation and in-depth interviews, and explores challenges faced by women in digital activism. It reveals many promising and potentially fruitful research directions. It is recommended that the research question be made clearer and more focused, with further exploration of structural factors, the commercial logic and characteristics of online actions, and a more nuanced discussion of the features of online activism and the definition of "success" in such actions. Regarding methodology, follow-up interviews could be added to the current in-depth interviews to explore how participating women's lives, thoughts, and mindsets have changed and developed after their involvement in the sanitary pad mutual aid initiative.

"New Dilemmas and Pathways for Abused Women Seeking Shelter During the Pandemic in a Risk Society: A Case Study of Beijing"

           Author: Yang Jing, Zhou Shuhan, Ding Xiang, Fang Miaowen, 

                    Wang Yinghan,  Zhang Xinyin, Feng Ping, Hu Chen, Xu Yuan

           Presenter: Yang Jing

Abstract    Since the outbreak of the COVID-19 pandemic at the end of 2019, unprecedented shocks have been inflicted on global social and economic development, exposing various structural gender inequalities worldwide—one of which is the worsening of domestic violence against women. This paper, from the perspective of risk society and using Beijing as a case study, employs literature review, case study, and semi-structured interviews to identify new challenges faced by abused women seeking shelter and to propose possible solutions. The main findings are as follows: (1) Pandemic-related isolation policies and resulting economic burdens are primary factors exacerbating domestic violence; (2) The difficulties abused women face in seeking shelter during the pandemic mainly stem from weakened personal support systems and social support networks; (3) In the post-pandemic era, resolving these challenges requires not only enhanced self-rescue awareness among women but also joint efforts by public authorities and other organizations.


Excerpt from Reviewer Comments    This study is highly timely and significant both academically and practically. The article is well-documented, comprehensively describing risks at different levels, and the risk society perspective aligns well with the research content. The data cited are generally accurate and complete, providing a comparative analysis between mainland China and the global context. The reviewer suggests that the theoretical analysis could be better integrated with real-world conditions—for instance, by conducting a deeper exploration of structural imbalances exposed in areas such as public health policy—to further enhance the study's practical implications in terms of institutional and policy recommendations.

Domestic Violence in Europe After the Outbreak of COVID-19

             Author: Gao Tianyue, Xiong Shiyu, Marceline 

                       Hu Yunping Dong Zhijie, Zi Yiran, Jiang Jingwen

             Presenter: Gao Tianyue

Abstract    Thanks to the initiation of the Istanbul Convention, many European countries have accumulated relatively mature experiences in preventing and combating domestic violence. However, due to the negative impact of the COVID-19 pandemic on domestic violence, there were significant differences among countries in their response policies and the effectiveness of policy implementation. This report focuses on compiling data on domestic violence cases during the pandemic in 11 European Union countries that have signed the Istanbul Convention (Austria, Belgium, France, Italy, Spain, Portugal, Denmark, Sweden, Germany, Norway, Switzerland), as well as the United Kingdom, Russia, and Ukraine, analyzing whether domestic violence policies in these countries remained effective in addressing the issue during the pandemic. The findings reveal that, among countries that have signed and begun implementing the Istanbul Convention, most still exposed inadequacies in prevention and support services during the pandemic. In Russia, which has not signed the convention, while the total number of reported cases in the family domain decreased, non-judicial complaints, reports, and requests for help related to domestic violence surged during the pandemic—a phenomenon also observed in many other signatory countries. Among all 14 countries, Switzerland stood out: due to the government's long-standing determination and sustained investment in combating domestic violence, unlike most regions globally, domestic violence did not significantly increase during lockdown periods.


Selected Review Comments  This report contains a large volume of information, with rich and detailed materials compiled. It is recommended to enhance the presentation of information in both the article and presentation by incorporating charts and other visual tools to highlight and compare the complexity of domestic violence issues across countries. It is also suggested to conduct a deeper analysis of policy elements from different countries that could be drawn upon and adapted in practice, which would further highlight the report's practical significance.
