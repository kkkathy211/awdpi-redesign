1


A piece of jewelry can tell the moment that belongs to you


Is there something you've never been able to throw away?


A faded necklace, a pair of inexpensive earrings, a stone picked up during a trip, or a button removed from an old piece of clothing. They may not be useful, yet years later they still quietly rest in your drawer.


Because what's truly preserved is never just the object itself—but the slice of life it has kept for you.



Jewelry stays close to the body, accompanying us through many unspoken moments. It can commemorate a person, or a city, a period of life, an emotion. It can certainly be beautiful, but we want it to carry meaning beyond decoration.



What a piece of jewelry means can be decided by the person who wears it.


So we began to wonder: if a story could be turned into a piece of jewelry, what would it look like?


2


Starting from a piece of jewelry


"Wear Your Story" is a DIY jewelry public welfare project initiated by AWDPI Asian Women Development Plan International .


We invite you to handcraft a piece of jewelry and embed your life experiences, memories, or imagination into it. There are no restrictions on type—necklaces, bracelets, earrings, rings, or brooches are all welcome, as are upcycled old jewelry pieces and original works made from fabric, beads, natural materials, etc.


Professional skills are not required, but the piece must be completed by you personally, and you must be able to explain the connection between the work and yourself.

Story themes

You may begin from your own experience, or from an old object, a material, a memory, or an imagined scene.


4


How to participate


You don't need a "perfect" story or professional craftsmanship. Start with a bead, an old object, or a moment you've always remembered.



L1

Micro Story

1 photo of your work + a one-sentence statement within 30 words. Suitable for those who want to participate in the simplest way. The work and sentence will be included in the project's content pool.

Lightest participation

L2

Standard Story

1–3 photos of your work + a story of 300–500 words. Selected works that meet the criteria will be featured in the online exhibition, with creator credit retained.

Entry into exhibition

L3

In-Depth Story

Multiple photos + a story of 800 words or more, optionally accompanied by a 60-second audio or video tour. We will select some for creator interviews and featured display, and prioritize invitations to participate in charity sales.

In-depth showcase + priority for charity sale


If you prefer to express yourself through voice, you may directly submit an audio story within 60 seconds, and project volunteers will assist in transcribing the text.


📮 Submission email: xiran.fan@awdpi.org


5


From exhibition to charity sale



After the collection phase, we will establish an online exhibition. Works will be displayed by theme, and each featured piece will include images, creator credit, and the story behind it.


After the first phase of collection concludes, we will invite selected creators to participate in charity sales. All works must have explicit authorization from the creator to enter the charity sale. After the sale, the project will publicly record and disclose income, expenses, and final fund usage.


 1. Work Collection → 2. Online Exhibition → 3. Creator Sharing → 4. Authorized Charity Sale → 5. Fund Transparency



Someone creates, someone sees, someone buys, and this support continues flowing toward the next story that needs to be seen.



6


More than one form


What we aim to do is not an exhibition about "what women should wear."


Jewelry is often assigned fixed meanings: delicate, soft, romantic, or associated with love, family, and female identity. But we'd like to loosen that definition a little.


A piece of jewelry can commemorate a mother, or a long journey; it can belong to a relationship, or only to oneself. It can be soft, or sharp; refined, or raw.


It doesn't need to meet any standard answer.



When a person personally chooses the materials, colors, and shapes, and decides what to carry on their body, she is also redefining: what is worth remembering, what belongs to me, and how I want to define myself.




7


Let stories flow



In the first phase, we begin with story collection and an online exhibition in China, inviting individual creators as well as women's communities, youth groups, handmade and eco-craft communities to participate.


As more works and creators join, we will continuously update the online archive and share the stories through platforms such as Xiaohongshu and WeChat Official Account.


Later, we also plan to gradually launch international collection, inviting women from different cities and cultural backgrounds to participate via platforms like Instagram and TikTok, and establishing an English-language online exhibition.


We hope to eventually see a map composed of countless real lives. Different people, using different materials, telling different stories. They don't need to be the same, nor do they need a unified answer—only to truly belong to the person who created them.
