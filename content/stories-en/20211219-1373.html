Catherine

Catherine has lived in Australia her whole life, progressing from a conventional academic path to independent entrepreneurship and founding a podcast for women over 30. Having suffered domestic violence, she has explored her own healing methods through constant reflection and experimentation, and now shines independently and confidently in her chosen field.



01

Part

The people-pleasing personality


"My parents were quite strict with me from a young age—for example, my mother enrolled me in many tutoring classes, pushing me to study hard and get into a good university, and our way of interacting was very traditional."


Catherine's father was a university professor and her mother worked in finance. Because of their professions, she grew up hearing stories about the so-called "model children": attend a prestigious university, pursue a glamorous career, get married and have children—go around in a circle of societal success, and you'll be worry-free for life..


But unlike those "model children," what accompanied Catherine throughout her childhood was only the loud sounds of shouting and violence: her father not only subjected her mother to domestic violence, but also frequently resorted to physical abuse against Catherine herself. An insecure childhood creates early personality traits of inferiority and sensitivity, and this impact can last for a very long time.


"In high school, I constantly faced verbal bullying from others; during my undergraduate years, I was someone with a people-pleasing personality and didn't know how to handle friendships properly. I wanted to become more confident, but I didn't know exactly how to do it."


Catherine, who had long been aware of the "weak" aspects of her personality, finally left home to attend university. In this fresh chapter of life, the young woman slowly broke through the boundaries of inferiority and people-pleasing, carving out her own territory.

02

Part

Breaking boundaries


This was Catherine's first small achievement after starting university: founding a English Conversation club.


In a business school with a large number of Chinese international students, she noticed a lack of genuine English-speaking environments, so she founded a language exchange club, inviting local students and people from other countries to participate. As more and more people joined the events, the positive feedback she received greatly encouraged her.


"Even today, I still feel this was very meaningful—not only helping Chinese students improve their spoken English, but also deepening their understanding of Western culture; although I graduated in 2016, there must still be people who remember having joined this club."


With continuous affirmation and encouragement, Catherine wanted to explore and try even more, so "part-time jobs" became part of her plan: from working off the books in Chinatown, to selling health supplements, to being a sales associate in a luxury store, her hourly wage rose from AUD 8 to AUD 25. The hardships behind this are self-evident, but through different role transitions, she gradually sensed the intersection of her "past self" and "present self," as if something was slowly melting away.


Meanwhile, experiences of robbery and legal disputes also became significant chapters in Catherine's academic journey.


"Because I was robbed, I even went to the police station to file a report; but what left the deepest impression was the lawsuit against my landlord, who arbitrarily withheld over AUD 1,700 from my deposit when my lease ended. So I spent an entire month fighting for my rights. With no legal experience, I didn't know how to sue him or how to win, so I kept gathering evidence. Unfortunately, this coincided with a breakup and my exam period, but in the end, I achieved a good outcome."


That same year, after constant persuasion and support from Catherine, her mother finally made the decision to divorce..


"I had urged my mother to divorce for a long time. If she hadn't, she might have been in life-threatening danger. The most serious incident was when she was hospitalized after being violently abused by my father. But she refused, saying it was for my sake, though I think it was more due to societal pressures."


Social influence, public opinion…These constraints, which had imprisoned a woman for twenty years, were finally broken.


Countless unforgettable experiences—looking back, both good and bad. With newfound praise and recognition, her mother even started dancing ballet again. And the difficult choices Catherine was forced to make, the solitary journey of fighting for her rights, also kept generating new positive feedback, allowing this once insecure and timid girl to gradually accumulate new energy..


"I really changed—became a confident person, at least very confident, and no longer felt the need to please others. I've been through so much that I've managed to forget those bad memories. Actually, women are very strong."


Source: Pixbay


03

Part

The pandemic and domestic violence


After landing her first job, Catherine also encountered a romantic relationship—because they worked in the same office building, they gradually got to know each other.


"He clearly noticed me first, then invited me to dinner and so on; after about a month and a half, we entered a serious relationship."

 

Her boyfriend's slightly awkward personality and gentle temper made Catherine let down her guard built from her family of origin..


"We dated from May to November 2019. Although we had minor conflicts in daily life, I thought these were manageable. That autumn, he went back to his hometown for medical treatment, and I visited him and met his parents. His family was exceptionally kind, and many of our customs were very similar—I really felt we were quite compatible."


From affection to romance, Catherine, like most young women, held hopes and dreams for the future. They decided to start living together.


01

Domestic violence


In early 2020, the COVID-19 pandemic broke out. After the winter break ended in late February, Catherine's company required remote work, while her boyfriend had to work on-site. After discussion, they returned to their shared home. By March, conflicts between them began escalating, with arguments nearly every week—sometimes over daily habits, sometimes over differing values.


"I gradually realized he was selfish and held very traditional patriarchal views. For example, during house cleaning, he assigned me the dirtier areas like the kitchen, while he took on easier, lighter tasks; and he used the excuse of 'not knowing how to cook' to make me cook every day."


Over time, Catherine felt her boyfriend's flaws were increasingly evident, and some of them completely contradicted her values..


"At the time, I was preparing to start an online business, but he firmly opposed and belittled my achievements. We once argued because he insisted women must have children, and when I said I wouldn't, he said we should break up."

 

By May, the conflicts grew increasingly severe. Catherine was even prevented by her boyfriend from having dinner with a former junior from university, and he gradually began interfering with her normal social activities: "No communication with male friends exceeding 10 minutes," "no outings longer than one hour." All of this made Catherine alert to the abnormal situation and sparked her desire to move out.


What truly sounded the alarm was an incident of domestic violence.


"I absolutely couldn't tolerate him escalating conflict into physical violence. Once, I accidentally made his nose bleed, and he said he would retaliate—scratching my leg with his fingernails, leaving deep red marks. I define this as domestic violence, a form of intentional harm. Seeing that he couldn't control his physical actions, I felt seriously threatened."


Nightmares seemed to repeat themselves—the image of her boyfriend in rage reminded Catherine of her father's grotesque expression and loss of emotional control, as if she were trapped in the same vicious cycle: her father's abuse of her mother, her boyfriend's abuse of her. After much deliberation, on a day in May 2020, Catherine packed her bags and resolutely left.


"The moment I got into the car,

I felt as if the sky had suddenly brightened."


Source: Pixbay


02

Reflections along the journey


While discussing business ideas with potential partners, Catherine, facing similar situations, kept reflecting on where things had gone wrong..


"I feel that the interaction patterns from one's family of origin shape one's intimate relationship patterns. For example, in intimate relationships, I tend to be anxious and avoidant, and in interactions with men, I still carry a people-pleasing tendency. This may be related to whether one receives positive feedback. In my career, from part-time jobs in Australia to my current entrepreneurship, I've received a lot of affirmation, but in relationships with men, the feedback has been scarce."

 

Searching for a way out within the vicious cycle of domestic violence, repeatedly examining and reflecting, Catherine gradually found a new path. How to move beyond confusion is not just a personal issue—it is actually a shared topic for women today who have had similar experiences.


04

Part

Be a tree that doesn't depend on anyone


Source: Pixbay


2021 was a year of awakening female consciousness. Today, Catherine is an independent entrepreneur and founder of a women's podcast.


"I think women should not focus too much on romantic relationships. Spending most of your time on emotions leads to an imbalance in the self. We need to explore our own meaning, discover our unique strengths, and do things that give us a sense of achievement, thus creating a positive cycle. When everything is within your control, you'll realize you possess boundless strength."


01

The founding of FreeLab


"FreeLab was founded in mid-2020, inspired by my experience of working from home during the pandemic."


Catherine grew tired of the outdated 8-hour industrial-era work model, and realizing that many jobs in the new information age could be done remotely via computer, she created this freelancing community.

"An important point is that we can work and make friends freely. Now many people have joined us, and at the same time we are launching social media platforms and organizing offline events to help people transition more smoothly from being wage workers to freelancers."


02

Around 30


"I started 'Around30' because I really enjoyed listening to podcasts. Last November, I discussed age-related anxiety among women around thirty with several friends—topics like pressure to marry, workplace discrimination, and society's objectification of women. I felt this group needed to be seen, so I created this podcast to focus on the lives of women in their thirties."

 

Through a platform, a channel, Catherine is using her own way to awaken more women and transmit more strength to help everyone. There are only three months left until her thirtieth birthday.


"I'm actually looking forward to it. Thirty is beautiful—I still feel I can live out infinite possibilities."

 

A two-hour interview, across the screen sits a clearly confident and radiant woman. In the video, tree shadows sway and birdsong can be heard. For her, a difficult past seems like a forging of life, gathering strength—struggling to find a way out within the vicious cycle of domestic violence, speaking out through podcasts to encourage more women toward independence..


This has been a long journey,

but fortunately, she has finally met the sun.
