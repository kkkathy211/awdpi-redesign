Ensuring Equal Imperial Succession Rights for Men and Women—UN Committee on the Elimination of Discrimination against Women Questions Japan's Imperial Succession System

According to Japan's Asahi Shimbun, on October 29, 2024, the United Nations Committee on the Elimination of Discrimination against Women (CEDAW), based in Geneva, Switzerland, released a report explicitly stating that Japan's imperial succession system contains gender discrimination. The committee recommended that Japan amend the Imperial Household Law to ensure equal rights for men and women in imperial succession. The report proposed two specific recommendations: first, revising relevant laws to allow married couples not to share the same surname; second, directly amending the Imperial Household Law to abolish gender restrictions in imperial succession.


However, the report sparked widespread debate in Japan. The following day, Chief Cabinet Secretary Yoshimasa Hayashi expressed strong dissatisfaction, stating that the UN action seriously interfered in Japan's internal affairs. This incident has once again drawn global attention to gender equality and highlighted the many challenges that remain on the international stage.

"Breaking Menstrual Taboos, Courageously Expressing Ourselves"—International Day of the Girl Child Celebration Concludes Successfully

On October 4, 2024, Beijing University of Posts and Telecommunications hosted a special event themed "Breaking Menstrual Taboos, Courageously Expressing Ourselves" to celebrate the International Day of the Girl Child. The event opened with a speech by Lu Daoqin, Secretary-General of the Beijing Renren Care Health Public Welfare Foundation. Secretary-General Lu emphasized that the International Day of the Girl Child is not only a celebration for girls and young women worldwide, but also an opportunity to advocate for gender equality and listen to their voices. Through artistic creation and sharing, the event aimed to help girls overcome menstrual shame, learn self-respect and self-love, and encourage them to bravely pursue their dreams. The event featured diverse formats, providing girls with a platform to speak up courageously and showcase themselves. The organizers stated that they will continue promoting gender equality education and creating safe, equitable environments for more girls to grow and realize their dreams.

Advancing Gender Equality—Human Rights Council Adopts Resolution Commemorating the 30th Anniversary of the Beijing Declaration and Platform for Action

On October 9, 2024, in Geneva—At the 57th session of the United Nations Human Rights Council, an important resolution commemorating the 30th anniversary of the adoption of the Beijing Declaration and Platform for Action was adopted by consensus among participating countries. The resolution was jointly submitted by China on behalf of Denmark, France, Kenya, and Mexico, aiming to emphasize the importance of gender equality and calling on the international community to accelerate progress toward gender equality and jointly address challenges in this field. (Xinhua News Agency, 2024)


The adoption of this resolution demonstrates the international community's broad attention and high regard for gender equality. A total of 114 countries co-sponsored the resolution, fully reflecting the consensus and determination among nations to advance gender equality. Following the smooth adoption of related resolutions by the UN Committee on the Elimination of Discrimination against Women (CEDAW), Nicole Ameline, a French committee member, gave an exclusive interview to Xinhua reporters. She highly praised China's leading role in advancing global women's rights. Ms. Ameline stated: "The successful adoption of this resolution is not only a review and commemoration of the global advancement of women's rights over the past thirty years, but also a vision and expectation for the future of gender equality. The Committee fully supports the resolution's vision, believing it marks a new step forward at the global level, which will drive new efforts and inspire new momentum. This is undoubtedly the best news we've received today."


The Beijing Declaration and Platform for Action, as key documents advancing global gender equality, have made positive contributions to the protection and development of women and girls worldwide since their adoption. The adoption of this resolution not only reviews and summarizes three decades of progress in gender equality, but also looks forward to and anticipates future development in the field.

Protect Your Safety, Beware of Telecom Fraud Traps—Three Chinese International Students Kidnapped in Thailand; Kidnappers Still at Large

In October 2024, three Chinese international students were kidnapped in Tak Province, Thailand. The three students were an 18-year-old male, a 20-year-old male, and an 18-year-old female, all from a well-known university in Bangkok's Thonburi area.


On October 17, the three students were lured away from campus to a resort in Mae Sot, Tak Province, and were later taken away in a pickup truck and kidnapped. At 9 p.m. that night, their friends received a distress message and immediately reported it to the police. Thai police responded swiftly and eventually found the three hostages in a nearby forest; their physical and mental conditions were normal.


According to the students, they had originally intended to find a job and accepted a high-paying offer to transport goods from Tak to Bangkok, but were unaware of the cargo contents or recipient information. After being kidnapped, they were taken to an office near the Thai-Myanmar border, where 6–7 armed individuals violently threatened them, demanding they contact their parents to pay a 1.5 million Thai baht ransom, or else be sent to a telecom fraud compound in Myanmar.


After negotiations, the mother of one student paid a 500,000 baht ransom, after which the three students were released. Police have confirmed this was a case carried out by an organized criminal gang, and the investigation is ongoing.

United States Continues Systemic Suppression of Asian Scholars

On October 8, China's Ministry of State Security published an article on its WeChat official account, strongly criticizing a U.S. policy known as the "China Initiative." It is reported that this initiative has already destroyed the careers of over 103 Chinese-origin researchers. Since its implementation, judicial authorities in multiple U.S. regions have frequently arrested Chinese-origin scholars on suspicion of espionage. These actions have not only caused immense psychological stress and fear among Chinese researchers, but have also severely damaged their professional development. Many Chinese scholars have lost their jobs and even face long-term imprisonment. China's Ministry of State Security stated that such U.S. practices reflect serious prejudice and discrimination against the Chinese community, violate international law and human rights principles, and harm scientific and technological exchanges and cooperation between China and the United States. China urges the U.S. to immediately cease these erroneous practices and take measures to rectify the damage already caused.


This incident has drawn widespread attention and discussion, once again reminding people to remain vigilant against the impact of racial discrimination and bias in the scientific and technological fields. It is hoped that the international community will work together to promote fair, just, and open development in science and technology.

Strengthening Asian Identity, Promoting Eastern Wisdom—The 27th China–ASEAN Leaders' Meeting and the 27th ASEAN Plus Three (China, Japan, and South Korea) Leaders' Meeting Successfully Held in Vientiane, Laos

On October 10, the 27th China–ASEAN Leaders' Meeting and the 27th ASEAN Plus Three (China, Japan, and South Korea) Leaders' Meeting were successfully held in Vientiane, Laos. The following day (October 11, 2024), Mao Ning, spokesperson for China's Ministry of Foreign Affairs, addressed multiple issues during a regular press conference, highlighting China's emphasis on Asian representation and voice. Speaking about the China–ASEAN Leaders' Meeting, Mao Ning emphasized mutually beneficial cooperation between China and ASEAN in emerging fields, reflecting China's role as a major Asian nation in promoting regional peace and development, and securing greater participation and voice for Asians on the international stage.


In response to U.S. Secretary of State Blinken's remarks on Taiwan, Mao Ning clearly stated that the Taiwan issue is China's internal affair and brooks no interference, reflecting China's firm support for Asian women on matters concerning their core interests. In responding to the European Parliament's resolution condemning China's human rights situation in Xinjiang and Tibet, Mao Ning strongly criticized the European Parliament's actions, emphasizing that the state of China's human rights should be judged by the Chinese people, thus firmly safeguarding the fundamental, legitimate, and lawful rights and interests of China and Asian communities in the international human rights arena.


Additionally, China has expressed concern and opposition regarding the U.S. deployment of missile systems in South Korea and the Philippines, demonstrating its commitment to regional peace and stability and enhancing the voice of Asian communities on regional security issues.

 Connie Chung to release memoir recounting her experiences growing up as an Asian woman

Renowned American news anchor Connie Chung is set to release her personal memoir, reflecting on her career in the media industry and her journey growing up as an Asian woman. The book not only shares her groundbreaking achievements in journalism but also delves into the challenges she faced as a woman of a minority ethnic group in both her professional and personal life.


Connie Chung is the first Asian American female news anchor in U.S. television history, with a career spanning several decades and positions at major mainstream media outlets including CBS and NBC. In her memoir, she recalls how she broke through gender and racial barriers in the industry and candidly shares her feelings and coping strategies when facing discrimination. She hopes that her memoir will inspire more women to bravely pursue their dreams and advance public awareness of racial and gender equality.


Connie's memoir also documents her inner journey while reporting on major news events and highlights her deep emotional ties with her family and community. The book is expected to attract a wide readership and further promote understanding of the lives and identity of Asian Americans.
