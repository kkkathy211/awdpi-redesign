The Global Picture of Legal Equality: A Warning That No Country Has Met the Standard


On March 4, 2026, UN Women released a report titled "Ensuring and Enhancing Access to Justice for All Women and Girls" ahead of International Women's Day, drawing global attention. The report's central finding is alarming: Globally, no country has achieved full legal equality between women and men. Women worldwide hold only 64% of the legal rights men have, a figure indicating that women still face widespread discrimination, violence, and exclusion under the law.


Specific data from the report further reveal systemic gender bias in legal systems: in over 54% of countries worldwide, "consent" is still not recognized as an element of rape, meaning that in more than half of all countries, the law may not even recognize rape as a crime when it happens to women; in nearly three-quarters of countries, laws still permit forced child marriage; and in 44% of countries, laws do not mandate equal pay for equal work.


UN Women Executive Director Sima Bahous issued a warning: "When women and girls are denied justice, the harm extends far beyond individual cases. Public trust erodes, institutions lose legitimacy, and the rule of law itself is weakened. A justice system that fails half the population cannot claim to uphold justice."

A Glimmer of Hope: CSW70 Reaches Historic Consensus


March 2026 also witnessed a significant development. From March 9 to 19, the 70th session of the UN Commission on the Status of Women (CSW70) convened at the United Nations Headquarters in New York. During the session, governments adopted a set of "Agreed Conclusions" by broad consensus, aimed at strengthening access to justice for all women and girls. The document calls on governments to review and revise discriminatory laws, including those related to child marriage, family law, and property rights, and urges stronger measures to prevent and respond to violence against women and girls both online and offline.


As a long-standing international nonprofit organization dedicated to women's rights and gender justice, AWDPI Asian Women Development Plan International was invited to host three CSW70 online parallel forums. Transmitting women's stories and strength through the digital network.


UN Women Executive Director Bahous emphasized in her closing remarks: "CSW70 sends a powerful message to women and girls around the world: their right to justice is essential, and discriminatory laws and practices have no place in legal systems, policies, or institutions."

Fifth Anniversary of the Atlanta Shootings: Asian-American Congressmembers Introduce Anti-Asian Hate Resolution


On March 16, 2026, exactly five years since the 2021 Atlanta spa shootings, an incident that killed eight people—six of them Asian women—occurred amid a surge in anti-Asian sentiment during the COVID-19 pandemic and remains one of the most emblematic hate crimes in recent years.


At this moment, U.S. Congressmembers Grace Meng and Judy Chu jointly introduced a House resolution commemorating the fifth anniversary of the Atlanta shootings and condemning the ongoing anti-Asian hate. Data cited in the resolution show that anti-Asian hate crimes remain nearly three times higher than pre-pandemic levels, with hate crimes against Native Hawaiians and Pacific Islanders reaching record highs.


The significance of this resolution lies not in directly changing laws, but in "maintaining sustained federal attention on anti-Asian hate"—ensuring the issue is not diluted in public discourse and remains recognized as an urgent matter requiring continuous government response.

UN Committee on the Elimination of Racial Discrimination Warns: Link Between Political Discourse and Hate Crimes


In March 2026, the UN Committee on the Elimination of Racial Discrimination (CERD) issued a warning, stating that "racist hate speech" by former U.S. President Trump and other political figures, together with intensified immigration enforcement measures, is fueling serious human rights violations. The Committee specifically noted that portraying immigrants as "criminals or burdens" is "likely to incite racial discrimination and hate crimes." This warning was echoed in specific incidents. In February 2026, a video went viral on social media showing an Asian-American boy crying after being taunted as an "illegal immigrant" by opponents after a soccer match. The video garnered over 6.4 million views within two days. The incident was seen as reflecting the direct impact of Trump's anti-immigration rhetoric on youth behavior.

Chinese Tailor in Italy

Harassed and Beaten by Gang of Teenagers


On the evening of March 7, 2026, a Chinese tailor who had lived in the city of Asti, Italy for ten years was violently attacked inside his shop by a group of delinquent youths. The attackers forced open the door and physically assaulted him, while his elderly mother was hit in the head with boxes from inside the store. Medical examination revealed bruising around the victim's left eye and multiple injuries to his hands and legs, requiring ten days to recover. The victim stated that the gang had harassed him for at least four months, including spitting on the shop window, damaging the door lock, and throwing garbage into the store. On March 15, the victim hired a lawyer and formally filed a police report. On the afternoon of March 19, the shop window was vandalized again, highlighting the ongoing security threats faced by Chinese business owners.


According to La Stampa on March 15, amid escalating violence and prolonged fear, the victim chose to no longer remain silent. He has engaged a lawyer and formally reported the incident to the police, hoping to hold the perpetrators accountable through legal means and seek justice for himself and his mother.

"Unforgettable Awards" Conclude

Asians Tell Their Own Stories Through Cultural Works


On March 7, 2026, the 23rd Unforgettable Awards, honoring outstanding achievements by Asians and Pacific Islanders in film, television, and culture, were held in Los Angeles. This year's honorees included Oscar-winning director Chloé Zhao, actor Bowen Yang, and Daniel Dae Kim.


In her speech, Zhao expressed pride in being Chinese and feeling fortunate to make a living through storytelling. "Using creativity to alchemize life experiences, no matter how difficult, is everyone's birthright." (It is the birthright of every human being to use their creativity to alchemize their life experiences, no matter how difficult they are.) These words precisely capture the core of Asian cultural voice: telling our own stories, replacing others' imagination with our own narratives.

New York State Supreme Court Appoints First

Asian-American Female Justice


On March 3, 2026, New York State marked a historic breakthrough as Judge Karen Lin was appointed the first Asian American woman to serve as an Associate Justice of the Appellate Term of the Supreme Court, Second Department. Judge Lin emphasized that the appellate term may be the only appellate body many ordinary New Yorkers ever encounter, and she pledged to serve "with fairness, compassion, and humility." Meanwhile, Benjamin Hsing, President of the Asian American Bar Association of New York, stated: "Increasing Asian American representation in the judicial system is crucial to building public trust in the courts."

A systematically forgotten group

Psychiatry paper calls for attention to Asian American mental health


In the field of health, a paper published in the Harvard Review of Psychiatry in March 2026 pointed out that Asian Americans are often perceived as a group with "virtually no mental health issues," but this perception stems from a misinterpretation caused by aggregated data. The authors emphasized that data must be disaggregated by specific ethnic subgroups to identify and reduce mental health disparities.


At the same time, new research published in Psychiatric Services found that foreign-born Asian adults use mental health services at a much lower rate than U.S.-born individuals, with only about 10% of Asian and Latino individuals overall reporting the use of any form of mental health treatment. These findings reveal a deeper issue: advancing Asian American voices must be accompanied by confronting and addressing structural and institutional inequalities.
