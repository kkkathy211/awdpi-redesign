In November 2025, Cindy Gui Jiaze, founding head of the Asian Women Development Plan International (AWDPI) Australia National Office, was once again invited for an exclusive interview with SBS, a leading Australian media outlet. As a representative of the new generation of Asian women's power, she systematically introduced to the public AWDPI's on-the-ground progress, community practices, and future plans in Australia..


Since its first appearance on SBS in April this year, the AWDPI Australia National Office has entered a phase of steady development. After seven months of systematic building, the team has completed the establishment of legal compliance systems, governance frameworks, a volunteer network, and internal processes, and has built a cross-sectoral collaboration network with government agencies, psychological and legal service providers.


Looking ahead, the AWDPI Australia National Office will continue to advance in three core directions:

First, building a localized, sustainable, and cross-professional collaborative support system;

Second, strengthening cooperation with major local domestic violence prevention organizations; Third, promoting

early prevention education on domestic and gender-based violence. Early prevention education.


In the interview, Gui Jiaze said: "This event is a milestone for AWDPI. We hope to tell all sectors of Australian society: we are here."


Every bit of attention and support from the outside world will be transformed into strength, empowering more solid and compassionate local actions. AWDPI will continue to work hard, committed to building a safer, more equal, and more empowering future for women worldwide.

Empowering Asian Women:

AWDPI Launches in Australia and Releases Domestic Violence Handbook


The Asian Women Development Plan International (AWDPI) will host its official launch event in Australia on Saturday, November 29, from 11:00 AM to 3:00 PM at the Chinese Museum in Melbourne, where it will also release a domestic violence handbook. The event will include a cross-cultural roundtable forum on domestic violence prevention, featuring participants such as Victoria Police officers, family lawyers, multicultural community leaders, and scholars from related fields. The event is free and open to community members. Click the audio to listen to an introduction by Cindy Gui Jiaze, head of the AWDPI Australia National Office.
