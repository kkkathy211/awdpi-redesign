Building a platform for collaboration and exchange

Promoting protection and empowerment of overseas Chinese women

Avoice successfully hosts the first Global Forum on Chinese Women's Development

On January 7, 2023, Beijing time, the inaugural Avoice Global Forum on Chinese Women's Development was successfully held. Co-organized by the Avoice Overseas Chinese Women Protection Project and the Hainan Dongfang Miao Xi Public Service Promotion Association, the forum aimed to build a knowledge-sharing network for organizations and scholars dedicated to Chinese women's development, create practical opportunities for engagement in gender-related issues, and further expand the living space and voice of overseas Chinese women.

The forum invited over ten domestic and international experts and scholars from UN Women, international organizations, academic institutions, renowned universities, and technology innovation companies to participate online. Hosted by Wiley and Claire, founders of The Wannabe Feminists blog, the event featured keynote speeches, thematic dialogues, and paper presentations and reviews. Discussions spanned gender-related data in technology, philanthropy, the workplace, education, law, psychology, and policy, enabling cross-generational, cross-sectoral, and cross-regional knowledge sharing and collaborative dialogue, offering participants profound and innovative insights into the theory and practice of combating gender-based violence.

Viewership statistics:

The Zoom livestream reached 532 attendees, with a total of 3,617 viewers across all online platforms.

Media coverage:

News of the forum's successful launch was reported by domestic media outlets including People's Daily Online Europe and Hong Kong-Macao Online (Philanthropy), as well as major domestic web portals such as Sohu, NetEase, Tencent, and Phoenix News.

At the opening of the forum, Yimar, founder of Avoice, delivered the opening speech, sharing the current situation of overseas Chinese women in achieving gender equality and outlining Avoice's mission and vision, officially launching the forum.

Session One: Keynote Speeches

Exploring domestic violence/gender-based violence faced by female migrants and the current legal landscape


Zhang Lining, China researcher at UN Women and expert at the International Anti-Discrimination Law Research Group, Berkeley Law, University of California, mentioned in her speech titled "The Stranger, Gender-Based Violence, and the Law" that female migrant groups face numerous challenges in foreign lands—including human trafficking, distorted beauty standards, and media stereotypes about women—that further increase their risk of various forms of violence. Zhang emphasized: "Law cannot act alone. We must work together to create a world with zero tolerance for gender-based violence."

Keynote speaker Zhang Lining


Taking domestic violence as an example

Exploring how young women can participate in and lead gender initiatives


Chen Zhenghong, council member of the Traditional Culture and Women's Development Committee at the Beijing Women's Theoretical Research Association, delivered a speech titled "How to Enable Young Women to Participate in and Lead Gender Projects." Chen stated that women's rescue is not merely about helping women who suffer domestic violence; participants must clarify their stance, make bold decisions, and pay attention to self-protection. In advancing specific initiatives, participants should attempt multi-departmental collaboration and improve dialogue skills. Chen concluded with guidance on how to sustainably advance gender projects.

Keynote speaker Chen Zhenghong


Divergent experiences of overseas Chinese women in promoting gender equality


Yingyi Wang, Assistant Professor in the Department of Gender Studies at St. Lawrence University, USA, delivered a speech titled "Transnational Migration, Gender, and Politics: Divergent Experiences of Overseas Chinese Women." Professor Wang discussed how gender inequality shapes and affects lives, concluding that achieving gender equality remains a long and difficult journey that cannot be changed simply by the success of individual women. She then presented, through structural gender analysis, how different institutional forms exploit women, calling for greater recognition of women's rights and gender equality.

Professor Wang said: "Although differences in identity and class may make mutual understanding difficult among humans, our task is precisely to connect people across different social strata, discover their commonalities and unique characteristics, unite them, and thereby see more possibilities."

Keynote speaker Yingyi Wang


Session Two: Thematic Dialogues

Can internet technology advance gender equality?


Vera Zhang, CEO of China Youth of Tomorrow (CYOT) and TEDx curator, moderated the dialogue on "Internet Technology and Gender Equality." Guests included Larissa Leng, author of Invisible Faces and founder of NOTINSTUDIO; Sally Shen, founder of Codopia EdTech and key opinion leader (KOL) in Web3 quality content; and Shi Lan, founder and CEO of Cafeyi Technology. Together, they discussed internet technology and gender issues.

The three guests analyzed whether the internet has advanced gender equality from perspectives such as algorithmic bias, hidden gender identities, and data bias. They discussed how to raise awareness, reduce prejudice, leverage role models, and encourage women to break through entrenched biases and achieve self-development.

How to promote Asian women breaking industry barriers and expanding career development paths in overseas workplaces?


Hosted by Guo Jiajia, founder and CEO of the ESG media platform TouchBase, the dialogue featured three guests: Emily, co-founder and CEO of Shangchuang Technology; Dr. Polly Ng, founder, chairwoman, and CEO of Global Women Connect; and Yang Qun, founder of Biorbyt biotech company and first chairwoman of the Chinese Women's Alliance in the UK. Combining academic insight with practical experience, they discussed how to promote and expand career development paths for Asian women overseas.

The guests shared personal experiences of identity discrimination faced by Chinese women and strategies for coping. They advocated for women to enhance their capabilities and continuously grow, called for shared parenting responsibilities between women and their partners, and encouraged women to bravely break free from traditional gender constraints and cultural differences, balancing career and family with confidence, independence, resilience, and optimism.

Session Three

Paper Presentation and Review


To promote research on gender issues and build an academic platform, the forum specially included a paper presentation and review session, inviting Ling Han, postdoctoral fellow at the Stanford Center on Philanthropy and Civil Society and assistant professor in the Gender Studies program at The Chinese University of Hong Kong; Yanping Wang, council member of the Avoice Overseas Chinese Women Protection Project; and Yingyi Wang, PhD in Gender Studies from the University of Washington and Assistant Professor in the Department of Gender Studies at St. Lawrence University, USA, to serve as reviewers.

Three papers were presented: "Women's Online Activism under New Media Empowerment: A Study Based on the Sanitary Pad Mutual Aid Box Campaign at a Chongqing University," "Risk Society: New Dilemmas and Pathways for Abused Women Seeking Shelter During the Pandemic—A Case Study of Beijing," and "Policy Research on Combating Domestic Violence in Europe After the Outbreak of the COVID-19 Pandemic." The reviewers noted that the three papers observed and studied topics such as women's menstrual health care and domestic violence during the pandemic, while also paying attention to the living conditions of overseas Chinese women.

About the Avoice Overseas Chinese Women Protection Project

The Avoice Overseas Chinese Women Protection Project (Against Violence to Overseas Chinese Women Program) launched its pilot phase in Belgium during the early days of the COVID-19 pandemic in 2020. Committed to eliminating gender-based violence and promoting gender equality, the project strives to establish a broad and effective anti-domestic violence support network, providing comprehensive and sustained protection and assistance to overseas Chinese women facing domestic violence.

By building a collaborative network with government agencies, overseas Chinese organizations, social organizations, and industry experts, the project unites broader philanthropic forces, advocates public attention and support for the living challenges of overseas Chinese women, and creates a safe and equal environment for their survival and development. It aims to help women transition from victims of domestic violence to advocates and practitioners of gender equality. Currently, the project has expanded to all European countries and parts of North America.
