How do we understand "who I am" when living between different cultures?


And when family expectations, cultural backgrounds, and the Australian social environment intertwine, how do we find our own place?


AWDPI Australia will officially launch the "Voices & Perspectives" Community Listening and Dialogue Series.


In this first session, we invite young Asian-Australian women living in Australia to come together and talk about:


identity, belonging, and real-life experiences in cross-cultural contexts.


This is not a one-way lecture, but a small-scale, facilitated peer dialogue. You can share your own experiences or listen to others' stories; you can actively participate or join in ways that feel comfortable for you.


Your experiences and voice will also help AWDPI Australia gain deeper insight into the real needs of young Asian-Australian women in Australia, guiding future community programs, resources, and support initiatives.




About "Voices & Perspectives"



"Voices & Perspectives" is a community listening and dialogue series initiated by AWDPI Australia.


Through conversations on different themes and with diverse groups, we aim to listen to experiences, needs, and viewpoints that are often overlooked in public discourse, and allow authentic community voices to help shape future programs and support.


The first session will focus on identity and belonging among young Asian-Australian women living cross-culturally, including international students, new migrants, Australian women of Asian heritage, and young women who are studying, working, or building new lives in Australia.



What will we talk about?



In this dialogue, we will explore together:


  • How does living between cultures affect how we see ourselves?

  • How do family, cultural, and community expectations shape our choices and identities?

  • What makes us feel accepted, understood, and a sense of belonging in Australia?

  • How do we build authentic and meaningful connections in our studies, work, and daily lives?

  • What kinds of loneliness, isolation, or barriers to seeking help might young Asian-Australian women face?

  • What kinds of community activities, resources, and support do we truly need?

We will also include light interactive activities, small group discussions, and shared reflection, so that every participant has the opportunity to join in ways that feel comfortable.



Why participate in this dialogue?



Let your voice be heard

Share your authentic thoughts on identity, culture, and belonging in a respectful, facilitated group space

Build connections with peers who have similar experiences

Meet other young women who are also navigating life between cultures, building their lives, and understanding themselves.

Gain new perspectives and insights

Hear diverse experiences and gain fresh understanding of your own feelings through psychological research and peer exchange.

Help shape future programs and support

Your input will help AWDPI Australia better plan future community activities, resources, and support services for young Asian-Australian women.

Who Should Attend?



This event is primarily for young Asian-Australian women currently living in Australia, including but not limited to:

  • International students

  • Recent migrants to Australia

  • Young women studying or working in Australia

  • Australian women with Asian cultural backgrounds

  • Those currently exploring cross-cultural identity and belonging

  • Those who wish to meet others and build community connections


You do not need any professional expertise, nor do you need to prepare a personal story in advance.



Please Note Before Participating



This event is a facilitated peer dialogue and community listening activity, and does not provide psychological counselling, psychotherapy, or clinical mental health services.


We will collectively maintain a respectful, inclusive, and non-judgmental environment. Personal sharing during the event should be respected, and participants should not share others' personal stories or identifiable information after the event.


Participation is always voluntary. You may decide how much to share and will not be asked to answer any questions that make you uncomfortable.
