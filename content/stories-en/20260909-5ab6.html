UN Women Promotes Gender-Equal Climate and Environmental Action


2026 is a pivotal year for advancing gender equality within the global environmental agenda. The triple planetary crisis of biodiversity loss, pollution, and climate change disproportionately affects women, girls, and gender-diverse groups. In August 2026, UN Women is promoting inclusive and equitable gender-responsive action plans, emphasizing that climate action becomes stronger, fairer, and more effective when women have equal access to leadership, resources, land, finance, and opportunities. This year, UN Women will release the "Progress of the World's Women" report on gender equality and climate change, and continue updating the "Gender Equality and Climate Policy Scorecard"—which has so far assessed the gender responsiveness of climate policies in 67 countries.

International Youth Day:

Global Young Women Face Multiple Structural Inequalities


On August 12, International Youth Day, UN Women issued a statement: globally, the rate of young women who are out of school, unemployed, or not in education or training is more than double that of young men; in low-income countries, large numbers of adolescent girls lack internet access; although many young women actively participate in climate action and digital innovation, gender barriers continue to restrict their development opportunities. The organization called for safeguarding women's equal rights to education, employment, and public participation.

News


Consul General Ren Faqiang of Cape Town

Publishes Signed Article on the Independent Media Website

"Shared Destiny: Advancing Women's Development Together"


In August 2026, South Africa commemorated the 70th anniversary of Women's Month under the theme "Empower Women, Empower the Nation," honoring the significant contributions of South African women to social development. The Chinese Consul General in Cape Town published a signed article, stating that women's development concerns the shared future of all humanity, sharing China's national policy practice of gender equality within Chinese-style modernization, and calling for international cooperation to eliminate the gender gap. In October 2025, China co-hosted the Global Women's Summit with UN Women, bringing together leaders and representatives from over 110 countries, as well as heads of the United Nations and international organizations, in Beijing to discuss advancing global women's development. The spirit of the Global Women's Summit closely aligns with the core values promoted during South Africa's Women's Month—comprehensive development of women not only concerns individual destinies but also national development and the shared future of humanity.

News


FBI Releases 2025 National Hate Crime Statistics Report


On August 17, 2026, the FBI released its 2025 hate crime statistics: 339 reported anti-Asian hate crimes, a 10.6% decrease from the previous year, but still more than 2.5 times higher than pre-pandemic levels. Civil rights organization AAJC warned that a large number of verbal harassment and intimidation cases go unreported due to language barriers, distrust of police, and immigration status concerns, meaning official figures severely underestimate the true extent of the problem; amid rising anti-immigrant rhetoric, the sense of safety within Asian communities remains fragile.

Three Chinese International Students in Sydney Attacked in Racist Assault


According to the Daily Mail, the incident occurred around 3 a.m. on the 25th of last month, when 29-year-old Chinese international student Lenny Tai and two friends were waiting for a ride-hailing vehicle near Sydney Central Station. Two white women in their twenties, standing across the street, initiated contact by asking about "Pokémon cards," deliberately drawing the trio's attention. To avoid conflict, the three students chose to leave the scene, but the women quickly followed them, launching into a barrage of racially discriminatory and sexually offensive verbal abuse.


During the confrontation, the two women lost control and physically assaulted the students. One of the attackers arrogantly shouted, "This is Australia, you'll never be able to come back to our country," while the other filmed the entire incident on her phone.


As of August 5, Sydney police have reviewed case details, released descriptions of the two suspects, and are appealing to the public for information, actively pursuing the suspects for investigation.

New Zealand Foreign Minister Makes Racist Remarks During Parliamentary Debate


On July 29, 2026, during a general debate in the New Zealand Parliament, Green Party Chinese-origin MP Lawrence Xu-Nan (born in Tianjin, China, moved to New Zealand with his family in 1994) asked Foreign Minister Winston Peters a question about the COVID-19 vaccine: "Have you been vaccinated?" Peters responded: "I know you've only been here five minutes. Go back to your country." He escalated further afterward, calling Xu-Nan a "conceited fool." AFP described the remarks as "racist statements." The Chinese Embassy in New Zealand lodged a serious protest with New Zealand's Ministry of Foreign Affairs and Trade, stating that Chinese overseas citizens in New Zealand "should be recognized and respected"; New Zealand Prime Minister Luxon publicly criticized the remarks as "highly offensive" and "inappropriate attention-seeking behavior"; Chinese Foreign Ministry spokesperson Mao Ning stated: "We hope relevant individuals in New Zealand will do more to promote friendly relations between the two countries."

Vincent Ho and Chak Au Join Voices:

Let Canada Recognize and Remember the Contributions of Chinese People to the Nation


On August 21, 2026, a press conference drew significant attention from the Chinese Canadian community in Toronto. Members of Parliament Vincent Ho and Chak Au jointly spoke out about Chinese Canadian history and the contributions made by generations of Chinese people to national development, calling on Canada to recognize Chinese history in a more complete, objective, and fair manner, and to formally acknowledge at the national level the long-term contributions of Chinese people to Canadian social development. This year marks the 20th anniversary of the Canadian government's formal apology for historical discriminatory policies against Chinese people. On June 22, 2006, the Canadian government formally apologized for discriminatory policies such as the head tax and the Chinese Exclusion Act. Twenty years later, Chinese-origin MPs are once again stepping forward, pushing Canada from "acknowledging historical wrongs" toward "affirming Chinese contributions."

Chinese Representative Criticizes Western Racial Discrimination by Name at the United Nations General Assembly


On August 8, 2026, during the human rights debate at the United Nations General Assembly, in response to coordinated attacks by eight countries—Britain, Australia, Canada, Japan, and others—on issues related to Xinjiang and Hong Kong, the Chinese representative refused to follow the script set by the accusers and instead directly turned the tables, listing the human rights violations of each of these countries. The Chinese representative criticized Britain for severe racism, noting that "reports of discrimination and even violent attacks against Black and Asian people frequently make front-page headlines"; pointed out the dire situation of Indigenous Australians and the harsh conditions in offshore detention centers; criticized Canada's residential school system for Indigenous peoples as "organized cultural cleansing"; and explicitly urged Japan to "face up to and correct human rights issues in Okinawa, and stop prejudice and discrimination against Okinawans and other Indigenous peoples." The reference to Okinawans as "Indigenous peoples" touched on the controversial legitimacy of Japan's historical annexation of the Ryukyu Kingdom, triggering strong backlash from the Japanese government and right-wing media.

US Media: White House May Welcome First Chinese American Press Secretary


According to the New York Post on August 12, incumbent White House Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt announced she will step down at the end of August. Steven Cheung, Director of the White House Office of Communications, is widely seen as the top candidate to succeed Leavitt. Born in 1982 and raised in Sacramento, California, Cheung's parents are Chinese immigrants. He has long served as an advisor and spokesperson for Donald Trump and was appointed Director of the White House Office of Communications and Assistant to the President in November 2024, becoming the first Chinese American member of Trump's new administration. If officially appointed, he will become the first Chinese American White House Press Secretary in U.S. history.
