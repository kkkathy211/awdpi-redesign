 Column Introduction:


November has seen issues of gender equality and the Asian diaspora come into focus from multiple angles. From gender-based violence and women's status to legal challenges, progress toward global gender equality has drawn widespread attention. At the same time, rising incidents of violence against Asians—such as cases in Nevada and South Africa—have reignited societal concerns about the safety of Asian communities. Meanwhile, Asians are increasingly gaining visibility and influence in culture, politics, and society. The recognition of Houston Chinese-American woman Coco Reggie with an Outstanding Contribution Award, Andy Kim becoming New Jersey's first

Asian American senator, and Hulu's popular series *Interior Chinatown* have all amplified positive voices within the Asian community. These events reflect the complexity of today's social transformations and the growing significance of Asians across various fields.

Global Overview of Female Domestic Violence Homicide Rates and the International Day for the Elimination of Violence against Women


According to the report *Global Estimates of Femicide by Intimate Partners and Other Family Members 2023*, released in 2024 by the United Nations Office on Drugs and Crime (UNODC) and UN Women, approximately 51,100 women worldwide were killed by intimate partners or family members in 2023. This figure indicates that around 60% of all female homicides globally in 2023 were committed by their intimate partners or other family members. Violence by intimate partners or family members remains one of the leading causes of death for women worldwide. November 25, 2024 marked the International Day for the Elimination of Violence against Women. Data shows that every 10 minutes, a woman is killed due to gender-based violence. UN Women emphasized the need for comprehensive efforts—including legislation and social support systems—to protect women from violence and to provide better support services for survivors.

UK Supreme Court Hears Landmark Legal Challenge on the Definition of "Woman"


On November 26, 2024, the UK Supreme Court began hearing a legal challenge concerning the definition of "woman," a long-standing dispute brought by the women's rights group "For Women Scotland" (FWS) against the Scottish government. The central issue of the case is whether transgender individuals with gender recognition certificates should be considered women under equality law.


The case involves a reinterpretation of the term "woman" in Scottish law, which aims to ensure that at least 50% of board members in Scottish public institutions are women—including transgender women who hold gender recognition certificates. FWS has objected, arguing that this definition exceeds parliamentary authority and is seeking to overturn it.


The ruling in this case is expected in the future and may have far-reaching implications for gender rights and definitions across the UK.

Case of Missing Chinese-American Woman in Nevada


On November 26, 2024, US media reported that Ms. Zhang, a Chinese-American woman in Nevada, had been missing since November 8. After police intervention, her body was discovered in the backyard of her husband's residence. When police questioned her husband, Daniel, he claimed his wife had "left home alone in a taxi," a statement that raised suspicion. Authorities later searched a property linked to Daniel and ultimately unearthed Ms. Zhang's body in the backyard of a residence in Fallon. Daniel, aged 71, has since been arrested by police on charges of first-degree murder.

Kidnapping of Chinese Shop Owner in South Africa


On November 18, 2024, news emerged that a 47-year-old Chinese shop owner in South Africa had been kidnapped by three men and remains missing. According to reports, the victim was ambushed and forced into a white Ford vehicle just as he opened his store on Govan Mbeki Avenue, before the suspects quickly fled the scene. Several hours later, the suspect vehicle was found abandoned in New Brighton, but the interior was empty. Local police have opened an investigation and are urging anyone with information to call the crime tip hotline.

Surge in Home Invasions Targeting Asian Families in Colorado


In November 2024, multiple Asian families in Colorado were targeted in a series of sophisticated home invasions. Police have warned that these incidents may be linked to a nationwide criminal gang known as the "South American Theft Group." The Douglas County Sheriff's Office reported that the thieves have stolen nearly one million dollars in cash and luxury goods from homes of various Asian business owners. These burglaries primarily target Asian families, especially new immigrants and Asian business owners, who often keep large amounts of assets at home rather than in banks. Local officers described this as a "national trend," noting that many criminal groups have become aware of this pattern and are now specifically targeting Asian households.

From Behind the Scenes to the Spotlight


In November 2024, Hulu released the new action-comedy series *Interior Chinatown*, adapted from Charles Yu's novel of the same name and starring Jimmy O. Yang and Chloe Bennet. The series follows the story of Willis Wu (played by Jimmy O. Yang ), a character trapped in the role of a background extra in a police procedural, as he struggles to break free from stereotypical roles and step into the spotlight. The production team and cast hope that this "play-within-a-play" format will explore the experiences of Asian Americans in the US television industry. The show aims to portray the complexities of cultural and identity struggles among Asian Americans through a blend of comedy and crime drama.

Houston City Comptroller Awards Outstanding Contribution Award to Chinese Community Development Director Coco Reggie


On November 13, 2024, Houston City Comptroller Christopher G. Hollins announced the awarding of the "Outstanding Contribution Award" to Chinese Community Development Director Coco Reggie, in recognition of her exceptional leadership and outstanding contributions to community development. This award not only acknowledges Reggie's professional achievements but also highlights her significant impact—as an Asian American—on Houston's diverse community building. In her acceptance speech, Reggie said: "This award does not belong to me alone; it belongs to the entire team and everyone who has worked hard for community development."

Andy Kim Becomes New Jersey's First Asian American Senator


In November 2024, in the US Senate election in New Jersey, Democrat Andy Kim defeated Republican candidate Curtis Bashaw, winning the seat previously held by former Senator Bob Menendez, and becoming the first Asian American senator in New Jersey's history. This victory is seen as a symbol of the rising political power of New Jersey's Asian community and reflects the growing influence of Asian Americans in US politics. Kim called on more Asian Americans to engage in public service, stating: "Don't exclude yourselves. If you want to run, if you want to serve, then try. I hope people will hear this call." He also expressed hope that through his actions, he could change perceptions about Asian American political participation and demonstrate that Asian Americans are equally entitled to represent all residents of the state.
