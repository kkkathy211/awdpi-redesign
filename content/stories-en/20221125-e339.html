16 Days of Activism against Gender-Based Violence

The annual United Nations "16 Days of Activism" to end gender-based violence is approaching. This year's campaign, themed "Activism! UNiTE to end violence against women and girls," (Activism! UNiTE to end violence against women and girls) calls on more people to join efforts to eliminate gender-based violence and support women and girls worldwide in securing their rights.

Every year, November 25 marks the UN's International Day for the Elimination of Violence against Women. The 16 Days of Activism begins on this day and continues until December 10, Human Rights Day. The campaign aims to mobilize society, the public, governments, and UN agencies to expand impact and encourage action to end gender-based violence.

According to the latest gender-based violence survey report by UN Women conducted across 13 countries during the COVID-19 pandemic, nearly half of the women surveyed said that they or women they know have experienced gender-based violence since the pandemic began. Women who have experienced gender-based violence are significantly more likely to suffer increased psychological and emotional stress. The findings also show that due to increased household conflicts during the pandemic, about one in four women feel unsafe at home, with physical abuse being one of the main reasons. And beyond the home, the risk of gender-based violence remains ever-present. 40% of respondents said they feel less safe walking alone at night since the outbreak of COVID-19.

Although gender-based violence is extremely common, growing evidence shows that social intervention can have a significant preventive effect, with women-led civil society organizations and other groups dedicated to preventing and responding to gender-based violence playing a crucial role. For decades, the relentless efforts of these organizations have moved violence against women and girls (VAWG) from the margins to the center of the global agenda, making it a core component of the global women's rights agenda. Yet their contributions and impact are often underestimated, or even overlooked. At times, these organizations and service providers face challenges due to insufficient public awareness of gender-based violence issues and new policies.

Due to lack of funding and human resources, the implementation of women's rights protection policies and laws developed through the work of these organizations remains inadequate in many countries, and perpetrators often escape punishment. In light of these harsh realities, to ensure that no woman or girl is left behind, we must continue to act, recognize the contributions of these organizations and individuals, provide them with greater support, and become part of the movement ourselves.


16 Days of Activism against Gender-Based Violence



As part of this year's UN-led "16 Days of Activism against Gender-Based Violence," Avoice (Overseas Chinese Women Protection Project) will collaborate across all platforms (WeChat, Xiaohongshu, Weibo, Instagram) with women-led civil society organizations, other groups dedicated to preventing and responding to gender-based violence, gender equality advocacy organizations, more men, and survivors, to host a series of online and offline public education and advocacy activities on gender-based violence, and we look forward to your participation and engagement.

Orange is the official color of the 16 Days campaign, symbolizing alertness to gender-based violence and the harm and cost it brings. The warm, bright orange also represents hope, encouragement for survivors, and the aspiration for a future society free from the threat of violence.
