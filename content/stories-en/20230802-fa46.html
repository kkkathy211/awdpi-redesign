Introduction:


HSTAR Scotland, full name Healed Scars Trauma and Abuse Recovery (hereinafter referred to as HSTAR), is a self-initiated, community-based organisation managed by a diverse team of professional volunteers with extensive knowledge in community development. This charity, dedicated to trauma healing for women in Scotland, provides multicultural and multilingual counselling and healing support to women affected by abuse and trauma, and empowers women and stakeholders with awareness of abuse, trauma, and mental health through enhanced mental health awareness, targeted training, and volunteer opportunities.


Avoice is deeply honoured to have invited Ms. Ewelina Maria Chin, the director of HSTAR, for this interview, engaging in an in-depth conversation on topics including women's empowerment, rescue work processes, and the founder's personal experiences.



Guest Profile

Ewelina Maria Chin

Ewelina is the founder of the charity HSTAR. During the Covid-19 pandemic, the number of female victims of domestic violence was shockingly high. Ewelina realised how crucial it was to break the silence and raise awareness about trauma and abuse. She wanted to bring hope and relief to female survivors in a different way, helping these women grow stronger.




01

Establishment Original intention: Born in the pandemic era

During the pandemic, many places were under lockdown, and psychological trauma accumulated in various forms, increasing the demand for healing. HSTAR emerged in response to this need. The organisation was officially registered as a charity in August 2020, right at the height of the pandemic.


"Because cases of domestic violence clearly increased during the pandemic, the need for healing also rose accordingly, and the situation was quite urgent, so we decided not to delay any further. At that time, we primarily focused on women currently experiencing domestic violence, offering them psychological healing and related services. But in reality, no one cared whether women who had previously suffered domestic violence needed healing, and they could not even find resources for help—no financial, material, or human support. They could do nothing but silently endure, even though their psychological trauma remained."


"That is why we established this organisation," "to provide psychological healing to women in need, regardless of their age or language—any woman in need can receive our support." "

Unlike Avoice, which primarily offers targeted assistance to women facing threats of domestic violence, HSTAR's definition of the target group for support is broad. Ewelina says that behind trauma, many things may be hidden—anything can cause psychological trauma.


"We will provide assistance to any woman experiencing psychological trauma, regardless of the cause."




02

Trauma Healing: Respecting Diverse Cultural Needs

Ewelina believes that today the concept of "trauma" is used very broadly—events that bring long-term negative impacts on personal physical and mental health, social life, material possessions, or even emotions can all be called "trauma.".


"For example, sexual abuse; long-term living with family members who have mental health issues, disabilities, or substance use disorders may lead to trauma; the death of a partner, relative, or close person. Also poverty, gender inequality, racial bias, discrimination, oppression, war, terrorism, political instability—these large-scale violent events in broader environments all cause trauma."


Regarding how to assist help-seekers from different backgrounds, Ewelina says she believes every culture has its own beliefs and values, and help-seekers' different cultural, historical, racial, gender, geographical, and linguistic backgrounds directly affect both the trauma and the healing provided by HSTAR. As a trauma healing organisation, they recognise that communication difficulties may arise during the healing process, so they provide personalised support according to people's different needs.


"If there is a need for healing, we strive to understand the help-seeker's situation and provide healing methods based on their individual circumstances. We continuously encourage them. We believe that adjusting healing methods according to the person's cultural background is essential, as it helps remove barriers in the healing process, overcome inner discomfort, resolve social difficulties, and strengthen family connections. Therefore, it is important to advocate for care towards traumatised families, women, and their families, to give help-seekers a sense of cultural belonging, to understand their culture and respond accordingly."




03

From Contact to Healing:

Person-Centred, Individualised Support

HSTAR's healing services do not simply offer healing; instead, they create a person-centred healing journey by focusing on and listening to each individual's needs. Ewelina emphasises the importance of individualised healing—everyone's feelings and responses are deeply personal, so HSTAR does not operate according to a fixed model.


"As I mentioned at the beginning, feelings and responses are deeply personal matters. If we operated according to one single model, our organisation would have no reason to exist. Because then it wouldn't be person-centred."


"Of course, we could use data to build a model, but that would go against the very nature of healing." "Everyone is different—regardless of height, weight, skin colour, or language spoken—we see each person as an exceptional individual. We deeply understand that she carries trauma, so we must apply different healing methods." "

During the process of engagement, HSTAR respects each person's privacy and needs, engaging in extensive communication with help-seekers to understand their various needs, so as to arrange suitable healers and service methods. Ewelina notes that approximately 90% of help-seekers find HSTAR through external referrals, such as NGOs, hospitals, or local organisations, while the remaining 10% contact HSTAR through the website. Regardless of the method, HSTAR staff will contact and assess each help-seeker, understanding their cultural background, beliefs, socioeconomic status, and employment capacity, in order to provide services tailored to individual needs.


In just three years since its founding, HSTAR has supported over 700 people, including women who have completed healing or are currently receiving healing. In terms of format, in addition to providing healing services, HSTAR also offers legal advocacy support and accepts various communication methods, including in-person, video calls, or phone consultations. Whether through face-to-face meetings or online healing, HSTAR is committed to providing a safe and comfortable environment for help-seekers.


"As a small charity, we have our own advantages because we uphold the principles of being person-centred and open, paying attention to and listening to everyone's needs—something that is impossible for very large organisations. But fortunately, we can do this, and I hope this will never change."




04

Story Listening Workshops:

Rediscovering Joy and Reintegrating into Society

In April last year, HSTAR launched an activity called the Story Listening Workshop. This was not merely a support activity that encouraged women to boldly speak about their trauma. On the contrary, it provided a safe environment that encouraged women to step out from their realities, participate in discussions, and bravely imagine and communicate, breaking through inner loneliness.


Ewelina points out that in the workshops, HSTAR's healers guide participants to share their stories, rather than forcing them to disclose trauma. Most importantly, it truly offers a space where women feel safe.

"Our workshops are designed to help them forget trauma and let them feel those beautiful things. Here, everyone can encounter different cultures, different insights, and different perspectives. They don't have to talk about their trauma—we encourage them to create beauty and overcome trauma. Every meeting here is an adventure, taking people into different ages and cultures. We do many activities, and all are conducted under the premise of making everyone feel safe."


According to Ewelina, the workshops have achieved significant results in empowering women, reshaping positive mindsets, and facilitating social reintegration. HSTAR consistently focuses on women who may struggle socially, making efforts to bring these women together. Although participants may need courage to attend, no one leaves midway. Each session exceeds the scheduled 90 minutes, often lasting over two hours, because participants feel empowered and are reluctant for the activity to end.


"Those few hours are beautiful—everyone gathers together doing creative and meaningful things, each person filled with positive energy, very active, very happy. From the photos of the event, you would never know these women had inner wounds.


We hope everyone can shift their mindset, gain a sense of relaxation, and rediscover the joy they have lost. I know our help-seekers have been through a lot, but here, no one needs to dwell on those negative things. Instead, they should relax, forget the past, and create a richer, more colourful life with a smile."



05

Founder Ewelina:

A Personal Transformation from Trauma to Hope

Having lived in Scotland for nine years, Ewelina says it took her seven years to bravely step out of her comfort zone. She herself had experienced trauma and sought help from public health services, but did not receive a satisfactory resolution. She longed for someone or somewhere that would truly listen to her story, allowing her to vent and overcome her trauma. This experience gave Ewelina the motivation and reason to begin creating hope. She longed for someone or somewhere that could truly listen to her story, allowing her to release her emotions and overcome her trauma. This experience gave Ewelina the motivation and reason to begin creating hope.


"Actually, I don't want much—just someone or somewhere that can truly listen to my story and let me release it. I only hope to be happy again, but unfortunately, I haven't been able to achieve that. There are times when I feel lost, angry, and helpless, but I tell myself that getting angry won't help, so I pull myself together again, reminding myself to learn forgiveness, forgetting, and gratitude, and to bring hope to women like me. That's why I found the motivation and reason to start doing something. But at first, I didn't know what I could do. The loneliness brought by these years of pandemic gave me strength and answers."

As someone fully devoted to charity work, Ewelina gave up her 14-year career and now sees HSTAR as her entire life, deeply loving this cause. "I am now, and will always be, just an ordinary staff member of HSTAR. Although I often feel exhausted, I remain deeply grateful for everything I have, and I feel like the happiest person in the world."


Every time she receives feedback from someone seeking help, she feels immense gratitude. She treats every woman who comes into contact with HSTAR as an individual and shows respect to each person. "I remember the name of every single person I've met at HSTAR because I've always seen them as independent individuals, and I respect each and every one of them." "I might not be able to write down all of these more than 700 names word for word, but as long as you tell me a name, I will definitely remember her. Perhaps this is what I have gained—" "giving, receiving, and in turn giving back to women, to society, and to the world.". "


The names of more than 700 women who have been helped are deeply engraved in Ewelina's heart, becoming her motivation and nourishment, inspiring her to give back to more women and to society as a whole. And the women who have passed through HSTAR will carry their healing strength into the new worlds that await them.
