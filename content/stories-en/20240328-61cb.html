[Belgium Chapter]Are you or someone you care about experiencing domestic violence?


1. [Legal definition]

  • In Belgium, the term "family" in domestic violence covers a broad scope. It includes not only spouses, cohabitants, romantic partners, ex-partners, and anyone in a long-term emotional or sexual relationship, but also other family members such as parents and children, and extended relatives.

  • "Violence" can include physical acts (e.g., beating), sexual acts (e.g., rape), psychological acts (e.g., harassment, defamation, insults), economic acts (e.g., abandonment of family), as well as verbal abuse and restricting freedom.


2. [Current situation of domestic violence in Belgium]

  • According to reports from 2022, Belgian police receive an average of 50,000 reports of domestic violence annually (including physical violence, verbal attacks, psychological intimidation, deliberate neglect, and sexual assault within families). On average, victims report only after experiencing 35 incidents. In other words, by the time they call the police, victims have already endured threats, beatings, or rape 35 times.


3. [Common types]

  • Physical violence: slapping, punching, kicking, shoving, hitting with objects, etc.

  • Verbal abuse: insults, degrading personal dignity, etc.

  • Psychological violence: insults, degrading personal dignity, controlling, dominating, or isolating the other person; making threats; imposing one's own views or values; reducing or preventing contact with their family, friends, and neighbors; using their "mistakes" for moral blackmail or harm; refusing to take responsibility for one's violent behavior.

  • Sexual violence: forcing sexual relations against the partner's will, or engaging in abusive or humiliating acts during sex without consent.

  • Economic violence: preventing the other person from having their own bank account, depriving them of income, using their credit card without consent, etc.

  • Threats of suicide: threatening suicide or self-harm to force the other person to accept certain conditions.

  • Flanders Government Domestic Violence Helpline: 1712 (Monday–Friday, 9:00–18:00)


1.3 Preserving evidence

  • The principle of free evaluation of evidence applies in Belgian criminal law, so there are no strict limitations on types of evidence. Even if a single piece of evidence alone cannot fully reflect the nature of the domestic violence, it can still corroborate other evidence. Therefore, the more evidence preserved, the more favorable it is for holding the abuser legally accountable. Examples of evidence include:

    • any videos, photos, text messages, letters, or emails related to the cause or course of the domestic violence;

    • any objects related to the domestic violence (e.g., damaged clothing, fallen hair, etc.);

    • medical records or medical/psychological examination reports following physical or psychological trauma;

    • police reports;

    • witness statements from any observers, etc.


2. [Protection orders]

2.1 Introduction

  • In Belgium, protection orders can be applied for and enforced under both criminal and civil law, with the aim of restricting the abuser's behavior to protect the victim. The most detailed and specific provision is the "Law on Temporary Residence Ban in Cases of Domestic Violence" enacted on 15 May 2012 (15 MAI 2012. - Loi relative à l'interdiction temporaire de résidence en cas de violence domestique), which specifically outlines the procedures for applying for and enforcing a temporary residence ban (l'interdiction temporaire de résidence) after domestic violence. This will be introduced in section 3.3. This section will cover the general provisions on protection orders under criminal and civil law.


2.2 Eligibility criteria

  • Criminal law In Belgium, protection orders can be applied for and enforced under both criminal and civil law, with the aim of restricting the abuser's behavior to protect the victim. The most detailed and specific provision is the "Law on Temporary Residence Ban in Cases of Domestic Violence" enacted on 15 May 2012 (15 MAI 2012. - Loi relative à l'interdiction temporaire de résidence en cas de violence domestique), which specifically outlines the procedures for applying for and enforcing a temporary residence ban (l'interdiction temporaire de résidence) after domestic violence. This will be introduced in section 3.3 [Shelter and Temporary Residence Ban]. This section will cover the general provisions on protection orders under criminal and civil law.


2.3 Application procedures

    (1) Criminal law

  • In the context of criminal case mediation, you may negotiate the restrictions directly with the other party; legal counsel may assist if needed. (Article 553, paragraph 4, of the Code of Criminal Procedure)

  • If you wish to enforce a protection order during the trial or enforcement phase of a criminal case, you may submit a written request for the enforcement court to be informed and to review it. You may be represented or assisted by a lawyer or a government agency.

    (2) Civil law

  1. In cases of domestic violence,

    under Article 223 of the Belgian Civil Code, you may apply to the court for emergency measures.

  2. In urgent situations during divorce proceedings,

    under Article 1253.3.4 and Article 1280 of the Belgian Judicial Code, the court may also issue preliminary injunctions in emergencies.


2.4 Legal effects

    (1) Criminal law

  • The establishment and enforcement of a protection order are typically decided by a judge, who considers factors such as the likelihood of the offender reoffending or destroying evidence. Legal effects may include, but are not limited to, requiring the offender to leave the shared residence and prohibiting contact with you.

    (2) Civil law

  • Under Article 223 of the Civil Code and Articles 1253.3.5 and 1253.3.6 of the Judicial Code, a protection order may prohibit the other party from entering your residence or contacting you, and from disposing of your joint property arbitrarily, among other measures.

3. [Temporary Residence Ban for Protection]

3.1 Introduction

  • The Belgian Law on Temporary Residence Ban in Cases of Domestic Violence (15 MAI 2012. - Loi relative à l'interdiction temporaire de résidence en cas de violence domestique), enacted on 15 May 2012, specifies the procedures for applying for and implementing a temporary residence ban (l'interdiction temporaire de résidence) following domestic violence.


3.2 Eligibility Conditions

  • Condition one: The respondent and the applicant reside at the same address.

  • Condition two: The respondent poses a serious and immediate threat to the applicant's safety.


3.3 Application Procedure

  • The Law on Temporary Residence Ban in Cases of Domestic Violence (15 MAI 2012. - Loi relative à l'interdiction temporaire de résidence en cas de violence domestique):

    • First, contact the local police and provide relevant evidence; the police will then contact the prosecutor.

    • If the prosecutor determines that you meet the criteria for a protection order, they will immediately notify your former partner about the protection order, including specific restrictions and duration (Article 3, paragraph 4). The protection order takes effect as soon as the person subject to the ban is informed.

    • The protection order will also be communicated to the chief of police in your area.

    • The prosecutor will contact victim support services to provide assistance.

    • Please note that a protection order issued directly by the prosecutor is valid for only fourteen days from the moment the party is notified. The prosecutor may refer your case to the family court (tribunal de la famille de l'arrondissement dans lequel est située la résidence concernée) in your area. The family court will hold a hearing, and may extend the protection order for up to three months after the judgment..


3.4 Legal Effects

  • According to the Belgian Law on Temporary Residence Ban in Cases of Domestic Violence (15 MAI 2012. - Loi relative à l'interdiction temporaire de résidence en cas de violence domestique) enacted on 15 May 2012, a protection order can protect you in three ways:

    • The person subject to the ban must leave the applicant's residence;

    • The person must not enter or loiter near the applicant's residence;

    • The person must not contact or approach the applicant.

5. [Free Legal Aid]

5.1 Introduction

  • Generally, victims of violence are entitled to legal aid. However, since Belgium's legal aid system reform in 2016, the eligibility criteria have been significantly narrowed and the application process has become more complex. The Belgian government offers two types of legal aid services to the public—first-line legal aid (aide juridique de première ligne) and second-line legal aid (aide juridique de deuxième ligne).


5.2 Application Procedure

  • First-line legal aid primarily provides initial legal consultation services and is completely free to the public. The Legal Aid Commissions (Commissions d'aide juridique) arrange for lawyers to offer free legal consultations on specific dates and times, usually held at courts, justices of the peace, justice centers (maison de justice), and municipal authorities.

  • Second-line legal aid mainly involves legal representation and usually requires a fee, though fees may be reduced or waived under certain conditions. You must submit an application at the legal aid office (bureau d'aide juridique) located within a court and provide proof that you meet the criteria for fee reduction. The legal aid office will decide within two weeks whether to approve your application and will send you the contact information of the assigned lawyer. Alternatively, you may first contact a lawyer independently to ask if they are willing to work under the second-line legal aid scheme; if the lawyer agrees, they can represent you in contacting the legal aid office and submitting the application.

  • You can seek legal assistance through the contacts listed on this webpage: https://www.avocats.be/fr/bureaux-daide-juridique-baj. They primarily offer the following services:

    • Provide advice regarding contract negotiations.

    • Provide advice on financial arrangements in marital cases.

    • Assist in mediating divorce cases.

    • Represent you in court.

6. [Divorce and Criminal Prosecution of the Abuser]

6.1 Introduction

  • When you wish to pursue criminal liability against the abuser through legal means, you may refer to the following charges for prosecution.


6.2 Possible Charges Against the Abuser

(1) Threats

  • According to Article 327, paragraph 1 of the Belgian Penal Code: Anyone who threatens to attack a person or property verbally or in writing, anonymously or with signature, by command or condition, shall be sentenced to six months to five years' imprisonment and a fine of 100 to 500 euros.

  • Article 329 of the Belgian Penal Code states: Threatening to attack a person or property by gesture or sign is punishable by 8 days to 3 months' imprisonment and a fine of 26 to 100 euros.

(2) Indecent Assault

  • According to Article 373 of the Belgian Penal Code, Article 117 of the Belgian Government's 21 March 2022 Law Amending the Penal Code Regarding Sexual Offenses (21 MARS 2022. - Loi modifiant le Code pénal en ce qui concerne le droit pénal sexuel), Article 49 of the Belgian Government's 15 May 1912 Law on Child Protection (Loi du 15 mai 1912 sur la protection de l'enfance), and Article 9, paragraph 4 of the Belgian Government's 1 February 2016 Law Amending Various Provisions Regarding Indecent Assault and Voyeurism (Loi modifiant diverses dispositions en ce qui concerne l'attentat à la pudeur et le voyeurisme): Indecent assault committed against a person of any gender or with the assistance of a person of any gender, by violence, coercion, threat, surprise, trickery, or by exploiting the victim's physical or mental illness or disability, shall be punished by six months to five years' imprisonment.

(3) Rape

  • According to Article 375 of the Penal Code and Article 50 of the Belgian Government's 15 May 1912 Law on Child Protection (Loi du 15 mai 1912 sur la protection de l'enfance): Anyone who commits rape by means of violence, threat, deception, or by abusing a person who has lost or been deprived of their senses due to illness, sensory impairment, or other accidental causes shall be punished.

(4) Assault and Battery

  • According to Articles 398 and 399 of the Penal Code: Anyone who intentionally injures or assaults another person shall be sentenced to 8 days to 6 months' imprisonment and a fine of 26 to 100 euros. If the offender acted with premeditation, they shall be sentenced to 1 month to 1 year's imprisonment and a fine of 50 to 200 euros.

    • If the assault or injury results in illness or inability to work, the offender shall be sentenced to 2 months to 1 year's imprisonment and a fine of 50 to 200 euros. If the offender acted with premeditation, they shall be sentenced to 6 months to 3 years' imprisonment and a fine of 100 to 500 euros.

7. [Reporting the Crime]

(1) Going to the Police Station

  • In emergencies, dial 101. In non-emergencies, victims of domestic violence may report the incident at any police station, so it is sufficient to locate the nearest police station. Upon arrival, tell the officer at the reception desk that you wish to file a complaint regarding domestic violence (plainte pour violences conjugales).

(2) Preliminary Interview

  • Most police stations have a dedicated officer for handling domestic violence cases (if you encounter language difficulties, inform the officer that you would like a Chinese interpreter). The officer may ask questions about what happened and whether you are willing to undergo a medical examination. Additionally, if you have other evidence, you may provide copies to the police (but remember to keep the original evidence).

(3) End of Interview

  • At the end of the interview, the officer will ask you to read the record; if you find any passages that do not reflect the facts, you must request corrections and then sign. Usually, the officer will give you a complaint report (attestation de dépôt de plainte); if not provided, you should request it, as it is extremely helpful for subsequent procedures.

  • You may also provide your contact information to the police so they can keep you informed about the case's progress. The police may provide you with contact details of various support organizations.


8. [Divorce]

8.1 Types of Divorce

  • According to Articles 229 and 230 of Belgium's Civil Code, there are two types of divorce in Belgium:

    1. Irretrievable breakdown of marriage: The breakdown is irretrievable if the spouses are unable to continue or resume cohabitation.

    2. Divorce by mutual consent


8.2 Grounds/Conditions for Divorce

(1) Irretrievable Breakdown of Marriage

  • In this case, divorce can be obtained in two ways:

    ① Proving that the breakdown of the marriage is irretrievable.

    • The breakdown is irretrievable if the spouses are unable to continue or resume cohabitation.

    • The judge will grant the divorce upon finding irreconcilable differences between the spouses. Such differences are irreconcilable if the spouses cannot continue living together or resume living together after a separation. Irreconcilable differences may be proven by any lawful means.

    ② Based on a de facto separation that has lasted for a certain period.

    • If the spouses have been living apart de facto for more than six months and jointly file for divorce, the marriage is considered irretrievably broken down. (Article 229, paragraph 2 of the Civil Code)

    • If the de facto separation has lasted less than six months and both spouses wish to jointly file for divorce, the marriage shall be deemed irretrievably broken down when both spouses appear in court a second time after a period of reflection and reaffirm their intention to divorce. (Article 229 paragraph 2 of the Civil Code, Article 1225 paragraph 1 of the Judicial Code).

    • Unilateral application after more than one year of de facto separation: If one spouse files for divorce unilaterally after more than one year of de facto separation, the marriage shall be deemed irretrievably broken down. If one spouse files for divorce unilaterally before one year of de facto separation has elapsed, the marriage shall be deemed irretrievably broken down if the applicant appears in court a second time after a period of reflection and again expresses the desire to divorce. (Article 229 paragraph 3 of the Civil Code, Article 1225 paragraph 2 of the Judicial Code).

(2) Divorce by mutual consent

  • A comprehensive prior agreement covering all consequences of the divorce must be submitted by both spouses, and both spouses must continuously express their wish to proceed with a divorce by mutual consent before it is approved.

    • The comprehensive prior agreement includes a settlement agreement specifying the spouses' arrangements regarding their respective assets (Article 1287 of the Judicial Code), and a divorce agreement addressing the spouses' residence during proceedings, parental authority, management of marital and children's assets, visitation rights during and after divorce, child support obligations of each spouse toward their children, and any spousal support obligations during and after divorce (Article 1288 of the Judicial Code).


8.3 Divorce Procedure

(1) Irretrievable Breakdown of Marriage

  • According to Article 628 paragraph 1 of the Civil Code, applications for divorce or legal separation based on irretrievable breakdown, or applications to convert legal separation into divorce, shall be heard by the court of first instance (tribunal de première instance) located in the place of the last marital residence or the domicile of the respondent.

    • Applications may be filed in the following ways:

      ① Under Article 229 paragraph 1 of the Civil Code, by service of notice through a judicial officer (huissier de justice);

      ② Under Article 229 paragraph 2 of the Civil Code, spouses may file jointly. According to Article 1254 paragraph 1 of the Judicial Code, the application must be signed by both spouses or by at least one lawyer or notary.

      ③ Under Article 229 paragraph 3 of the Civil Code and Articles 1034 paragraph 2 to 1034 paragraph 6 of the Judicial Code, a unilateral application may be filed.

  • In all cases, the initiating document must contain, in addition to the usual required information, a detailed description of the facts and an explanation of the situation regarding any children (Article 1254 paragraph 1 of the Judicial Code). The marriage certificate, birth certificates of any children, and proof of identity and nationality of both spouses must also be submitted, unless these are already recorded in the population register or the register of foreign nationals (Article 1254 paragraph 2 of the Judicial Code).

(2) Divorce by mutual consent

  • In cases of divorce by mutual consent, the spouses may choose the court of first instance (Article 1288 paragraph 2 of the Judicial Code).

    • An application for divorce by mutual consent is submitted to the court. In addition to the documents required for divorce based on irretrievable breakdown, the prior agreement reached by both parties must be submitted, along with an asset inventory where applicable.


8.4 Child Custody

  • According to Article 302 of the Civil Code, once the marital relationship is dissolved by divorce, parental authority and the management of children's assets shall be exercised jointly by both spouses, or by the spouse entrusted with such responsibilities under an agreed arrangement, or as determined by the presiding judge in summary proceedings.


8.5 Child Support

  • According to Article 203 of the Civil Code, both spouses are responsible, according to their means, for the housing, maintenance, supervision, education, and upbringing of their children until they reach adulthood or complete their studies, and must contribute their respective share of expenses arising from this obligation. The allocation of child support is typically determined by court decision or by agreement between the spouses.


8.6 Spousal Support

(1) Irretrievable Breakdown of Marriage

  • The spouses may agree on spousal support, the amount, and terms for reviewing the agreed amount after divorce.

  • In the absence of an agreement, the court may order one spouse to pay support to the other upon request by the spouse in need.

    • The court may deny a request for support if the respondent proves that the applicant committed serious fault leading to the impossibility of continuing cohabitation. Support shall in no case be awarded to a spouse who has been found to have committed physical violence against the other spouse. If the respondent proves that the applicant's state of need results from their own deliberate choice rather than family necessity, the court may exempt the defendant from paying support or may reduce the amount (Article 301 paragraphs 1, 2, and 5 of the Civil Code).

  • The amount of support must be at least sufficient to meet the beneficiary's needs but must not exceed one-third of the income of the spouse obligated to pay. The duration of support payments must not exceed the length of the marriage. This period may be extended in exceptional circumstances (Article 301 paragraphs 3, 4, 6, 8, and 9 of the Civil Code). The duration of support payments must not exceed the length of the marriage. This period may be extended in exceptional circumstances (Article 301 paragraphs 3, 4, 6, 8, and 9 of the Civil Code).


(2) Divorce by mutual consent

  • The spouses determine their respective rights in advance through a comprehensive prior agreement. During and after divorce proceedings, they may agree on any spousal support amount and the conditions for modifying it (Article 1288 paragraph 4 of the Judicial Code).

  • In all cases, if new circumstances beyond the control of either party render the support amount inappropriate, the court may increase, reduce, or terminate the support. If the divorce is based solely on irretrievable breakdown and results in a change in a spouse's financial situation, the court may also adjust the amount of support.

[Belgium Chapter]How domestic violence and divorce affect your visa?


Situation 1

  • If you already have permanent residency, divorce will not affect your right to reside in Belgium.


Situation 2

  • If you hold an F-card for family reunification and can prove that you are a victim of domestic violence, your right of residence will not be revoked even after divorce. See Article 13 of the EU Freedom of Movement Directive (Directive 2004/38/EC of the European Parliament and the Council):

    2. Without prejudice to the second subparagraph, divorce, annulment of marriage or termination of the registered partnership referred to in point 2(b) of Article 2 shall not entail loss of the right of residence of a Union citizen's family members who are not nationals of a Member State where:

    (c) this is warranted by particularly difficult circumstances, such as having been a victim of domestic violence while the marriage or registered partnership was subsisting;


Situation 3

  • If your marriage has lasted more than three years and you have resided in Belgium for at least one year, the Belgian government may also maintain your right of residence.

    Provided that:

    • You are employed, or you are self-employed, or;

    • You have sufficient resources to support yourself and your family without becoming a burden on the Belgian social assistance system, and you have comprehensive health insurance in Belgium, or;

    • You are a member of a household in Belgium that meets these requirements.
