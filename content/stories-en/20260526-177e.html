"Trauma That Triggers Like an Allergic Reaction"


We are used to defining trauma as "ongoing suffering." Yet we overlook another, more hidden kind of harm—It doesn't flare up constantly, but instead, like an allergy, is quietly triggered at specific moments.


"I'm fine" doesn't mean I'm not hurt.


Your idea of "trauma" might look like this: persistent inner turmoil, obvious anxiety, frequent sadness, or an urgent need to talk.


But some impacts are not present every day.


They only strike suddenly when you encounter certain people, scenes, or tones of voice—

anger, resistance, helplessness, breakdown.


These emotional fluctuations are like allergies: they flare up intensely only during certain seasons or when specific "triggers" appear.


When time covers the wound, you return to normal.


You may even feel confused: Why do I react so strongly?


After everything passes, you carry on with life, smile, and say, "I'm fine."



And because you're usually fine, you become the one seen as "more resilient."


Your stability becomes taken for granted; your emotions are postponed, brushed aside, assumed to "not need care."


And yourself—you, simply because you really are okay most of the time—feel you don't even have the right to say you're tired.


You numbly, silently accept all the surface-level assumptions.


No one sees your scarred-up allergic reactions—not even you.



But,

Not being constantly sad doesn't mean no traces remain;

Not being constantly drained doesn't mean you haven't experienced suppression, endurance, or powerlessness.


This time, we want to see this state.


Not the broken, helpless person,

But those—

"Who are mostly fine,

But at certain moments, flash back to the past."


In this art healing salon,

We won't ask you to open yourself up completely or express everything thoroughly—you only need to come with a heart willing to look inward.


What matters to us is not "how painful you are," but whether you're willing to acknowledge that it did affect you.

"I'm fine" can be a wall, or it can be a slightly ajar door. 


We don't deny your stability—we simply invite you to see: 


That "out-of-proportion" intense reaction is not losing control, not proof you haven't moved on, but your genuine way of protecting yourself.


You being fine most of the time is real. That moment of breakdown is also real.


These two things can coexist. You don't have to suffer constantly to have been hurt.


"I'm fine, don't worry"


You can keep using it to respond to the world. But this time, we invite you to use it to gently knock on your own door.
