Content Warning: This questionnaire addresses gender-based violence and related sensitive topics, including but not limited to physical violence, sexual violence, and psychological harm. These topics may trigger emotional distress, discomfort, or memories of personal experiences. Please carefully decide whether to continue based on your own circumstances, and answer the questions in a safe and comfortable environment. If you feel emotionally unstable, we recommend pausing or stopping the questionnaire and seeking support from friends, family, or professional help.


Gender-based violence is a widespread yet often overlooked social issue globally, involving multiple dimensions and causing profound impacts on victims and their families. This questionnaire aims to help you gain a more comprehensive understanding of the diverse manifestations of gender-based violence and the mechanisms behind it, promoting self-awareness and enhanced social consciousness. Through simple self-assessment questions, you can evaluate your level of understanding of this topic and identify blind spots or misconceptions in your knowledge. We also hope that this questionnaire will draw greater attention to the issue of gender-based violence.



Welcome to participate in the Gender-Based Violence Awareness Interactive Quiz

This questionnaire was developed by AWDPI members based on relevant knowledge and literature

and specially released to commemorate the fifth anniversary of AWDPI's founding


Questionnaire Section


★


Gender-Based Violence Interactive Quiz

10 points per question, including multiple-choice and single-choice questions, totaling 10 questions.

For multiple-choice questions, please select in order; the answer and explanation will appear once you click the last correct option.

01

Which of the following most accurately reflects the core definition of "gender-based violence"? (Single choice)

A, All violent behaviors occurring within the family.

B. Sexual violence directed against women.

C, Violence or threats based on gender that result in physical, sexual, or psychological harm.

D. Conflicts and arguments between romantic partners.


02

Which of the following behaviors constitute gender-based violence? (Multiple choice)

A, Husband beating his wife

B, Company defaulting that women cannot be promoted to senior management positions

C, Trafficking of women and forced prostitution

D. Uploading intimate videos of one's boyfriend/girlfriend to the internet


03

In which of the following settings can gender-based violence occur? (Multiple choice)

A, Family

B, Workplace

C, School

D, Community

E, Online


04

What are the possible forms of gender-based violence? (Multiple choice)

A, Sexual violence

B, Physical violence

C. Psychological violence

D, Economic violence

E, Verbal violence

F, Symbolic violence

G, Structural violence


05

Why is the emergence of terms such as "sexual harassment," "emotional labor," and "motherhood penalty" considered important? (Single choice)

A. Because they are legal terms.

B. Because they make it easier for people to blame others.

C. Because they help people accurately express and recognize experiences that were previously overlooked.

D. Because they are popular terms on social media.


06

What does the "counter-narrative" in the #MeToo movement mean? (Single choice)

A, Women begin to refuse to participate in discussions about sexual violence.

B. Women use legal language to describe their experiences of victimization.

C. Women collectively deny the existence of gender-based violence.

D, Women tell their stories in their own words, challenging mainstream definitions.


07

Why are many forms of invisible violence in gender-based violence (such as symbolic violence) not easily recognized? (Single choice)

A. Because they occur in secret places.

B, Because society views them as normal or traditional.

C. Only gender determines the victim's experience.

D, Because these forms of violence are not real violence.


08

Intersectionality theory emphasizes that multiple identities (such as gender, race, class) simultaneously influence a person's experience of discrimination and violence. This means: (Single choice)

A. Only gender determines the victim's experience.

B, The intersection of different identities may expose victims to more complex and severe forms of violence.

C. Race and class are unimportant; only gender matters.

D. Intersectionality theory denies the existence of racial discrimination.


09

Sometimes people claim that certain violence against women is an issue of "cultural tradition." Which of the following illustrates the potential consequences of such a statement? (Single choice)

A, This makes the issue seem like a private family or community matter, ignoring legal and social responsibilities.

B, This means the law will intervene more quickly to solve the problem.

C. This indicates that culture always takes precedence over law.

D. This can increase protection for victims.


10

When a woman describes her bodily experience, for example, feeling someone staring at her with ill intent in the dark, how should we interpret this situation? (Single choice)

A. Being paranoid and overthinking.

B. Exaggerating the situation just to attract attention.

C, Her body is performing an analysis; this experience should be respected.

D. It depends on whether her verbal expression is rational enough.




If you wish, you are also welcome to share your score in the poll below. Please remember, the score is not the key point; what truly matters is every awakening and elevation of awareness. If you have any questions or suggestions, feel free to leave a message and communicate with us.


Thank you for your participation! We hope this self-assessment helps you gain a deeper understanding of gender-based violence in yourself and society, enhances attention to the safety of yourself and others, and collectively promotes the development of respect and equality.
