On May 28, 2026 (World Menstrual Hygiene Day), the Fifth International Conference on Paid Period Leave was held at the House of Lords, UK Parliament. The conference brought together policymakers, scholars, public health experts, women's rights advocates, and representatives from social organizations across multiple countries to engage in in-depth discussions on "Menstrual Dignity," "Workplace Inclusion," and "Gender Equality."


AWDPI was invited to participate in this international conference and joined representatives from the UK, India, Ghana, Nigeria, China, France, Spain, and other countries in witnessing the continued advancement of this global issue on the public policy agenda.



1

From a "Silent Issue" to Global Public Discourse

One of the core consensus points of the conference was: Menstrual health is not only a personal health matter but also a critical issue concerning public health, workplace systems, and gender equality.


Participants widely agreed that advancing gender equality cannot avoid addressing women's real bodily experiences. How to build a more inclusive workplace support system at the institutional level is an essential issue that global labor and social policies must confront in the future.


Throughout multiple discussions, the concept of "menstrual dignity" was repeatedly emphasized, whose significance lies not only in the establishment of policy tools but also in breaking the long-standing social stigma and silence surrounding menstruation.

2

Visual Documentation: From Personal Experience to Global Advocacy

During the conference, attendees viewed content from the documentary *Paid Period Leave*, which documents the journey of advocate Ms. Ranjeeta Priyadarshini in advancing the global "menstrual equity" movement.


The film presents her path from personal experience to public advocacy and international dialogue, recording her ongoing communication with policymakers, civil society organizations, and communities across different countries.


This visual narrative not only illustrates the formation of a policy issue but also reflects how women's health issues can evolve from individual experiences into transnational public agendas.

3

Honoring Advocates and Organizers

AWDPI extends sincere gratitude to the conference organizers, including Global South Corridor, Vishv Umiya Foundation (VUF), ECOF Enterprises, and MHM-KIT, for jointly creating this globally influential dialogue platform.


At the same time, AWDPI especially honors Paid Period Leave the global movement founder Ms. Ranjeeta Priyadarshini to express respect. She has long advocated for "menstrual dignity" to be included in international policy and public discourse, with the core principle—


"We are equal, but not identical."


has become a key intellectual starting point for global discussions on women's health and workplace fairness.


In addition, AWDPI also expresses special appreciation to Lord Rami Ranger for supporting and speaking out for this initiative. He shared his personal experiences during the conference and called for greater societal and policy attention to the structural challenges women face in real life and the workplace, advocating for a more dignified and inclusive institutional environment.



4

Policy and Practice Exploration from a Global Perspective

During the conference, representatives from various countries and institutions shared their regions' explorations and progress in menstrual health policies and practices.


Several speakers pointed out that an increasing number of countries and regions are beginning to attempt integrating menstrual health into workplace support and public policy frameworks, marking a shift of these issues from advocacy to institutionalized discussion.


AWDPI believes the key significance of this trend lies in the emergence of a cross-regional, cross-cultural common language globally, redefining the meaning of "workplace fairness."

5

AWDPI's Focus and Ongoing Actions

As an organization long committed to women's development, education, and cross-cultural exchange, AWDPI has always maintained that:


the essence of menstrual health is a structural issue concerning dignity, equality, and opportunity.


Going forward, AWDPI will continue to monitor developments in this field and advance work in the following areas:

  • tracking the latest global developments in menstrual health and workplace policies

  • promoting cross-cultural dialogue on women's issues and international exchanges

  • supporting evidence-based public discourse and raising social awareness

  • exploring collaboration opportunities with international partners in the field of women's well-being



Conclusion


When a long-ignored issue enters public discourse, change has already begun.


AWDPI will continue to participate in and follow this global dialogue process, working with all stakeholders to promote a more inclusive, equitable, and respectful social environment that honors diverse bodily experiences.
