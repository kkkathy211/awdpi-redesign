Introduction:


February 6 is the United Nations' International Day of Zero Tolerance for Female Genital Mutilation. The practice of female genital mutilation not only harms women's physical health, but also represents an obstacle to their access to equal rights and opportunities. Furthermore, systemic gender discrimination in the workplace similarly restricts women's potential for development. In response, Canadian professor Alison Konrad has proposed targeted recommendations for increasing female representation across different industries.


However, for Asian women facing the dual oppression of sexism and racism, the historical label of "model minority" may lead to disastrous consequences. Recently, the suicide of a Chinese-American girl at Gunn High School in Silicon Valley, who jumped in front of a train, tragically revealed the struggles faced by Chinese-American families.


Despite this, Asian communities continue to demonstrate strong resilience and potential, making ongoing contributions to human well-being. In the United States, a team of Chinese-American scientists made a new discovery offering greater possibilities for cancer treatment. Additionally, more Asian contributions are being recognized and celebrated. During the Lunar New Year, several Chinese-American community organizations jointly held a media roundtable, calling for "respecting, loving, and honoring elders" to acknowledge the contributions of Chinese-American seniors to their communities. At the end of February, the Outstanding Asian American Women Awards Ceremony was held in Los Angeles, recognizing Asian American women's efforts in eliminating gender bias, violence, and advancing gender equality. In Canada, Richmond Chinese residents organized to oppose radical drug policies and took action to advocate for a drug-free community.

Commemorating February 6

"International Day of Zero Tolerance for Female Genital Mutilation"


Every year on February 6, the United Nations observes the International Day of Zero Tolerance for Female Genital Mutilation. This year's theme is "Her Voice. Her Future. Investing in Survivor-Led Movements to End Female Genital Mutilation." On February 6, U.S. Secretary of State Antony Blinken issued a statement calling for an end to female genital mutilation.


Female genital mutilation refers to procedures involving partial or total removal of the external female genitalia or other injury to the female genital organs for non-medical reasons, which may result in severe infections, chronic pain, depression, infertility, or even death.


According to the United Nations Population Fund, in 2024, more than four million girls are at risk of undergoing female genital mutilation, and their opportunities to realize their rights and potential may be severely limited as a result. There is still a long way to go to achieve the goal of ending female genital mutilation by 2030.

Canadian Professor Offers Targeted Recommendations to Increase Female Representation in Business

propose targeted recommendations


Recently, a report based on research by Alison Konrad, Professor of Organizational Behavior at the Ivey Business School, Western University, Canada, titled "Trickle-down and Bottom-up Effects on Workplace Gender Diversity," was released, offering targeted recommendations for organizations and decision-makers to increase female representation at all levels of business.


The report points out that the first step towards achieving gender equality in the workplace should be to analyze the gender composition structure of a specific workplace to assess its gender bias. Previous research has categorized different industries into three types: male-leaning (women account for 16-39%), female-leaning (women account for 61-84%), and balanced (women account for 40-60%). In male-dominated industries, decision-makers should consciously hire more women into mid-level management positions, as they can help convey and validate women's voices and encourage other women to take on non-managerial roles—this is the "trickle-down effect" proposed in the report. Conversely, in female-dominated industries, more women should be hired into non-managerial positions, as this can promote their subsequent advancement and increase female representation in senior and upper management—this is the "bottom-up effect." At the same time, to ensure women are hired, supported, and empowered at all levels, it is also important to hire and promote women into top management.

16-Year-Old Chinese-American Girl at Top Silicon Valley High School Dies by Suicide After Jumping in Front of Train, Possibly Due to School Pressure  


On February 20, a 16-year-old Chinese-American girl at Gunn High School in Silicon Valley, United States, died by suicide after jumping in front of a train near the school's nearby San Antonio train station. According to media reports, she was the captain of the school's cheerleading team, and classmates were unaware of her reasons for suicide, though it was likely related to high school stress.


This is not the first student suicide at Gunn High. The prestigious school in Palo Alto is well known among students and parents for its intense academic pressure and fierce competition. Data shows that as early as 2009, four students died by suicide within seven months. Between 2006 and 2016, Gunn High's suicide rate was four to five times the national average in the U.S., with many of the students who died being of Chinese descent.


A student who attended the school said he had seriously considered suicide multiple times during his time there. He described being tormented by parents' high expectations, self-dissatisfaction, and hatred and jealousy arising from peer comparison. Despite the repeated tragedies, Gunn High's outstanding college admission results continue to attract many Chinese-American families to settle in Palo Alto.

Multiple Chinese-American Elderly Women in San Francisco Fall Victim to "Blessing Scam," Mayor Urges Residents to Stay Alert


On February 20, San Francisco Mayor London Breed visited the Lady Shaw Senior Center to warn residents in the Chinese-American community to be vigilant against the "blessing scam."


The San Francisco Police Department reported that since November of last year, four Chinese-American female residents, aged between 61 and 84, have fallen victim to the "blessing scam," with losses ranging from $20,000 to over $1 million. In these scams, perpetrators first convince victims that their family members are in danger, then tell them that only by bringing their most valuable possessions to a blessing ceremony can the danger be averted—only for the items to be switched when returned.


According to police reports, similar scams occurred in 2012 and 2018. Scammers tend to target vulnerable elderly Chinese-American residents, approaching them when they are out alone, using dialects to gain their trust, and exploiting their relatively disadvantaged social status and anxiety about personal safety. More troubling is that victims may be too afraid or ashamed to report the crime after being defrauded.


Police advise elderly residents to refuse unsolicited approaches when out alone, not to hand over property to strangers, and to seek help from law enforcement promptly.

Chinese-American Scientist Leads Team at U.S. Cancer Research Institution "City of Hope" to Discover Potential New Cancer Treatment


Recently, a research team at "City of Hope," one of the largest cancer research and treatment institutions in the United States, discovered a cellular mechanism that plays a key role in the pathogenicity of cancer cells. On February 5, the study was published in Nature Structural & Molecular Biology, a subsidiary journal of Nature (https://www.nature.com/articles/s41594-024-01211-y), offering new possibilities for cancer treatment.


The team is led by Chinese-American scientist Dr. Chun-Wei (David) Chen, Associate Professor of Systems Biology. They identified two cell surface proteins—integrin αV and β5—that jointly promote cancer cell growth, and then pinpointed a region of integrin αV—the β-propeller domain—that controls the interaction between the two proteins. Dr. Chen's team used digital applications to discover a potential drug compound, Cpd_AV2. When applied to human cancer cells in the lab, integrin αV and β5 rapidly separated and their connection was disrupted, leading to cell death and effectively halting the growth of cancer cell lines.


Although the technology is not yet ready for clinical application, Dr. Chen noted: "This breakthrough opens new pathways for the development of next-generation cancer therapies and marks a significant milestone in City of Hope's fight against cancer."

Multiple Chinese-American Community Organizations Jointly Celebrate Lunar New Year for the First Time, Calling to Honor Elders and Oppose Anti-Asian Discrimination


On the afternoon of February 12, Chinese-American community organizations including On Lok, Self-Help for the Elderly, Asian Pacific Health Care Venture, and the Chinese Community Development Center jointly held a media roundtable in San Francisco's Chinatown to celebrate the Lunar New Year, using the theme "Honor, Love, Respect our Seniors" to promote cultural values of respecting and caring for elders, and emphasizing the importance of culture and heritage.


Several organizational leaders attended the event, with Dr. Michael Schrader, a physician, delivering the opening remarks. He shared that throughout his medical career, he had observed Asian elderly patients facing racial discrimination and potential threats of violence, leading to negative experiences such as fear, loneliness, and depression, and he called on the community to speak up for and care for victims. San Francisco City Councilmember Samantha Chen affirmed the life value of Chinese women and their significant role in cultural heritage during her speech, and expressed gratitude to the organizational representatives present for their contributions toward reducing discrimination against the elderly and improving their living conditions. However, Grace Li, CEO of On Lok, stated that how to continuously provide a safe living environment for the elderly and the shortage of human resources within community organizations remain challenges currently facing Asian community groups.

Canadian MP Kerry-Lynne Findlay condemns racial discrimination and supports Asian residents in fighting for a drug-free community


On February 14, during the 44th Parliament debate in the Canadian House of Commons, Conservative Party MP Kerry-Lynne Findlay (https://openparliament.ca/politicians/kerry-lynne-findlay/) spoke on the issue of anti-Asian discrimination, condemning radical drug policies and supporting Richmond's Asian residents in their campaign for a drug-free community.


In early February, a large number of Chinese residents in Richmond held a massive protest around City Hall, opposing the city council's motion to establish a supervised drug consumption site, chanting "No to drugs." Subsequently, a video circulated online showing a white woman shouting at the protesters, "Go back to Hong Kong" and "Go back to your home country."


MP Kerry-Lynne Findlay condemned such racist remarks and actions, and pointed out the harms of radical drug policies to communities. She stated: "Eight years after the Canadian Prime Minister implemented radical drug policies, our community streets are filled with crime, chaos, drugs, and disorder. The Conservative Party will stand with all Canadians who want to raise their children in a drug-free community, and will always defend their right to say 'no.'"

The Third U.S. Outstanding Asian American Women Awards Gala held in Los Angeles honors ten distinguished Asian American women


On the evening of February 27, the 2024 "Women Who Dared" U.S. Outstanding Asian American Women Awards Ceremony and Charity Gala, jointly hosted by the China Institute and the Yue-Sai Kan One World Foundation, took place in Los Angeles and concluded on March 4.


Wang Wei, spouse of the Consul General of the People's Republic of China in Los Angeles, attended and delivered a speech. She stated that the Asian community is an important part of American society and an inseparable whole, making positive contributions to U.S. economic and social development and cultural integration. She affirmed the tremendous efforts made by Asian women in eliminating gender bias and violence and promoting gender equality.


The ten distinguished Asian American women honored were: Dr. Judy Chu, the first Chinese American woman elected to the U.S. House of Representatives; renowned Chinese American actress Lisa Lu; Anne Shih, Chinese Chair of Bowers Museum; Chinese American fashion designer Sue Wong; Janet Yang, President of the Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences; Chanel Miller, a writer and artist at the forefront of the "Me Too" movement; Maya Royers, CEO of Tetris and Partner and Blue Starups; Shirley Wang, founder and CEO of Plastpro Inc., one of the largest fiberglass door manufacturers in the U.S.; and Lu Zhang, founder and managing partner of Fusion Fund, a top Silicon Valley venture capital firm.


Ms. Yue-Sai Kan, Co-Chair of the China Institute and Chair of the Yue-Sai Kan One World Foundation, said in her speech: "Given the disturbing rise in anti-Asian sentiment in the United States in recent years, honoring and celebrating the achievements and contributions of outstanding Asian American women is more important than ever."
