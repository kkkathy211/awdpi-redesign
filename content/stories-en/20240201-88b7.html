We are living in a dark time, and "hope" is the ability to foresee light even when surrounded by darkness. Your actions can inspire the actions of others. May you find solace, direction, and courage in this era filled with pain, uncertainty, and fear. —Jane Goodall



01


A great adventure


All Creatures Great and Small is a British television series adapted from the book of the same name, telling the story of a young veterinarian who, after graduation, moves to the English countryside on the eve of World War II. The series follows his journey of healing animals and forming warm, healing relationships with the townspeople.


Jane Goodall is much the same. From a young woman to a global public speaker, as a researcher she has long been committed to bringing "hope" to the world. Through founding the Roots & Shoots organization and launching the "Compassionate Communities" program, she has not only helped impoverished women access microloans and education to escape poverty, but also emphasized caring for people first—enabling them to have greater capacity to care for nature. Answering the call of her mission, Jane stepped out of her comfort zone, found strength, and used her talents, wisdom, and down-to-earth approach to embark on a great adventure.



02


The meaning of "hope"


Jane once told a story from rural China: Jia Haixia and Jia Wenqi planted over 10,000 trees, helping restore degraded and polluted land around their village—despite one being blind and the other having no arms.



"Hope" is an indomitable spirit. Just as suffering can cause pain, it can also elevate people—it all depends on individual choice. If we can find meaning in suffering and use it to benefit ourselves and others, then suffering can become a gift in life.


03


The Babel dilemma


In the Book of Genesis in the Old Testament, it is recorded that humanity once united to build a tower reaching heaven—the Tower of Babel. But God, aware of humanity's plan, sought to stop them by making people speak different languages, rendering communication impossible. Thus, the Tower of Babel project failed, and humanity scattered across the earth. Today's "Sixth Mass Extinction" has already wiped out 60% of mammals, birds, fish, and reptiles from the planet. Humanity faces a modern "Babel dilemma"—not only due to linguistic barriers that prevent mutual understanding, but also due to a lack of respect for nature, leading to ecological collapse.

In the 1960s, Rachel Carson's Silent Spring was published, revealing the immense ecological damage caused by the pesticide DDT. "Understanding leads to care, care leads to helping others, and helping others leads to helping ourselves." Despite standing on the brink of extinction, humanity still has the chance for redemption through action. Don Merton brought the critically endangered black robin back from the edge of extinction; today, there are about 250 of them. Jane launched a microloan project in Gombe, helping local women escape poverty, access education, and become partners in conservation efforts there.



04


Gathering stones to form a mountain


True hope requires action and commitment; even the smallest action can bring change Hope does not deny the existence of suffering and danger, but neither is it trapped by them. Taking action and recognizing that every individual can make a difference is crucial. We need to set realistic goals and establish achievable paths to reach them, while also cultivating the confidence to succeed and the support needed to overcome difficulties along the way, Social support is essential for sustaining hope.


Jane believes people should bring children into nature and let them learn through practice. In 1991, Jane launched the youth program "Roots & Shoots," whose core philosophy is "Every individual matters. Every individual has a role to play. Our choices determine the difference we make." She believes even children can drive real change.



In Uganda, a group of girls held up signs reading "Keep Our Village Green," calling for environmental protection. In Tanzania, Roots & Shoots members are living out the organization's values of knowledge, compassion, and action. In the Republic of the Congo, children are preparing to join a tree-planting project… Today's youth are not only better informed, but also more engaged in decision-making and political processes. Even in the most desperate circumstances, as long as humanity still believes in hope, change is still possible. The future is in our own hands.



Everyone has their role. Everyone has a reason for being here. Each person can become a messenger of hope, creating positive change now and in the future.
