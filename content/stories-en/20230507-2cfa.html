UK Chinese Women Connect (UKCWC) is a UK-based charity established to connect and empower Chinese women in the UK, share life wisdom, inspire one another, showcase their talents, enhance the influence of Chinese women in Britain, and meet the mutual support needs of the Chinese community.


UKCWC hosted its annual awards ceremony and Lunar New Year banquet at the University of Cambridge. Diane and Rainey from the Avoice team were invited to attend, joining over 100 members of the network and guests including the King's representative, the Mayor of Cambridge, and Cambridge city councillors at Corpus Christi College, University of Cambridge, to witness and honour outstanding Chinese women who are shining in fields such as science and technology, education, and public welfare.


This exchange not only brought deep reflections to the invited participants but also provided the Avoice National Office with fresh ideas and new perspectives for future offline work.



Diane and Rainey were invited to the banquet as core members of the Avoice UK National Office. Diane joined Avoice in 2021 and currently serves as Administrative Secretary and Trustee of the Avoice UK National Office, primarily responsible for work related to the Language Division at Avoice headquarters as well as collaboration and event organisation in the UK.


Rainey joined Avoice in October 2020, having worked for one year each in the Liaison Management Division and the Partnership Development Division. She is now Director of the General Office and Trustee of the Avoice UK National Office, responsible for collaboration and event organisation in the UK. They will share from a first-person perspective their observations, experiences, and feelings from the banquet.





The Invitation and Attendance—

          A Synchronized "Mutual Journey"


Avoice and UKCWC have maintained close cooperation and communication. Ms. Yang Qun, founder of UKCWC, has also provided valuable advice on Avoice's development direction and approach. Therefore, when this valuable opportunity for in-person exchange arose, Ms. Yang invited members of the Avoice UK Office to attend. Diane and Rainey, inspired by Ms. Yang Qun's compelling speech at the Avoice Global Development Forum, had already developed interest and curiosity about the UKCWC organisation. Carrying deep admiration and respect for Ms. Yang as an outstanding woman, Diane and Rainey entered the banquet venue.


The formal dress code and rigorous event schedule initially made Diane and Rainey feel slightly nervous, but as they mingled with the women around them and received kindness and encouragement from those present, all their anxiety instantly dissipated.



Diane: "Rainey and I were among the younger guests that night. When the older sisters heard what we were doing and learned the roles we held, they showed us tremendous kindness and kept offering us advice. Then we realised that the process of socialising wasn't as difficult as Rainey and I had imagined."


In addition, the entire banquet—from preparation to execution and post-event—was filled with thoughtful details and heartfelt touches. The banquet hall, adorned with traditional Chinese red lanterns, brimmed with festive atmosphere. A vibrant mix of magic shows, singing, dance performances, and red envelope lucky draws immersed all attendees and quickly narrowed the distance between them.


Rainey: "These Chinese-style decorations inside a Cambridge hall gave me a strong sense of East-West cultural interaction. Being wrapped in this Chinese cultural atmosphere made me feel less tense—I felt as if I had returned home, just like celebrating the Lunar New Year back in China."


Discovery and Visibility—

Chinese Women Overseas Need to Be Seen




Recalling the most memorable part of the banquet, both Diane and Rainey mentioned a speech on how Chinese women navigate self-acceptance within a foreign cultural environment.


Speaker Ms. Yang Qun emphasised that Chinese women often face cultural conflicts between their native heritage and local British culture. As modern women bearing Eastern roots, we must break through external stereotypes, shed the mindset of being outsiders, avoid being confined by traditional habits, and actively express and present ourselves, striving to integrate into mainstream British society so that more Chinese women can be seen.


As Chinese women living and studying overseas themselves, Diane and Rainey deeply resonated with Ms. Yang's views and found new ways to balance and resolve cultural conflicts. Diane also shared with us a small story about standing together with the Chinese community to speak out against unfair treatment from others, reiterating that the voices of Chinese women must not be ignored.


Diane: "I strongly agree with one approach Ms. Yang Qun mentioned: don't immediately place yourself in a different identity, because when we communicate with others, we do so as individuals. As long as you approach with sincerity and friendliness, I believe most people are quite kind. As for certain stereotypes, we can't change everyone's views, but when they directly attack our community, I think we need to speak up for the group we belong to.. If we don't address it, they might continue doing so."


Rainey: "In my year in the UK, I haven't encountered serious discrimination against East Asian women, but as a woman in a foreign country, I remain cautious about safety issues. Regarding discrimination, my view is quite similar to Diane's: I don't want to fall into a cycle of self-proving. I'll live my life well. If others hold certain views, I can't change them—so I'll focus on my own work and gradually spread my values to influence those I can reach." "


The Future and Expectations—

Avoice and UKCWC Moving Forward Together


At the gala, Ms. Yang Qun shared that in UKCWC's early days, she received pleas for help from women experiencing domestic violence. Lacking professional expertise in domestic violence rescue, she realised the importance of collaborating with specialised organisations to support overseas Chinese women. She then introduced Avoice to the audience and invited Diane and Rainey to stand up, presenting them to all members and guests.


"When the two of us stood up, the entire room burst into applause. The power of that applause was incredibly moving. At that moment, I felt Rainey and I weren't representing ourselves as individuals, but rather a microcosm of Avoice—everyone seemed to be clapping for Avoice itself." "


As representatives, Diane and Rainey actively sought collaboration opportunities for Avoice at the banquet, but in truth, when a group of people with shared goals come together, mutual attraction, connection, and inspiration happen naturally.



After Ms. Yang introduced Avoice, many guests approached Diane and Rainey directly, offering valuable suggestions and feedback for Avoice's long-term development. They expressed deep appreciation for the kindness of all Avoice members and said they would do their best to support Avoice in the future. Moreover, their affirmation and high praise for Avoice's work brought tremendous encouragement and confidence to every Avoice member.


Diane: "There is such a large and outstanding community of Chinese women here in the UK. I'm truly excited about Avoice's development here, and I also hope we can offer space and hope to women experiencing domestic violence who don't know where to turn. Deep down, I truly believe we can do meaningful work here. "


The winner of the evening's "Women in Finance" award told Diane and Rainey: 'The fact that you're willing to do this work is already meaningful. And your sincerity is so pure—countless people will surely reach out to help you.'


'That sentence deeply moved me. It made me feel that what we're doing matters. Although our organisation still has many areas to improve—such as fundraising in the future—ultimately, our hearts remain pure, and we genuinely want to do rescue work well. This has strengthened our determination to carry out this rescue mission in the UK.'



After the banquet, Diane and Rainey had breakfast the next morning with Ms. Yang Qun, visited a friend with Ella—an Avoice Youth Advisor they had met at the banquet—and went on a "Cambridge adventure" with Silas, a local UK volunteer dedicated to protecting Chinese women. Under the vast night sky of Britain, this banquet connected this lovely, kind, and like-minded group of people, empowering one another. We believe that in the future, Avoice and UKCWC will continue to walk hand in hand, showcasing the unique charm of Chinese women and enhancing the influence of Chinese women in the UK.





A single drop of water can easily evaporate, but when gathered together, water becomes infinitely powerful. This UKCWC dinner showed us women shining and living freely in various fields, revealed the possibilities created when women with shared ideals empower, inspire, and draw strength from one another, and further highlighted the value and significance of Avoice's work. "With persistence, even metal and stone can be carved." Avoice will move forward courageously on the path of protecting overseas Chinese women, carrying our pure original aspiration and the encouragement and expectations of our predecessors. As Diane said, "I hope Avoice can accompany more women on their journey toward freedom. I hope that women who encounter mud and hardship along the way can reach out and hold Avoice's hand, and keep moving forward."
