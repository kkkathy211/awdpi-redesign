On March 15, the international nonprofit AWDPI (Asian Women Development Plan International) launched an online specialized training session on methodology for contract drafting and review, led by volunteer lawyer Chen. This training aimed to strengthen the team's professional capabilities in contract management, reinforce legal compliance in organizational operations, and enhance the standardization and professionalism of contract drafting and review.



The training focused on the core value and practical essentials of contract management in the AI era, systematically explaining the end-to-end methodology for contract drafting and review. Drawing on her experience in international legal work and AWDPI's nature as a nonprofit organization, Attorney Chen began by highlighting the importance of contract management and analyzed how the core value of legal professionals lies in evaluating, adapting, and optimizing AI-generated outputs, while also emphasizing, given the organization's sensitivity around fundraising funds and diverse external collaborations, the critical importance of standardized contracts at the early stage in mitigating legal and reputational risks later on.



During the training, Attorney Chen thoroughly broke down a five-step practical approach to contract drafting and review, clearly outlining the structural logic of key clauses such as party information, rights and obligations, intellectual property, liability for breach, and dispute resolution, and focusing on the review priorities for high-frequency risk areas such as party qualifications, payment and delivery, intellectual property, and confidentiality clauses. Using real organizational cases, she explained the selection of dispute resolution methods, standard requirements for contract signing and archiving, and differences in contract effectiveness across legal jurisdictions. Additionally, she shared practical resources such as the professional book *The Perfect Contract* and legal AI tools like Fatianshi (Lawyer Angel), guiding participants on how to appropriately use AI tools for foundational tasks and combine practical scenarios with professional judgment for precise review.


In her summary, Attorney Xia Mengya emphasized that AI tools can efficiently handle basic review tasks, while industry experience, communication skills, and final decision-making ability have become core competencies, encouraging team members to enhance both their use of AI tools and professional expertise to meet the contract management needs of nonprofit organizations.


This methodology training on contract drafting and review marks a key step by AWDPI toward building a systematic compliance framework, effectively enhancing the team's professional competence and practical skills in contract management. Going forward, AWDPI's Legal Affairs Department will continue to conduct a series of internal professional trainings, continuously improving the organization's compliance management system and strengthening risk prevention across all operational areas, safeguarding the organization's public welfare operations and external collaborations with professional legal expertise, and promoting the steady implementation of public welfare projects within a legal and compliant framework thereby achieving dual advancement in organizational development and philanthropic impact.
