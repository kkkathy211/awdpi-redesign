THERE'S STILL TOMORROW


Do you also do this?

That fitness plan you signed up for but never started…

That bestseller you bought half a year ago, still sealed in plastic…

That "I'm sorry" or "I love you" you've rehearsed in your mind countless times, yet never spoken aloud…

And that movie lingering on your watchlist, always postponed with the thought, "I'll watch it tomorrow"



We always assume there will be countless tomorrows

So we comfortably waste away today

So we leave the books we meant to read untouched on the shelf

We set aside the people we wanted to spend time with, and defer our dreams to "later"


But will tomorrow really come?

Or is it just an excuse we use to avoid today?

We squander "today" and overdraw on "tomorrow"

Until life's sudden blow wakes us up:


What if there is no tomorrow?


Or perhaps you've also had moments like this:

Surrounded by endless trivialities—work, family, relationships…

Like an invisible net, gradually making you forget who "I" really am

We endure, we compromise, we tell ourselves "tomorrow will be better." But we bury our true selves within every "never mind" of today


But, If "tomorrow" is just a repeat of today, what is the point of this endurance?


What if the hope for "tomorrow" depends on breaking free today…



This time, we want to invite you to temporarily set aside your anxiety and fantasies about tomorrow. Let's step into a film that has touched countless hearts—Tomorrow Is Another Day


In 1946, a woman named Delia lived exactly this kind of "daily routine." She was not only a mother of three children but also had to care for her paralyzed father-in-law, all while enduring a violent husband. Her living space was confined to the kitchen stove, the sickbed, and the fists of her husband.


The most remarkable aspect of this film is not its portrayal of suffering, but its precise capture of the moment when "awakening" occurs: When she realizes her daughter's fate is about to mirror her own, and when she first understands that the "voter card" in her hand represents an unprecedented power of choice—something dormant within her suddenly awakens.



Tomorrow Is Another Day is not a distant hymn; it is a mirror reflecting our own lives


It makes us reflect:

How can we fulfill family responsibilities while protecting personal space and aspirations, finding our own balance and anchor?


We explore:

When external expectations clash with inner desires, how can we reconcile them and find a path to live out our values in reality?


It pushes us to ask even deeper:

In an environment full of real-world constraints, how can individuals maintain inner motivation and direction to achieve continuous growth and breakthrough?



We don't want this film to become another item on your "I'll watch it tomorrow" list. We've decided to host a special offline screening and themed sharing event this weekend, on November 15


This is not just a film screening, but a collective reflection and gentle farewell to the concept of "time"


We are not inviting ordinary audiences, but fellow travelers who long to break free from the "tomorrow cycle"

Organizers

Organizer


The Global Shapers Community (GSC) was born from the World Economic Forum (WEF). Founded in 2011 by WEF's founder Professor Klaus Schwab, GSC Shenzhen is the only branch of the World Economic Forum's Global Shapers Community in Shenzhen, serving future leaders aged 18–28 with entrepreneurial experience and social contributions. The project aims to leverage young people's innovation and passion to build a more prosperous and inclusive world. Community initiatives include social enterprise promotion, AI literacy, women's growth, social-emotional learning, and mental and physical well-being.

Lean In Shenzhen is a non-profit public community dedicated to professional women, committed to building a diverse platform that awakens self-awareness, uncovers women's charm, and unlocks women's potential.


[Special thanks to our co-organizer, Shenyuan Youth Innovation Center]


Sunyard Youth Innovation Hub, founded in 2018, is a purely nonprofit growth platform under Guangdong Sunyard Investment. The platform integrates think tank resources from "Mentors from a Hundred Industries," upholding the core values of "Moral Integrity, Doing Good, and Great Love." Through a multi-stage curriculum system, it helps young entrepreneurs comprehensively enhance their mental strength, intellectual capacity, and practical skills—from principles to techniques—saving them three to five years of detours. Since its establishment, nearly ten thousand young people from the Greater Bay Area have registered for training at Sunyard Youth Innovation Hub. In the future, the hub will be based in Shenzhen and extend its influence across the Greater Bay Area, continuing to strive to become a cradle for young entrepreneurial leaders and a community for development.
