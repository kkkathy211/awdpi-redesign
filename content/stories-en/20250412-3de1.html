Amy once traveled to France full of hopes for love and the future. In France, she met her current husband, and the two quickly got to know each other, fell in love, and married. Not long after their marriage, Amy became pregnant and gave birth in April 2023. However, this marriage soon turned into a prison that trapped her.


Shortly after giving birth, Amy's husband suddenly abandoned her and attempted to strip her of custody of their child. Alone in a foreign country, she faced numerous challenges: her husband's false accusations, unfamiliarity with the French legal system, and the fact that all of her child's identity documents were under her husband's control. Not only did her husband obtain a restraining order in France prohibiting her from contacting him, but he also used legal means to evict Amy from their home, leaving her with nowhere to go while physically and mentally exhausted. Amy said: "I think the most unfair thing is that I reported to the police very early on, but the police took no action at all. Then, when he turned around and accused me, I was immediately arrested."


The collapse of this cross-border marriage forced Amy to travel back and forth between China and France, facing complex legal procedures and deep psychological trauma.



1

Judicial abuse and gender bias: victims who are blamed instead


After being abandoned by her husband, Amy tried to protect her rights through legal channels, but she quickly discovered that the French judicial system was not on her side. She reported to the police multiple times, accusing her husband of psychological and physical abuse, but received no effective response. Yet, when her husband made a false accusation, Amy was swiftly detained.


"The police took you to detention based solely on his one-sided account—with no evidence at all?" Amy recalled in the interview: "I don't know about that, because at the time we were already separated." Amy explained that she was the one who had called the police, but her husband turned the tables and accused her of being the abuser. Without verifying the facts, the police took her away.


Under the French legal system, her husband easily manipulated the legal process, obtaining a one-sided restraining order from the court that prohibited Amy from contacting him, while he remained free to initiate legal actions against her at any time. Amy said helplessly: "The law is enabling his abandonment of me." In this legal battle, Amy was completely on the defensive.


French courts issued a mandatory court summons to Amy. If she failed to appear in court on time, she could face up to three years in prison. Amy said: "If I don't appear in court, they can directly sentence me to three years in prison. That's clearly stated in the law." At that time, due to financial hardship and health issues, she could not even afford the cost of traveling to France for the court hearing.



2

Transnational Legal Dilemmas: Identity Dependency and Custody Battles


Amy's right to reside in France was entirely dependent on her husband. After the marriage broke down, her legal status fell into crisis. Her husband unilaterally terminated the lease agreement with the landlord, resulting in Amy being forcibly evicted while she was ill. Although she legally refused the termination, the French law protecting tenants from winter evictions failed to protect her. Amy lamented: "I was kicked out while sick and homeless."


Amy's child has remained stranded in China, with all important administrative documents and identification held by her husband and his family. This has made it extremely difficult for Amy to fight for her child's rights. She said: "Although my child is now with me, all the administrative documents are in the man's hands—that's why the child is stranded in China."


Facing the upcoming custody battle, Amy is filled with anxiety. According to Chinese law, custody of children under two years old is generally granted to the mother. However, the lengthy divorce litigation process has made this legal protection fragile. "I'm afraid that by the time the final judgment is made, my child will already be over two years old."

3

Dual oppression of mind and law: a mother with nowhere to turn


According to the interview, Amy's husband and his family subjected her to long-term psychological control and emotional abuse, even sexually assaulting her during and after her pregnancy. Due to prolonged psychological and physical torment, Amy was diagnosed with PTSD (post-traumatic stress disorder). She said: "I've been bullied for a long time by my husband and his family, and I've already developed PTSD." Despite this, Amy's mental and physical condition has not been adequately considered in the judicial process.


She has repeatedly tried to seek legal aid, but under French law, divorce proceedings require hiring a lawyer, and Amy, facing financial hardship, cannot afford the high legal fees. "What I need is not just legal consultation, but a lawyer who can truly represent me in court," Amy explained.


Even more despairing, women's protection organizations in France are overstretched, and she has yet to receive any substantial help. Meanwhile, the divorce proceedings in China are progressing slowly due to complex procedures, and her child's custody and her husband's illegal actions remain unaddressed by the law.

4

Persistence and resistance: her voice deserves to be heard


Despite being deeply trapped in hardship, Amy has not given up. She hopes to defend her rights through the legal system, and more importantly, to raise public awareness through media attention and expose the oppression she has suffered from her husband and his family. "The most important thing I want to do is create public pressure," Amy said firmly. "I want to know—can this society really allow them to act so brazenly?"


She possesses audio recordings, photos, and other evidence of her husband's violence and psychological abuse. However, her family fears escalating the conflict and does not want her to file criminal charges immediately. Amy is also seeking an appropriate way to reveal the truth in order to gain more legal and social support.


Facing an uncertain future, Amy continues to persevere. She said: "My life goal is to seek justice and revenge. That's the belief that keeps me going."




If you encounter similar difficulties in France, you can:

Apply for free legal aid (aide juridictionnelle) in France. Consult local legal aid centers or community service agencies;


Collect evidence as much as possible (such as photos, recordings, medical diagnoses, witness statements, etc.). Even if falsely accused of being the abuser, explain the situation to the police and submit evidence;


France has many organizations that provide shelters specifically for victims of domestic violence (such as Fédération Nationale Solidarité Femmes), which can offer temporary housing and support;


Contact French child protection agencies (such as ASE - Aide Sociale à l'Enfance) to protect your child's rights. These agencies can intervene to investigate the child's safety and custody issues; if documents are withheld by the man, they can be reclaimed or reissued through a lawyer or court order;


French law allows victims of domestic violence to apply for an independent residence permit (titre de séjour), even if they previously relied on a spouse visa. You can consult the local prefecture (préfecture) for application procedures;


Contact local Chinese organizations or women's rights groups (such as La Maison des Femmes or SOS Femmes). They may provide translation, legal aid, or psychological support.
