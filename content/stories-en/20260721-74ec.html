Women's bodies and choices have never seemed to be fully their own to decide.


Finding a partner, having children, or even simply wanting to live life on one's own terms—these are always entangled with an invisible set of "standards": what counts as appropriate, what counts as respectable, and whose stories are considered worth telling?


This time, we have invited three scholars, coming from different yet interconnected research fields, to discuss together the complex entanglements among gender, race, and the body.

Is the body merely a "biological existence," or is it a political site that carries gender, racial, and class orders?

Are women's reproductive choices truly autonomous? Are our "free choices" in intimate relationships also shaped by certain unspoken norms?

How do changes in the social environment affect women's sexual and reproductive health rights? And what role does masculinity play in this context?

When "race" enters the Chinese context, how does it intersect with gender, class, and nationality?

Which bodies can move freely across different spaces, and which are always seen as "strangers"? Which women remain under constant scrutiny, and which remain nearly invisible?

And can the "intersectionality" theory, born out of Western contexts, adequately explain gender, race, and class issues in China?

When we talk about women's bodies, what are we really talking about?

Panelists





Bai Yiyun

PhD candidate in Sociology at the University of Cambridge, holding a Master's degree in Sociology of Media and Culture from Cambridge and a Master's in Media and Communications from the London School of Economics and Political Science.


Her doctoral research examines cross-border assisted reproductive experiences among single women and lesbian couples in China through an intersectional lens, with a particular focus on how racialized notions shape gamete donor selection. She primarily employs qualitative methods such as multi-sited ethnography and is also interested in arts-based and creative research methodologies.


Beyond academic research, she actively engages in anti-racism, public engagement, and early-career scholar community work. She previously served as the PhD student representative in the Department of Sociology at Cambridge and is currently a member of the Young Researchers' Committee at Cambridge Reproduction.



Zhong Yue

PhD candidate in Sociology at the University of Cambridge, researching Chinese women in intimate relationships with Black African men, focusing on how race, gender, nationality, and intimacy intersect and shape women's partnerships, family life, and everyday social experiences.


Her research combines ethnographic fieldwork in Guangzhou, in-depth interviews with women across different Chinese cities, and analysis of online discussions on related topics.


Prior to her PhD, she earned a Master's in Intercultural Communication and Education from Durham University and completed her undergraduate studies in English and Arabic at Sun Yat-sen University. Beyond academic research, she has long been committed to gender equality in academia and the workplace and has participated in community and public welfare initiatives supporting the development of young female researchers.



Yang Jinhong


PhD candidate at the School of Nursing and Midwifery, Queen's University Belfast. She completed her undergraduate and master's studies at Xiangya School of Nursing, Central South University, and has studied abroad at the National University of Singapore.


Her research focuses on sexual and reproductive health and related rights, particularly on gender norms, masculinity, sexual health, and HIV prevention among university students in China. She aims to promote more inclusive and equitable sexual and reproductive health services by exploring the relationship between gender norms and health behaviors.
