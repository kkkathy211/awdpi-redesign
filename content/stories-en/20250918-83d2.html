In addressing domestic violence, we often rely on Western research and intervention models. Yet, these models may not always be culturally appropriate in different cultural contexts.


Guest Information



Dr. Romina Istratii is the Director of the Institute for Domestic Violence, Religion and Migration (IDVRM) and a Research Fellow at the Institute for Orthodox Christian Studies (IOCS), Cambridge. Until May 2025, she was a UK Research and Innovation (UKRI) Future Leaders Fellow, Lecturer in Religion, International Development and Violence at the School of History, Religions and Philosophies, SOAS, University of London, and Co-Chair of the Centre for World Christianity at SOAS. Between 2020 and 2025, she served as Principal Investigator of the "dldl/ድልድል" project, which developed culturally sensitive and faith-informed responses to domestic violence in Africa and Europe.


Dr. Romina Istratii has 15 years of experience conducting and leading impact-oriented research, specializing in domestic violence and related forms of violence within religious, ethnic minority, and migrant communities in Africa and Europe, as well as developing intervention strategies. She has been at the forefront of decolonization debates in international research and development, with a focus on epistemological reflection, decolonial ethics, and equitable collaboration models. She has published innovative interdisciplinary research across development studies, religious studies, anthropology, gender-based violence studies, and decolonial studies, with over 130 publications recorded under her ORCID ID. Dr. Istratii is currently a member of the UK Civilian Stabilisation Group's Expert Pool on Sexual Violence in Conflict, and frequently speaks at events for organizations such as ARMA (Association of Research Managers and Administrators), UKCDR (UK Collaborative on Development Research), RLUK (Research Libraries UK), UKRIO (UK Research Integrity Office), and other UK research institutions on topics including research ethics, decolonization, and international collaboration.



Join our conversation


Addressing domestic violence does not have a single "one-size-fits-all" solution. Women and families in different societies face unique circumstances and challenges; only by respecting local knowledge and culture can interventions truly take root and be effective.


Romina Istratii will share insights from her project—Project dldl—in this lecture, offering us a culturally sensitive perspective on domestic violence intervention..


We call on more practitioners, researchers, and all those concerned with intersectionality, equity, and cultural inclusivity to join this dialogue.
