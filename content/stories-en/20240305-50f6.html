The silenced "minor ailments"



Body "abnormal" signals have existed for a long time before one finally gathers the courage to seek medical help, often still doubting whether it was their own fault.


When visiting a gynecologist, facing a male doctor, one may feel too embarrassed to speak up.


After being diagnosed with vaginitis, corpus luteum rupture, polycystic ovary syndrome... when reporting the condition to a male supervisor, one might be "advised" to step down from the project team.


……


Do these scenarios trigger feelings of guilt and shame in you?


But in fact, these are not your fault..



According to "Yentl Syndrome," women must provide additional evidence to doctors to prove their medical conditions are as serious as men's [1].


Clinically, symptoms described by women are more likely to be dismissed as psychological issues; even the English word "hysteria" originates from the ancient Greek word for "womb" (Hystera) [2].


In healthcare, male-centered models of pain assessment and treatment are more commonly adopted.



So...


do you also want to let your "minor ailments" breathe?


Do you also want to share your "minor ailment" stories with us?





The chat window is already open



With International Women's Day approaching, we want to collect "minor ailment" stories from 100 women, to understand what you've experienced and how you felt.


We also want to tell everyone that it's okay to speak up about minor ailments at a normal volume, that a body with minor ailments is still a lovable body ❤
