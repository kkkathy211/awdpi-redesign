The buzz of International Women's Day is gradually fading. The flowers, gifts, and celebration posters in social media feeds resemble a warm burst of fireworks. They are beautiful, but they will eventually dissipate.


After the fireworks fade, what remains—the daily emptiness—is the true backdrop of most of our real lives.


Today, we want to talk to you about something more tangible—about the places the fireworks don't reach, about those quiet, aching moments deep inside us that we rarely speak of.


01

Scenario One

You and your male colleague have equal qualifications, and your performance is even better.

But the promotion goes to him.

The reasons given: "Girls don't need to work so hard," "She'll have children later; her stability is lower."

02

Scenario Two

You work late into the night and step out of the office building.

You instinctively grip your keys tightly, your knuckles turning white.

You don't dare wear headphones, and you quicken your pace.

It's not because you're timid—it's because the world has taught you: nighttime is not particularly kind to women.

03

Scenario Three

Returning home for the Lunar New Year, sitting at the dinner table.

"Do you have a boyfriend yet?"

"Why haven't you married? In a couple more years, no one will want you."

As a woman, you're constantly racing against the social clock.

04

Scenario Four

A phrase you've heard since childhood:

"Girls, well, their logical thinking in science is just a bit weaker."

"Girls lack staying power; they fall behind once they reach high school."

"A female driver? Never mind, I'll drive instead."


These words are like dust in the air—invisible, yet with every breath, you swallow them in.


If these scenarios feel familiar, then you've already touched upon a term: structural injustice


If society is a sophisticated, complex machine, structural injustice causes every small part of that machine to tilt slightly, making it less accurate.


The history, culture, rules, and habits of this society all carry a subtle tilt. From the day they are born, women carry a little extra burden, take a few more detours, and endure a few more cold stares.


That's why we've created two surveys. Not to complain, but to sincerely ask you, the person reading this: In these everyday gaps, how do you perceive this tilted "social" machine?


And more importantly—


Back in daily life, are you willing to do something—just a little—to tighten that crooked screw, even slightly?


A small correction can take many forms:


01

In the workplace

Next time in a meeting, speak up with your ideas.

Don't fear being called "aggressive."

02

At home

Write down the household chores and divide them clearly.

No longer defaulting to "washing dishes is my job" or "taking care of the kids is my responsibility."

03

In education

Tell your daughter: "You can become anyone—scientist, athlete, president."

And tell your son: "You must learn to respect, listen, and empathize."

04

In situations where you witness injustice

Don't stay silent—just say, "This is wrong. This is unfair."

Standing beside your sisters is itself a form of strength.


Every small correction is a crack breaking through the soil

Change never happens overnight. It comes from ordinary people like us—after seeing the truth—still choosing to

not pretend not to see.

not tell ourselves, "Forget it, I'm used to it."
