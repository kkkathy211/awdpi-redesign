Good Luck to You, Leo Grande


Good Luck to You, Leo Grande stars renowned British actress and Oscar winner Emma Thompson. Through a light-hearted comedic format, the film explores popular women's issues such as female aging, appearance anxiety, and sexual experience. As the film illustrates, middle-aged and older women also have sexual needs and desires. Yet in traditional patriarchal societies—both in the West and in East Asian societies—women's sexual needs are often overshadowed by those of their male partners. In matters of sex, women are frequently seen as objects rather than subjects of desire. Social expectations around the roles of wife and mother further contribute to a certain desexualization, in stark contrast to the stereotypical image of the promiscuous, unmarried single woman, who is often assumed to have a rich sex life, while the sexual lives of married women are seen as serving only procreation.


Like Nancy in the film, her sex life with her husband is dull, boring, and unsatisfactory, and she has little confidence in her own sexual appeal. But this does not mean she lacks sexual desire. On the contrary, she has written a perfect sexual experience on her bucket list. After meeting the sex worker Leo, she begins a journey of repeatedly embracing and satisfying herself, placing her own needs first.


This film carries the color of the Western sexual liberation movement, normalizing sex itself and emphasizing that middle-aged women should also acknowledge their sexual needs and desires. Having sexual desire is nothing to be ashamed of, and pursuing a perfect sexual climax even less so. The film's ending does not suggest that Nancy suddenly embraces herself or achieves sexual liberation. But at least she has taken the first step toward dual liberation of body and mind.

Erin Brockovich


Based on a true story, this film depicts a single mother with no legal background who, through immense hardship and with unwavering courage and perseverance, wins the largest civil compensation case in U.S. history. The protagonist, Erin, a former beauty queen, had spent six years as a housewife—such sacrifice leading to two divorces, less than one hundred dollars in savings, inability to pay her phone bill, and constant pressure from overdue bills.


Yet her kindness, compassion, honesty, straightforwardness, and relentless determination enable her to eventually earn people's respect and achieve success. With extraordinary resilience, Erin overcomes unimaginable difficulties, proving to the world the value of a so-called "weak woman" and carving out a new path in life.

Intimate War

In this documentary, director Lucia Gaja interviews five women who have experienced domestic violence, each from a different country—Mexico, Spain, the United States, India, and Finland.


It shows us that violence against women is not caused solely by low levels of national economic development or specific cultural factors, but is a globally widespread issue. This documentary won the 2018 Ariel Award for Best Documentary Feature at the 60th Mexican Academy of Film Arts and Sciences, and was also nominated for Best Picture.

Desert Flower


The film is based on the true story of international supermodel Waris Dirie, telling the real-life experience of a girl brutalized by the African practice of female genital mutilation. Across the vast African desert, the optimistic and resilient desert flower blooms stubbornly, bringing a glimmer of life to this barren land. In this nation covered in endless sand, if we compare the backward customs that harm women to the desert, then Waris, through her own strength and determination, was the first to make a flower bloom in this desert.
