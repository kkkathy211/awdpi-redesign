According to a report released by the China Internet Network Information Center (CNNIC), internet penetration has exceeded 70%, marking the arrival of a digital society where digital media has become the mainstream channel for information dissemination.


While we rely on digital media to access information, digital media is also profoundly shaping people's understanding of the world. From the perspective of communication in the digital media era, why is enhancing women's data literacy crucial for their development? How can women improve their data literacy?


In this episode of [Voice of Data], our online salon, we are honored to invite Dr. Xu Yingchun to share insights on "Feminism and Enhancing Data Literacy." Dr. Xu is a former associate professor at Zhejiang University of Media and Communications, holds a Ph.D. in Communication from Zhejiang University, and is currently studying in the United States at Rutgers University, pursuing a Ph.D. in Media Studies..


Through this session, we hope more modern women will pay greater attention to and actively learn about data literacy in the digital media era, enabling personal growth and development, gaining momentum to move forward, and narrowing the gender gap.

       Xu Yingchun     

Ph.D. candidate in Media Studies at Rutgers University, USA

Ph.D. in Communication, Zhejiang University

Holds a Ph.D. in Communication from Zhejiang University. During her career, she worked as a news journalist at Zhejiang Television and served in communications for the Yangtze River Conservation Program at the World Wide Fund for Nature (WWF), the world's largest environmental organization. She also taught courses in data journalism, media criticism, and communication studies for many years at Zhejiang University of Media and Communications as an associate professor.


She is currently studying at Rutgers University in the United States, pursuing a Ph.D. in Media Studies. Her primary research interests lie in media and marginalized cultural discourses, including environmental, women's, and ethnic minority cultures.
