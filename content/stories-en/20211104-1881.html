In March 2020, during the global pandemic, Avoice's founder learned in Belgium about many Chinese women suffering from domestic violence and in need of help. After investigation, the number of victims and the severity of their situations were far greater than we had imagined...


Violence, threats, humiliation, discrimination, torment... 

The truth is far harsher than ideals.


Shortly before receiving a plea for help from Ms. Xia Qing'an (pseudonym), she had again suffered domestic violence. The police who came only offered brief mediation before hastily leaving.


Ten years ago, Xia Qing'an came to Belgium as an international student, where she met her husband from the Dutch-speaking region of Belgium. At the time, Xia Qing'an did not realize that this seemingly good "marriage" was actually the early sign of misfortune baring its fangs.


"During these years in Belgium, I still miss the days of being an international student, at least I could control my own life. The family life that followed, being with such a foreigner, has been unbearable... Every time domestic violence occurred and I called the police, the police's words made me deeply feel the helplessness of being a stranger in a foreign land. Foreigners' thinking always sides with their own, no one considers the bitterness and hardship of a woman from another land... But I have never been willing to tell my family about these sufferings to worry them. After coaxing the child to sleep, all the grievances and pain can only be buried in the pillow with tears..."


Image source: The Sunday Morning Herald


Xia Qing'an and her abusive husband have two young children together. Three years ago, while she was pregnant with their daughter, he punched her hard on the head. She went to the hospital and the police were even called. Yet, this violent act ended absurdly with the court merely fining the man 200 euros.


And the disaster did not stop there—Xia Qing'an suffering violent beatings over minor issues had long become routine.


Last year, she was violently kicked several times, her tailbone slamming into a cabinet drawer, leaving her in severe pain for two months.


In addition, the man occasionally used words like "bitch" and "whore" to insult her, even forcing her to admit that these labels were true.


"Countless times, I've wanted to flee with my children, but changing nationality requires negotiation with him, and he would never agree...


I really want to escape this life I hate so much, but because the children are still young and I must care for them personally, I haven't been able to work. Without financial independence, I still have to rely on him for now..."


The pandemic has further deepened Xia Qing'an's predicament, and when facing recurring violent abuse, due to lockdowns and limited resources, she could only remain trapped with her abuser, unable to escape even momentarily.


Image source: Unsplash


"This pandemic, due to differences between Chinese and Western thinking, I've suffered a lot. No matter how many times I remind him, I still don't receive respect. Now under home isolation, I've experienced even more frequent and severe domestic violence. I reported it again, but during the pandemic, the police are busier than ever, and still only offer simple mediation. In this extraordinary time, my children and I don't even have another place to hide..."


Home should be a sanctuary for everyone, yet for victims of domestic violence, it becomes a hidden nest of crisis.


During the global pandemic, rising unemployment, increased psychological anxiety and financial pressure, scarce community support resources, and social isolation—all these factors have made domestic violence an invisible time bomb quietly detonating around the world. At the same time, the number of domestic violence cases handled by local police, as well as their capacity and efficiency in follow-up responses, have significantly declined, creating a difficult situation of widespread harm and limited protection.


Image source: centreforneweconmicstudies


Domestic violence is already deeply distressing; when such painful experiences happen to a Chinese woman living overseas, they become even more heartbreaking.


Further investigation revealed that immigrant women are a high-risk group for domestic violence, with ethnic Chinese women having an even higher victimization rate.


Many overseas Chinese women suffer long-term domestic violence and sexual violence, finding themselves in dire straits with nowhere to turn for help due to issues such as language barriers, lack of awareness, cultural differences, nationality, and limited social circles. Meanwhile, the pandemic has further exacerbated the exclusion and discrimination against overseas Chinese communities in various regions. All these factors combined make overseas Chinese women a primary victim group for various violent crimes, yet they struggle to reasonably defend their rights due to objective constraints. Therefore, support from other Chinese individuals is especially crucial for Chinese women suffering domestic violence during the pandemic.


Image source: UN News


The experiences of the overseas Chinese women mentioned above reveal only the tip of the iceberg; the following data may help you see the more turbulent currents beneath the surface.


According to UN Women's report on violence against women, survey data from over 80 countries show that globally, approximately 30% of women have experienced physical or sexual violence by an intimate partner in their lifetime. At the national and regional level, the proportion of women who have experienced physical or sexual violence by an intimate partner ranges from 23.2% to 37.7%.


Statistics from the National Coalition Against Domestic Violence (NCADV) in the United States show that one in four women and one in nine men in the U.S. have experienced severe physical violence, sexual violence, or stalking by an intimate partner, leading to post-traumatic stress disorder, sexually transmitted diseases, and other consequences. In the UK, over 90% of domestic violence victims are women, and additionally, 83% of women have experienced more than ten violent crimes. Domestic violence poses a serious threat to women's safety and lives.


Around the world, incidents of intimate partner violence against ethnic Chinese women occur frequently, and Chinese women account for the highest proportion among all domestic violence victims. Taking New York City in 2009 as an example, the proportion of domestic violence cases involving immigrants rose from 49% to 62% over the previous five years; and among all domestic violence crimes in the city, cases involving Chinese families accounted for 70%, exceeding 4,000 cases. This situation is not limited to New York—Chinese women worldwide are facing this crisis.


Behind every exposed or hidden case of domestic violence lies long-term psychological and physical harm to the individual victim. The reasons why overseas Chinese women suffer harm from domestic violence involve family, society, culture, and many other aspects. Protecting their rights requires joint efforts from multiple parties, providing timely assistance and sustained support.



Avoice Overseas Chinese Women Protection Project is dedicated to eliminating gender-based violence and promoting gender equality, offering timely and continuous protection and assistance to overseas Chinese women facing the threat of domestic violence.


This project originated from an international research study conducted during the COVID-19 pandemic in 2020. A group of Chinese international students passionate about public welfare noticed that the pandemic was worsening the risk of domestic violence for overseas Chinese women. They spontaneously organized themselves, building a cooperation network with government departments, overseas Chinese organizations, social organizations, and industry experts, uniting youth-driven public welfare forces to advocate public attention and support for Chinese women in domestic violence situations, helping them transform from victims into advocates and practitioners promoting gender equality.


In October 2021, the project received support from the Shanghai Overseas Chinese Foundation and we will strive together to build a Home of Love and Equality for Overseas Chinese Women ".


The project launched its pilot in Belgium, aiming to establish a broad and effective domestic violence support network for Chinese women. To date, the pilot project in Belgium has been successfully completed, and the project model has been extended to all European countries and some countries in North America. Over the next three years, the project will focus on expansion in North America, Australia, and Southeast Asia.


Starting from the issue of domestic violence, the project helps overseas Chinese women escape unsafe and unequal situations, gradually achieve self-empowerment, and create a virtuous cycle of mutual support through community building.

Shanghai Overseas Chinese Foundation is a charitable organization founded in 2006 by several overseas and domestic Chinese individuals, and approved by the Shanghai Civil Affairs Bureau as a 5A-level public fundraising foundation. It widely conducts charitable activities such as poverty alleviation and assistance, and establishes, funds, and implements various public welfare projects that contribute to the development of overseas Chinese affairs.

The Avoice project receives fundraising qualifications, funding, project management, and comprehensive public welfare support from the Shanghai Overseas Chinese Foundation.



The project will be divided into two main phases: preliminary and later. The support provided in the preliminary phase will focus on comprehensive management support, language support, psychological support, shelter support and community support five areas. The later phase will gradually establish and expand upon these. legal support, medical support, educational support, channel support, and financial support.

Basic process for applying for protection:

● Add the WeChat account of the "Women's Protection Assistant" in the "Need Help" section at the end of this article, and please note  your region and name (a nickname is acceptable);

● The assistant establishes a three-person WeChat group for the seeker according to regional teams and shift schedules, and arranges for a person in charge from the Comprehensive Management Department;

● The women seeking help can fully communicate their situation and needs in the WeChat group. The assigned person in charge will develop short-term and long-term assistance plans for each victimized woman, and carry out management, documentation, support, and communication tasks.
