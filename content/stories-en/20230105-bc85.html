News Commentary







2022 Nobel Laureate Accused of Sexually Harassing Multiple Chinese Women


I. Summary of the Incident

The overseas student information platform's official WeChat account "Yi Mu San Fen Di" published an article about Philip Dybvig, the 2022 Nobel Prize in Economics laureate from Washington University in St. Louis. However, in the comment section, a female student named Xiang bravely shared her experience of being sexually harassed by the professor during her graduate studies at the WUSTL Business School. Soon after, a second woman also courageously came forward with her own account of harassment by the same professor. Many netizens in the comment section offered comfort and support to the two brave women. Subsequently, the incident drew widespread attention from WUSTL Business School alumni, Bloomberg reporters, Washington Post journalists, and CNN reporters.


On December 16, Bloomberg published a detailed investigative report on the case. Washington University in St. Louis also launched an investigation into the sexual harassment allegations against Philip Dybvig. In response to the multiple accusations, Dybvig's lawyer Andrew Miltenberg stated that Dybvig had never engaged in inappropriate physical or verbal contact with any student. Tore Ellingsen, chairman of the Nobel selection committee, told the media, "The committee and the academy are not qualified to investigate researchers' workplace behavior," and added that as long as the university has not confirmed the harassment, they will continue to celebrate his great scientific achievements. Washington University in St. Louis stated that it would not comment on individual cases or share personal information at this time.

II. Commentary Perspectives

Encourage women who have suffered sexual harassment

to bravely speak up for their rights


According to Bloomberg's investigation, the office at WUSTL responsible for handling campus sexual harassment complaints has received additional allegations from other students since October. However, most of these female victims are unwilling to disclose their names, as they are still working in academia or living in the U.S. on employer-sponsored visas. Since the #MeToo movement, many women have overcome the fear of slut-shaming and actively defended their rights. Yet, in reality, saying "no" loudly remains extremely difficult due to specific social relationships with the accused. We hope more women who have suffered sexual harassment will find the courage to speak out, and we must also work to address their concerns!


Beware of "Asian fetish"—reject disguised racial discrimination


"Asian fetish" is not a positive form of affection; it is rooted in racial discrimination, dehumanizing and demeaning, representing a risk and stigma faced by Asian women. Some white men's preference for Asian women is not about pursuing rational, equal relationships, but stems from a distorted fetishization of Asian women. This stems from racially discriminatory stereotypes that portray Asian women as "submissive, obedient, easy." Asian women are often socialized to be kind, gentle, cute, and considerate. These white men may appear to admire Asian culture, but in reality, they impose their imagined notions onto Asian women to satisfy their own sense of superiority.


Fame and achievements become shields for perpetrators,

victims need more social care


We always teach girls to protect themselves—even going to extremes like advising against wearing skirts—yet rarely do we warn men to respect women instead of objectifying or sexualizing them. In response to the multiple accusations, Dybvig's lawyer Andrew Miltenberg stated, "As an educator, the professor takes his role very seriously and rigorously. He believes his relationships with students have always been appropriate and professional." Regarding Xiang's account of being pulled onto the professor's lap for a photo, Miltenberg said the professor remembers taking the photo but insists Xiang voluntarily sat on his lap, denying any forceful act. However, Xiang was a first-time international student in the U.S., with no apparent motive to sit on a professor's lap in public. Miltenberg further argued that the allegations only surfaced after the Nobel Committee announced the award on October 10, suggesting they may stem from professional jealousy, and even implied that other economists might have encouraged students to come forward with these "questionable" accusations.


A powerful professor can afford professional legal representation and hide behind academic achievements; yet women must pay a much higher price to prove their innocence and validate their suffering. Therefore, legislation, law enforcement, and social oversight must be strengthened to better support victims and deter potential perpetrators.


Crime







Chinese American man attacks in-laws across state lines to retaliate against wife


According to WGN9 on December 6, 36-year-old Michael Y. Liu, a Chinese American man from Wisconsin, has been charged with stabbing his mother-in-law and father-in-law at a house in Will County.


Police said: "The 66-year-old female victim was stabbed several times after Liu entered the house. The 68-year-old male victim was also stabbed multiple times while trying to protect his wife. The male victim fought back and stabbed Liu 17 times in the process of defending himself and his wife."


All three were taken to a nearby hospital in critical condition. Police revealed that Liu had previously been convicted of domestic violence and had violated a protection order. He was supposed to report to the Waukesha County Huber detention center on December 4. His attack was an act of retaliation against his wife, who was in the process of divorcing him.


Liu had previously parked his car near the couple's home in the Crete area and fired several shots at them while they were sitting in the living room. Fortunately, they were unharmed. He then entered the house and carried out the attack.


Liu faces two counts of attempted murder, five counts of burglary, two counts of aggravated domestic battery, four counts of aggravated battery, one count of aggravated discharge of a firearm, one count of burglary, and one count of unlawful entry into a residence.

To ronto woman of Chinese descent randomly stabbed on the street


According to CBC on December 5, 29-year-old Chinese Canadian woman Junlan Li was stabbed with a needle in her lower back while waiting at a red light at Yonge and College streets in Toronto.


Li said she was lost in thought when someone suddenly bumped into her lower right back. A kind stranger then reminded her to check her belongings to see if anything was missing. Only then did she realize the bump had been intentional.


Halfway down the next block, Li realized her back was injured. "I realized the area where I was bumped felt like a needle prick." She went to the nearest hospital and was told she had a puncture wound on her back, suspected to be from a needle. Li must now undergo a series of blood tests and infectious disease screenings, including post-exposure prophylaxis for HIV.


Li has already reported the incident to the Toronto Police, who told her that if the suspect is arrested, the charge will be assault with a weapon.

Asian man stabs ex-wife—final verdict reached one year later


According to "Huaren News Network" on December 9, on December 19 last year, 49-year-old Chinese national Liu Shuangmou attempted to kill his ex-wife with a 25-centimeter screwdriver near a train station in Italy.


The victim suffered severe stab wounds to the head, abdomen, and pelvis. Upon arrival, police immediately arrested Liu Shuangmou. It is reported that although the couple had not yet officially divorced, they had already separated. During this period, Liu Shuangmou repeatedly harassed the victim, ranging from threats and illegal detention to forcing the victim into sexual relations.


Initially, the local court sentenced Liu Shuangmou to six years and eight months in prison, but the prosecuting officer requested the sentence be increased to 13 years. A year later, the court finally issued its final ruling: Liu Shuangmou will serve eight years and 15 days for attempted murder and will be deported upon release.

A Chinese businessman shot dead in Milan


According to the Italian New Times' WeChat public account "Yi Xin Overseas Chinese Network," at around 7:15 a.m. local time on December 19, a gunman entered a Chinese-run bar located at Via Bessarione 42, on the corner of Angilberto II Square in Milan's Corvetto district, and shot dead the 35-year-old Chinese owner who was working at the time. Passersby, hearing the gunshots, immediately called the police and the emergency number 118. Tragically, the Chinese owner died at the scene.


Police revealed that the killer fired at least four shots at close range. The entire incident lasted only a few minutes, with no signs of robbery or theft. According to preliminary investigations, this appears to be a targeted act of retaliation against the victim, although the motive behind the shooting has not yet been determined.

Gender equality







U.S. provides $200 million to promote gender equality in Pakistan


According to Geo News on December 24, the United States has set aside $200 million for gender equality and strengthening democracy in Pakistan as part of its annual spending plan.


The omnibus bill, a single document consolidating several measures into one, will fund the U.S. federal government until September 2023.


The allocation for Pakistan in the omnibus bill is part of the U.S. Gender Equality Fund and represents a 20-fold increase from 2020. In December 2020, the U.S. Congress allocated $10 million to promote gender equality and $15 million to strengthen democracy in Pakistan. In 2000, Pakistan received $25 million for the same purposes. According to the World Economic Forum's "Global Gender Gap Report 2022," Pakistan, with a female population of 107 million, ranks second to last in gender parity, placing 145th out of 146 countries.

In countries with greater gender equality,

the gender gap in subjective well-being may be larger


A new analysis of data from the Programme for International Student Assessment (PISA) collected across 78 countries in 2015 and 2018 found that in countries with greater gender equality, the difference in subjective well-being between boys and girls is larger. Researchers report that gender equality may increase boys' subjective well-being but does not improve girls' subjective well-being. The study was published in the Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin.


The researchers wrote: "The results of this study provide the strongest evidence to date of a strong positive association between the level of societal gender equality and the gender difference in adolescent subjective well-being… Specifically, compared to countries with lower gender equality, countries with higher gender equality exhibit a larger gender gap in adolescent subjective well-being, favoring boys. While boys' subjective well-being is higher in countries with greater gender equality, girls' subjective well-being is unaffected by the level of national gender equality."


This study offers very important insights into the relationship between gender and subjective well-being worldwide. However, it should be noted that the study design does not allow for any causal conclusions, and the sample includes only students enrolled in secondary school at age 15.

Economic historians find gender equality benefits economic growth


A study by economic historian Alexandra de Pleijt of Wageningen University in the Netherlands and Jörg Baten of the University of Tübingen shows that over the past 500 years, in parts of Europe with better economic development, women tended to marry in their twenties rather than in their teens. Their research was published in the journal World Development.


Jörg Baten, Professor of Economic History at the Tübingen School of Business and Economics, said: "Early marriage shortens the time young women have to seek work on farms or in the labor market. After marriage, women are confined to domestic life." Therefore, economists regard women's age at marriage as a measure of gender equality.


The research team has now demonstrated that because women remained more independent for longer, they developed stronger numeracy skills. These skills include handling numbers, ratios, and probabilities, which can be acquired without formal education. Economic historians view numeracy as a cornerstone of socioeconomic development.

Gender parity is the only path to achieving gender equality


On December 12, the UN Secretary-General stated that gender equality is fundamentally a matter of power, and to change the culture of male dominance and achieve balance, we need equality in leadership, decision-making, and participation at all levels.


Overall, "we have come a long way," he said, citing the first time in UN history two years ago when gender parity was achieved within the senior leadership group. Five years ago, women held only 25% of these roles. In 2018, parity was achieved among 130 resident coordinators, representation of women at headquarters locations has now reached parity, and the number of UN entities with at least 50% women staff has increased from 5 to 26.


"At the same time, gaps remain," he warned. He said that outside the main offices of humanitarian and peacekeeping operations, "progress is slow, and in some cases, we are regressing. We should be particularly concerned that the number of women recruited into entry-level positions in the Secretariat has decreased. This could have serious implications for future prospects of equality." By 2025, three years before the deadline, overall staff in the Secretariat should approach parity at the "Professional" level, but this figure masks the fact that parity in this category may not be achieved until 2028. He said this means the overall strategy must now focus on maintaining progress in this area.

Asian voices







Ted Lieu, the third Chinese American member of the U.S. Congress,

elected as the first Asian American vice chair of the House Democratic Caucus


California Congressman Ted Lieu was recently elected Vice Chair of the House Democratic Caucus, becoming the first Asian American to hold this position and ranking fourth in the House Democratic leadership.


Ted W. Lieu was born in Taiwan, China, in 1969 and is a Chinese American member of Congress. He immigrated to the U.S. with his parents at age three, graduated from Stanford University and Georgetown University Law Center, served in the U.S. Air Force, and attained the rank of lieutenant colonel. In 2002, Lieu was elected to the city council of Torrance, Southern California; he served as a California State Assemblymember from 2005 to 2010. On November 4, 2014, he was elected as the third Chinese American member of the U.S. Congress, joining Judy Chu and Grace Meng as the third Chinese American representative in Congress.


Reflecting on his journey, Lieu said that moving from a flea market vendor to a leadership role in the House Democratic Caucus demonstrates the enduring nature of the "American Dream." His experience also reflects, to some extent, the significant evolution within the Asian American community in recent years. Lieu noted that over the past several years, political engagement, influence, and visibility within the Asian American community have greatly increased, and the community is increasingly accepted by American society—a transformation that aligns with his personal journey.

Perth Asian high school student awarded the Beazley Medal for Vocational Achievement,

the highest academic honor at the high school level


Jessica Doan, a Vietnamese graduate of Perth Modern School, was awarded the Beazley Medal for Vocational Achievement on Wednesday, December 21, in recognition of her outstanding performance in her Year 12 exams, achieving top scores in five subjects. She also won a subject exhibition for ranking first in chemistry.


Forty-one years ago, Jessica Doan's family first arrived in Australia. Her mother, Minh Doan, was only seven at the time. Jessica's father, electrical engineer Kanh Doan, was 12 when he fled Vietnam by boat with his parents, arriving in Australia after spending two months in a refugee camp in Malaysia. Minh Doan said, "We admire the Vietnamese people's resilience and hard work—effort really does pay off."


Although they rarely spoke to their children about their past, their humble beginnings instilled in the entire family a determination to work hard, contribute to society, and be grateful for the opportunities they received. Experiences returning to Vietnam and volunteering at orphanages also played a role in Jessica Doan's decision to become a doctor. Even contracting COVID-19 during her first semester exams did not deter her. Jessica Doan said, "I just wanted to aim as high as possible and see where my scores would take me."

Chinese New Zealander suffers home invasion and violent assault by criminals,

diagnosed with concussion,

local Chinese community holds rally to speak out on their behalf


On the afternoon of December 7, a clinic in Glenfield, North Shore, Auckland was robbed. The perpetrator severely assaulted Mr. Lang, leaving him bleeding from the face, before walking away. Mr. Lang reported the incident to the police immediately. After being hospitalized, doctors diagnosed him with concussion and serious after-effects. Police described this as a serious burglary that escalated into violence when the perpetrator discovered someone present. Dr. Sun, Mr. Lang's wife, said the psychological impact of this incident on their family has been enormous. "We've heard many friends or colleagues have experienced home invasions, and almost every time, nothing comes of the police reports." Dr. Sun does not wish to frame this incident as a hate crime based on race, but with growing public attention, she hopes this moment can spark deeper reflection across society.


After the incident, Chinese community groups launched intense discussions. One key topic was whether the Chinese community can truly stand up and speak out amid worsening public safety. Cindy, a Chinese resident of Auckland, said: "This victim is the family member of a doctor whose clinic I recently visited—it really hit close to home, and I feel we can no longer stay silent." Bruce Xu, a Whau local board election candidate, said the violent attack on the North Shore TCM clinic is not an isolated case and must not be ignored. He hopes to organize a public demonstration by Chinese New Zealanders soon to express support for the victim and submit a petition to relevant authorities, urging the government to take crime seriously. On the morning of Sunday, December 18, over 200 Chinese and other Asian individuals gathered at Aotea Square on Queen Street in central Auckland to voice their call for stronger government action against crime. Sunny Kaushal, chairman of the Indian community's grocery and business owners' organization, also participated in the event.

Purdue University president invents a so-called "Asian version" language at commencement ceremony,

drawing condemnation from American netizens who accuse him of racism.


On December 10 local time, James D'Agostino, a Purdue University alumnus and owner of WJOB radio station, was invited as the keynote speaker at the university's commencement ceremony. D'Agostino shared views on freedom of speech and mentioned that he invented a "language" to amuse his young granddaughter. When Thomas L. Keon, chancellor of Purdue University Northwest, returned to the stage to follow up on the speaker's joke, he said: "Well, all I can say is…" and then made a series of incomprehensible sounds. Chancellor Keon then claimed this was his "Asian version" of D'Agostino's invented language.


The video quickly sparked controversy on social media. American netizens criticized Keon's performance as embarrassing and racially offensive. Many Purdue graduates also spoke out, condemning his behavior and calling for his immediate resignation. On December 14, Keon issued an apology on the official website of Purdue University Northwest, expressing regret for his "offensive and insensitive" remarks. He said he would learn from this incident and pledged that both he and the university would take action to prevent such mistakes from happening again.
