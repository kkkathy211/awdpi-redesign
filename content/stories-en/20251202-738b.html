On November 17, 2025, local time, the results of the 80th United Nations General Assembly elections were announced—Zhang Lingling, a senior judge of the Supreme People's Court of China, was elected as a judge of the United Nations Appeals Tribunal with the highest number of votes, United Nations Appeals Tribunal, and will assume office on July 1, 2026, for a term of seven years.


This moment marks not only a milestone in the judicial career of an outstanding Asian woman, whose exceptional professional competence and extensive practical experience have set a shining example, but also symbolizes the rising strength of women from the Asia-Pacific region in the international judicial arena. Zhang Lingling's election brings a new and indispensable female perspective and regional representation to the United Nations internal justice system.

Election by high vote margin:

international society's trust in and expectations for Asia-Pacific women's legal power


In this election for judges of the United Nations Appeals Tribunal, Judge Zhang Lingling was the only candidate representing the Asia-Pacific region among eight candidates. She won by a significant margin, fully reflecting the international community's confidence in her professional capabilities and judicial philosophy, as well as its growing expectations for judicial talent from the Asia-Pacific region, especially women.


In today's world where globalization is increasingly embraced, the United Nations judiciary needs diverse voices, and Zhang Lingling's inclusion is a powerful representation of Asia-Pacific legal principles and practices on the United Nations platform.



At 44 years old, Zhang Lingling has a strong background in legal theory and adjudication experience. She has served in China's People's Court system for over fifteen years, participated in the adjudication of thousands of civil, commercial, and administrative cases, contributed to the drafting of multiple judicial interpretations, and published over 60 legal papers and monographs. Her combination of theoretical and practical expertise has made her a prominent figure on the international judicial stage and set an important example for the next generation of Asian women in law worldwide.


The United Nations internal justice system:

the core mechanism safeguarding fairness and order


To understand the significance of Zhang Lingling's election, one must first understand the platform she is about to join. The United Nations internal justice system was established in 2009 following a major reform under General Assembly Resolution 62/228, aiming to provide independent and efficient legal remedies for tens of thousands of staff members across the United Nations and its specialized agencies. The system consists of two core tribunals:


  • United Nations Dispute Tribunal: as the court of first instance, it handles administrative disputes filed by United Nations staff concerning employment, duties, benefits, and related matters. The tribunal's judges hold sessions on circuit in different locations worldwide, ensuring timely adjudication of staff members' claims from all regions.


  • United Nations Appeals Tribunal: as the court of final appeal, it hears appeals against judgments of the Dispute Tribunal. Composed of seven judges, the Appeals Tribunal is based at the United Nations Headquarters in New York, and its decisions are final and binding on all parties. The tribunal typically holds sessions in spring, summer, and autumn each year, with hearings conducted in New York, Nairobi, or Geneva.



Together, these two tribunals form the cornerstone of justice and the rule of law within the United Nations system, ensuring that this vast international organization operates effectively on a rules-based foundation, directly safeguarding the rights of United Nations staff and organizational stability.



The rise of Asian women's power:

from "participation" to "shaping" in judicial practice


Zhang Lingling's election is not the first appearance of a Chinese judge on a United Nations tribunal. In 2022, Judge Gao Xiaoli was elected to the United Nations Appeals Tribunal, and in 2023, she was successively elected as First Vice-President and President of the tribunal; at the same time, Judge Sun Xiangzhuang was elected to the United Nations Dispute Tribunal.


However, Zhang Lingling's success as an Asian woman elevates this progress to a new level. For a long time, women—especially women from non-Western worlds—have been underrepresented in international judicial institutions, yet judicial decisions often require acute insight into social structures, individual circumstances, and disparities in rights.


As a woman judge shaped by Eastern cultural values and honed through handling thousands of complex civil, commercial, and administrative cases, Zhang Lingling's inclusion will bring a unique perspective that promotes a more inclusive and human-centered balance between procedural justice and substantive fairness within United Nations jurisprudence. She is better able to perceive the potential for invisible injustices that institutional practices may impose on female staff and pays closer attention to the delicate balance between procedural and substantive justice. This "her power" will make the judgments of the United Nations Appeals Tribunal more inclusive, more humane, and more representative of the global public's shared sense of justice.



Moreover, serving in international organizations—especially in high-level fields such as the judiciary—Asian women often face invisible barriers. Zhang Lingling's election with the highest number of votes proves, through sheer merit, that Asian women can not only enter these spaces but also excel within them. Her success illuminates the path for countless Asian women who dream of contributing to international rule of law, demonstrating that professionalism and relentless effort are passports that transcend all boundaries.



As a female representative from the Asia-Pacific region, Zhang Lingling's very presence strengthens the legitimacy and credibility of the Appeals Tribunal. She ensures that this highest internal judicial platform, which adjudicates disputes among global employees, can hear and understand voices from the Asia-Pacific region and from women, thereby making fairer and more universally applicable rulings that truly embody the United Nations' core value of "justice for all."


When Zhang Lingling steps into the solemn chamber of the United Nations Appeals Tribunal at the New York Headquarters in 2026, she will carry not only judicial wisdom from the East, but also the persistent pursuit and powerful strength of women across the Asia-Pacific region and the world for fairness and justice. Her tenure will leave a profound "female imprint" on the United Nations' internal justice system and on the democratization of global governance. Her story will inspire more women to step to the forefront of power and knowledge, and will infuse the future of global governance with a force that is both resilient and gentle.
