When art meets reality, stories become a force.


On January 16, 2026, AWDPI co-hosted with Vermilion Theater the event "Out of Their Shadow—Don't Be Afraid, They Can't Catch Up Anymore". Held in Boston and supported by Boston City Hall, the event featured a special post-performance dialogue and community healing space that brought together survivors, advocates, artists, mental health practitioners, and community members to discuss gender-based violence and women's safety..


Beyond the theater, we see each other.



A conversation that began with theater

"Juliet & Jul-Yingtai" is a bilingual Chinese-English play that fuses Shakespeare's "Romeo and Juliet" with the Chinese folk legend "The Butterfly Lovers," re-examining through cross-cultural storytelling women's fate, love, and freedom. Created entirely by a female-led team, the work reimagines the destinies and agency of these two iconic female characters from a contemporary perspective.



The "Out of Their Shadow" dialogue session held after the performance extended the story from the stage into the real world.


During the sharing session, participants discussed the following topics:

  • gender-based violence and cultures of silence

  • safety challenges faced by immigrant and racial minority women

  • how art can become a space for healing and expression

  • how communities can collectively build support systems

In an open and inclusive atmosphere, participants from diverse backgrounds shared personal experiences and observations, collectively reflecting on how to break the silence and rebuild safety and trust.


The event's theme quotes a line from the play, spoken by Juliet to Zhu Yingtai:

"

"Don't be afraid, they can't catch up anymore." 

"

This is the moment when two women see each other across time, culture, and language. From this moment on, they are no longer written by others—they will co-create their own room.


When voices are spoken, when stories are heard,

shadows are no longer the only reality.


Why do we need such spaces?


In real life, many women facing violence or oppression often encounter multiple barriers:

language and cultural gaps

lack of knowledge about legal and social resources

community pressure and cultures of shame

psychological trauma and feelings of isolation

······


For Asian women living overseas, these barriers are often even more complex.


Therefore, creating a safe, trusting, and mutually supportive public space is especially important. Art, storytelling, and community activities can make experiences that are difficult to express become visible, bringing individual pain into public discourse.


This is precisely the significance of this event.


When shadows exist in reality


In many sociocultural contexts, gender-based violence does not always appear in extreme forms. It often hides within silence, shame, and normalized power dynamics.


After experiencing harm, many women do not follow a clear path to "seeking help." Instead, they often face more complicated realities: concerns about family and community judgment, unfamiliarity with legal procedures, difficulty adapting to language and cultural environments, and doubts about whether their experiences will be understood or believed.


For Asian women overseas, these challenges are often layered. Feelings of isolation in cross-cultural environments, unequal access to social resources, and long-standing racial stereotypes can all make it harder for individuals to speak up when facing violence and oppression.


Therefore, change often does not begin at the level of large-scale institutions, but rather in smaller, more intimate ways:

"

a space where stories can be told, a conversation where experiences are listened to, a moment of cross-cultural understanding and connection.

"

It is precisely within this context that more and more community organizations are beginning to use research, public discussions, and cross-sector collaborations to bring these long-overlooked experiences into public view.


AWDPI is one such organization. As an international nonprofit dedicated to advancing the development and rights protection of Asian women, AWDPI has long focused on the structural challenges Asian women face overseas in terms of safety, development, and social participation. Through research projects, international conferences, community advocacy, and cross-cultural collaborations, the organization aims to foster broader public discussions on gender-based violence, women's development, and social support networks, while connecting resources and actors across countries and regions.


In this sense, the collaboration with Vermilion Theater is an experiment:

"

using art as an entry point to discuss reality, allowing personal experiences to meet social issues.

"


When stories are told, when experiences are heard, the situations once hidden in shadows may finally be truly seen.


Let stories become power


Art cannot replace institutions,

but art can open dialogues.



When the play ends and the lights come up, people remain in their seats, still discussing, listening, sharing—by then, the boundary between stage and reality has already disappeared.


Change often begins with storytelling.

When more stories are told,

when more people are willing to listen,

society will slowly change.


In the future, AWDPI will continue collaborating with global partners through research, advocacy, and community action to promote a safer, more equal, and dignified social environment.


Because every woman deserves a future of her own—one where she can move forward without fear.


This is also the meaning of this event.
