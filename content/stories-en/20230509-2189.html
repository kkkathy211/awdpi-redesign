[Introduction]

After searching for some anti-domestic violence organizations, Yuyuan learned about Avoice. Having truly experienced pain and despair, she deeply empathizes with other women facing similar situations, and thus decided to speak publicly about her own experience to inspire more people to overcome their difficulties. She also hopes to contribute to expanding the influence of anti-domestic violence organizations, so that more people will know how to seek support and help.


Yuanyuan began studying in Norway at age 17 and settled in France at 22. Yet during her two years abroad, she endured repeated stalking and threats—her personal space was invaded and destroyed, and her physical and mental health suffered serious harm. We sincerely thank interviewee Yuanyuan for bravely sharing her experience of gender-based violence.


Yuanyuan received maximum possible assistance from relevant institutions and schools in two countries, and she says she considers herself lucky. However, a sound judicial system, comprehensive gender education, and a zero-tolerance attitude toward domestic violence should not depend on luck, but should become basic protections for everyone.


1. The Seeds of Violence



Norway's winters are beautiful and spectacular, but the weather is extremely cold and rapidly changing. With only a few hours of daylight during Norwegian winter, the long nights are often unbearable—many international students actively seek partners to get through the loneliness.


At 19, Yuanyuan met her ex-boyfriend, who had long been living in France. She never expected that when he began gambling, his mood swings would soon escalate into slaps across her face. After discovering his infidelity, Yuanyuan resolved to end the relationship. However, this decision led to her dorm room being vandalized. The computer and personal belongings shattered on the floor bore witness to the long and arduous struggle required to break free from such a toxic relationship.


The road ahead was long, and the first challenge was confronting her own internal conflict. "I know survivors are not at fault, and my Norwegian friends told me this behavior is unacceptable. But the conservative values I grew up with made me feel that family shame should not be exposed publicly." Although Yuanyuan was already experiencing early signs of violence, and although passersby or roommates had called the police and collected evidence during arguments with her ex-boyfriend, she chose twice not to press charges.


However, not pressing charges did not stop the emerging violence. After transferring to France, her ex-boyfriend's abusive behavior intensified. "He owed a lot of money and became increasingly irritable. One day he thought I was mocking him, but I really wasn't—it was all due to his fragile ego."



2. Omnipresent Threats



In France, Yuanyuan experienced the most severe act of violence in her life. Afterward, she visited the emergency room and discovered her entire body was bruised. She had corneal damage, blood clots in her eyes, and symptoms of concussion and bleeding behind the ear. Medical staff confirmed this was domestic violence and quickly issued documentation so Yuanyuan could report the incident to the police. In addition, the schools Yuanyuan attended in France and Norway sent lawyers and interpreters, and contacted the ISOS international rescue organization.


After this violent incident, Yuanyuan completely deleted her ex-boyfriend's contact information and stayed with friends in another city for several days. But when she returned to her dorm, she panicked to find signs of tampering near the peephole. She immediately contacted a classmate from the same building to stay with her as a precaution. As expected, that night her ex-boyfriend came again, frantically banging on the door. He demanded Yuanyuan come out to talk, or else "I'll make sure all Chinese international students know about us." "I told my classmate to wait for me in the dorm—if I didn't return within five minutes, call the police immediately." That time, Yuanyuan returned all the rings and necklaces he had given her, face to face, believing her life could finally return to peace.  


Later, Yuanyuan moved in with her then-boyfriend, now her husband. But the harassment from her ex-boyfriend continued: dozens of daily phone calls, attempts to intercept her on the intercity train she took to school. "He said he paid school security guards to try to enter the campus and search every classroom for me. He even printed flyers to distribute at school. Once, he brought a knife onto the train, knelt in front of me begging for forgiveness, and said he would kill anyone else I was with."


Fortunately, after getting off the train, Yuanyuan ran into her classmates and managed to escape. Upon learning of the incident, the school immediately investigated all security personnel and strengthened access control. "This person is terrifying—this time he threatened me with a knife, next time I don't know what else he'll bring." The potential threats were everywhere, making escape nearly impossible.



3. When the Shadow Begins to Fade



One time, while shopping with her husband at Galeries Lafayette, Yuanyuan encountered her ex-boyfriend again. He tried once more to drag her into his car for a talk, but as they began struggling, a police car happened to pass by. Her husband stopped the police car and explained the situation. Based on previous statements and documents, the officers quickly pieced together the full context.


At that moment, her ex-boyfriend still resorted to verbal humiliation—"The first thing he said to my husband was that I have depression, and he shouted in Chinese on the street that I had miscarried four times for him, which was completely fabricated." Reflecting later, Yuanyuan recalled how her husband stood firmly by her side, restoring her confidence in intimate relationships.


After being questioned and warned by the police, her ex-boyfriend was prohibited from coming within ten meters of the survivor. He thus disappeared from Yuanyuan's life. However, he remains a dark shadow—Yuanyuan mentioned, "Occasionally when I go downstairs, I still find him lingering near my apartment."


Norway is a country with zero tolerance for gender discrimination and domestic violence. In the 2021 Global Women's Safety Index, Norway ranked first among 170 countries [1]. If perpetrators are convicted, they face imprisonment or fines, and may be required to undergo psychological treatment to control violent behavior..


Upon learning of Yuanyuan's situation, her Norwegian school assigned five professional staff members to support her individually through various processes, each responsible for legal matters, school procedures, daily affairs, and contacting the Norwegian church. When Yuanyuan first arrived in France for her exchange, she did not yet have local health insurance and could not access free medical services—but the Norwegian government covered all expenses, and one teacher followed up specifically on this matter. Normally, Norwegians finish work after 4 p.m., but out of concern for Yuanyuan, teachers called her every evening at nine or ten o'clock to check on her—This care, going far beyond professional duty, deeply moved Yuanyuan.


4. Finding the Perpetual Engine for Solutions



"Before, I always viewed domestic violence from an outsider's perspective. I would wonder: Why don't survivors fight back? Why do they endure violence? If they know the person is bad, why don't they leave? Is this related to education level?" As an only child raised in a happy family, Yuanyuan felt domestic violence and suffering were far removed from her life. "In the past, I didn't want to see others' suffering—I thought that if I didn't see it, my emotions wouldn't be affected."


Now, after searching for some anti-domestic violence organizations, Yuyuan learned about Avoice. Having truly experienced pain and despair, she deeply empathizes with other women facing similar situations, and thus decided to speak publicly about her own experience to inspire more people to overcome their difficulties. She also hopes to contribute to expanding the influence of anti-domestic violence organizations, so that more people will know how to seek support and help. "I don't just want to expose the existence of the problem—I want to find solutions. This will become my life's work."


Yuanyuan believes she was lucky to receive free legal and support resources as an international student. But the real question is: for most women facing similar situations, how can they find the "perpetual engine" to solve their problems?


On this issue, Yuanyuan believes "empathy" is key—only those with similar experiences can truly understand. Therefore, she hopes more survivors will become leaders themselves, making "girls help girls" a self-sustaining cycle. Having lived in Norway, a country with zero tolerance for violence, Yuanyuan believes that teaching gender equality from an early age is also crucial. Additionally, regarding how domestic and international women's organizations assist survivors, Yuanyuan suggests that collaboration between organizations across borders can combine strengths and advance together.


As for how one can move beyond the shadow, Yuanyuan wants to tell girls: "Imagine yourself as the heroine in a novel, and think about how she reaches a happy ending." Throughout this journey, she recommends researching others' experiences and learning their coping strategies. At the same time, she wants to tell girls: "Always believe in yourself. Don't deny or erase this experience—accept it, face it, and don't let it make you afraid of love."
