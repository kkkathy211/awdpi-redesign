Sorrow is everywhere

Visible everywhere

As ever-present as the sky above

When they see children in cradles starving and freezing

All mothers weep

Even if the children are not their own

Those children may never wake again

               —From *Little Palestine: Diary of a Siege*






On October 7, 2023, the morning calm was shattered by gunfire and explosions from Gaza, as armed militants crossed the border into Israel and launched attacks on Israeli areas. The trails of rockets plunged cities like Tel Aviv and Jerusalem into the shadow of war. On October 9 of the same year, Israel's Defense Minister announced a "complete siege" on Gaza, cutting off electricity, food, and fuel—deepening the darkness of Israel's 16-year blockade on the region.


Image source: Pixabay


The scene of this total blockade echoes the siege of Yarmouk refugee camp depicted in the documentary *Little Palestine: Diary of a Siege*, sharing a mournful resonance and telling stories of suffering and struggle in enclosed lands. More than a decade later, the echoes of history remain heartbreakingly painful.


The documentary *Little Palestine: Diary of a Siege* is set against the backdrop of the Syrian civil war, focusing on the despair and resilience of the Yarmouk refugee camp amid the conflict. Yarmouk is a Palestinian refugee camp established in 1957 on the southern outskirts of Damascus. Before the war, it was Syria's largest Palestinian settlement. However, after the outbreak of the Syrian civil war, Yarmouk became a microcosm of the war, with the Palestinian community caught in the struggle among various forces—used, abandoned, and ultimately forgotten.


Image source: Pixabay


In 2011, during the Syrian civil war, Yarmouk refugee camp became one of the key battlegrounds. Specifically, starting in 2012, the Free Syrian Army (opposition forces) and the Popular Front for the Liberation of Palestine – General Command (a pro-government Palestinian armed group supported by the Syrian regime) engaged in fierce fighting there. The two sides turned Yarmouk into a battlefield for control, causing the situation in the camp to deteriorate rapidly. Statistics show that during the Syrian civil war, 160,000 Palestinians were displaced, forced to leave their homes and livelihoods behind. In the documentary, the blockade intensified over time, eventually evolving into a full siege. With all supplies cut off, food, medicine, and fuel became extremely scarce, forcing residents to struggle for survival under the dual threats of hunger and disease.


This documentary not only presents the brutal reality of the siege of Yarmouk, but also offers a lens through which we can understand the current situation of civilians in the Israeli-Palestinian conflict—especially women and children. Children who should be in classrooms are instead seen bending over in ruins, cutting wild herbs they once never ate, as the roar of artillery echoes behind them. The director asks, "Aren't you afraid?" She replies softly, "I'm used to it." The camera shifts, drawing attention to a pair of dirty feet. They belong to a thin little girl, bare and exposed to dust and gravel, with visible scrapes on the top of her feet.


Our gaze follows the camera up and down, trying to piece together the full image of this small body: she wears tattered clothes, the hems stained with mud, holding a torn plastic bag containing a few freshly picked weeds. The girl's eyes are cast downward, as if she has long grown accustomed to everything around her. When our eyes finally meet hers, those large, bright eyes—filled with exhaustion—suddenly pull the world beyond the lens into a suffocating reality.


Image source: Pixabay


A camera meant to reveal the everyday now reveals a deeper truth: on this land, innocent women and children have nowhere to run. They bear the most direct harm of war. Why do we pay such close attention to women and children in war? When the proportion of women and children among casualties is disproportionately high, it is not just a number—it reveals the extreme and disproportionate devastation war inflicts on civilians. Those least able to protect themselves often become the first lives to fall beneath violence.






In the Israeli-Palestinian conflict, women and children are often the most direct victims, accounting for more than two-thirds of the casualties. They not only face violent attacks, but also suffer from malnutrition, disease, and higher mortality risks due to limited access to maternal and child healthcare. Between October 7 and 31, 2023, Israeli security forces arrested 1,760 Palestinians in the West Bank and East Jerusalem, including 145 women, 55 children, and 17 journalists, and issued 872 administrative detention orders. These arrests were often accompanied by beatings, humiliation, and inhumane and degrading treatment, sometimes amounting to torture.


At the same time, the government further restricted detainees' access to basic necessities and legal aid, leading to severe food shortages and poor sanitary conditions, with reports of violence against children, women, and the elderly. In some cases, these acts amounted to torture or other forms of abuse. The weight of war is not only reflected in casualty figures, but also in the systematic violation of the most vulnerable groups' right to survival.


Image source: Pixabay


When women and children become an unavoidable part of casualty statistics, we are compelled to ask: is it possible to reduce this disproportionate harm? Perhaps paying attention to these stories alone cannot change the current reality, but it allows us to more clearly recognize that the value of peace lies not only in ending conflict, but also in preventing the most vulnerable from continuing to bear the cost of war. Understanding and reflection are steps toward a more just future.
