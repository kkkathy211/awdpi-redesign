In traditional Asian beliefs, women are often expected to fulfill roles associated with emotions and intimate relationships: they must have a husband and children to be considered happy, are always expected to give or seek in close relationships, and are pressured to focus more on physical appearance... However, with the rise of feminist movements, these rigid gender norms are increasingly being questioned and dismantled. Society has begun to pay attention to gender-based violence incidents that were once overlooked, encouraging women to pursue self-growth and break free from the constraints of traditional gender roles.


Yet, when exploring new possibilities, confusion is inevitable: What does intimacy mean for women? How do emotional and bodily experiences shape our relational patterns?



To address these questions, the Psychological Support Division of AWDPI's U.S. Office has specially invited two mental health therapists based in the U.S. to host a roundtable discussion, guiding participants from dual perspectives of emotion and body to understand, confront, and rethink intimacy. The event will explore the following themes:

Women and Emotional Growth

Women and the Body

Women and Intimate Relationships

Other related topics


Through sharing of theoretical and practical experiences, we will collectively explore the challenges and experiences Asian women face in intimate relationships, and reflect on how to build healthier, more compassionate connections with ourselves and others.



Guest Introductions

Deng Yushan


Ph.D. candidate in Clinical Psychology at Yale University and Psychology Researcher at the Department of Behavioral Medicine, Yale New Haven Hospital. She specializes in providing trauma-informed psychological care for diverse populations, with particular focus on gender diversity and psychological support for end-of-life care patients. She is also the founding artistic director of a nonprofit theater, creating emotional healing spaces for the Chinese diaspora through artistic expression.



Zhang Ni


Licensed psychotherapist in California (Registered Associate Marriage and Family Therapist), with over 4,000 hours of clinical experience. She specializes in adult, child, and couples therapy, and has organized and led over 80 experiential workshops for inner exploration. Her primary focus includes emotional distress, psychological trauma, and rebuilding self-worth in intimate relationships, with expertise in integrating Eastern and Western cultural perspectives.
