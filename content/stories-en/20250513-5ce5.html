The Netherlands has reduced the gender pay gap to 10.5% over the past decade,

maintaining its position among the top three in the EU—driven by policy advances amid persistent challenges


According to Statistics Netherlands (CBS), from 2010 to 2023, the average hourly wage gap between men and women narrowed from 19% to 10.5%. Rising female education levels and increases in the minimum wage—benefiting women in low-paid jobs—were the main drivers. Women's hourly wages grew by 52% (compared to 38% for men), and in large companies and public administration, the proportion of women earning more than men rose to 15% and one-third respectively. However, only 23% of women work full-time (compared to 63% of men), and annual income for women remains nearly one-third lower than for men.


The Netherlands ranks third in the EU on gender equality with a score of 78.8, leading in health (93.4) and knowledge (70.3). However, gender segregation persists in education choices, with women more likely to enter healthcare and education fields. Collective wages will rise by 5.5% in the first quarter of 2025, and the part-time minimum hourly wage will increase to €14.06 (up 2.75%), potentially further narrowing the gap. Yet deeper challenges—such as unequal distribution of unpaid labor and workplace culture—remain to be addressed.


The Netherlands has narrowed the hourly pay gap through wage adjustments, improving the economic status of low-income women and promoting shared family responsibilities to optimize workplace structures. Its top-three EU performance in gender equality offers a global model, while also highlighting gender segregation in education and the need for greater occupational diversity. These measures enhance social equity, inject inclusive momentum into the economy, and lead labor systems and cultures toward greater equality.

Bridging the Digital Gender Divide: Global Calls to Empower Women in the Tech Revolution


On April 23, 2025, the International Telecommunication Union (ITU) released a report stating that nearly 189 million women worldwide still lack internet access in 2024. The persistent digital gender divide continues to harm women's health, education, and development opportunities, and the ITU called for a restructured, inclusive digital future through technological empowerment.


As AI reshapes the global economy, women remain excluded from the digital world, struggling to access knowledge, employment, and participation in technology development. ITU Secretary-General Doreen Bogdan-Martin emphasized that true digital inclusion requires affordable access, widespread skills training, and cybersecurity. The 2025 "Girls in ICT Day," themed "Driving Inclusive Transformation," calls for expanded digital education for girls and encourages them to shift from "users" to "creators." The event fosters technological passion and bridges gender gaps through cross-continental live streams (e.g., connecting Kyrgyzstan and Mauritania) and intergenerational dialogues (where girls discuss with leaders and experts).


Eliminating the digital gender gap can significantly improve women's health and education, create economic empowerment opportunities, and reduce intergenerational poverty. Women's participation in technology drives diverse innovation, enhances social equity, and breaks the stereotype of male-dominated tech. Global coordinated action not only unlocks women's potential but also builds a more inclusive digital future, promoting sustainable economic growth and social stability.

Olivia Orchowski and Kyri: Using Women's Health Technology to Fill the Physiological Data Gap


In 2020, Olivia Orchowski founded the health tech company Kyri (formerly FemTek) due to personal health challenges and witnessing female clients struggle with conditions like polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS). Recognizing that wearable devices on the market were designed based on male physiological data, Orchowski developed a "basal body temperature ring" that uses biometric indicators to help women manage fertility and health. In 2024, Kyri secured a million-dollar funding round led by Arcanys Ventures and rebranded (inspired by the Norse Valkyrie), symbolizing female empowerment.


Orchowski pointed out that the medical system often dismisses symptoms with treatments like birth control pills, and the industry has long neglected women's health research. Kyri aims to promote root-cause health management and enhance women's bodily autonomy. As a woman entrepreneur, Orchowski urged others to confront challenges: "If you identify a problem, other women are likely experiencing the same—create solutions for them."


The emergence of women's health tech products like Kyri breaks the tradition of male-centered technological design, giving women access to precise health data and enhancing their understanding and control over their bodies. This not only fills the gender data gap in healthcare systems but also promotes fairer distribution of health resources and inspires women to participate in technological innovation. When women's needs are placed at the core of technological development, it improves individual well-being and fosters more inclusive growth in the healthcare industry, creating a more equitable health future for society.

Las Vegas Man Pleads Guilty in Hate Crime Assault on Elderly Asian Victim: Prosecution Reaffirms "Zero Tolerance"


On May 30, 2023, Christian Lentz of Las Vegas was accused of committing a hate crime against his 75-year-old Asian neighbor, Amadeo Quindara. Two years later, a plea agreement was reached. Lentz admitted to the crime while claiming "mental disturbance," with charges including burglary with hate crime enhancement and elder abuse.


Surveillance footage showed Lentz breaking into the victim's garage, dragging him to the ground, shouting "die," and fleeing, leaving behind blood. Quindara suffered severe head injuries. Lentz had previously told police he was "the target of a conspiracy." The prosecution emphasized that hate-based violence in communities will not be tolerated. Sentencing is scheduled for July 21, with a combined maximum penalty of 30 years in prison.


This case highlights the continued need for strong legal deterrence against anti-Asian hate crimes, as well as the need to improve judicial evaluation mechanisms for offenders with mental illness, balancing punishment and treatment to safeguard the safety of minority communities and social justice.

Chinese Filipino Businessman Abducted and Murdered in Philippines, Sparking Panic—Police Suspect Links to Illegal Gambling Networks


 Anson Que, a Chinese Filipino tycoon and CEO of Elison Steel Company, and his driver were abducted on March 29 in Bulacan Province. On April 3, Que's body was found by the roadside in neighboring Rizal Province, hands and feet bound with nylon rope, mouth sealed with tape, showing clear signs of strangulation and beating. Philippine police labeled it an "organized crime" and replaced Colonel Elmer Lagui, the acting head of the anti-kidnapping division, in response to criticism over poor investigation. Authorities are investigating possible links to offshore gaming operators (Pogos)—an industry banned earlier this year due to organized crime ties but suspected of continuing operations underground. This brutal case has heightened public concern over deteriorating security and the expansion of criminal syndicates in the Philippines.


This case exposes weaknesses in the Philippine security system and deep penetration by organized crime. Strengthening international cooperation against transnational crime, especially in response to the underground evolution of gray industries, is essential to protect the safety of foreign nationals in the Philippines and maintain economic stability.

Chinese American Boy in Virginia Racially Bullied, Sparking Outrage—Community Safety and Bystander Apathy Prompt Reflection


On April 5, a 5-year-old Chinese American boy in Loudoun County, Virginia, was racially bullied by a middle school student near his home. The attacker mocked him with phrases like "Do you eat dumplings?" accompanied by threats. The boy clutched his blanket and cried for help, but no bystanders intervened. The family, Chinese immigrants who chose the community for its quality education, now face despair over "where can we feel safe?" The middle school acknowledged the attacker was its student but evaded direct responsibility, citing the incident occurred off-campus, and only offered psychological counseling. Police have launched an investigation, but details remain undisclosed due to the minor's age. After the incident went public, one parent tearfully informed the victim's family of the truth, highlighting the community's shock and helplessness toward racial violence.


This case reveals the threat of covert racial discrimination faced by minority children and exposes the moral dilemma of the bystander effect. Strengthening anti-bullying collaboration mechanisms both inside and outside schools, along with racial equality education, is key to building inclusive communities.

United Nations Chinese Language Day: Building a Bridge of Civilizational Exchange Through Language


On the occasion of the 80th anniversary of the United Nations, the 16th "UN Chinese Language Day" series of celebratory events kicked off on April 15 at the UN Headquarters in New York. Themed "Poetic China, Romantic Elegance," the event showcased the cultural charm of the Chinese language through diverse forms such as poetry, calligraphy, painting, and tea art.


Co-organized by the Chinese Permanent Mission and the UN Chinese Language Unit, the opening ceremony featured Ambassador Fu Cong quoting Tang dynasty poetry to emphasize the concept of a community with a shared future for humanity, calling for dialogue and the bridging of differences through language. Under-Secretary-General Abeyrathne commended the contributions of the Chinese language to multilateral diplomacy at the UN. The series of events included bilingual recitations in Chinese and English of classics such as the *Book of Songs* and "Spring Dawn" by UN staff and diplomats; AI-generated poetry demonstrating the integration of technology and tradition; live calligraphy performances and online lectures by calligrapher Xia Pengcheng explaining the aesthetics of Chinese characters; tea art demonstrations conveying the spirit of harmony and respect; and the launch of three new books on cultural exchange between China and the world. Additionally, the exhibition "Between the Lines – The Civilization Code in Chinese Characters" traced the evolution of Chinese characters, while an open day hosted by Beijing University of Chinese Medicine showcased Eastern wisdom through tai chi and traditional Chinese medicine experiences.


The two-week celebration not only enriched the cultural dimension of the UN's 80th anniversary but also highlighted the unique value of the Chinese language as a bridge for global civilizational exchange, as well as China's commitment to promoting a community with a shared future for humanity through cultural soft power.

Professor Yu Kai from Shanghai Jiao Tong University elected ISCA Fellow, becoming the first mainland Chinese speech scientist to receive the honor


The International Speech Communication Association (ISCA) announced its 2025 Fellow list, with Yu Kai, specially appointed professor at Shanghai Jiao Tong University and co-founder of AISpeech, becoming the first scholar from mainland China to be included, recognized for his groundbreaking contributions to speech recognition, dialogue systems, and technology commercialization.


Only three in every thousand ISCA members are elected as Fellows, and to date fewer than ten Chinese scholars have received this honor. Yu Kai has led the development of speech technologies that have won multiple international competitions, authored authoritative books including *Reinforcement Learning (2nd Edition)* (regarded as the "bible of the field") and *Artificial Intelligence: Speech Recognition, Understanding, and Practice*, and spearheaded the formulation of domestic speech technology standards. Among the eight newly elected Fellows this year are Li Hongyi from National Taiwan University and Nancy Chen from Singapore's A*STAR Research Institute, highlighting the growing influence of Chinese scholars in global speech technology.


Yu Kai's achievement marks international recognition of China's independent innovation in speech technology, and his integrated path of academic research, industry application, and entrepreneurship provides a model for local scientists to participate in global technical standard-setting and enhance their international academic voice.

Tu Youyou and other Chinese scientists elected to the U.S. National Academy of Sciences, marking another peak in China's international scientific influence


On April 29, 2025, the U.S. National Academy of Sciences announced the election of 120 new members and 30 international members. Professor Tu Youyou from the China Academy of Chinese Medical Sciences, Nobel laureate, was elected as an international member for her research on artemisinin, becoming the first scientist from China's traditional Chinese medicine field to receive this honor. This year's list also includes multiple Chinese scholars, alumni of Peking University, Wuhan University, Zhejiang University, and others, whose research spans life sciences, engineering, and related disciplines.


Founded in 1863, the U.S. National Academy of Sciences selects members based on the core criteria of "originality and sustained outstanding contributions," and membership is considered one of the highest academic honors in the United States. The Academy now has a total of 2,662 members, including 556 international members. The collective election of Chinese scholars this year signifies a breakthrough in China's scientific strength within the international academic discourse system, demonstrating authoritative recognition of China's contributions to global fundamental research.
