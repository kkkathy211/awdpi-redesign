27–30 April 2026, Women Deliver 2026 Conference in Naarm (Melbourne), Australia. As one of the world's most influential gender equality conferences, this year's event brought together women leaders, advocates, policymakers, community organizations, scholars, and social innovators from around the globe to engage in deep dialogue on gender equality, gender-based violence, women's leadership, economic empowerment, health rights, climate justice, First Nations leadership, and intersectional issues. engage in in-depth exchanges.


The Australian National Office of Asian Women Development Plan International (AWDPI) represented AWDPI at the conference, engaging with gender equality organizations, multicultural community institutions, and social impact partners across Australia and internationally, to further advance AWDPI's potential for connection, dialogue, and collaboration across the Asia-Pacific region.


Cindy Gui (left) and Cathy Zhang (right), representatives of the AWDPI Australia National Office, at Women Deliver 2026



Standing at the intersection of global agendas and local practices



Women Deliver 2026 marks the first time the Women Deliver conference is hosted by the Oceania and Pacific region, taking place in Naarm, Melbourne. This year's conference emphasizes First Nations leadership, regional solidarity, feminist movement-building, and shared responsibility for global gender justice.


For AWDPI, this was not merely participation in an international conference, but a significant opportunity for learning, connection, and strategic positioning.


Cindy Gui takes a photo after exchanging with Julia Gillard, former Prime Minister of Australia and the country's first female Prime Minister

Cindy Gui takes a photo with Amina J. Mohammed, Deputy Secretary-General of the United Nations


During the conference, the AWDPI Australia National Office, representing AWDPI, supported and signed the Melbourne Declaration for Gender Equality, and participated in discussions at the Declaration Consultation Hub. The Melbourne Declaration for Gender Equality is not only an advocacy document, but also a collective reflection on the global gender equality ecosystem. It reminds us that advancing gender equality requires not only commitments, but also resource support, accountability mechanisms, and rights protection, as well as the need to create greater space and legitimacy for feminist movements and civil society, while continuously examining unequal power relations. Through these co-creation dialogues, we further reflected on how to translate the care, solidarity, and justice emphasized in the Declaration into concrete practices within AWDPI's work in Australia and other National Offices—particularly in supporting Asian women, multicultural women, and local community support systems through culturally responsive and community-rooted actions.


Supporting and participating in the Melbourne Declaration for Gender Equality


also aligns closely with AWDPI's long-standing focus. AWDPI has long been dedicated to Asian women living, studying, and working overseas, especially attentive to the complex challenges they face regarding gender-based violence, domestic violence, cultural barriers, immigration status, language difficulties, social isolation, and access to services.


These discussions have deepened our understanding: gender equality is not a single-issue agenda, but deeply intertwined with cultural background, migration experiences, economic conditions, social identities, and institutional accessibility. For Asian and multicultural women, truly effective support systems must be built on cultural understanding, community trust, and localized practices.



Engaging with diverse partners to explore future collaboration



During the conference, the AWDPI Australia National Office held exchanges with multiple organizations and representatives focused on women's rights, multicultural services, domestic violence support, community development, and social impact, including the Australian Multicultural Women's Alliance, Victorian Women's Health, Equality Now, Diversity Council Australia, and Settlement Services International.


These conversations further helped us reflect on how to translate global gender equality agendas into actions that are truly visible, accessible, and sustainable within local communities across Australia and beyond.


We also introduced the development direction of the AWDPI Australia National Office, including:



first, advancing safer, more understandable, and culturally appropriate support for Asian and multicultural women in Australia;


second, enhancing societal understanding of the structural challenges faced by Asian women through community education, public advocacy, and cross-sector collaboration;


third, building long-term impact for the AWDPI Australia National Office based on research, programs, partnerships, and public engagement.


Women Deliver 2026 provided AWDPI with a vital platform to build connections across diverse sectors, and opened new possibilities for future collaboration in community projects, research initiatives, public education, policy advocacy, and inter-organizational partnerships.

Making Asian women's experiences visible, heard, and responded to



In global gender equality discussions, Asian women's experiences are often subsumed within broader frameworks of "women" or "migrant communities," yet not always fully understood.


In reality, many Asian women face highly intersectional challenges. Language barriers, cultural pressures, family responsibilities, uncertain visa or immigration status, unfamiliarity with mainstream service systems, and concerns about privacy, shame, and community judgment during help-seeking processes—all may affect whether they can access support in a timely manner.


What AWDPI seeks to advance is precisely a more nuanced and context-sensitive approach to support.


"

We believe that only when service systems can truly hear the authentic voices of women from diverse cultural backgrounds can gender equality move beyond grand international commitments and become tangible safety, dignity, choice, and opportunity in women's everyday lives.


Women Deliver 2026 reaffirmed for us: real change comes from connection, and from sustained action. Global gender equality agendas must ultimately return to every community, every woman, and every lived experience.


Going forward, AWDPI will continue to leverage its global network, advancing local practices through its National Offices, and working with more partners to build safer, more equitable, and culturally responsive support systems—ensuring that Asian women's voices are seen, heard, and responded to.
