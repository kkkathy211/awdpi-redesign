Introduction to the Trauma-Informed and Ethics Seminar Series


(Swipe up to read)


The Trauma-Informed and Ethics Seminar Series is jointly initiated by the AWDPI Curriculum Development Department and the Psychological Support Department, aiming to focus on the multiple forms of trauma, especially deep-seated trauma arising from experiences of intimate partner violence, domestic violence, and gender-based violence, and how such trauma intertwines with childhood memories, social structures, and power relations. The seminar series will particularly explore how helpers should respond and practice with trauma-informed ethical awareness during action and accompaniment. The target audience includes helpers (all AWDPI volunteers, social workers, psychotherapists, legal professionals, etc.), relevant scholars and students, activists and advocates, survivors, and any members of the public interested in this topic.


The motivation for initiating this seminar series stems from our recognition that within our organization and the broader field of helping practices, "trauma-informed" approaches remain a relatively neglected gap. Even in areas where it is discussed more frequently, it often functions as a buzzword without systematic training or practice. We deeply feel this is an issue that urgently needs to be acknowledged, discussed, learned, and practiced, as it concerns not only the enhancement of professionalism and ethical awareness, but also how to build more sustainable and responsible humanistic care relationships between people. For both helpers and those being helped, we hope this seminar series can open a path toward mutual understanding, support, and response.


This series will offer diverse online formats—including workshops, lectures, and roundtable discussions—drawing on multidisciplinary and multi-field perspectives to provide as rich a discussion as possible on trauma-informed care and ethics. The entire series is expected to run for six months, starting from December 2025. The sixth session will be held as an online workshop, and we are honored to invite Teacher Zhou Chaoying to lead an online empty-chair experiential workshop themed on seeing trauma and perceiving its impacts.


In this workshop, Teacher Zhou will guide us through the experience of empty-chair therapy, gradually helping us regulate our emotions and clarify objective facts during the process of trauma healing and self-exploration. Based on the Gestalt school and professional guiding language, we will gain insight into our frozen perceptions and reach the authentic thoughts deep within our hearts.


Guest Introduction

Zhou Chaoying, Teacher


  • First registered psychotherapist in the Chinese Psychological Society's Registration System for Counseling and Clinical Psychology (2008)

  • Registered supervisor (2019)

  • Committee member of the Youth Mental Health Special Committee, Jiangsu Psychological Association

  • Trained in multiple psychotherapy schools including Sino-American Family Therapy, Sino-German Gestalt Therapy, and Shi Qijia's Psychoanalytic Therapy

  • Over 30 years of experience in psychological counseling, over 10 years of experience supervising psychotherapists, and author of nearly 30 academic papers

  • Has profound understanding of psychological issues caused by psychological trauma, and has achieved significant results in many short-term counseling cases by integrating empty-chair therapy

     

Workshop Focus


  • Learning to recognize trauma that you may not have been aware of

  • Perceiving how trauma affects you in interpersonal relationships

  • Using the empty-chair method to move from a frozen world into a warm, sunlit space


Workshop Highlights


  • Using scientific methods to locate the origin of our trauma

  • Externally presenting the impact of trauma through relational interactions

  • Practical applications of empty-chair therapy in trauma healing
