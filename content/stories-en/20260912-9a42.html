Sometimes, we encounter moments that are hard to explain.


Someone says something, and you feel a bit uncomfortable, but your first reaction is:



"Am I being too sensitive?"


You've lived in a place for a long time, your English is becoming more fluent, you have a job, friends, and your own life, but occasionally you still feel:


"I still seem like an outsider."


Or perhaps, since childhood, we've been used to working hard, being well-behaved, and achieving good results.


So when we feel tired, wronged, or unhappy, the first thing that might come to mind isn't:


"How am I feeling right now?"


But rather:


"Am I just not working hard enough?"


These feelings might be small.


So small that we don't even know how to name them.


But precisely because of that, they're easily overlooked.



This time, we'd like to read Cathy Park Hong's *Minor Feelings: An Asian American Reckoning* together.

This book draws from the author's personal experiences and touches on many questions we may have all pondered before:


race and identity, language, family, immigration experiences, womanhood, belonging, and emotions that are hard to put into words.


It's not a book that tells us "how we should live."


Instead, it invites us to pause and ask:


Why do I have these feelings?


Why are some experiences hard for others to understand?


Why do we sometimes even doubt our own emotions?



What will we talk about this time?



In 90 minutes, we won't try to "analyze" the entire book.


We'd rather start from the stories and ideas in the book and slowly talk about our own lives.


The discussion can be conducted in Mandarin or English..


We won't ask anyone to share experiences that make them uncomfortable. You can choose to speak or simply listen.


① Did you see "yourself" in the book?


We'd like to start with a simple question:


After reading *Minor Feelings*, was there a particular story, passage, or emotion that resonated deeply with you? Why?


It might be a single sentence. A certain experience. Or just one moment that made you think:


"So I'm not the only one who feels this way."



② The feelings that "aren't worth mentioning"


The term "Minor Feelings" in the title reminds us of many emotions that we or others tend to brush aside lightly.


Have you ever experienced something that felt "not a big deal" at the time, but later realized you've actually remembered it all along?


Why do we sometimes choose to doubt our own feelings before questioning the events that happened to us?



③ What makes a place feel like "home"?


For those of us who have lived across different cities, countries, or cultures, "home" might not be such a simple concept.


For you, what makes a place truly feel like "home"?


Is "fitting into a place" the same as "belonging to a place"?


After living in the UK, has your understanding of "home" and "belonging" changed?



④ As Asian women, what expectations do you think we carry from our families, cultures, or societies?


For example:


Be well-behaved. Work hard. Have a respectable job. Don't be too direct. Take care of others. Be "successful."


Among these expectations:


What are the things you truly identify with?


And which ones are you gradually learning to let go of?



⑤ If you could say one thing to your younger self when you first arrived in the UK, what would it be?


Maybe:


"Don't be so afraid."


"You'll slowly find your friends."


"You don't need to prove you deserve to be here."


Or simply:


"Actually, you've already done a great job."


What would you say to your past self?


Finally, we hope this can become a safe base—a space where everyone can sit down, share experiences, and get to know one another.
