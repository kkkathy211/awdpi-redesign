News


World Economic Forum Releases the *2026 Global Gender Gap Report*


On September 16, 2026, the World Economic Forum released the 20th-anniversary edition of the *Global Gender Gap Report*, covering 145 economies worldwide and serving as a core authoritative benchmark for measuring gender equality in the international community. The report shows that the current global gender gap closure rate stands at only 69.2%, a mere 0.4 percentage point increase from 2025, indicating extremely slow progress. At the current pace, it will still take 120 years to fully close the global gender gap and achieve comprehensive gender equality, signaling an overall stagnation and weak foundation in global gender equality governance.


The report divides gender equality into four dimensions, revealing significant structural imbalances. Gender gaps in health and education are nearly closed, with closure rates reaching 96.2% and 96.9% respectively; however, economic participation (61.7%) and political empowerment (22.1%) remain critical weak spots. Political empowerment is the area with the largest global gender gap and has seen a slight regression in recent years, with women rarely holding core national leadership positions. Meanwhile, the proportion of women in emerging fields such as AI remains low, both as professionals and decision-makers, highlighting a new digital gender divide that has become a major obstacle to advancing gender equality in the new era.

UNGA81 Media Report Launch:

"Progress on the Sustainable Development Goals: A Gender Snapshot 2026"


This report, jointly released by UN Women and the United Nations Department of Economic and Social Affairs, was officially launched on September 17, 2026, at the United Nations Headquarters in New York. Centered on equal female leadership and decision-making power, the report indicates a significant slowdown and even regression in global gender equality progress, challenging the superficial notion of merely achieving equal numbers of women in positions. It emphasizes that true gender equality lies in ensuring women hold core decision-making power and real influence. Data show that, at the current pace, it will take another 63 years to achieve gender parity in global parliamentary seats, and only 31 out of 175 chief justices worldwide are women, highlighting severe gender imbalance in high-level power.


In the past two years, global gender equality institutional systems have continued to weaken, with one-third of official gender equality agencies experiencing reduced authority and significant cuts in funding support, leading to ineffective policy implementation and gaps in rights protection. The report confirms that regions where women hold substantive leadership demonstrate better outcomes in sustainable development, livelihood improvement, and ecological conservation. In response, the report calls on countries to repair gender equality institutional frameworks, increase financial and policy support, focus on substantive power equality, prevent setbacks in gender governance, strengthen women's rights protection and empowerment systems, and support the realization of the 2030 Sustainable Development Goals.

United Nations General Assembly "Programme of Action on a Culture of Peace" High-Level Forum,

China Elaborates on Women's Equal Participation in Peace Processes


On September 2, 2026, the United Nations General Assembly held the High-Level Forum on the "Programme of Action on a Culture of Peace," focusing on women's leadership and peacebuilding. Sun Lei, Deputy Permanent Representative of China to the United Nations, attended and delivered a statement, systematically outlining China's core position on women's participation in global peace processes. China emphasized that women are key forces in promoting inclusive cooperation, maintaining world peace, and conducting post-conflict reconstruction, playing a unique and irreplaceable role in global peace governance, and that the construction of a culture of peace in the new era urgently requires the full activation of women's potential.


China put forward three core proposals: first, upholding the security baseline by strictly following the purposes and principles of the UN Charter, promoting ceasefires in conflict-affected areas, resolutely opposing all forms of violence against women and girls, and strengthening the security foundation for women's participation in peace processes. Second, focusing on empowerment and development by honoring the commitments of the Beijing Declaration and Platform for Action, centering on gender equality, fully leveraging women's agency, and comprehensively enhancing their capacity to participate in and lead sustainable development. Third, deepening mutual learning among civilizations by utilizing UN multilateral platforms to establish cooperation and exchange mechanisms for women, eliminating gender discrimination and prejudice, and promoting people-to-people connectivity to advance global peacebuilding.


China stated that it has always upheld its mission in advancing global women's development and world peace, and is willing to unite global women's forces to inject sustained momentum into humanity's peace and development endeavors.

WHO Report States: Global Women Face Dual Gaps in Pay and Power in Public Health Technology Positions


On September 17, 2026, the World Health Organization (WHO) released a special monitoring report on gender equality in the global health workforce, focusing on structural gender injustice in smart healthcare and digital public health, revealing the dual gaps in pay and decision-making power faced by women in the digital governance context. Survey data show that in Europe, female professionals in smart healthcare and public health big data technologies earn 28% less than their male counterparts in similar positions; globally, women's representation in core governance roles such as AI diagnostic algorithm review, public health intelligent dispatch, and medical digital standard setting remains persistently low, indicating a severe lack of technical voice.


WHO explicitly pointed out that gender exclusion in health technology directly leads to structural flaws in AI governance. Due to the absence of female technologists and governance participants, existing medical AI algorithms and public health intelligent systems commonly exhibit gender data bias and inadequate adaptation to women's health needs, significantly weakening the inclusivity and fairness of intelligent public services. In response, the report formally calls on countries to fully integrate gender equality mechanisms into digital governance and intelligent public service systems, using institutional interventions to address the shortage of female technical talent and ensure women's deep participation in the entire process of technical standard setting, algorithmic ethics review, and intelligent public governance, thereby overcoming AI algorithmic bias and governance imbalances through gender-inclusive governance.

UN Women Releases Climate and Environment Gender Strategy,

Addressing Women's Shortfall in Environmental Governance Voice


On September 1, 2026, UN Women officially released the *2026 Climate and Environment Strategy* and its accompanying Conference of the Parties (COP) position brief, systematically clarifying for the first time the deep interconnection between gender equality and global environmental governance. The report points out that international frameworks on global climate governance, ecological conservation, and desertification control have long suffered from gender underrepresentation. Women, as the groups most directly affected by climate disasters, are rarely involved in core processes such as environmental policy-making, resource allocation, and emergency decision-making, resulting in structural imbalances in voice.


The document outlines three key action directions: promoting the establishment of gender equality assessment mechanisms in environmental governance across countries, ensuring the participation of female experts and grassroots women practitioners in international climate negotiations, and providing targeted capacity-building and financial support for women in developing countries. This strategy fills a critical gap in gender equality governance within the global environmental arena and stands as a landmark document for advancing women's rights globally in 2026.

Senior U.S. Congressional Leaders Officially Condemn Rising Anti-South Asian and Asian Hate Violence


On September 18, 2026, Hakeem Jeffries, House Democratic Leader of the United States, issued an official congressional statement publicly condemning the recent surge in anti-South Asian and anti-Asian hate violence across the nation. The statement emphasized that recent unprovoked attacks targeting Sikh and Asian professionals are not isolated incidents, but a social safety crisis fueled by systemic racial bias and the proliferation of online hate speech. The official statement highlighted that Asian and South Asian communities have long suffered from unwarranted discrimination, verbal abuse, and physical assaults, severely undermining the sense of security and social inclusion for minority groups. Congress called on the U.S. Department of Justice and the FBI to strengthen oversight of hate crimes, rigorously prosecute racially motivated violence, and urged online platforms to govern hate speech and curb the spread of divisive rhetoric.

A racially targeted hate crime against an Asian couple occurred in Queens, New York,

police launch special investigation into hate crime


At 8:30 p.m. local time on September 10, 2026, a racially motivated hate crime targeting an Asian couple occurred at Hunters Point South Park in Queens, New York. A 21- and 22-year-old Asian couple was walking along the waterfront promenade when they were suddenly intercepted by an unknown woman. The suspect shouted racial slurs at them before launching a physical assault, striking the female victim in the back of the head, hitting the male victim's hands, and spraying an unknown irritant liquid at the man's head.


The two victims immediately fled the scene and sought help at a nearby restaurant, where staff members provided shelter and called the police. The New York Police Department's Hate Crimes Task Force took over the investigation and officially classified the incident as a hate crime driven by racial bias. Police reviewed surveillance footage from the park to identify the suspect and launched a citywide manhunt. As of September 12, no arrests had been made. Local crime statistics show that hate crime incidents in the area surged by 40% year-on-year in 2026, with Asian communities emerging as one of the primary targets of street-level targeted violence, highlighting systemic gaps in urban safety protections for Asians in the U.S.

National Asian American anti-discrimination organizations release September monitoring report:

Escalating issues of anti-Asian discrimination and law enforcement disparities


On September 15, 2026, the leading Asian American civil rights organization Stop AAPI Hate released its latest special monitoring report, disclosing a significant increase in incidents of discrimination, unfair law enforcement, and deportation control targeting Asian American and Pacific Islander communities across the United States from 2025 to 2026. The report points out that the frequency of specialized law enforcement and control faced by Asian immigrant groups continues to be higher than that of other ethnic groups, indicating clear issues of differential law enforcement and governance based on stereotypes and prejudice. Simultaneously, offline harassment of Asians, workplace discrimination, and targeted verbal abuse in public spaces remain persistently high, while the spread of online anti-Asian rumors and hate speech has increased substantially year-on-year. The organization calls on all levels of government in the United States to eliminate racial bias in law enforcement, improve protection mechanisms for minority rights, promote platforms to remove racially hateful content, and build an equal and inclusive social governance environment.

U.S. Congressional Asian Pacific American members speak out collectively:

Advancing institutional enhancement of Asian political voice


On September 13, 2026, Congresswoman Judy Chu, a senior Asian Pacific American member of the U.S. House of Representatives, chaired a national hearing on Asian American civic voice, directly addressing the long-standing structural challenges of underrepresentation and weak public voice within the Asian community.. During the hearing, multiple Asian American members of Congress pointed out that Asian Americans have long been marginalized in U.S. politics, public decision-making, and social discourse, and that a root cause of the frequent anti-Asian hate incidents is the lack of ethnic political voice and inadequate channels for public opinion expression. This congressional session formally proposed several reform initiatives, including empowering Asian American youth to participate in politics, improving mechanisms for minority voices to be heard, increasing Asian representation in public policy and urban governance, and shifting Asian voice from "reactive rights defense" to "proactive participation in institutional building."

Indonesia officially assumes chairmanship of the ASEAN Committee in Washington,

strengthening collective global voice for Southeast Asian Asians


On September 19, 2026, Indonesia officially succeeded Cambodia as the rotating chair of the ASEAN Committee in Washington (ACW), with the core goal of systematically enhancing the collective voice and agenda influence of Southeast Asian Asian nations within Western-led international systems. Indonesian officials stated clearly that the current global governance system has long been dominated by Western powers, resulting in the consistent marginalization and neglect of Southeast Asian Asian countries' demands, positions, and development narratives. Following this transition, ASEAN will coordinate the unified positions of its ten member states to speak collectively on U.S. foreign policy, trade and economic issues, climate change, and digital governance, overcoming the shortcomings of fragmented and dispersed influence previously seen among Asian nations. This move marks a landmark diplomatic initiative in 2026 for Southeast Asian countries to elevate their overall international voice, signifying the institutionalization and normalization of regional Asian groups' participation and expression within Western-dominated international forums.

Top Asian scholar Kishore Mahbubani speaks out:

Global power is shifting toward Asia, but the overall Asian voice remains constrained


On September 9, 2026, Kishore Mahbubani, a leading scholar at the Asia Research Institute of the National University of Singapore, released a new public analysis that sparked widespread discussion in the global international relations community. He pointed out that the world is currently at a historic turning point: The global economic and strategic center of gravity continues to shift eastward, and Asian nations' comprehensive strength has significantly recovered, yet the governance voice of Asian nations and Asian ethnic groups still lags far behind. The West continues to monopolize the formulation of international rules, control over media agendas, and dominance of value narratives, leaving Asian countries in a state of "rising strength but absent voice." Kishore Mahbubani called on all Asian nations to overcome internal divisions, unify their positions, actively participate in reshaping global rules, and systematically enhance Asian agenda-setting capabilities in politics, technology, ethics, and media governance, reversing the long-standing pattern of passive silence.
