Come on!

A journey from zero to one.

01

For Asian Women Development



     Annie, Head of Human Resources at AWDPI


Annie enjoys studying philosophy and religion, and she loves music. She speaks quickly, with an upward inflection at the end of every sentence. At the beginning of our conversation, Annie tells us she deeply values "forming deep connections with people."

 

This is the Annie who encountered AWDPI in its earliest, budding stage.

 

In May 2021, Annie, then majoring in HR at university, began working at the newly launched AWDPI, driven by her passion for public welfare activities.

 

In the early stages of an organization's development, many tasks require building from scratch. The first step for Annie and her team was to start from a blank organizational structure and gradually build one suited to AWDPI. "Some work structures or frameworks from commercial organizations cannot be fully transferred to our nonprofit context." Annie tells us, "The challenges and difficulties faced by these two types of organizations are actually not that similar."

 

Initially, Annie and her team set up six small teams based on typical commercial organizational models. But as new situations arose during operations, Annie and her team chose to reflect and adjust in a timely manner, eventually shaping the HR department into a structure that fits AWDPI's unique development path.

 

Today, the HR department consists of four teams: Recruitment, Training, Performance Evaluation, and Employee Relations. Together, they support and empower the entire process of selecting, employing, and retaining talent at AWDPI. "This is actually quite unique—most HR departments either follow the six-module model or adopt something like the three-pillar HR model," says Annie.

 

This process of reflection and adjustment continues. Starting last year, Annie and her team began working on digitizing and streamlining their workflows.

 

Annie recalls that when she first joined AWDPI, all recruitment information had to be handled manually. "This included tracking the entire recruitment progress through Excel spreadsheets, as well as communicating with heads of various departments." But such processes inevitably consumed unnecessary time and energy for team members.

 

Thus, AWDPI began building a digital and online work model.

 

"We gradually started using online tools like Feishu and multidimensional tables, and this year, we also adjusted some of our models and processes based on issues we encountered in practice."


 02

    Stop violence in Love

  We are the organization's caretakers.           

Every year during recruitment, Annie likes to ask one question: Why do you want to join us? Usually, she gets answers like: Because I really want to help those in need. Yet in Annie's view, HR work doesn't directly generate such feelings.

 

Compared to frontline departments, most of HR's work is internal. "Our direct connection to the people we serve isn't that strong," says Annie.

 

But at the same time, HR work is deeply tied to a powerful perception from another group. "I always tell my team: we in HR are the first point of contact. Whether or not someone joins, every volunteer's first interaction is with us. So the HR department shapes how candidates perceive our organization."

 

For HR staff at AWDPI, helping often takes an 'indirect' form: by empowering volunteers across the organization, including those in frontline departments like the Psychological Support Unit and Legal Consultation Unit who directly serve beneficiaries; or by identifying outstanding volunteers so they can better support those in need within the organization. "The caretaker of the organization." This is how Annie describes HR work.


In fact, this is another challenge Annie feels beyond digital efficiency—"how to bring in people who are truly suitable for this role and for our organization."

 

Annie believes the key lies in alignment at the foundational level of values. "What behavioral principles represent our organizational culture? What kind of person fits us best?" Today, Annie and her team are continuously working to concretize corporate values and distill organizational culture, hoping to use this as a reference and support for volunteer recruitment.


And after selecting people, how to "empower" these outstanding volunteers is another question actively on Annie's mind.


"We in HR are always thinking about how to enhance everyone's sense of belonging, and how to help people enjoy their work." Says Annie. She defines enjoyable work as doing what you are "good at" and "love to do."

For Asian Women Development

Inclusive, participatory, and friendly.

For Annie, HR work at AWDPI might just be exactly that kind of "good at" and "love to do" activity. She doesn't like to call her three years at AWDPI "work"; she prefers to describe it as "happily doing things together."


"A group of us, not receiving any salary, spending a lot of time and energy doing things together with people who share similar values." Beyond material rewards or money, the "return" for Annie and her HR teammates comes from elsewhere. Perhaps it's the precious connection of unexpectedly meeting like-minded, diverse teammates at AWDPI, collaborating and exchanging ideas, even holding casual "teatime chats" outside of work; perhaps it's the small satisfaction of contributing, as volunteers, to advancing gender equality; or perhaps, it's the discovery of another dimension of life through AWDPI.

 

Whatever it is, at AWDPI, Annie and her teammates from all over have spent these three years together—years marked by inclusion, participation, and friendliness.

 

Over these three years, as the "small seed" of AWDPI gradually sprouted and grew, Annie's own life was also undergoing changes. At that moment, this small seed—rooted in inclusion, participation, and friendliness—seemed to have grown mature enough to steadily support Annie through her occasional moments of anxiety.

 

Recently, Annie was unable to host her department's welcome meeting due to personal reasons.. "I was really torn—it was a department-wide event, and so many teammates were involved." After careful consideration, Annie ultimately decided to postpone the event. When she first tried sharing her true situation in the group chat and announced the postponement, she was pleasantly surprised to find so much support and warmth from everyone. Unconsciously, at AWDPI, Annie began receiving energy and nourishment from another dimension of life.


04 

     For Asian Women Development

A self-reflection of an Asian woman.

Before the conversation between Ta Power and Annie began, Annie expressed her concerns, worrying she might not be able to offer insights with sufficient academic depth. But when it came to the challenges faced by Asian women, Annie sincerely and openly shared her valuable reflections as an ordinary Asian woman.

 

In Annie's view, the struggles of Asian women emerge from all aspects of their upbringing—racial discrimination, gender discrimination, and some deeply rooted traditional values. After confronting various challenges in her own growth, Annie has found her most comfortable state: building a sense of security as an Asian woman.

 

In Annie's words, she hopes to create a sense of self-worth as a woman that comes from within and endures even when societal identities are stripped away. "It might be the core internal drive and motivation that supports us to go further and longer into the future."


In fact, at AWDPI, beyond frontline support departments like Psychological Support and Legal Aid, there are many outstanding volunteers like Annie holding the fort in the background, providing equally critical support for the organization's development. Collaborating with these dedicated volunteers and various sectors of society to address these challenges is exactly what AWDPI is committed to doing.

 

Including the Ta Power column, AWDPI is working through various media platforms to promote women's awareness. Each year, AWDPI hosts a series of Global Asian Women Forums, inviting accomplished women from the workplace to showcase the diversity and talents of Asian women. At the same time, AWDPI is striving to build support networks, aiming to connect resources so that those in need can access rescue and emotional support more quickly.

 



"I truly hope that AWDPI, this inclusive and warm family, will welcome more and more teammates who wish to contribute to advancing gender equality." Annie tells us. And for her, the precious connection she began with AWDPI three years ago is far from over.
