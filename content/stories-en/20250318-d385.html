Hit Korean Drama "A Good Fight"

Reveals Identity Struggles of East Asian Girls


Recently, South Korea's release of the GL (Girl's Love) drama "A Good Fight" sparked widespread online discussion and strong resonance among East Asian women. The series explores parent-child relationships under East Asian patriarchal society, modes of same-sex and heterosexual interaction, class issues, and the alienation caused by educational competition pressure.

 

Through delicate cinematography, the series narrates the academic pressure and mental health challenges brought by college entrance exams on East Asian students. Its portrayal of adolescent girls' sexuality, in a media landscape where female sexuality is often treated as taboo, promotes public understanding and normalization of female sexual desire. The ambiguous relationship between the female leads has become a focal point of audience discussion. In a media world dominated by heteronormativity, where gay male communities have historically occupied a dominant position in discussions and representations of sexual minorities due to gender privilege, this focus on lesbian characters clearly demonstrates high public acceptance and support for this group.

 

The success of "A Good Fight" also marks an important transformation in East Asian society's representation of women's issues. Amid a conservative media landscape, its open treatment of sensitive topics has clearly advanced societal recognition, understanding, and inclusion of adolescent girls and lesbian communities.

2024 Marks the First Achievement of Gender Equality on Screen


A recent research report from the Annenberg Media Center at the University of Southern California shows that in 2024, the proportion of female leads in the highest-grossing films in North America reached gender parity for the first time. The report notes that among the top 100 highest-grossing films of 2024, 54 featured female protagonists or co-leads, a significant increase from 30 in 2023.


Among the top five highest-grossing films of 2024, three were led by women, including Disney/Pixar's "Inside Out 2," which combined commercial success with themes of teenage girls' emotional fluctuations, sparking widespread social discussion. However, the report points out that the film industry made no progress in racial representation in 2024. The number of films led by minority actors declined compared to 2023.


Dr. Smith, founder and co-creator of AII, stated that the success of female-led films is not due to a sudden market awakening, but rather the result of sustained advocacy, film studio efforts, and DEI (Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion) initiatives. However, the decline in minority representation reflects ongoing shortcomings in inclusive storytelling within the film industry. This report not only witnesses major progress for women in the film industry but also reveals that the industry must work harder on broader inclusivity to more fully reflect the diverse identities and story needs of audiences.

From Tennis Associations to Gender Equality


Tennis Canada and National Bank recently released the GSE (Game. Set. Equity.) Impact Report, summarizing the progress made over the first two years of their five-year gender equality strategy.


The report covers key areas including increasing female participation, advancing female leadership, ensuring equal voice, promoting business development, and creating inclusive and safe environments. Key achievements include a significant increase in female participation in the U12 program, gender balance achieved in the board and senior management, record-high audiences for women's tennis events, and a commitment to equal prize money for male and female players by 2027. In addition, Tennis Canada received the highest honor from the International Tennis Federation (ITF) for its work in gender equality.


This report not only demonstrates Tennis Canada's sustained efforts in advancing gender equality but also highlights the critical role of sports in social change. Through this long-term strategy, Tennis Canada is actively breaking down barriers, creating a fairer environment for female athletes, and driving tennis toward a more inclusive and diverse future.

Say No to Online Extremist Anti-South Asian Rhetoric


According to a report released by the nonprofit organization Stop AAPI Hate, anti-South Asian hate speech in extremist online spaces has sharply increased since the 2024 U.S. election. The report shows that anti-Asian online attacks in January 2025 reached the highest level since monitoring began in August 2022, with anti-South Asian rhetoric showing particularly significant growth—increasing by 75% compared to November 2024.


These hate speeches primarily target Indian communities, including accusations of "stealing job opportunities" and "threatening white lifestyles." At the same time, violent threats against Asians have increased significantly, and anti-East Asian rhetoric has also risen due to political concerns about China. The report notes that after the government expressed support for high-skilled legal immigration, some far-right individuals launched attacks and violent threats against Asians on social media. Although Trump expressed support for the H-1B visa program, he had previously criticized it, calling it "very bad for local workers."


The report indicates that online hate speech against Asians, especially South Asians, has continued to rise in recent years in the United States, a trend closely linked to the rising political prominence of South Asian figures. The report calls for more measures to address this issue and ensure safety and inclusivity in online spaces.

Man in Southern California Sentenced for Assaulting Asian Woman


In the early hours of June 14, 2021, Jesse Allen Lindsey, a man from Southern California, attacked an Asian woman on the streets of Culver City. He not only stalked the victim but also shouted racist slurs, and after beating her to the ground face-down, continued to verbally abuse her. The victim sustained serious injuries.


In December 2024, Lindsey pleaded guilty to hate crime charges and was sentenced to 51 months in federal court in Los Angeles. After the incident, Lindsey fled California out of fear of punishment and was eventually apprehended by law enforcement within a state prison. He used stereotypes to describe the victim and falsely claimed self-defense. However, evidence showed that the victim was small in stature, while Lindsey was a large, strong man, making the attack entirely unjustified.


This case not only highlights the ongoing issue of hate crimes in American society but also reflects the challenges Asian communities face regarding public safety. Lindsey's conviction and sentencing send a clear message to society: racial hate crimes will be severely punished.

Popular New Zealand Restaurant Discriminates Against Chinese Tourists:

Involved Staff Have Been Fired


On February 17, a tourist from Taiwan, China, received a receipt containing racially discriminatory language while dining at Fergburger, a popular burger restaurant in Queenstown, New Zealand. The incident sparked widespread attention, with many netizens expressing anger and criticism over the staff's inappropriate behavior.

 

On February 20, the restaurant apologized to the Consulate General of China in Christchurch and promised on social media to conduct an investigation and implement corrective measures. The restaurant stated it had conducted a full review of the incident, formally apologized to the affected customer, and strengthened staff training on respecting different cultures. To emphasize the seriousness of the incident, the involved employee has been dismissed.

 

The Consulate General of China in Christchurch demanded that the restaurant prevent similar incidents from happening again. This incident not only reveals the lack of cultural sensitivity in the overseas food service industry but also reminds businesses to strengthen staff training and create a more inclusive and respectful consumer environment.

Shirley Chong Joins THE World 100 Reputation Network (W100)


Shirley Chong, Director of Communication and Institutional Research at City University of Hong Kong, has officially joined THE World 100 Reputation Network (W100) as a committee member and Asian representative of this global university reputation management network. This appointment marks Asia's higher education sector contributing to global development.


Chong stated that the decision to join the W100 Committee stems from City University of Hong Kong's global development strategy. Through this platform, Chong hopes to promote broader global recognition of Asian universities and share City University of Hong Kong's experience in brand building. In recent years, the university has performed excellently in global university rankings and was named the world's most international university in 2024 by Times Higher Education.


In the future, City University of Hong Kong plans to collaborate with W100, a partnership that will not only advance the internationalization of Asian universities but also enhance their influence in the global higher education arena. Chong's participation has boosted the visibility of Asian universities on the global stage, offering fresh perspectives and strategic insights into reputation management in international higher education.

Gong Youchai becomes the first Chinese-language mentor at Crypto Content Creator Campus


On February 14, 2025, Crypto Content Creator Campus (CCCC) announced that Gong Youchai (Gong Youchai) has joined its KOL incubation school as the program's first mentor to deliver instruction in Chinese. This move reflects the growing influence of the Chinese-speaking community in the cryptocurrency space and highlights CCCC's commitment to global, diverse education.


Phoebe Peng, head of CCCC, said that Gong Youchai's involvement is a significant asset for participants in the KOL incubation school. Gong's market insights and close ties with the Chinese-speaking community will greatly enhance the program's impact. Gong also expressed enthusiasm for the role, stating that the power of education and community can help more content creators find their own path in the world of cryptocurrency.


This appointment not only strengthens CCCC's global reach but also creates more growth opportunities for Chinese-speaking communities in content creation within the Web3 and cryptocurrency fields.

University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill

Establishes Asian American Center


The University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill (UNC-Chapel Hill) has recently officially launched the Asian American Center (AAC), which aims to promote understanding of Asian American culture and history and provide support for Asian American communities on campus.


The center is directed by Heidi Kim, associate professor in the Department of English and Comparative Literature, and has set a fundraising goal of $5 million to ensure long-term operations. The project has received strong advocacy and financial support from students and alumni. The AAC plans to host a series of seminars addressing topics such as Asian American identity, ethnic relations, and historical issues. Kim noted that Asian Americans are the fastest-growing minority group in North Carolina, and 17% of the university's student body identifies as Asian or Asian American, making the establishment of a dedicated center highly significant. Kim hopes the center will eventually secure a physical space to provide a stronger sense of belonging for Asian American students and the broader community.


The establishment of this center not only fills a long-standing gap at UNC but also symbolizes the increasing attention and support that Asian American communities are receiving within U.S. higher education institutions.
