The United Nations' 2024 International Women's Day theme is "Invest in Women: Accelerate Progress." By 2030, the annual funding gap for gender equality initiatives could reach as high as 360 billion US dollars, making it urgent to advance financing for gender equality.


During the United Nations 2023 International Day for the Elimination of Violence against Women "16 Days of Activism," we hosted five online workshops, discussing topics such as "Occupational Diseases from a Gender Perspective" with over 300 audience members. The 2024 International Women's Day theme continues the same thread as the 2023 International Day for the Elimination of Violence against Women theme: "Eliminating gender-based violence through adequate funding." Without significant investment to address violence against women and girls, expand prevention programs, and implement effective policies, countries will not be able to eliminate gender-based violence by 2030.


This year, AWDPI Asian Women Development Plan International will present four workshops across China, Germany, and Ecuador during International Women's Day, joining people worldwide who care about gender issues in expressing support for survivors of gender-based violence.


#ThisWorkshop



Li Xiwen

Gender, Development, and Equity & Inclusion Analyst


Whether from Palestine to Afghanistan, Somalia to Myanmar, or Haiti to Syria, we often see humanitarian aid workers in regions affected by war and natural disasters. However, beyond commonly discussed gender challenges in humanitarian crises—such as gender-based violence or gender-based educational inequality—humanitarian aid workers themselves face numerous gender-related challenges in their work. These challenges are mostly overlooked or unknown to the public.


In this workshop, we will focus on the gender challenges faced by humanitarian aid workers. From workplace sexual harassment and hiring inequality to gender challenges within aid projects, and the intersectional inequalities involving gender, race, nationality, and age manifested in humanitarian work, we will deeply explore existing problems in humanitarian aid and further discuss the potential for greater inclusion and equality in the future of humanitarian work, as well as its possible impact on other sectors in recipient regions.
