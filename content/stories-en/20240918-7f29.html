OCF Forum .

The Oxford China Forum (OCF) is an annual summit initiated by Oxford students and is one of the largest and most academically rigorous forums on China in the UK. By inviting world-class politicians, entrepreneurs, media figures, and scholars to discuss and exchange ideas, OCF provides diverse perspectives for the public to observe, understand, and reflect on a changing China.

12th Oxford China Forum Summer Forum

Riding the Tide: Exploring New Opportunities of the Era


In this rapidly changing post-pandemic era, technological advancement accelerates, industrial upgrading becomes imperative, de-globalization gains strength, trade protectionism resurges, the international landscape shifts dramatically, and regional conflicts occur frequently. Each of us, shaped and influenced across all levels of social life, strives to find our footing amid the challenges and opportunities brought by changing external environments. This footing often manifests in individual life choices through engagement with specific industries, organizations, or institutions. Therefore, carrying forward the spirit of last year's theme "Seeking Significance in the Details," this year's forum focuses on organizations formed by people, offering a fresh perspective to examine the texture of our times.


In the face of difficulties, change leads to solutions, and solutions lead to longevity. To effect change, both courage and methodology are essential. Courage is not empty boasting, but the confidence born of thorough analysis of vast amounts of information. Methodology is not armchair theorizing, but a clear and practical action guide. There will be a time to ride the wind and cleave the waves; we shall set up our cloud-sailed raft and cross the sea. This past summer, the successfully held offline forums focused on the Intelligent Revolution and Sailing Toward the Future. The former aimed to build a bridge between theory and practice, while the latter explored the relationship between clean energy and smart transportation. Next, the online session on September 22 will echo the previous forums, continuing to address the needs of the times, gathering wisdom from all sectors, promoting the integration of academic insights and practical applications, and bringing tangible reflection and inspiration to a broader audience.






The Oxford China Forum (OCF) was founded in 2013 and is an officially registered student academic organization at the University of Oxford. It has now grown into one of the largest and most academically rigorous China-focused forums in the UK. In addition to its annual thematic summit, OCF has developed a range of regular offline activities—including lectures, publications, and visits—as well as an online exchange platform.


As one of the most influential China forums overseas, OCF is committed to bringing together leading experts on Chinese topics and students from across the UK, both Chinese and international, to engage in in-depth discussions about a changing China. Past forums have been live-streamed globally by platforms such as Sohu, Douyin, and Phoenix News, accumulating over 10 million viewers.


In previous forums, we have been honored to invite over 300 guests, including former UN Secretary-General Ban Ki-moon, former Australian Prime Minister Kevin Rudd, former British Ambassador to China Sir William Ehrman, CEO of Huayi Brothers Wang Zhonglei, ZhenFund founder Xu Xiaoping, Harvard political philosopher Michael Sandel, anthropologist Xiang Biao, Lin Wei, President of Southwest University of Political Science and Law, Academician Wu Zhiqiang, former Vice President of Tongji University, Zhang Weiwei, Director of the China Institute at Fudan University, Shi Jianzhong, Vice President of China University of Political Science and Law, Zhang Xinbao, Professor of Law at Renmin University, Yan Yunxiang, Professor of Anthropology at UCLA, Lin Chufang, former Vice President of ByteDance, filmmaker Xie Fei, artist Xu Bing, writers Xu Zhiyuan and Nanpai Sanshu, and Li Zexiang, Chairman of DJI Innovation Technology Group, to discuss topics related to China and explore the frontiers of academic fields such as social sciences and cognitive sciences.







Sub-forum I: AI×Law Series II: 

Smart Rule of Law and Its Practice


When we examine technology from a legal perspective, how should existing legal frameworks regulate AI, a technology that is becoming increasingly mature? And when we review the law from a technological standpoint, how can new technologies be applied within the legal system to make it smarter, more cost-effective, and more impactful in people's daily lives? When artificial intelligence converges with law, the future of AI regulation and legal intelligence is bound not to be confined within any single country, but to become a subject of global discussion. The future integration of law and AI will be a grand endeavor involving multiple disciplines, fields, and nations. It will not only enhance the intelligence of legal systems but also promote the development and refinement of the rule of law globally, profoundly influencing our social structures and everyday lives.


To better present the practical trajectory of smart rule of law and foster intellectual exchange in the AI era, we have invited six top domestic experts from related fields. Their research spans intellectual property law, data law, and artificial intelligence, and they are engaged in both theoretical research and judicial adjudication. They will share their insights on AI and the rule of law from diverse perspectives.



1. How can judicial authorities reduce over-reliance on artificial intelligence and avoid the monopoly of "computing power as power"?

2. While generative AI improves evidentiary efficiency, how should judicial authorities remain vigilant against the "black box" of technology?

3. How can lawyers in different practice areas become rational users of AI software?

4. From the perspective of originality, can AI-generated content be recognized as a work protected under intellectual property law?

5. Intellectual property perspectives on the ethical governance of artificial intelligence








Sub-forum II: Opportunities and Compliance Challenges for Enterprises Going Global


Amid the great wave of globalization, Chinese enterprises expanding overseas has become a significant strategic choice, bringing not only new growth opportunities for the enterprises themselves but also injecting fresh vitality into China's international economic development. "Going global" refers to the process by which enterprises promote their brands and products into overseas markets, seeking transnational operations and international development, and is now facing unprecedented opportunities and challenges.


On one hand, Chinese enterprises can benefit from policy support and international cooperation during their overseas expansion. With the advancement of the Belt and Road Initiative, the accelerated internationalization of the RMB, and the expansion of domestic demand, enterprises have more opportunities to enter international markets. The rise of emerging markets also provides a broad stage, and the emergence of the digital economy offers new platforms and tools for going global. Enterprises can more easily access overseas markets through e-commerce and digital services, enhancing brand influence and market competitiveness.


On the other hand, enterprises face numerous challenges when going global. Increased uncertainty in the global political and economic environment, the complexity of past multilateral relationships, and the rise of trade protectionism all heighten the risks of overseas expansion. To flexibly address these new challenges, enterprises need to upgrade their global competitiveness strategies in supply networks and R&D investment, consider long-term, diversified supply chain structures, and prioritize R&D investment to maintain and catch up with technological leadership. The urgency of compliance construction is also increasingly evident. Enterprises need to strengthen preparedness and management in areas such as data protection, intellectual property protection, and anti-monopoly regulations to ensure they stay current and adapt to changes, operating in compliance within a diverse and competitive environment.


This forum will focus on the real challenges and opportunities faced by enterprises going global, inviting experts and scholars to jointly explore solutions and help Chinese enterprises move steadily forward on their internationalization journey. We hope that through in-depth discussion and exchange, enterprises can better understand the dynamics of international markets, formulate effective overseas strategies, and strengthen compliance frameworks to ensure stable and sustainable progress in global competition. We hope that this forum will provide strategic advice and support for the international development of Chinese enterprises and contribute to the takeoff of China's economy.




Morning Session: How to Identify Opportunities for Enterprises Going Global


1. What is the significance of new macro paradigms and industrial trends for enterprises going global?

2. In the coming era of semi-globalization, where do China's development opportunities lie?

3. Which industries and sectors are better suited for overseas expansion? How should they assess and select investment directions during the process?

4. In overseas markets where legal systems, business practices, and consumer behaviors differ, how can Chinese enterprises effectively resolve cultural conflicts?

5. Under the current global economic landscape and geopolitical shifts—such as the impact of tariffs imposed by the US and EU on Chinese automakers—how should enterprises maintain competitiveness?


Afternoon Session: Compliance Issues for Enterprises Going Global


1. Data compliance issues.

2. Intellectual property compliance issues.

3. Investment compliance issues.

4. Trade dispute resolution issues.

5. Tax and financial compliance issues.
