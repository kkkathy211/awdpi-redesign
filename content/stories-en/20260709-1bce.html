Domestic violence does not always appear in the most obvious form.


It may involve physical harm,

emotional manipulation, economic control, isolation, threats, surveillance,

or ongoing coercive control within intimate relationships.


Often, individuals experiencing violence and control need more than just a simple suggestion to "leave."


They may be facing language barriers, cultural pressures, immigration concerns, lack of resources, economic dependence, and fears about safety consequences. For immigrant women, members of cross-cultural communities, and linguistic minorities, seeking help is never a simple decision, but a process that requires understanding, respect, and support.


July 16, 9:00 AM (Eastern Time) Beijing Time, July 16, 9:00 PM, AWDPI (Asian Women Development Plan International) will partner with La Casa de las Madres to co-host a Virtual DV101 Training focusing on foundational understanding of domestic violence, risk identification, community response, and survivor support. The session will include sharing and discussion.




Partner Organization Introduction




La Casa de las Madres is a professional organization that has long served domestic violence survivors, dedicated to providing support, education, and resource connections for individuals experiencing domestic violence, intimate partner violence, and related risks.


With extensive experience in community education and survivor support, La Casa has consistently focused on domestic violence prevention, crisis intervention, community awareness building, and safe response strategies. This DV101 Training will start from basic concepts, helping participants understand common manifestations of domestic violence, the impact of coercive control, and the boundaries, methods, and resource pathways community members should consider when supporting survivors.



We will discuss together



This training will cover the following topics:

  • How to understand domestic violence and coercive control;

  • How to identify violence, control, and risk signals in intimate relationships;

  • What barriers to seeking help survivors in immigrant and cross-cultural communities may face;

  • How friends, volunteers, and community members can respond in safe and respectful ways;

  • What practices might cause secondary harm when supporting survivors;

  • How to connect those in need to more professional and safer support resources.

Significance of the Event



Often, domestic violence is not just an issue that happens "in someone else's life."


It may hide within intimate relationships,

within silence, control, and fear,

within the hesitation of "not wanting to trouble others,"

and within the self-doubt of "is this serious enough?"



For many survivors, seeking help is not easy.


What they may need is not immediate judgment,

not being rushed into decisions,

and not being asked to prove their suffering.


Instead, they need a safer space,

the possibility of being truly heard,

a visible pathway to support,

and responses from those around them that are more conscious, more respectful, and more mindful of boundaries.


This training aims to leave participants with more than just basic knowledge about domestic violence.


It aims to foster a more sensitive understanding:

violence exists not only in visible wounds, but also in long-term control;

support involves not only showing care, but also requires methods, boundaries, and safety awareness;

community response is not just about "helping one person," but about collectively building a more reliable support network.


AWDPI hopes this Virtual DV101 Training will help members, volunteers, and community participants enhance their foundational awareness and practical sensitivity regarding domestic violence issues. We also hope this training will help more people learn to respond with less hasty judgment and more understanding, respect, and safety when facing such situations.
