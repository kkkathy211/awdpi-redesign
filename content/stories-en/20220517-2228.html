Speaker



Professor Leigh Crout is a C.V. Starr Senior Legal Lecturer at the Peking University School of Transnational Law and a practicing attorney. She earned her Master of Arts in International Development with honors from Cornell University and holds a Doctor of Juridical Science in Civil Law and International Human Rights Law from the University of Notre Dame Law School. She has worked with Kentucky Legal Aid of the Bluegrass to represent victims of domestic violence, helping them obtain and enforce protection orders against abusive partners or family members. Her current research focuses on constitutional law and the rights of immigrant women.

Overview

The topic of this lecture is an introduction to U.S. laws and judicial practices on domestic violence, with a focus on the legal procedures for protection orders available to victims. The lecture will particularly emphasize economically disadvantaged clients seeking legal aid and introduce resources available to domestic violence victims outside the courtroom..


CONTENT

Today, we will deepen our understanding of how domestic violence occurs and how the U.S. judicial system responds.

Key Points:

1. Common legal terms related to domestic violence and its litigation procedures

2. How to use relevant laws to defend clients' rights in court

3. Common forms of assistance available to victims outside the courtroom


The U.S. Judicial System

The U.S. court system is complex, with multiple judicial systems coexisting and complementing each other. It is primarily divided into two major systems: federal courts and state courts, each operating under their own constitutions and laws and handling different types of cases and jurisdictions.


The U.S. federal courts are part of the federal government.


State courts are established by individual U.S. states and primarily handle matters related to everyday social operations, including education, healthcare, criminal law, contracts, and more.


Since domestic violence incidents are typically treated as criminal cases, most domestic violence laws fall under state law. There are also some complementary federal laws, such as the Violence Against Women Act (VAWA), signed by Bill Clinton in 1994. This law makes it illegal to assault a victim or violate an existing protection order. However, during the Trump administration, the law expired, though the Biden administration has listed restoring its effectiveness as a priority, including implementing new policies to better protect women.



Today's discussion will focus on Kentucky state law.


Kentucky is surrounded by mountains, with the Appalachian Mountains isolating it from other parts of the U.S., making the region somewhat isolated. The local economy is primarily based on coal mining, but the U.S. coal industry has a bleak outlook, leading many to lose interest in the region and turn elsewhere. These factors have contributed to widespread poverty.


According to data released by the

National Coalition Against Domestic Violence:

  • 45% of women and 35% of men in Kentucky have experienced domestic violence.

  • The state ranks 11th highest in the U.S. for the rate of women murdered.

  • Kentucky does not prohibit domestic violence offenders from owning firearms. When abusers have access to guns, the risk of women being killed increases by at least fivefold.

  • Kentucky must make systemic changes. A significant movement is currently underway in many other U.S. jurisdictions to comprehensively ban domestic violence perpetrators from possessing firearms.


Kentucky law categorizes relationship violence into two types: domestic violence and dating violence. Domestic violence specifically refers to violent acts between individuals living in the same household (i.e., family members).


Covered individuals include: "spouses (including former spouses), grandparents, grandchildren, parents, children, stepchildren, or any other person from the same family, or any other person living in the same household as the child (when the child is the victim)."


Violence between unmarried partners also falls under domestic violence.


Covered individuals include: "unmarried couples who have a child together, any child of either partner, or unmarried couples who are currently cohabiting or have previously cohabited."


Due to the state's poverty, cohabitation without marriage is common. Many people receive monthly government assistance. Marriage might reduce or eliminate their benefits, so many choose to cohabit without marrying to maintain regular government support.


Who qualifies as a victim of dating violence?

Dating violence refers to violence occurring within an intimate romantic relationship.


It can be difficult to determine whether a relationship qualifies as a dating relationship. Parties may claim they are not in a dating relationship to avoid penalties under domestic violence laws.



Based on Professor Crout's courtroom experience, judges often use text messages or social media to determine whether two people are in a dating relationship.



Common elements of domestic violence and dating violence


  • Physical injury

  • Serious physical injury

  • Stalking

  • Sexual abuse

  • Strangulation

  • Sudden assault, or fear of any of the above


What options are available to victims of domestic or dating violence?


  • Report the domestic violence incident immediately

  • Apply for a protection order


If the perpetrator is not imprisoned and could further harm the victim, the victim may seek a protection order.


If the court finds that domestic or dating violence has occurred and is likely to recur, it may issue protective measures.

These include, but are not limited to:

  • Prohibiting all forms of contact (the abuser must not contact the victim via text, email, or other means, nor express apologies; must not contact the victim through the victim's family; all such actions are strictly prohibited, or the abuser may violate the protection order);

  • Prohibiting the abuser from coming within 500 feet of the protected person;

  • Prohibiting the abuser from coming within 500 feet of protected locations (home, school, workplace, etc.);

  • Prohibiting the abuser from disposing of or otherwise damaging the protected person's property. (If the victim left belongings at the abuser's residence, the abuser must not damage them. They are obligated to return the property, which also safeguards the victim's economic security.)

Other measures:

  • The court may prohibit the abuser from taking other actions if it deems such measures helpful in preventing future domestic violence and abuse;

  • The court may require one or both parties to attend community-provided counseling (such as parenting counseling, substance abuse counseling, etc.) in domestic violence and abuse cases;

  • The court may order the abuser to leave the shared residence (in Kentucky, this is particularly important because it allows the victim to terminate the lease. Before this reform, victims remained bound by the lease agreement, either continuing to pay rent or, if neither the victim nor the abuser paid, suffering damage to their credit report. In the worst case, they had to continue living with the abuser, remaining in the same household, with the abuser continuing to threaten the victim's economic and even physical safety);

  • Grant temporary custody of children;

  • Provide temporary child support.

Steps to obtain a protection order:

  • File a petition for a protection order

  • The judge reviews the petition and determines whether domestic violence has occurred

  • A hearing is held within 14 days of filing the petition;

  • After the hearing, the judge issues the protection order; if there is no basis or no evidence of domestic violence, the petition may be dismissed.


How can a victim apply for a protection order?

Go to the local court, which should be open 24 hours a day, seven days a week to accept petitions.


The victim will then submit a complete petition, including all necessary information for the judge to decide whether to hold a hearing.


♦ A protection order petition typically looks like the image below:


▲ The image includes: the defendant's name, address, and occupation, to help court staff locate the defendant; A warning to court staff about whether the defendant possesses weapons and whether approaching them is dangerous;


▲ The underlined section is for the petitioner to fill in details and memories of the domestic or dating violence;


▲ Whether the petitioner is a minor, or whether they are filing on behalf of a minor who cannot file independently; the relationship with the defendant, to help the judge determine whether it is domestic or dating violence;


▲ Information about any children shared by the two parties (whether the children are jointly owned, whether the petitioner is seeking help on their behalf);


▲ What protections the petitioner wants included in the protection order: prohibiting the defendant from committing domestic violence; deciding whether they want to maintain any contact with the defendant, such as only for child custody matters;


▲ Whether to prohibit the defendant from approaching specific areas or being within certain zones, including home, family residence, workplace, etc.;


▲ The end of the petition also includes space for the judge to write the decision, informing the petitioner whether a hearing will be held and when.


♦ A protection order petition typically includes the following information:

  1. The defendant's name, address, and occupation, to help court staff locate the defendant;

  2. A warning to court staff about whether the defendant possesses weapons and whether approaching them is dangerous;

  3. Details and memories of the domestic or dating violence;

  4. Whether the petitioner is a minor, or whether they are filing on behalf of a minor who cannot file independently;

  5. the relationship between the petitioner and the defendant, to help the judge determine whether the case involves domestic violence or dating violence;

  6. information about any children shared by the two parties (whether the children are jointly shared, and whether the petitioner is seeking help on behalf of the children);

  7. the specific protections the petitioner wishes to include in the protection order (prohibiting the defendant from committing acts of domestic violence; deciding whether the petitioner wants to maintain any contact with the defendant, such as only for child custody matters; prohibiting the defendant from approaching or being present in specific locations, including the home, family residence, workplace, etc.).


After determining whether domestic violence or dating violence has occurred based on the petition, the judge may issue two types of special protection orders, namely emergency protective orders and temporary interpersonal protective orders.


Both of these special protection orders are designed to protect petitioners whose personal safety is currently under threat, and are typically valid for six months. After expiration, petitioners must reapply by submitting a new petition. (Since many areas in Kentucky are mountainous or rural, court staff may find it difficult to locate the defendant, and the defendant may also deliberately evade service, leading to the protection order becoming ineffective.)


The role of lawyers

Lawyers provide legal consultation to petitioners or defendants, or represent them during hearings and enforcement matters;


if petitioners or defendants are low-income, they may seek assistance from legal aid organizations;


regardless of whether they have legal representation, petitioners must file their petition at the local court on their own (usually, petitioners have not yet consulted a lawyer before filing);


if a lawyer meets with the petitioner before the petition is filed, they can do the following to help increase the petitioner's chances of success:


♦ confirm the type of protection order the petitioner wishes to apply for;

♦ emphasize the evidentiary acts of violence or harm included in the petition

(but cannot tell the petitioner exactly what to say or write);

♦ encourage the petitioner to present evidence honestly, directly, and as completely as possible

(new evidence introduced during the hearing but not mentioned in the petition may be disregarded by the judge, as the defendant would not have had prior notice and thus no opportunity to respond. Therefore, lawyers should encourage petitioners to write down all experiences and memories related to the incident of domestic or dating violence).


Pre-hearing preparation

After the client files for a protection order, the court will notify both parties of the specific hearing date. By the time the client finds a lawyer, the lawyer often has only a few days to prepare. Therefore, evidence collection during this period is crucial.



During this time, the client and lawyer usually have their first conversation. In this conversation, the lawyer should pay attention to the following points:


♦confirm whether your client feels safe. When clients do not feel safe, they may be unable to describe what happened. You should comfort them and assure them that their current environment is safe. If the client's mental state is so poor that they cannot describe the abuse, you may encourage them to seek help from professional psychological counseling services.


♦listen patiently. clients may have suffered severe trauma, so you must be patient and give them space to speak. Victims in domestic violence cases often experience long-term social isolation before seeking legal protection and are unable to communicate with family or friends. For them, recounting the abuse in front of a lawyer is equivalent to reopening the deepest wounds. Therefore, in handling domestic violence cases, you must offer not only professional assistance but also humanitarian support.


♦take notes and maintain eye contact. If communicating remotely, you should record relevant legal facts and involved individuals. During in-person meetings, lawyers should record the conversation only after obtaining the client's consent. While taking notes, maintain eye contact and avoid constantly looking down.


During the conversation, the client may be unwilling to discuss the details of the abuse. In such cases, you should explain why you need to know these details and how they are important to the outcome of the case. After the client finishes describing the abuse, you should ask targeted questions about prohibited behaviors specified in the statute, such as "Did the defendant harass you by phone?" or "Did the defendant stalk you?" Additionally, you should ask the client to anticipate how the defendant might respond to certain aspects or details, which helps the lawyer conduct a vulnerability analysis and respond confidently during the hearing.


Aspects to consider during evidence collection:

1. photos or videos showing injuries or physical harm to the client at the time of the domestic violence incident

2. medical reports issued by hospitals documenting injuries

3. police reports

4. eyewitnesses who directly witnessed the domestic violence

(friends, acquaintances)

5. text messages from mobile phones

(since text messages can be interpreted in multiple ways, they should be used cautiously as evidence to prevent the defendant from using the content to counterattack)

All of the above evidence must be verified during the hearing.


Lawyers should maintain contact with clients as much as possible to stay informed about their current situation and thoughts. For example:


  • has the defendant attempted to contact the client? Has the defendant damaged the client's property?


  • has the client changed their mind about any condition in the emergency protective order or temporary interpersonal protective order?


  • is the client considering withdrawing the petition? Why? (There are relevant cases where clients missed the hearing or withdrew the petition out of fear of facing the defendant in court. In such cases, the lawyer should provide psychological support to the client.)


On the day of the hearing

Basic procedure:

1. the plaintiff makes a statement

(the lawyer should inform the client to establish the relationship during this stage)

2. after a recess, the defendant makes a statement. The defendant presents evidence and cross-examines.

3. after the defendant finishes speaking, the plaintiff presents evidence and cross-examines.


If the judge rules that domestic violence or dating violence occurred and finds that there is a 51% or greater likelihood of recurrence, the judge will issue a protection order. The duration and conditions of the protection order may vary, but the order cannot exceed three years. After three years, the judge may decide at their discretion whether to renew it.


After the hearing

Kentucky enacted relevant laws in 2020 to protect victims' rights. Under the law, victims have the right to be informed about their rights, the progress of the case, and the status of the defendant (i.e., whether the defendant has been arrested, released, or escaped).


After the hearing concludes and the protection order takes effect, some complex situations may still arise, such as the defendant appealing the decision, the plaintiff wishing to modify child-related terms with the defendant, or the defendant violating the protection order.


Based on Kentucky's real-world conditions, Professor Crout and her colleagues pay special attention to the needs of vulnerable groups. Kentucky has many non-English-speaking residents whose cases require interpreters or translators. Some undocumented immigrants also suffer domestic violence. Since their immigration status could lead to deportation, Kentucky judges, when handling such cases, avoid mentioning their status and strive to provide assistance.


In handling difficult youth-related cases, judges must consider many factors, such as the fact that both parties may attend the same school, making encounters unavoidable when issuing interpersonal protection orders.


Q&A:

1. What should be done during evidence collection if first-hand evidence, such as evidence of verbal abuse, cannot be obtained?

In Kentucky, some people do not have mobile phones or do not use internet communication. In such cases, verification of evidence must rely on cross-examination by the judge and both parties.


2 Can victims in domestic violence cases apply for government or social assistance? How can they apply?

First, victims can apply to us for legal aid. However, the economic hardships victims face are also addressed by specialized agencies and programs.
