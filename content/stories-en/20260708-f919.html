Fifth Ministerial Conference on Feminist Foreign Policy Held in Madrid


From June 2 to 3, 2026, Spain's Ministry of Foreign Affairs, European Union and Cooperation hosted the Fifth Ministerial Conference on Feminist Foreign Policy: Building Peace and Democracy in Madrid. The conference brought together over 700 participants, including ministerial delegations from around 60 countries, representatives from international organizations, and 150 civil society organizations and feminist movements. At the closing, 28 countries signed the Madrid Joint Political Declaration, formally linking gender equality, human rights and democracy, and peace agendas. Notably, compared to the 2025 Paris conference (31 signatories), the number of signatory countries slightly decreased. Canada and Luxembourg, though having moved away from using the label "feminist foreign policy" domestically, still chose to sign the declaration.

Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD)

Releases Report on "Masculinity and Gender Equality"


In June 2026, the OECD released its report "Masculinity and Gender Equality," based on data from Côte d'Ivoire and Senegal, revealing how societal expectations of male roles impact progress toward gender equality. The survey found that over 90% of respondents in Côte d'Ivoire believe a man's primary role is to be the breadwinner, and over 80% agree that final decisions in the household should be made by men. These norms not only restrict women's economic autonomy and fuel gender-based violence but also cause over 90% of men to feel anxious due to breadwinning pressures. The report notes that people generally overestimate men's support for these norms, a misperception that further entrenches conservative behaviors. It recommends combining the promotion of equitable, non-violent models of masculinity with structural reforms such as expanded education, childcare, and social protection, using community-based channels tailored to local contexts.


Source:

OECD (2026), Masculinity and Gender Equality: Insights from Côte d'Ivoire and Senegal, Social Institutions and Gender Index, OECD Publishing, Paris, https://doi.org/10.1787/3973b760-en.

Canada's Anti-Hate Act Comes into Force

Strengthening Safety for Ethnic Minorities


On June 19, 2026, Canada's Combatting Hate Act (Bill C-9) received royal assent and will come into effect on July 18. The new law criminalizes acts of intimidation or obstruction preventing individuals from entering places of worship, schools, or community centers. It also introduces hate motivation as a standalone criminal offense and explicitly defines "hate" in the Criminal Code. The Act bans the public display of specific hate and terrorist symbols, including Nazi insignia and nooses. Justice Minister Sean Fraser stated: "Hate continues to spread in Canada, and communities have been calling for stronger protections—those protections are now law." The law explicitly does not restrict religious teachings, peaceful protest, or freedom of expression, targeting only deliberate acts of intimidation, obstruction, and incitement to hatred. Background data shows that hate crimes reported by police in Canada doubled between 2018 and 2024, with race and religion being the most common motivations.

Xenophobic Protests Escalate in South Africa

Chinese Businesses Warned to Suspend Operations


The South African Chinese Police-Community Cooperation Center issued an urgent safety alert urging overseas Chinese to suspend business operations on June 30 and July 1 and to strengthen security measures. Recently, South African anti-illegal immigration civic groups have set June 30 as a "final deadline" for all undocumented migrants to leave the country and plan nationwide protests. Provinces have seen ongoing demonstrations, some escalating into violent riots involving looting and arson, with Chinese-owned shops affected. The center warned that large-scale unrest is highly likely around June 30, and Chinese businesses located in Black residential areas, townships, and remote regions should close early and evacuate. Chinese malls should also consider shortening hours or suspending operations. The community is advised to avoid going out, reinforce shop security, and eliminate illegal employment practices.

Korean World Cup Fan Faces Racial Abuse

Mexican Man Involved Removed from Position and Apologizes


On June 11, 2026, following a World Cup group match between South Korea and the Czech Republic in Guadalajara, Mexico, a South Korean influencer posted a video on social media showing a Mexican man behind her making a "pulling the eye" racial gesture and laughing. The video quickly sparked public outrage, amassing over 65,000 comments and 120,000 shares. The individual, Ulises Bernal, later issued a public apology, stating he was "deeply sorry for everything he caused," and was removed from his position as president of the Jalisco State Engineering Association. Many Mexican netizens commented under his apology, condemning the act and emphasizing that it does not represent all Mexicans. The incident once again highlighted the risks of racial discrimination faced by Asian spectators at international sporting events.

Wang Hui Becomes First Asian Scholar to Receive Italy's Feltrinelli Prize


On June 8, 2026, the Accademia Nazionale dei Lincei (Italian National Academy of Sciences) announced the 2026 winners of the Feltrinelli Prize. Wang Hui, Distinguished Professor of Humanities at Tsinghua University and Director of the Institute for Advanced Humanistic Studies, was awarded the International Prize for Social Sciences (under the category of History and Moral Sciences), becoming the first Asian scholar to receive the honor since the prize's establishment. The Feltrinelli Prize was established in 1942 through a bequest by entrepreneur Antonio Feltrinelli and is among the highest honors in Italian science and culture. Its international awards cover five disciplines: History and Moral Sciences, Natural Sciences, Humanities, Arts, and Medicine. The jury described Wang Hui as "one of the most influential thinkers in the world today," recognizing his foundational work in interpreting the historical roots of Chinese modernity and his critical analysis of globalization. The jury also noted that Wang Hui's theoretical framework centered on the "inter-systemic society" has advanced international academic discussions on the roles of the state, market, and social movements in the 21st century, offering a key analytical perspective on the transformation of sovereignty in a globalized context.

Asian Political Voice in New York City:

From "What Is Asian?" to Who Counts as a True Asian Representative


During the 2026 AAPI Heritage Month in May, the issue of Asian American political representation once again became a focal point of discussion. New York State Senator John Liu recalled in an interview that after becoming New York City's first Asian American council member in 2002, he was once asked by a colleague to translate documents into "Asian language," a request reflecting the colleague's complete lack of awareness of the differences between Chinese, Korean, and Tagalog. More than two decades later, New York City's Asian population has surpassed one million, encompassing over 30 ethnic groups and more than 50 languages, with eight Asian American council members now serving in the City Council. In 2025, Zohran Mamdani was elected as New York's first South Asian mayor, with exit polls showing that Asian American voters gave him the highest support rate among all racial groups. However, Council Member Chi Ossé pointed out that South Asians and mixed-race Asian Americans are still often marginalized in the discourse on "Asian representation," and that Asian representation should not merely rest on ethnic identity but should focus on advancing substantive policies.
