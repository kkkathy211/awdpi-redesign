On April 24, 2024, amidst green willows, orioles singing, and a profusion of blooming flowers, the 2024 AWDPI Interview and Writing Skills Training was successfully held. The training was organized by AWDPI (Asian Women Development Plan International), an organization that advocates for attention to the living challenges faced by Asian women overseas and continuously supports Asian women in accessing opportunities for comprehensive development.


AWDPI invited Wang Yanping, Vice President of the Fifth Council of the Shaanxi Women Legal Workers Authors Association and senior social worker, and Tian Bo, international organization communication expert, to share insights and conduct training on brand building in international organizations and domestic violence research and practice. This training created a platform for internal volunteers to engage with professionals from international organizations and domestic violence practitioners, aiming to empower brands through communication and to advance solutions to domestic violence through research-informed interview and writing practices, helping Asian women build safety barriers.


1

Thematic Training: Branding and Interview-Writing


Thematic Training Speaker: Tian Bo


Tian Bo, an international organization communication expert and a seasoned practitioner with over fifteen years of experience in the public welfare sector, shared insights on two key topics: branding and interview-writing.


Teacher Tian Bo first elaborated on the brand connotation, brand composition, and the importance of brand tone, emphasizing that a brand is not only about external visual expression; what it conveys and embodies regarding the organization's strategy, values, and expression methods are the pillars and essence of the brand. Beyond the external expression of a brand, its essence permeates all aspects of a non-profit organization's work—recruitment, project implementation, fundraising, volunteer coordination, publicity writing, etc. Therefore, how a brand is built, with whom, and how it is done profoundly influence the shaping of its brand image. Secondly, Teacher Tian connected brand image with writing work, emphasizing that interview-writers must position themselves at the level of brand expression, integrating brand concepts into the smallest details of their writing.


Then, drawing on her fifteen years of professional experience, she offered interview-writers several valuable recommendations: first, become familiar in advance with global, national, and local policies related to the organization's work and understand the local cultural context; second, maintain neutrality and objectivity during the interview-writing process and ensure the protection of victims' information and privacy; third, pay attention to personnel allocation, information coordination, and collaborative communication in interview-writing work; fourth, maintain sensitivity and pay attention to risk management in both the interview-writing and dissemination processes.


Finally, from a branding perspective, Ms. Tian recommended that the organization further improve its brand system, draft and refine relevant documents, and place greater emphasis on staff training and division of labor.


2

Thematic Training: Domestic Violence Prevention and Writing with Critical Awareness


Thematic Training Speaker: Wang Yanping


Wang Yanping, Vice President of the Fifth Council of the Shaanxi Women Legal Workers Association and a senior social worker, drew on her practical and research experience in domestic violence social work to address several needs of the AWDPI interview-writing team, focusing on two main areas: "Who might the interviewees be—individuals, departments, or organizations—and how much do we know about them?" and "Conducting interview-writing from the perspective of theories and research findings related to domestic violence prevention."


First, Ms. Wang discussed direct service recipients in domestic violence work—battered women, perpetrators, and children who witness or experience domestic violence—elaborating on the definition and types of domestic violence, child protection, and perpetrator typologies. She provided interviewers with concrete analytical perspectives by examining the ecological environments, impacts of violence, and service needs of these three groups.


Second, focusing on "three-tier prevention and intervention in domestic violence," she used the article "How Can Domestic Violence Prevention Be Effective Through Collaboration?" as an example to illustrate collaborative anti-domestic violence measures involving direct service recipients, relevant government departments, social organizations, and the public. This inspired volunteers to integrate the process of identifying, analyzing, and solving problems throughout the entire interview-writing workflow. She emphasized that interview-writers must keep pace with the times, possess basic knowledge of relevant laws and policies, understand how various organizations conduct domestic violence prevention work, and continuously study solutions to domestic violence, using interview-writing to empower anti-domestic violence efforts.


Finally, Ms. Wang shared research findings on domestic violence prevention, calling on interview-writers to adopt an interdisciplinary theoretical perspective on domestic violence, widely engage with theories from sociology, law, and social work, use theory to guide their writing, reflect larger societal issues through small entry points, and advance domestic violence prevention through professional insight, helping Asian women build safety barriers.


This training was grounded in practice, directly addressed pressing issues, and offered targeted solutions, concluding successfully. Ms. Tian Bo and Ms. Wang Yanping offered volunteers fresh perspectives on brand building and new approaches to interview-writing. Their knowledge, experience, wisdom, and passion for women's rights protection have advanced AWDPI's organizational development and domestic violence prevention work. In the future, AWDPI will draw on this experience to improve its brand and internal organizational development, continuing to advance gender equality and women's rights protection. We believe that only by accumulating small steps, gathering stars across the sky, and building a nest of collaboration, can we ignite a torch.
