Safe Haven for the Soul: Support for Overseas Chinese Women Facing Domestic Violence



"When my eye socket was injured and stitched up with seven stitches, when two of my ribs were broken, I did think about calling the police. But every single time, after the violence, he would desperately apologize, swearing it would never happen again. I knew deep down these were just empty words, yet he would tell me to think about the children, think about the elders—this is what made me struggle each time over whether to report it, clinging to the faint hope that he might change, only to be abused again."

"I know he loves me. Aside from hitting me and drinking, he's never done anything to betray me. Once, I really picked up my phone to seek help, but then I thought about all the emotions I'd invested over the years, and I felt resentment and shame. I even began to think there was something wrong with me, that I must have done something wrong—otherwise, why was I the only one suffering like this?"

"I've brought up divorce, but he refuses. Every time I mention it, he starts threatening me. Over time, divorce began to feel like an even worse option. I don't even know myself how much longer this dark, hopeless life will continue."



Data released by the World Health Organization in 2018 confirmed that globally, one in three women has experienced physical or sexual violence from an intimate partner or non-partner. As high as 38% of female homicides are committed by male intimate partners. Yet only less than 40%of women have ever sought help, and fewer than 10% choose to report it to the police. In 2018, approximately 100 Chinese women sought assistance from the Chinese Information and Advice Centre, a charity in London's Chinatown, asking how to leave husbands who abused them. According to data cited by The Beijing News in 2018 from the All-China Women's Federation, behind the doors of China's 430 million households, a woman is beaten by her husband every 7.4 seconds. Yet on average, victims endure 35 incidents of domestic violence before choosing to report it.

 

Domestic violence is no longer an isolated case, and addressing it should not fall solely on the victims.



The most popular public-voted entry in Sichuan Province's Top Ten Defense Statements was the defense statement written by two female lawyers. Thanks to their defense, a case in which the defendant killed her husband after enduring long-term domestic abuse was ultimately reclassified as "excessive self-defense." An increasing number of women are using the weapon of law to protect their legal rights and personal safety. At the same time, national legal and judicial systems are continuously improving relevant laws and judicial practices to safeguard women's rights.

The "Avoice Overseas Chinese Women Protection Project" is dedicated to helping overseas Chinese women suffering from domestic violence and improving the living conditions of vulnerable groups of women overseas. It builds a bridge between women in need and various resources, becoming their solid support and warm shelter..

We emphasize self-empowerment among our target group of women, while also providing multi-dimensional support, including language, psychological, legal, medical, shelter, channels, education, economic assistance, storytelling and amplifying voices, and community support—expanding the breadth of aid while creating a virtuous cycle of mutual support.
