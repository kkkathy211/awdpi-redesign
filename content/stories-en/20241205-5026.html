Since Hamas announced the launch of its "Al-Aqsa Flood" operation on October 7, 2023, Israeli military operations in the Gaza Strip have caused, as of November 27, 2024, 44249 Palestinian deaths, 104746 injuries.


According to statistics from the Palestinian Ministry of Health, approximately 70% of the deceased are women and children.. United Nations Security Council Resolution 1325 states that women and children are increasingly becoming targets in war, and protecting their rights is crucial to international peace and security.. However, the Israeli military's operations in Palestine, especially in the Gaza Strip, clearly deviate from the principles of "human rights" and "humanitarianism" advocated by Resolution 1325.





One

Women and children severely affected: accounting for 70% of deaths



According to United Nations data, by January 2024, more than 24,620 Palestinians had been killed in the Gaza Strip, among whom 70% were women and children.. Over 1.9 million people (85% of Gaza's total population) have been displaced, including about one million women and girls. The brutality of the conflict is shocking: every hour, two mothers lose their lives in the war, and at least 3,000 women may have become new widows or primary caregivers for their families. In addition, at least 10,000 children may have lost their fathers. As early as January 2023, estimates indicated that about 2.1 million people in the occupied Palestinian territory required some form of humanitarian assistance, among whom 49.2%were women.



From 2008 to October 7, 2023, hostilities resulted in 6,542 Palestinian and 308 Israeli deaths, with women and girls accounting for less than 14%. However, from October 7, 2023, to January 2024, Palestinian deaths have already reached three times the total of the past 15 years, and the demographic composition has significantly changed: today, 70% of Palestinians killed in Gaza are women and children.



Casualties are almost evenly distributed between 13% in northern Gaza and 28% in central Gaza, further highlighting that no area in the Gaza Strip is safe. The bloodshed and trauma brought by war are everywhere, and no one is spared.




Two

The crisis of war: physical, psychological, and educational


01

Physical crisis: high incidence of disease and resource shortages



As of September 2024, UN Women estimates that 177,000 Palestinian women face life-threatening risks, primarily from non-communicable diseases, hunger, and malnutrition during pregnancy. Among them, over 162,000 women suffer from non-communicable diseases such as hypertension, diabetes, cardiovascular disease, and cancer, 30,841 are at risk of diabetes, 107,443 are at risk of hypertension, 5,201 are at risk of cancer. More than 5,000 female cancer patients urgently need treatment; interruption of basic care could lead to complications or even death.


The suffering of cancer patients has multiplied during the war, as Gaza's only cancer treatment facility has ceased operations, and patients face severe shortages of radiotherapy and systemic treatments, as well as inadequate nutrition. Between March and April 2024, among seven female cancer patients interviewed by UN Women, none could access life-saving treatment or medication. .


Childbirth further increases women's health risks.. In October 2023, among approximately 50,000 pregnant women, an average of 183 gave birth daily, with 130 premature infants relying on electric incubators to survive. In February 2024, midwives delivered over 70 babies daily under extreme conditions, and some newborns died due to lack of postnatal care. About 155,000 pregnant and lactating women face severe barriers to prenatal and postnatal care, with 69% experiencing complications such as urinary tract infections, anemia, and preterm birth. The proportion of women reporting emergency medical conditions is higher than that of men (42% vs. 39%), yet they face greater difficulty affording healthcare.


In addition, since January 2024, the rate of acute malnutrition among children has doubled, with about one-third of children under two suffering from malnutrition. By April 1, 2024, hospitals in northern Gaza reported that 28 children had died from malnutrition and dehydration, 90%and over 50% of children under five have contracted at least one infectious disease.


Due to overcrowded shelters and inadequate water and sanitation facilities, hundreds of thousands of cases of acute respiratory infections, jaundice, diarrhea, and rashes have surged. Women and girls face higher infection risks while caring for the sick; women account for twice as many skin infection cases as men and two-thirds of gastrointestinal disease and hepatitis A cases. Moreover, women with disabilities account for approximately 45%of the total number of disabled people in Gaza. However, most shelters, as well as water, sanitation, and hygiene facilities, are not accessible to people with disabilities. Shelters often lack proper mattresses and beds, which may lead to untreatable complications in unsanitized environments.



Meanwhile, medical facilities in Gaza have been severely damaged, preventing women and children from receiving timely treatment. In October 2023, 64% of UNRWA and Ministry of Health primary healthcare clinics were non-operational, and 34% of hospitals could not function normally. Seven major hospitals were operating beyond capacity, with bed occupancy reaching 112%, and Shifa Hospital reaching 147%. Additionally, 55% of health cluster partners had stopped or restricted operations due to security concerns and infrastructure damage. By April 30, 2024, only 12 out of 32 hospitals were partially operational, and hospital beds had decreased by 80%. At Kamal Adwan Hospital in northern Gaza, 15 infants are hospitalized daily due to acute malnutrition and dehydration.


Due to forced displacement, many families have been broken apart, and resource scarcity has worsened, forcing women and girls to seek shelter with other families for survival, often leaving them last in line to access food and other resources. By age group, most reported cases involve women aged 18 to 59, followed by girls aged 12 to 17.



02

Psychological crisis: 75% of women feel depressed



UN Women surveyed 600 respondents in Gaza, representing 1,520 children across their households, with an average of 2 to 3 children per household, nearly 40% of whom are under five years old. Seventy-seven percent of mothers are responsible for feeding, cleaning, and caring for their children, and 57% are responsible for their education. Respondents stated that they face immense pressure in protecting their children's physical and mental health, while often lacking safety, security, and basic needs themselves. Approximately 75% of women said they frequently feel depressed, 62% often suffer from insomnia, and 65% feel anxious and experience nightmares.


In addition, women and girls account for about 45% of the disabled population in Gaza. They are often the primary caregivers for disabled family members, a role that increases their stress, anxiety, and feelings of guilt, especially in the face of war-related disabilities or injuries. When women and girls themselves have needs, their ability to care for others is limited, making their need for psychosocial support even more urgent.


03

Educational crisis: over 95% of schools destroyed


Moreover, the Gaza Strip is also facing a severe educational crisis. Data shows that by October 21, 2023, a total of 205 educational facilities in Gaza had been affected, including at least 29 UNRWA schools. By April 23, 2024, 412 schools and universities had been destroyed or partially damaged. In addition, civil infrastructure has also suffered severe damage, including Gaza's central archives. On November 8, UNICEF stated that at least 64 attacks on schools occurred in Gaza in October, averaging about two per day. Since the outbreak of the latest large-scale Israel-Palestine conflict last October, over 95% of schools in the Gaza Strip have been partially or completely destroyed by the Israeli military.



Three

Is international aid sufficient? The needs of women and children urgently require attention



According to international emergency humanitarian standards, the minimum daily water requirement per person is 15 liters, but by mid-December 2023, the average daily water supply in Gaza had dropped to 1.5 liters—only one-tenth of the minimum standard. Pregnant and lactating women require even more—7.5 liters per day to maintain health, five times the current supply. Dehydration not only affects breast milk supply but also exacerbates maternal fatigue and lack of energy.


A report by the United Nations Population Fund (UNFPA) shows that approximately 540,000 women of reproductive age live in Gaza; within 47 days of the war's outbreak, over 7,000 women had given birth, and an estimated 5,500 births are expected by March 2024. Among 175 surveyed households with lactating mothers, 55% reported being unable to exclusively breastfeed or needing formula supplementation. Additionally, 337,057 children under five in Gaza are vulnerable to waterborne diseases, meaning approximately 1.1 million women and girls urgently need adequate and safe water.

Furthermore, more than 690,000 women and girls face inadequate privacy in menstrual hygiene management. An estimated 10 million disposable sanitary pads or 4 million reusable menstrual cloths are needed monthly, yet aid distribution has failed to meet demand. Overcrowded shelters lack basic sanitation facilities, increasing women's risks of reproductive and urinary tract infections. In addition, women face safety risks when seeking toilets and bathing areas, and protection threats intensify as they strive for access to water, privacy, and dignity.


The lack of shelter, water, and basic supplies poses severe challenges for 540,000 women and girls of reproductive age. Before the escalation of tensions, Gaza already faced a 48% shortage of essential medical supplies, including 20 items critical for obstetric and pediatric services. Although some aid organizations have set up camps and imported resources, funding shortages, bureaucratic obstacles, and Israel's rejection of shelter supplies have left resources insufficient. UN Women analysis shows that in the 2023 Flash Appeal funding, only 0.09% reached local women's organizations, and many organizations have ceased operations due to lack of funding. Surveyed organizations stated they urgently need more funding, partner support, and safe investment.



Four

The dilemma of media coverage: bias and avoidance of attribution


Kareem found that Western mainstream media outlets such as The New York Times exhibit bias in their reporting, These media outlets report Israel's "relentless bombing" of Palestine as a "conflict," describing Israelis as "killed" while Palestinians are portrayed as "dying." Furthermore, these media frequently explicitly or implicitly depict Palestinians as combatants, suicide bombers, Islamic radicals, kidnappers, and terrorists—creating a stark contrast with the portrayal of Israelis.

Another study on Bild.de, Germany's most-read website, regarding its coverage of the Israeli-Palestinian conflict shows that when reporting on the plight of children in the Gaza Strip, adjectives such as "innocent" are often used to describe Palestinian children, yet there is a complete absence of emotive words like "pain," "hunger," or "suffering" that would reveal the children's own experience of hardship. In addition, the website lacks sufficient reporting on the killing of thousands of Palestinian civilians—including thousands of children—by Israeli forces.



FAILLA analyzed coverage of the Israeli-Palestinian conflict from The Guardian, The Times, The New York Times, The Wall Street Journal, and Al Jazeera between 2023 and 2024. In terms of volume, the five media outlets showed a declining trend in reporting on the conflict. Regarding reporting bias, The Wall Street Journal and The Times, traditional Western newspapers, tend to use terms such as "occupation" and "seizure" to describe Israel's military actions, whereas Al Jazeera tends to use words like "massacre" and "attack." In reporting on the March 1 massacre, The Times wrote that most Palestinians died in a stampede, thereby evading identification of the true perpetrator of the deaths. Moreover, although Western media have reported on the famine, malnutrition, and lack of medical equipment in Gaza, they remain silent on the causes of these resource shortages, thus reducing Israel's responsibility.





Béchara conducted a causal inference-based comparison of BBC and Al Jazeera English's coverage of the Russia-Ukraine conflict and the Israeli-Palestinian conflict since October 7, 2023. Among 784 BBC headlines on the Russia-Ukraine conflict, approximately 236 (30%) contained causal language, while only 225 (28%) of the headlines on the Israeli-Palestinian conflict did so. The BBC uniquely avoids naming perpetrators in headlines—for example, phrases like "killed in recent attacks" or "dozens killed in a refugee camp" fail to clearly identify Israel as the cause. Only 1% of sentences describing death and destruction directly attribute causality to Israel. This tendency reflects an implicit bias produced through omission, thereby absolving one party of direct responsibility.

In contrast, Al Jazeera's headlines are more straightforward in attributing causality. Statements such as "Israeli tanks in Rafah city, new deadly strikes target Gaza civilians" and "Israel has launched two attacks since Sunday, killing dozens in horrific conditions" explicitly identify Israel as the agent of action. In 57% of headlines involving death and destruction, Israel is directly named as the cause. This direct attribution of responsibility aligns with Al Jazeera's editorial stance, which is often perceived as more sympathetic to the Palestinian perspective.



Meanwhile, biased media coverage and avoidance of attribution directly influence global public attitudes toward Palestinians and Israelis. El-Ghawi analyzed the textual content of 13,500 Facebook posts related to the Israeli-Palestinian conflict and found that most word pairings carried emotional connotations that shape readers' perceptions of certain entities—for example, using "terrorist" to refer to Palestinians or "occupier" to refer to Israelis. By labeling the emotional tone of the posts, the study found the most frequent label was "biased against Palestine," accounting for 34% of 1,800 annotations, followed by "fair" (27%), "biased against Israel" (16%), "unclear" (8%), "biased against both Palestine and Israel" (6%), "biased against others" (6%), and "not applicable" (3%).



In this endless conflict, innocent Palestinian women and children have become the most vulnerable victims. International humanitarian law clearly stipulates, "Under all circumstances, parties to a conflict must respect the dignity and lives of civilians" (Geneva Conventions). Yet this principle remains far from realized in Gaza. Women and children suffer not only fatal physical violence but also carry deep psychological trauma; their education and living environments have been completely destroyed, and their futures are cast under a heavy shadow. Despite efforts by international relief forces to deliver aid, life-saving assistance consistently fails to arrive in time due to insufficient resources and restricted access. More heartbreakingly, Western media coverage of the conflict lacks fairness, overlooks the profound suffering of the Palestinian people, and blames the victims rather than the aggressors who escalate violence and conflict.


As stated in the Universal Declaration of Human Rights: "Everyone has the right to life, liberty, and security of person." The basic rights of Palestinian women and children—rights to life, health, and education—are being severely violated in this war! The tragedy of war must be attributed to those who choose conflict over dialogue and violence over peace. The plight of Palestinian women and children is a painful cry exposing the global community's failure in its duty. In this world filled with violence and conflict, the international community has a responsibility to protect civilians and advance peace, and each of our voices is a defense of innocent lives.
