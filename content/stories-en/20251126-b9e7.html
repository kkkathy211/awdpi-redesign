Saturday, November 29, 2025, AWDPI Australia (Asian Women Development Plan International – Australia National Office) will hold its launch ceremony in Melbourne, simultaneously releasing the anti-domestic violence support handbook *You Are Not Alone*, authored by the AWDPI Australia legal support team, and hosting a cross-sector roundtable forum on domestic violence prevention.


This event will bring together representatives from the Australian federal government, the Victorian state government, municipal institutions, multicultural community leaders, legal and mental health experts, Victoria Police, and cross-cultural advocates, to jointly explore how to better protect, support, and empower Asian women in Australia.


This is not only a launch ceremony, but also a cross-cultural public dialogue centered on "women's safety and empowerment," involving diverse sectors and stakeholders.


To help the public better understand the full arrangements of this event, AWDPI Australia now officially releases the final run sheet and the speakers lineup poster for the launch ceremony.


We sincerely invite you to be part of this significant moment!

ANTI-VIOLENCE ROUNDTABLE DISCUSSION


"Roundtable Panelist Dr Anne Pang"

  • Founder and Instructor, Anne Pang Self-Defence System

  • Expert Advisor, Asian Women Development Plan International (AWDPI) Australia


Since founding her personal protection system in 1990, Anne Pang has dedicated over thirty years to teaching self-defence courses for women, consistently empowering women and enhancing safety awareness. She has taught in numerous secondary schools and universities, impacting thousands of women, and has won multiple martial arts championships, including the Australasian Kung Fu Championships. In recognition of her outstanding contributions, she became the only female self-defence instructor inducted into the *Blitz Magazine* Martial Arts Hall of Fame in 1988, and in 2023, the only woman to receive the Martial Arts Australia Masters Award – Kung Fu. Anne completed her PhD in Asian Studies at Monash University in 2009; her doctoral thesis was published in both Chinese and English and is held in the libraries of 16 leading universities worldwide. As the first person to emigrate from Taiwan to Australia in 1970, she has remained deeply committed to assisting new migrants and actively contributing to both Chinese and broader Australian communities, advocating for gender and ethnic equality. For example, she participated in the AFL Multicultural Focus Group, serving as a cultural bridge between Australia and China. Her work integrates education, martial arts, research, and cross-cultural advocacy, continuously contributing to the creation of safer and more equitable communities.


"Roundtable Panelist Dr Sabi Kaphle"

  • Senior Lecturer in Public Health, University of Queensland, Australia

  • Founding Chair and Executive Committee Member, Multicultural Women's Alliance Against Family Violence


With over two decades of experience in international health and community development, Sabi Kaphle is a multidisciplinary professional bridging practice, research, and academia. She specialises in implementing culturally safe, effective, and sustainable health and social interventions in resource-limited and vulnerable communities. She has long collaborated with multicultural populations and diverse stakeholders, engaging across multiple levels—from project planning and service delivery to policy development—working with organisations ranging from the United Nations to grassroots community groups. Dr Kaphle has published extensively on gender equality, health, and cultural safety, and in 2022 published her book *Sociocultural Insights into South Asian Childbirth* with Routledge. Her recently edited volume, *Communities of Care in Migration*, presents stories from migrant women across different countries. Dr Kaphle has served as a director for several organisations, including Women's Health In the North and CALD Communities Voices. As Founding Chair and current Executive Committee Member of the Multicultural Women's Alliance Against Family Violence, she continues to advocate for safer and more supportive environments for multicultural women.

Building a Safer Future for Asian Women


Asian women play an irreplaceable role in Australia's communities, economy, and culture. Yet when facing family violence or seeking help, they often encounter multiple barriers—including language, culture, visa status, and community pressure—making the path to support more complex and difficult.

The establishment of AWDPI Australia aims to bridge these invisible gaps, making support more accessible, information more transparent, amplifying more voices, and ensuring that every woman can feel, at critical moments: Safe, empowered, and with choices.


You are welcome to become part of this collective effort.

Together, let's drive change—starting today.
