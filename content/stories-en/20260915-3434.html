Twin-City Experiment

THE SISTER-CITIES EXPERIMENT


This past August, in Shanghai, over twenty young creators spent one weekend using generative AI to transform the invisible, personal cities in their minds into more than twenty short films, each 1–3 minutes long. From memories of southern summers to a cat lost in a lane, to an "urban gap replica" for the ordinary—these films have already been publicly screened in Shanghai, stepping from "invisible" into "visible."


Now, it's your turn to complete the other half of this twin-city experiment.


The "Invisible Cities" Shanghai–Boston Twin-City Youth Film Project: Boston Chapter, hosted at the Boston Public Library, is now officially open for registration.


In Calvino's tale, Marco Polo describes fifty-five imagined cities to Kublai Khan, yet never mentions Venice—because each city is a variation of Venice. We invite you to do the same: through an imagined other city, reflect back upon your own lived experience, and re-examine the city you've lived in or are living in now—whether it's a morning by the Charles River or a street deep in your memory that belongs only to you.


Two Creative Pathways

TWO CREATIVE PATHS


This workshop offers two creative pathways—


First, grounded in memory. Begin with a specific sensory moment: a scent, a sound, a beam of light.


Second, making the invisible visible. Use generative AI to transform imagery from dreams, the subconscious, or fantasies—things that have never truly existed—into visual form.
