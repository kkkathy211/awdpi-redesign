Summary

On July 13, 2025, the first event of the AWDPI Women's Empowerment Lecture Series—" Empowering Her Power on the Frontlines of Education, Soft Power, and Entrepreneurship"—was successfully held online. This lecture was organized by AWDPI (Asian Women Development Plan International), a nonprofit dedicated to eliminating discrimination and gender-based violence against overseas Asian women and advancing their empowerment and development. The inaugural session invited three female experts from different fields to discuss issues including workplace voice, sex education for working mothers, and financing challenges for women entrepreneurs. workplace voice, sex education for working mothers, and challenges in female entrepreneurship financing and other issues.


Speaker 1: Xie Qiqi (Spencer)

Lecture Topic

"Women's development cannot be just talk—

the necessity of soft power in women's advancement"



"Women should not limit themselves in skill acquisition; we must grow strength in places others cannot see."

Ms. Xie previously worked at the United Nations Development Programme, the Food Summit, and the Environment Programme, and currently serves as Country Lead at a global financial institution ranked among the top 30 worldwide. She has participated in cross-departmental collaborations related to UN Women and SDG investment and financing projects, with a particular focus on women's rights and gender balance. She advocates that women should proactively develop soft power and hard skills to secure a stable position in the workplace.


She emphasized and explained what she means by women "needing skills more": this "more" is not in comparison to men, but in contrast to the prevailing academic credentialism commonly found in mainstream female development paths. While academic qualifications are undoubtedly important as a gateway into the workforce, it is hard skills that truly determine whether women can "stay" and "climb up" in their careers.. Currently, women are primarily concentrated in administrative, human resources, and new media roles—"functional positions"—while their representation in technical and managerial roles remains low. She suggests that women can use their spare time to study fields dominated by men, such as programming, engineering, urban planning, and asset management, as directions for career breakthroughs. Additionally, learning practical life skills (such as escape techniques, anti-surveillance methods) and cultivating interests labeled as "masculine" (such as football, racing, wine tasting) in daily life can also enhance women's voice in the workplace.


Ms. Xie emphasized that women should avoid falling into the trap of "pseudo self-strengthening" when seeking empowerment.: They should not rely solely on academic credentials to demonstrate their worth, nor should they take pride in being different from other women. Learning skills labeled as "masculine" is not about pleasing men or standing out, but about truly gaining strength and the right to autonomous choice. She encourages women with energy and willingness to actively explore unfamiliar fields, especially those closely tied to male-dominated power structures, thereby building stronger professional barriers and a foundation of confidence for the future.


During the audience Q&A session, Ms. Xie shared specific insights based on her own experience around the question, "Which skills are more useful than academic credentials?" She believes that the value of skills lies in their practical application in projects and career development, not in whether they "seem important," nor should they be limited by traditional academic credential thinking. She also emphasized the importance of curiosity: broad curiosity is not only a motivation for learning but also the foundation for discovering industry structures, social mobility, and project opportunities. For example, by observing social trends and population movements, she developed sensitivity toward issues such as urban planning, public communication, and community operations. Skills may not be immediately "useful," but as long as one continuously learns and dares to express, they will eventually become advantages at different stages of life. At the end of her talk, Ms. Xie opened her personal contact information, welcoming further communication and questions. She said: "Everyone has knowledge and experiences I haven't yet mastered, so everyone is my teacher."



Speaker 2: Deng Fangting

Lecture Topic

"How Working Mothers Can Provide Sex Education for Their Children"



"Sex education is not just about knowledge transfer; it's about building relationships and trust."


Ms. Deng is an advocate for "empowerment-based sex education" and has been involved in the field since 2013. She served as a core trainer for the United Nations CGF's first sex education program and has authored several books on sex education. As a working woman and mother, she experienced a brief period as a full-time mother before returning to the workforce. She emphasizes discussing sex education practices and gender equality from the perspective of a "working mother."


Sex education is an essential part of everyone's growth, yet many parents struggle to initiate this topic due to their own lack of such education.. She highlighted the challenges working mothers face in sex education, including conflicts among multiple roles, time management pressure, and insufficient social support, and suggested naturally integrating sex education into parent-child interactions through picture books and family news. She explained that the goal of sex education is to help children build self-awareness and the ability to respect others., Gender education should begin in childhood, helping children understand physiological differences (such as menstruation and physical differences) and psychological development. Parents need to educate not only daughters but also sons about sex education, helping them understand and respect women.


Sex education is especially important for cultivating values of gender equality.. Ms. Deng suggested starting with details in daily life—such as gender stereotypes in advertisements or gender ratios in different professions—and encouraging children to reflect on existing gender stereotypes. At the same time, she promotes shared household chores, decision-making, and dialogue among family members to enhance gender awareness at home. Additionally, co-reading children's sex education picture books can emphasize boundaries and the concept of respect between genders. When children ask questions, parents should respond honestly and openly, learning and growing together with their children. She not only provided theories and methods but also emphasized from a working mother's perspective that sex education is crucial for helping children build a sense of security, judgment, and self-worth.. Her sharing was both practical and emotionally resonant, helping both mothers and fathers learn how to enhance children's gender awareness.


During the audience interaction session, Ms. Deng responded to the question, "Are there sex education picture books suitable for adults?" She pointed out that picture books are not limited to children's reading. Sex education picture books essentially transform abstract general knowledge into intimate, conversational tools for parent-child reading through illustrated formats. For example, picture books such as "The Story of the Little Penis" and "The Story of Breasts" are suitable for parents to read together with their children. Many of these books are originally designed for children, but adults can also engage in "relearning" by reading them, gaining different levels of understanding and emotional connection. Teacher Deng encouraged the audience not to subconsciously equate picture books with early childhood education, but to treat them as open tools for sex education that support cross-generational communication and understanding. Ms. Deng also shared in the audience group two sex education books she contributed to, encouraging everyone to read, share, and spread them to further understand the key content and practical pathways of sex education.


Speaker 3: Yang Wenxue

Lecture Topic

"Challenges Faced by Women Entrepreneurs in the Financing Environment"



"Women must unconditionally believe in their abilities during the entrepreneurial process."


As a woman actively involved in the investment and financing sector, Ms. Yang specializes in capital connection for technology, manufacturing, and socially impactful enterprises. Drawing on global data and personal experience, she clearly identified the structural challenges women entrepreneurs face in the current funding ecosystem, along with strategies to address them.


Ms. Yang pointed out that in the first three quarters of 2024, all-female founding teams received only 1.6% of venture capital, highlighting the gender gap in the financing environment. She stated that women entrepreneurs face three systemic challenges: first, investment bias and stereotypes. Women often find it harder to gain opportunities to speak and showcase themselves; even when their capabilities are equal to or stronger than those of male entrepreneurs, they must exert extra effort to gain recognition. Second, social bias and diminished confidence: women are often perceived as lacking leadership or experience, which affects their confidence and the sustainability of their ventures. Third, lack of support networks. Due to the absence of female support networks, women often find themselves isolated when facing entrepreneurial bottlenecks.


Ms. Yang shared her own entrepreneurial experience and recommended that women entrepreneurs should build mutual support communities and use new financing channels such as crowdfunding platforms to bravely claim resources and rights.. After entering the industry, building a support network is crucial. Creating strong knowledge and social networks is key to overcoming isolation and increasing chances of success. She called on women entrepreneurs to stay confident, never doubt their abilities, and actively claim the resources and space that rightfully belong to them.. Women entrepreneurs should learn to speak up for themselves and fight for their right to expression, especially in unfavorable environments.


During the audience Q&A session, Ms. Yang answered two questions about common challenges women face in the workplace and entrepreneurship, particularly how to handle the intersectional challenges of gender discrimination and being an Asian woman.. She pointed out that one should first assess the specific context before responding., But the core attitude is: be brave to speak up for yourself and firmly defend your rights. When facing unfair treatment, maintaining an objective stance and clearly expressing your views is crucial.. She emphasized: "If you are right, you should have the courage to say 'no'." In situations where immediate rebuttal or explanation is not possible, the most powerful response is demonstrating your capabilities through the actual outcomes and progress of your projects.. This strategy of "responding to bias with results" is both firm and pragmatic. Women encountering skepticism in the workplace or in entrepreneurship is the norm rather than the exception; the key lies in inner conviction and a clear sense of purpose—don't let others' voices alter your direction. "Focus on your own path of development, and don't allow yourself to be defined by those who don't understand you." Ms. Yang introduced the mutual support group she initiated for women entrepreneurs, and sincerely invited all women entrepreneurs present to join. She hopes that through this platform, like-minded partners can come together, support one another, share resources, and collectively build a warm and powerful community for women entrepreneurs.


Summary



This lecture centered on the theme of "women's growth and gender equality in the workplace." The three speakers addressed the topic from different angles—workplace capacity building, sex education and motherhood balance, and gender bias in entrepreneurship along with pathways to breakthrough—forming a comprehensive discussion chain from individual empowerment to social change.


From the workplace to the family, from funding challenges to gender education, from building soft skills to claiming public space, we have heard their stories and also seen our own situations and possibilities reflected.


This conference reminds us that women's growth is not an isolated individual struggle, but a complex process involving collective social construction, discursive negotiation, and practical negotiation. Every woman striving to advance in her career, every mother teaching her children about gender, every entrepreneur persistently raising her voice on the funding journey—each is a force driving gender equality from theory into reality.


Empowerment does not end with one lecture, nor is it confined to a single moment.
