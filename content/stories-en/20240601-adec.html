Protecting women's rights is not an achievement of a single day, nor can it be accomplished by one person alone. At AWDPI, there are countless down-to-earth volunteers who start with small, incremental actions—some leveraging their expertise, others gradually exploring unfamiliar fields—all striving to make contributions and bring change to women near and far.


"Having experienced the rain, I want to hold an umbrella for others" is the motivation behind many volunteers' involvement in women's protection work. While empathy allows us to feel others' pain through shared stories, we don't need to have identical experiences to walk together; yet hearing each other's cries fosters deep mutual understanding. Many volunteers, though not personally trapped in hardship, are moved by distant cries and long to bring even a glimmer of light to those in darkness. Let us get closer to these humble yet great volunteers, learn their stories of women's protection, and see the deeply touching faces behind each umbrella.

Human Resources Department – Zhou Wenxin

Majoring in finance, Zhou Wenxin has always been passionate about participating in public welfare. She has left her busy footprint in various charitable activities, including teaching in rural areas, social work, and community service.


In August 2022, after joining the Employee Relations team, Zhou Wenxin mainly handled member onboarding, offboarding, and transfers. Later, she moved to the Performance Management team, where she became team leader, responsible for volunteer-related incentive work such as timekeeping, issuing certificates, and nominating outstanding volunteers. She also participated in Project Thriven volunteer incentive projects. Her greatest gain has been experience in management roles. From employee relations to performance management, she gradually became proficient with the Feishu system, mastering procedures and automated forms, and eventually evolved from a training participant into a training instructor.


Zhou Wenxin believes the significance of women's organizations does not lie in accomplishing sensational feats, but in making women visible and valued—which in itself is profoundly meaningful. Regarding the future of women's protection organizations and women's rights, she holds her own unique insights and hopes. "As society increasingly values women and awareness of gender equality rises, we will eventually approach true feminism. Currently, some people hold biases against feminism, and even resent gender-related issues, causing some women to feel ashamed to identify as feminists." Zhou Wenxin hopes that in the future, more people will access professional popular science education and realize that feminism is not an extreme trend, but genuine equality.



Belgium National Office – Weiwei Miao (Miao Weiwei)

2007 After graduating from university, Weiwei worked as a teacher and later in marketing at a design institute, 2016 and began living in Europe in [year]. Weiwei had female elders who suffered domestic violence, and the older generation's response to such abuse shocked and disappointed her, sparking her desire to join anti-domestic violence organizations. Once, seeing a volunteer recruitment post from AWDPI on her social media feed, Weiwei submitted her application and joined AWDPI.


Weiwei is a member of the Communications Department and the Belgium Project Collaboration Team. She has organized a series of activities including social integration classes, flyer campaigns, offline meetups, and online lectures. In her volunteer work, she has faced many challenging moments, and completing each task has given her a clearer vision for the future.


In Weiwei's view, volunteers are the sparks that can ignite a prairie fire for women's protection organizations. " The development of women's protection organizations and the safeguarding of women's rights is a long and arduous journey—this is her vision for the future. " She firmly believes that by passing on her own energy, she will receive even greater energy in return.



Communications Department – Kylan Wang Zi

Kylan graduated from a domestic art academy. Working in brand design, his daily job closely aligns with the work of AWDPI's Communications Department, allowing him to thrive in his volunteer role. 


Kylan is a thoughtful and passionate designer. However, after graduation, he noticed that most designers tend to pursue commercially valuable, high-paying projects, while socially meaningful design initiatives are often neglected. Kylan, instead, seeks the social value and impact that design can generate.


At AWDPI, Kylan serves as a graphic designer. He has been responsible for designing WeChat public account header animations, footers, and the 2023 New Year red envelopes. He also contributed to the main visuals and peripheral designs for the 2023 Global Women's Creative Summit, the main visual for the 2023 1125 campaign, the 2024 New Year poster, and the main visual for the 2024 Women's Day event. The design that left the deepest impression on him was the 2023 1125 campaign's main visual—the first time he used AI to create a campaign's main image. Kylan realized that the emergence and application of AI greatly empower NGOs and other social organizations, increasing the potential for effective visual communication. Kylan remembers spending three days learning and experimenting in MidJourney before the design began to take shape. In his regular design work, he rarely has such high freedom for experimentation and exploration, which excited him. Being able to combine image generation with social value is, to him, a wonderful thing.



Communications Department – Sun Yuxin

Yuxin is a content marketing planner who recently started her career. During university, she came across Avoice-related information on her social media feed. Eager to engage in volunteer work and care for vulnerable groups, she joined Avoice—now AWDPI—and has been with the organization for four years. Witnessing Avoice's transformation into AWDPI, she has shared the mission with fellow volunteers to build a world of love and peace for women.


Yuxin serves as the team leader in AWDPI's Communications Department design team, responsible with her team for creating visual materials for outreach campaigns. Everyone is always enthusiastic, and the materials they produce convey strong calls to action and a sense of women's power, giving Yuxin a deep sense of accomplishment. Yet there are challenges in practice, such as balancing time between her job and volunteer activities. However, when motivation and desire are strong enough, solutions can always be found.


"Our volunteers are all highly responsible and mission-driven. Being able to provide a safe space and tangible help to women harmed by intimate relationships is truly remarkable." Being part of such an organization has given Yuxin many touching moments, and even more opportunities for intellectual exploration and reflection. "I reflect on how I may have previously only imagined feminism in theory, so now I often ask myself: Do I truly care about this issue? Only when we truly care can we solve it. Never forget that we share a common vision."



Communications Department – Du Wanting

Wanting is a third-year student at the School of Journalism and Communication, Central China Normal University, who joined AWDPI in spring 2023. She often comes across content on social media about emotional conflicts or domestic violence. In the comment sections of legal bloggers who specialize in resolving emotional disputes, she frequently sees numerous graphic and bloody images of women suffering domestic violence—images that expose the physical and psychological scars and wounds of abused women to the public eye. These vivid images made Wanting acutely aware of the immense harm domestic violence inflicts on women, and she deeply empathizes with their powerlessness and helplessness. Because she did not want to see such incidents harm more and more women, this girl who loves painting and design joined the public account team of AWDPI's Communications Department without hesitation, hoping to use her professional skills and hobbies to contribute her own strength to women's protection.


Wanting is a member of the Communications Department's public account team. Passionate about drawing and design, she enjoys exploring fun features in Canva and Xiumi, and takes pleasure in carefully considering how to layout posts for maximum visual appeal. Every time she sees her designed cover used in AWDPI's posts, Wanting feels she has genuinely contributed to the cause of women's protection, bringing her great satisfaction. Beyond her role, Wanting has gained much: through regular organizational updates, she has learned a wealth of information and developments related to women's protection. Before each layout task, she reads numerous posts on women's protection for reference, thus acquiring previously unknown legal knowledge and gaining insight into global women's protection efforts, greatly deepening her understanding of the field.


"I hope to push the global women's protection cause to a higher level and raise greater awareness and attention;

I hope to help women worldwide who have suffered domestic violence or unfair treatment escape their suffering, regain new life and freedom, and find their own light;

I hope feminism will no longer be just a labeled slogan;;

I hope women's rights protection worldwide can be truly implemented."


Wanting believes that when every small contribution comes together, they generate tremendous power. She remains steadfast in her passion, true to her choices, continuously breaking through, growing, and giving.



Communications Department – You Ge

You Ge is a junior student in Beijing. When she joined AWDPI in 2021, she had just finished a brief gap period and was trying to get back on track with her life. People often remain confined within their personal perspectives, constantly focused on their own lives. Through volunteer work, You Ge had the chance to temporarily step outside her own narrative and listen to other people's stories, forcibly pulling herself out of a relatively isolated environment, moving from her personal life into spaces that connect with society, seeing a different world, doing things that can actually help women—this was the coming-of-age gift You Ge gave herself.


In 2021, You Ge joined AWDPI as a member of the official account editorial team. She enjoyed giving her all to complete every task, big or small. "In an inclusive organization like AWDPI, as long as you are willing to learn and invest your time, everyone is given full respect, trust, and time to grow." It was in this warm and supportive environment that You Ge gradually grew into the leader of the official account team.


You Ge's main work involved designing promotional posts for the official account, exposing her to entirely new fields. She slowly learned on the job and improved various skills. She participated in the full process of designing and producing promotional posts for the 2023 AWDPI Asian Women Development Forum. As a student, especially during exam season, she had to balance her academic workload with her responsibilities. Although she often had to work late into the night, looking back now at the series of promotional posts for the 2023 AWDPI Asian Women Development Forum, she believes every effort was worth it.


Liu Feng, the team leader, gave You Ge high praise: "She led the official account team in shouldering the core communication and outreach responsibilities of the organization, moving between departments and meticulously organizing every piece of content—interview articles, research reports, monthly literature digests, and event promotions. Many people came to know and understand AWDPI through the official account, and many women found pathways to support through it."



Publicity Department, Luo Fei


Covers designed by Luo Fei


Luo Fei is a second-year graduate student majoring in psychology. Influenced by her advisor, she has developed a strong interest in sexuality and gender studies. In addition to sharing academic materials, her advisor often shares promotional posts from nonprofit organizations, and it was through these posts that Luo Fei first learned about AWDPI.


It was precisely through her mentor's tweet sharing that she came into contact with AWDPI, which made Luo Fei realize that "dissemination" is a very important matter; otherwise, people might not know when they would learn about this organization. Therefore, when volunteers were being recruited, Luo Fei called on fellow students who also cared about women's protection to join AWDPI together, and without hesitation, she chose the public account team as her first choice.


Luo Fei joined the official account team in May 2023, primarily responsible for cover design and post formatting. Her current work mainly includes the Her Perspective monthly report and translated article posts.


She believes that communication is highly valuable. At this stage, it doesn't require significant manpower or financial investment, yet it allows everyone who encounters AWDPI to understand the current progress in women's protection work. Only when people become aware of the existence of domestic violence will they realize that it is not merely a conflict between partners, but also includes tangible harm inflicted through physical force, weapons, language, and other forms, even beyond partner relationships. Only when people develop this awareness will they take the next step to stop such behavior and protect victims.


Luo Fei's greatest hope for women's rights is "that in the future, we won't need to talk about women's rights or women's protection anymore—we will have achieved true equality." She also hopes to see women's protection organizations like AWDPI flourish, starting from specific issues such as domestic violence and gradually incorporating broader topics related to women's rights. In addition, attention must also be paid to issues such as children's sex education and child abuse.



Being always on the move, forever retaining sensitivity to suffering and injustice, forever maintaining inner tenderness and resolute action, never satisfied with the status quo or past achievements, and always looking forward to a better tomorrow—these are their common traits, and also the collective portrait of AWDPI volunteers.
