Introduction to the Trauma-Informed and Ethical Practice Seminar Series



(Swipe up to read)


The Trauma-Informed and Ethical Practice Seminar Series is jointly initiated by the AWDPI Curriculum Development Office and the Psychological Support Office, aiming to focus on the multiple forms of trauma, especially deep-seated trauma arising from experiences of intimate partner violence, domestic violence, and gender-based violence, and how such trauma intertwines with childhood memories, social structures, and power relations. The seminar series will particularly explore how helpers should respond and practice with trauma-informed ethical awareness during action and accompaniment. The target audience includes helpers (all AWDPI volunteers, social workers, psychotherapists, legal professionals, etc.), relevant scholars and students, activists and advocates, survivors, and any members of the public interested in this topic.


The motivation for initiating this seminar series stems from our recognition that within our organization and the broader field of helping practices, "trauma-informed care" remains a relatively neglected gap. Even in areas where it is discussed more frequently, it often functions as a buzzword without systematic training or practical implementation. We deeply feel this is an issue that urgently needs to be acknowledged, discussed, learned, and practiced, as it concerns not only the enhancement of professionalism and ethical awareness, but also how to build more sustainable and responsible humanistic care relationships between people. For both helpers and those being helped, we hope this seminar series can open a path toward mutual understanding, support, and responsive engagement.


This series will offer diverse online formats—including workshops, lectures, and roundtable discussions—drawing on multidisciplinary and multi-field perspectives to provide as rich a discussion as possible on trauma-informed care and ethics. The entire series is expected to run for six months, starting from December 2025. Introduction of Our Facilitator


Dr. Kate Hudgins

  • Ph.D. in Clinical Psychology

  • Over 30 years of dedicated work in psychodrama and trauma healing

  • Certified Trainer, Educator, and Practitioner (TEP) by the American Board of Examiners in Psychodrama, Sociometry and Group Psychotherapy (ABE)

  • Registered Gestalt Therapist

  • Recipient of an American Psychological Association (APA) research award for her work on "doubling" in psychodrama

Kate spent years searching for, and eventually discovered,a way to heal the lingering effects of childhood trauma, both for oneself and for others. Her work emphasizes a key principle: spontaneity and creativity remain enduring sources of healing after trauma, helping us cultivate resilience, connection, and the ability to respond with flexibility and presence.



Workshop Topic: The Ethics of Touch


When physical involvement plays a significant role in experiential therapies, such as psychodrama and art-based healing, where touch is an integral component, where do we draw the line between touch and non-touch? When is touch genuinely therapeutic? Can it cause harm unintentionally? In what ways can we establish an ethical framework that protects clients as well as practitioners?


Workshop Highlights


  • Get a first-hand understanding of the original Therapeutic Spiral Model (TSM) and Trauma-Informed Psychodrama approach from the founder.

  • Participate in live demonstrations and interactive sessions to gain a deeper understanding of its theoretical foundations and historical background.

  • Address the "grey areas" and uncertainties surrounding physical contact in the helping professions.



This workshop will explore

 the following topics:


  • A demonstration of the role and meaning of touch in psychodrama and creative healing will be provided.

  • A trauma-informed approach to establishing physical boundaries and fostering a sense of safety.

  • Dialogue regarding ethical dilemmas related to the use of touch in experiential practices.
