Emma




In the work Emma, the protagonist Emma is beautiful, intelligent, and wealthy, as well as an incurable dreamer. She eagerly involves herself in the romantic affairs around her, yet stubbornly insists she will never fall into love herself.

She is brave, free-spirited, independent-minded, and courageous in resistance—pursuing equality and freedom in a patriarchal society, refusing to be confined by traditional values and marriage norms. Yet she is also selfish, snobbish, and lacking in compassion. It is precisely this "imperfection" that makes her more real.

Mustang




In a small village in northern Turkey, five sisters strive tirelessly to escape their unbearable fate and pursue freedom.

But the "freedom" they long for is not accepted by everyone. They are shackled with chains named "virtue":

"You can't play around with boys—it will make you a laughingstock."

"If you don't bleed on your wedding night, you must have lost your virginity."...

French romantic aesthetics intertwine with stark realism in the visuals: laughter by the sea, the excitement of sneaking out to watch a football match, and young, radiant skin glowing under the sun—all vividly convey the unrestrained bloom of youth. In sharp contrast are black dresses, rigid hospitality rituals, and the cold indifference of their grandmother and uncles. With bright tones and simple, fresh imagery, this film expresses profound issues concerning women.

My Salinger Year




She moves to New York to chase her dream, becoming an assistant to a renowned literary agent. As she reads letters from readers around the world, she is deeply moved by their sincerity. She breaks the rules and responds to fans in her own way, gradually clarifying what she truly desires in her heart. She holds fast to her dreams and passions, follows her inner voice, and does what she truly wants. When a relationship goes wrong, she cuts her losses promptly; when her career is thriving, she bravely walks away. She follows her heart and pursues her dream of becoming a writer.

This is a "her story" imbued with the power of female self-discovery and growth.

Till the Break of Dawn at the Bus Stop




On a midnight street in Japan, a middle-aged woman is brutally murdered.

Why did such an ordinary-looking woman meet such a tragic end?

A real murder case, in which a homeless person dies simply for being "in the way."

Behind it lie shattered bubbles, failed dreams, female poverty, and institutional neglect...

"She is also us."
