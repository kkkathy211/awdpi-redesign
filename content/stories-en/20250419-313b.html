SBS introduction


SBS (Special Broadcasting Service) is one of Australia's most influential and innovative national public broadcasting organisations, profoundly shaping Australia's media landscape and cultural diversity. Since its establishment in 1975, SBS has been dedicated to serving multicultural communities and has evolved into a world-leading multi-platform, multilingual media network. It currently operates six television channels and eight radio stations, reaching over 60 communities through broadcasts in 74 languages. In addition, SBS ranks among the most trusted sources of Chinese-language news media in Australia. As Australia's only multilingual public media platform and one of the most linguistically diverse media organisations globally, SBS is not only a witness and promoter of Australia's multiculturalism but also a benchmark in the global media industry.




Foreword



In March 2025, Cindy Gui Jiaze, founder and head of the AWDPI Australia National Office, was invited for an exclusive interview with SBS, a leading Australian media outlet. As an outstanding representative of Asian women, she shared the founding background, core values, and strategic development of AWDPI in Australia.

Following China, the UK, the US, and Europe (Belgium), Australia has become another key node in AWDPI's global expansion—a reflection of AWDPI's enduring commitment and belief in empowering Asian women worldwide. This in-depth report by SBS not only acknowledges the long-term dedication and efforts of the founding team and members but also vividly portrays the journey of countless Asian women breaking through barriers and achieving growth.

Looking ahead, AWDPI will continue to channel every bit of attention and support into more substantial and caring local services and contributions, striving to build a world full of love and equality for women.

Welcome to join the AWDPI Asian Women Development Plan Australia office—let this spring blossom because of you!

SBS Report Article



In this episode, we feature Jiaze Gui, founder and head of the Asian Women Development Plan International (AWDPI) Australia National Office, sharing AWDPI's plans for launching in Australia and her reflections on women's empowerment. Click the audio to listen to the full interview.


Photo: AWDPI team attending the United Nations in New York; Jiaze Gui, head of the Australia office, is second from the left 


This episode focuses on an Asian woman—Jiaze Gui. A Harvard University graduate, she is a World Economic Forum Davos Global Shaper, serves as an ambassador for the Harvard Center for International Development, and has previously been involved in multiple women's empowerment projects in Indonesia.


Photo: Jiaze Gui during her research trip in Indonesia 


Now, she is preparing to launch the Asian Women Development Plan International (AWDPI) in Australia, promoting the development and participation of Asian women in local society.

Notably, Jiaze Gui's initial academic background was in applied mathematics, before gradually shifting toward the fields of international development and women's empowerment.


What experiences and reflections lie behind this cross-disciplinary transition? And how does she view the role of Asian women in multicultural societies?
