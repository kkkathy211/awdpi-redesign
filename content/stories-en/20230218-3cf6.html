Opening Remarks

Time and tide wait for no one. Over the past year, we have witnessed deepening economic reforms and rebalancing in the post-pandemic era, multiple crises triggered by geopolitical tensions, and the renewed focus of contemporary China's foreign relations. Looking ahead, we look forward to the surging momentum of a new era in China. Yet, amid the complex landscape of a once-in-a-century transformation and shifting international order, a sense of concern arises. Can international relations move toward détente? Can environmental-protection-driven international cooperation effectively advance bilateral ties between China and certain Western countries? What role do the humanities, arts, and media play in this process?


In 2023, the London School of Economics China Development Forum will join audiences in focusing on the new normal, engaging in global dialogue, and pursuing common development. We will discuss the obstacles and opportunities on China's future development path and collectively envision the future.

About the China Development Forum

Founded in 2009, the London School of Economics China Development Forum has become the largest China-focused forum in Europe.

Upholding the mission of building a bridge between China and the world and promoting mutual understanding, we are committed to providing students and professionals studying China with an active and in-depth platform for intellectual exchange, offering participants valuable learning and networking opportunities, and connecting them with global experts and future leaders.

Our forum consists of keynote speeches and panel discussions. Speakers are prominent figures from government, academia, and the business world, while panel discussions are debate-based, designed to stimulate in-depth dialogue among speakers. After each panel, we include a Q&A session to ensure the best possible interactive experience for the audience.

The theme of the 15th London School of Economics China Development Forum is "Focusing on the New Normal: Engaging the World, Pursuing Common Development" (China's New Normal: Challenges and Opportunities), composed of five sub-forums that will take place on the evolution of national soft power, China-EU bilateral relations and carbon neutrality challenges, neoliberalism and China's economy, China's relations with developing countries, and Sino-British relations, offering an academic feast on China!

Overview of This Year's Forum Topics


[Swipe left and right for more]


1. Arts and Media Sub-forum: China's Soft Power: Chinese Identity and the Media

The Arts and Media Sub-forum aims to reveal how China's artistic capital is a key factor in transforming foreign perceptions. We also seek to understand how art, the repatriation of cultural relics from overseas, and national cultural diplomacy are closely interconnected, as well as the opportunities for multilateral cooperation through art and culture. Understanding how China implements these policies and its overall vision is crucial to grasping China's macro-level strategy.


2. Environment Sub-forum: Facing the Carbon Neutrality Challenge: China-EU Emissions and Bilateral Relations 

This sub-forum explores the prospects and challenges of emissions trading schemes in Europe and China, examining how financial markets and public policy intersect to create a greener future. We will analyze the roles of three key players in climate policy: the European Union, the United Kingdom, and China. We will discuss their prospects and challenges in reducing greenhouse gas emissions at corporate, national, and international levels. Understanding this topic will help inform future global environmental governance and green business practices.


3. Economic Sub-forum: Challenges and Opportunities Amid Global Economic Turbulence  

This forum focuses on the challenges facing China's economy in the 2020s. As China continues its transition toward a market economy, its economic miracle is beginning to show cracks. With growth faltering, persistent urban-rural inequality, and increasing tensions between enterprises and the state, how will the Chinese Communist Party achieve its goal of becoming a middle-income country?



4. International Relations Sub-forum: Development Prospects and Conflicts: China and the Developing World 

As China's ties with the Global South deepen, understanding China's current ambitions and aspirations is essential. However, responses from developing countries to China's expanding leadership are varied and complex. Therefore, this forum turns its focus to developing nations, exploring diverse perspectives on the Belt and Road Initiative and related Chinese investments, and examining how developing countries balance their relationships with both China and the West.



5. Sino-British Relations Sub-forum: International Fragmentation and Change: The Future of Sino-British Relations 

From the Russia-Ukraine conflict to rising tensions across the Taiwan Strait, the world is gradually shifting from decades of peaceful coexistence and win-win cooperation toward a new Cold War. Amid this wave of division, as students and scholars connecting China and the UK, we hope to deeply explore the future of Sino-British relations in this forum—by analyzing China's foreign policy, economic ties between China and the UK, and geopolitical conflicts unfolding around the world.


Preview of Selected Distinguished Guests


[*Guests listed in no particular order / Swipe left and right for more]


Danny Quah (Ke Chengxing)


Dean and Lee Kuan Yew Professor of Economics at the Lee Kuan Yew School of Public Policy, National University of Singapore. 


Professor Ke previously served as Associate Professor of Economics at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology, Associate Professor of Economics at Harvard University, Professor at the School of Economics and Management at Tsinghua University, and Professor in the Department of Economics and the Department of International Development at the London School of Economics, where he also led the Southeast Asia Research Centre..



Mark Logan, MP

 



M Mark Logan has served as a Member of Parliament for the UK Conservative Party since December 12, 2019. Before becoming an MP, he worked at the British Consulate-General in Shanghai, handling media relations. Mr. Logan also worked for the Chinese multinational conglomerate Sanpower Group.

Asaph Hyman

  

Asaph Hyman is the Global Head of Chinese Ceramics and Works of Art at Bonhams, one of the world's largest and most renowned auction houses, and currently serves on the Council of the Oriental Ceramic Society.

He curated the record-breaking £24 million sale during Asian Art Week in London in November 2011, setting a historic benchmark for Chinese art auctions in Europe. 

Kerry Brown (Kerry Brown)

  

Kerry Brown is a renowned British scholar of Chinese studies, currently Director of the China Institute (Lau China Institute) at King's College London, Associate Fellow, Senior Research Fellow, and Head of the Asia Programme at Chatham House (the Royal Institute of International Affairs).


Fu Xiaolan (Fu Xiaolan))

  

Professor Fu Xiaolan is based at the Department of International Development, University of Oxford. She was personally appointed by the UN Secretary-General as a member of the UN Secretary-General's Ten-Member Advisory Group on the Technology Facilitation Mechanism for Sustainable Development. She is also a member of the Leadership Council of the UN Sustainable Development Solutions Network led by renowned American economist Jeffrey Sachs. 


Natacha Devillers


  

 Natacha Devillers is a film producer based in China. Some of her films premiered at the Cannes Film Festival and were nominated for Academy Awards.

She produced films such as Enter the Warrior's Gate, starring Mark Chao, Ni Ni, and Dave Bautista, and Super Express, co-produced with Chen He.
