November 25 is the United Nations' International Day for the Elimination of Violence against Women, marking the start of the "16 Days of Activism" against Gender-Based Violence, which runs annually until December 10. The "16 Days of Activism" aims to encourage people worldwide to take active steps to eliminate gender-based violence.


To date, violence against women and girls remains one of the most widespread human rights violations in the world. According to United Nations statistics, approximately 736 million women globally—nearly one in three—have experienced gender-based violence at least once in their lives, including violence in the workplace and online spaces.


This year, Asian Women Development Plan International (AWDPI) will host five online workshops during the "16 Days of Activism" and relaunch the "Moving Letterbox" initiative, joining people across countries who care about gender issues to express support for survivors of domestic violence. In the United Kingdom and Belgium, we will organize offline exhibitions against gender-based violence; in the United States, we will conduct research and interviews on the living conditions of minority ethnic caregivers.


#First Workshop


Dou Lu

Former Secretary-General of Beijing Da Ai Qing Chen Public Welfare Foundation

Columbia University SIPA '24


In an occupational disease predominantly affecting male patients, can we still see gender?


I have been working with pneumoconiosis patients and conducting related research for many years, yet I have never approached this issue from a gender perspective. When the occupation itself is already gendered, the single-gender composition of patients may seem obvious.


However, when we turn our attention to the families behind these patients, the impact of gender becomes worthy of deep reflection. Do gender stereotypes increase workers' vulnerability to illness? How do female patients emerge? After men fall ill, how do women's roles and power dynamics within the family change? How much invisible burden imposed by society do women carry?


Let us deconstruct the issue of occupational diseases from a new perspective.
