Have you been emotionally manipulated?


1

What is emotional manipulation?


23-year-old Dave constantly mocks his girlfriend Lily. Lily begins to feel worthless, so she starts pleasing Dave, trying to earn his praise. Christine wants to quit drinking and shares this goal with her husband Raymond. Raymond responds by accusing Christine of forcing him to give up drinking, since he dislikes drinking alone. Feeling deeply guilty, Christine abandons her plan to quit. Teresa is a painter, but her boyfriend Samuel forbids her from painting on weekends, claiming it's for her own good so they can spend more time together. Whenever Teresa objects, Samuel throws things and shouts.


Do these scenarios seem familiar to you? Many women have experienced PUA (emotional manipulation), and covert PUA, disguised as love, is extremely difficult to detect.


Emotional manipulation refers to using emotions as a "weapon" to control and coerce others to achieve one's own goals. Under manipulation and coercion, victims are forced into isolation from their external environment, making it difficult for them to fully realize their potential. They feel restricted, increasingly lost in their partner's values, adopt their partner's opinions as their own, and believe everything their partner does is for their own good, ultimately losing their independent identity and enabling the manipulator's success.



2

How to identify an emotionally manipulative relationship


A non-manipulative relationship is characterized by mutual support, shared decision-making, and the absence of fear of threats or punishment. Both partners can communicate equally, respect each other, and grow together.


An emotionally manipulative relationship is characterized by the manipulator using various tactics to control the victim. To avoid conflict and punishment, women may have to abandon their life plans, opinions, and dreams. She may occasionally resist, but this often leads to further isolation.


Manipulative entrapment refers to situations where women are subjected to various forms of inequality, cutting off all their avenues for help. Women often feel fear, isolation, and threats, losing their sense of self and independence.



If you have experienced any of the following, you may be suffering from emotional manipulation:

      Do you feel threatened?

      Are you afraid to speak your mind?

      Does the manipulator often show jealousy and possessiveness?

      Does the manipulator restrict your contact with family and friends?

     Do you work hard to escape the manipulator's criticism and belittlement?

      ......


Undoubtedly, emotional manipulation can not only lead to physical harm against victims but also damage women's self-esteem and independence. Therefore, we must pay attention to both the physical and psychological trauma suffered by victims.


3

Methods of emotional manipulation


Emotional manipulation takes many forms, some of which are extremely subtle. Skilled manipulators can be hard to identify. The main types are as follows:

One, Isolation : attempting to cut off the victim from all external connections

Two, Rules: "If you don't obey me, I will punish you"

Three, Surveillance: like an invisible chain tightly binding the other person

Four, Abuse: physical violence causes psychological and physical trauma to the victim

Five, Threats: "If I'm not happy, I won't let you be happy either"

Six, Punishment: "If I'm not satisfied, you must bear the consequences"

Seven, Control: depriving the other person of all rights to free choice

Eight, Belittlement: damaging the victim's self-esteem to establish the manipulator's superiority

Nine, Children: always the vulnerable point through which mothers are manipulated



Sometimes, skilled manipulators flexibly and comprehensively use multiple tactics. In the game "Tears of Themis," there is such a case: psychologist Mo Yi collaborates with the police to solve a serial PUA case and rescues the female victims. Surprisingly, they discover that the ringleader is a psychology professor from a prestigious university. Some manipulators, although using manipulative tactics, are unaware of their actions and operate at a subconscious level.
