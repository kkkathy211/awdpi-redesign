 Introduction:


The profound power held by women lies precisely in being uncompromising, non-conforming, delicate yet far-sighted, passionate and free—these qualities are being vividly expressed by women of the new era. This online workshop, "How You Become Yourself: Life History Performance and the Formation of Femininity," invites us to draw closer to the life force of women.

1

Icebreaker and Brainstorm


Participants engaged in deep conversations around the theme "the moment I became aware of myself as a woman." Xiao Yan (pseudonym) shared her growth and struggles in different social environments at home and abroad, and the resulting reflections and deconstruction of her self-identity, as well as the reconstruction and transcendence of her own life. As a former HR employee at a BAT company, Xiao Yan transitioned from "not wanting to be bound by materialism, not adapting to the domestic environment" to choosing to "give up a high-paying, comfortable job in China and start anew in Belgium." She said she has "gradually accepted herself and feels proud to be this kind of woman."

 

Another participant, Xiao Ge (pseudonym), outlined moments in her life marked by femininity, suggesting that women can equally be synonymous with freedom, strength, and independence. Inspired by the stories of guests Xiao Yan and Xiao Ge, other participants also reflected on their own female identities from personal perspectives, forming connections through online exchanges with others' life experiences.


2

Guest Sharing


We were honored to invite Ms. Sun Yan, a social worker and founder of "Disabled BEST Sisters," to share her life journey as a woman with disabilities—from advocating for gender equality to becoming a disability rights activist.


- Ms. Sun Yan -


Executive Director of Le Yirong Social Work Agency

Visiting Scholar in Disability Law, University of Galway, Ireland

Professional practitioner in the field of gender

Social worker


Ms. Sun Yan frequently visits campuses to promote gender equality awareness and the concept of gender diversity among university educators. At the same time, her work in the disability field has led her to notice that gender awareness among people with disabilities seems blurred—"everyone raises their needs around illness, around physical impairments," but the lack of awareness causes them to overlook gender dimensions. After recognizing this issue, Ms. Sun Yan began dedicating herself to delivering gender training and promoting diversity awareness in disability empowerment organizations nationwide.


Ms. Sun Yan and her fellow activists hope to break through the limitations of news reporting by sharing inspiring stories of women with disabilities, helping the public gradually recognize a more multifaceted and vibrant image of disabled women, and find common ground with these stories, thus dismantling stereotypes about people with disabilities. Finally, tying back to the theme of this event, Ms. Sun Yan shared this message: "Helping others to help themselves; life influences life—we walk this path together."


3

Exhibition of Portraits of Female Vitality


At the end of this event, we reached the long-anticipated session of sharing portraits of female vitality.

 


"I've always loved space and astronomy, so I used the image of an astronaut to represent myself. The stars beside me symbolize my ideas. I hope everyone can be themselves and pursue their dreams."

——Knox


In addition to live sharing, we selected a portion from the thirty to forty life portraits collected in advance for display. Other works will be exhibited together in the upcoming Life Portrait Exhibition:


First is a biology-related piece combining the mythological story of "Nüwa creating humans" with the DNA double helix from biology.



"Nüwa creating humans. The other day, while reading the textbook passage 'Nüwa Creates Humans,' I was deeply moved by the lively and lovely image of Nüwa. Does Nüwa change after becoming a mother? I don't know, but my mother has changed so much for her child. My mom had me when she was 27; she was only 33 when I started elementary school. Back then, I always resented her for focusing only on work. I'll soon be 21, and whether or not to have children has occasionally become a concern. I think I might not be able to be as good as her."

— Jiangjiang, 20, student


The next two works focus more on color and abstraction, and their creators have written some profound annotations:



"The warmth of women always makes me think first of the hugs they've given me"

— Xiao Bai, 28, oil painter



"Rights, equality, and freedom"

— Clear, 24, student



"We have the grace and composure to kiss thorny flowers; we need not be disheveled just because we are trapped."

— Zhang Hanqi, 21, student


The following works place greater emphasis on depicting specific female images, a more common form in life portraits:



"Stubborn, diverse, and vibrant life"

— Xuxu, 17, student



"Sinking into the green sea, blooming brilliantly, butterflies come naturally, seeking towards the light."

— Charlotte, 26, marketing manager


Up to this point, the life portrait sharing session comes to an end. More artworks will be displayed together in the upcoming exhibition—stay tuned!


 Closing Remarks:

"I was born a mountain, not a stream; I desire to overlook the mundane valleys from the peak. I was born a hero, not common grass; I stand on the shoulders of giants, looking down upon the timid and insignificant."


Everyone's inner canvas of femininity and strength is slowly taking shape—using resonance as the paper and talent as the brush, the unique image of "her" in our hearts is being painted: they are visible smiles, perceptible souls.
