On the golden banks of the Chao Phraya River, Thailand—a land kissed by tropical sunshine—has nurtured countless seeds of passion and dreams. In search of a larger stage, many Thai women have embarked on journeys to the United States, where for them, it is a new world filled with opportunities and challenges.


Siri, a Thai-American caregiver living in San Jose, California, carries not only her personal struggles and sacrifices in her story, but also reflects the deep-seated challenges brought by social inequality and cultural differences.


In the United States, many minority women caregivers like Siri are facing systemic difficulties: they work diligently and give silently, yet rarely receive recognition or understanding from American society.



01

Voices from the Margins: Silent Resistance

"

When Siri first arrived in the U.S., she could only work in restaurants. Later, with the help of relatives, she obtained a work visa and transitioned into a caregiving career.


Caregiving is often the first job choice for many Asian immigrants in the U.S., as the industry typically has low requirements for educational background and language proficiency. However, the caregiving sector commonly suffers from chronic understaffing, forcing existing caregivers to take on heavier workloads. Siri works up to 60 hours a week, from 10 a.m. to 9 p.m., and her exhaustion carries a trace of helplessness: "I can't schedule flexibly; the hours are long and exhausting."

Even with such long daily work hours, Siri's income remains discouraging: "After the pandemic started, living expenses surged, and I feel like I have even less money to spend."


According to DataUSA, as of 2021, approximately 1.4 million people in the U.S. work in caregiving. Yet, this industry has long struggled with low wages and poor benefits. Data shows that the average annual salary for female caregivers is only $27,610—$31,986 less than the national average of $59,596—highlighting the economic hardship faced by frontline caregivers like Siri.


When asked about her future plans, Siri paused and said she currently lives alone and has no immediate plans to marry. Siri's experience echoes, to some extent, the reality of Japanese caregivers described in the book Tokyo's Poor Women. The author, Atsuhiko Nakamura, notes that many caregivers view marriage and starting a family as luxuries, even sacrificing their own futures just to improve the lives of those they care for. This neglect of younger generations in the caregiving system leads to the spread of black labor and the formation of poverty.


02

Flower in Foreign Soil: Growing Resiliently

"

Siri also vaguely felt that she was falling into an economic predicament that was difficult to break, as all her energy was consumed by her current livelihood needs. However, she displayed an indomitable spirit, determined to break this deadlock.

Siri has enrolled in multiple training courses to broaden her professional skills—aiming for better job opportunities in the competitive caregiving industry. But these courses are expensive, ranging from $1,000 to $3,400. "I bear all these course costs myself, with no financial support from any organization." Siri said with regret.


These training expenses undoubtedly add a heavy burden to Siri's already tight budget. Although the U.S. has a relatively comprehensive vocational education system and a large number of NGOs and charitable organizations, when individuals like Siri need further training and learning, they seem unable to access these opportunities and effective support through friendly, convenient means, and thus must squeeze funds from their limited income to pay for their own education.


In terms of time, these training courses take anywhere from several days to several months. Siri often has to attend classes after her demanding work shifts or during work breaks.


She did the math: "Some courses only take two to three days, while others are longer, about three months. Three months means attending class from Monday to Friday, sometimes from 9 a.m. to 6:30 p.m., sometimes until 8 p.m. So I prefer the short-term courses of about two to three days." Because she must balance the time required for courses and work, Siri sometimes has to give up certain job opportunities, but these efforts are her way of seeking light in life.

03

Light of Dreams: Shining Brightly

"

Despite facing numerous challenges, Siri still strives to remain positive and optimistic in life. She smiled and said: "Communicating with care recipients isn't easy. Smiling and staying calm are important because they leave a good impression. On the contrary, if I'm in a bad mood, it could negatively affect the care recipient, making them unwilling to hire me again."


"I'm willing to work hard because I have a dream for the future." She paused for a moment, then said: "I want to open my own small shop in San Francisco." Siri shared her goal candidly, her eyes sparkling with hope for the future—her determination and dreams never shaken by life's hardships.









 In a distant foreign land, Siri is like a flower growing resiliently through hardship, still holding onto hope and dreams for a better future despite countless challenges. And this is precisely the survival narrative written by Siri and many minority women like her in a foreign country—a life story filled with challenges and opportunities, woven with both sorrow and joy.
