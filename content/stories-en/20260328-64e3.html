March 1–3, 2026 the 17th Women's Leadership and Empowerment Conference opened in Bangkok, Thailand.


The conference focused on women's empowerment, rights protection, mental health, career development, and technology empowerment and invited numerous female researchers, industry representatives, and heads of social organizations from different countries and regions.


As an international social organization representative, Asian Women Development Plan International (AWDPI) engaged in on-site exchanges with representatives from Bermuda, Bangladesh, the United States, Myanmar, Kenya, Sri Lanka, Singapore, Israel, and other countries and regions. Meanwhile, representatives from Germany, Canada, South Africa, Japan, and Tanzania presented via video, sharing their countries' research and practical achievements in the field of women's development.







On March 2, Dan Zhaoyun, head of the AWDPI Thailand National Office, delivered a keynote speech based on a research report by the AWDPI Academic Research Department—"Making Invisible Violence Visible: Measuring GBV at the Intersection of Migration, Race and Gender." The speech explored measurement blind spots and methodological challenges in assessing gender-based violence from an intersectional perspective of migration, race, and gender, and provided analysis drawing on practical experiences from the Asian region. This presentation sparked enthusiastic discussion among participants, with representatives from various countries engaging in dialogue on issues such as constructing intersectional frameworks and cross-cultural data collection.


In addition to keynote presentations, AWDPI also participated in roundtable forums and thematic discussions. Female representatives from different countries and regions exchanged views on "Women leaders navigating cultural expectations and structural barriers" and "Women's leadership in an era of uncertainty." During conference sessions and tea breaks, AWDPI delegates introduced the organization's work plans in the areas of







women's empowerment, capacity building, and rights protection, and invited scholars and organizations with shared interests to maintain academic communication in the future. This conference not only demonstrated AWDPI's ongoing engagement in international dialogues on women's development but also further connected global communities of women researchers and practitioners, creating space for AWDPI to better serve Asian women in the future.
