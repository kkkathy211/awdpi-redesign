This is the story of an organization that, driven by a dream of walking hand in hand with Asian women worldwide, has grown from its first tentative steps to setting sail toward broader horizons.


Over three years ago, this nonprofit was merely a project team operated by a group of passionate volunteers. Today, it is transforming into a well-structured, clearly divided, multi-method, and increasingly influential international organization. To date, numerous volunteers have provided assistance services to over a thousand Asian women.

AWDPI stands for Asian Women Development Plan International. It evolved from Avoice (the Overseas Chinese Women Protection Project, Against Violence to Overseas Chinese Women Program), established in 2020. Initiated by its founder in Belgium, Avoice gradually expanded its project work to multiple Western countries, striving to provide accessible real-world protection, effective professional assistance, and sustained empowerment support for Chinese women facing gender-based violence or other difficulties. Currently, AWDPI has obtained or is in the process of obtaining nonprofit registration and tax-deductible donation status in relevant activity countries such as the United Kingdom and the United States.


In Western societies, Asian women—including Chinese women—are among the largest and fastest-growing minority ethnic groups, playing an important role in global political, economic, cultural, and social development. However, as service capacity improves and the population in need continues to grow, we have observed that Western societies are saturated with stereotypes about Asian women. The dual identity markers of "Asian" and "woman" make it difficult for them to truly integrate into mainstream overseas societies, and they long endure injustice and harm caused by discrimination.


Under the "model minority" label imposed on Asian communities, Asian women are trapped by the stereotype of high household income, causing their economic struggles to be overlooked in discussions about racial discrimination. In the United States alone, during the 2020 pandemic, 44% of unemployed Asian women were jobless for more than six months—higher than Black women (40.8%), Latina women (38.3%), and the overall population (38.3%) [1]. In 2021, Asian women made up 1 in 15 of entry-level positions in U.S. companies, but only 1 in 50 of CXO (executive) roles [2].


In American society, the harmful stereotypes portraying Asian women as submissive or sexualized are even more destructive. These stereotypes are reflected in mainstream cultural narratives. A 2002 study found that Asian women were disproportionately cast in victim roles in violent pornography [3]. Stereotypes about Asian women were also linked to the killing of six Asian women in a 2021 mass shooting [4]. In the year preceding that shooting, anti-Asian violence and racist attacks had been rising [5], and when Asians frequently became victims of racial hate incidents in the U.S., Asian women experienced such hate events at 2.3 times the rate of Asian men [6].



To improve the development challenges faced by Asian women overseas, AWDPI remains committed to women's rights as its core focus, striving to eliminate violence and discrimination against Asian women and promote their protection, empowerment, and development. AWDPI has built a broad cooperation network with United Nations system organizations, relevant government departments in various countries, international organizations, multinational corporations, and academic institutions, serving as a solid bridge connecting Asian women with diverse resources.

AWDPI's work is primarily divided into four areas: combating gender-based violence, protection of Asian women overseas, violence-free spaces, and women's employment and leadership.
