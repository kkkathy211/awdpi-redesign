23–24 September 2023, amidst a gentle breeze and the spirit of autumn, the 2023 AWDPI Asian Women Development Forum was successfully held. This forum was organized by AWDPI (Asian Women Development Plan International), an organization advocating for attention to the survival challenges faced by Asian women overseas and committed to continuously supporting Asian women in accessing opportunities for comprehensive development. At the forum, AWDPI shared updates on its organizational progress with partners who care about Asian women, including its successful registration as a nonprofit and tax-deductible donation eligibility in key activity countries such as the United Kingdom and the United States. With the support of individuals dedicated to advancing Asian women, AWDPI was able to host diverse keynote speeches, panel discussions, and paper reviews at this forum. Centered on issues such as eliminating gender-based violence, overseas labor, women's career development, women's agency and influence, and women's health, the forum brought together nearly 30 leaders from the nonprofit, academic, and professional sectors to amplify voices for gender equality, building a nearly one thousand-person community network for exchange and dialogue, and engaging over 10,000 viewers in discussions on Asian women's empowerment and development.



Opening Remarks


Opening Remarks Speaker: Wang Xingjuan


Wang Xingjuan, founder and honorary chair of the Hongfeng Women's Psychological Counseling Service Center, delivered an impassioned opening speech that officially launched the forum. In her remarks, Wang Xingjuan first reviewed the collaboration history between the Hongfeng Center and Avoice, AWDPI's flagship project for the Avoice Overseas Chinese Women Protection Project. Experts from the Hongfeng Center have provided consultation and training support to Avoice, contributing to addressing the needs of Asian women. Wang Xingjuan introduced the background of the Hongfeng Center's founding in 1988, making it China's first nongovernmental women's organization, and highlighted its work over the past 30+ years in safeguarding women's rights and advancing gender equality. She also presented the public welfare brands initiated by the Hongfeng Center, their achievements, and plans to further expand service areas. Additionally, Wang Xingjuan explained the open-platform nature of the Hongfeng Center and emphasized the vital role played by its volunteer teams from all sectors of society. Finally, she extended her best wishes for the forum's success and expressed hope for collectively advancing the development and progress of Asian women.

Keynote Speech:

How Can Victims of Domestic Violence Effectively Seek Help?


Keynote Speaker: Wan Fei


Wan Fei, founder of the Lantianxia Women and Children Rights Protection Association in Jianli City and a National Most Beautiful Volunteer, shared practical insights on how victims of domestic violence can effectively seek help, drawing from his field experience across four aspects. The first aspect is avenues for abused women to seek help, primarily including confiding in family or friends, consulting professional organizations, and reporting to the police. Wan Fei recommended a dual approach—simultaneously reporting to the police and seeking professional consultation. The second aspect is collecting evidence of domestic violence, with key methods including installing surveillance cameras, audio recording, seeking medical treatment promptly to obtain medical records, and ensuring police take written statements after reports. Victims should ensure evidence is complete, objective, and legally obtained, and must also preserve and back up evidence. The third aspect is effective methods for reporting to the police. Wan Fei advised being concise and specific when reporting, making clear legal requests such as for official documents, rather than raising issues like divorce that the police cannot handle. Additionally, after reporting, victims must firmly obtain legal documents, especially administrative penalty decisions, which are crucial for subsequent rights protection. The fourth aspect is gaining negotiation leverage, primarily by using the threat of administrative or criminal liability to pressure the abuser into compliance. However, Wan Fei cautioned that the potential negative consequences of public exposure must be carefully considered. Finally, Wan Fei concluded that in facing domestic violence, we must think rationally, take effective measures to stop violence, collect evidence, and assert rights, in order to eliminate domestic violence and advance women's development.

Keynote Speech:

How to Use Legal Measures to Respond to Domestic Violence?

—Experiences and Advice from a Professional Female Lawyer


Keynote Speaker: Li Ying


Li Ying, Deputy Director and Partner at Beijing Zhenbang Law Firm, Council Member of the Chinese Society of Law's Marriage and Family Law Research Association, and Founder of Yuanzhong Center for Family and Community Development, stated that domestic violence is a serious social issue that violates women's human rights and must be firmly countered with legal tools. First, Li Ying briefly introduced the concept, manifestations, and characteristics of domestic violence, as well as common misconceptions, urging the audience to understand domestic violence correctly. Next, she focused on the main contents of China's Anti-Domestic Violence Law, including its legislative purpose, responsible agencies, preventive measures, and response mechanisms, particularly highlighting the significance of innovative measures such as protection orders and warning letters. Subsequently, using typical cases, Li Ying shared recommendations for responding to domestic violence, including promptly reporting to the police, collecting evidence, applying for protection orders, undergoing injury assessments, and initiating relevant legal proceedings—a comprehensive, multi-pronged approach. Finally, Li Ying briefly introduced Yuanzhong's public welfare projects, including China's first domestic violence assistance mini-program, and outlined future plans to further advance domestic violence prevention and response. Li Ying concluded by expressing hope that her speech would raise greater awareness of domestic violence and inspire collective efforts toward family harmony and gender equality.

Keynote Speech:

Psychological Healing for Women Under Domestic Violence


Keynote Speaker: Liu Yanan


Liu Yanan, Deputy Director of the National Psychological Counseling Vocational Training and Examination Center and counselor with the Shanghai Shuangyong Foundation Mental Health Alliance, emphasized that domestic violence is a serious social issue requiring collective attention and action. She called for joint efforts to promote anti-domestic violence awareness and to offer understanding and support to victims. In her speech, Liu Yanan first analyzed psychological traits and triggers of perpetrators, encouraging the audience to objectively understand the motivations behind abusers' behaviors rather than merely judging their actions. Next, she examined possible psychological characteristics and underlying causes among abused women, such as attachment disorders and "infantile mentality," helping the audience gain deeper insight into victims' situations. She then described potential negative psychological consequences of long-term domestic violence, such as low self-esteem, learned helplessness, and various emotional disorders, urging societal attention. Finally, Liu Yanan proposed several principles for psychological intervention, including building trust, minimizing secondary harm, and restoring confidence in life, emphasizing the importance of helping victims achieve self-growth and emerge from the shadows.

Keynote Speech:

Understanding the Evolving Needs of Overseas Workers Based on Hotline Data and Case Studies


Keynote Speaker: Huang Bingyao


Huang Bingyao, Director of the Innovision Community Development Agency, systematically introduced Innovision's hotline service model supporting Asian worker communities and highlighted labor challenges reflected in data. Huang Bingyao explained that Innovision was founded in China in 2007 and now operates in multiple Asian countries, focusing on labor rights and related issues. Through partnerships with multinational brands, leveraging their influence over supply chains, Innovision has established worker hotlines in factories across Asia, offering consultation and complaint services to help resolve labor disputes. Huang Bingyao shared specific cases from countries such as Thailand and India, illustrating how the hotline model operates and the common and individualized challenges faced by workers from different countries and linguistic backgrounds. Huang Bingyao pointed out that hotline data shows major issues faced by workers, including visa problems, lack of social security, and gender discrimination—manifestations of structural inequality that require collective attention and improvement. The hotline service continues to explore new possibilities based on local needs. Finally, Huang Bingyao called on all sectors of society to pay attention to labor rights to promote social progress.

Panel Discussion:

Women and Workplace Empowerment


Panelist: Hong Ling


Panelist: Li Xia


Panelist: Niu Wen


*Panelists listed in alphabetical order by surname


The discussion was moderated by Zheng Anni, Head of Human Resources at AWDPI and TED host, with guests including Hong Ling, Senior Strategic Project Director at a world-renowned investment bank (Europe) and founder of the career development platform Ling Shuo Zhi Chang Ren Sheng (Ling Speaks Career Life); Li Xia, CEO of the Financial Division at Geely Holding Group and Global Director at Saxo Bank; and Niu Wen, founder of Yexin Pai and Forbes U30 honoree in 2019. This dialogue deeply explored the challenges and opportunities women face and offered constructive suggestions on how to empower women. First, the panelists discussed workplace challenges women encounter, including internal factors such as lack of confidence, misjudgment of abilities, and fear of difficulty, as well as external environmental factors such as gender discrimination and stereotypes. Regarding responses to workplace challenges, the guests proposed a three-tiered approach to empower women. At the individual level, one can build confidence, practice mindfulness, and stay true to oneself. At the organizational level, mentorship support, setting targets for female representation, and establishing fair selection mechanisms are recommended. At the societal level, promoting female role models, claiming voice and representation, and eliminating gender bias are essential. In conclusion, this discussion inspired the understanding that efforts are needed at individual, organizational, and societal levels to help women gain respect, fair treatment, and the opportunity to realize their value—progress that not only concerns women's development but also the advancement of society as a whole.

Keynote Speech:

Practicing ESG Principles to Empower the Era of Her


Keynote Speaker: Xiong Ling


Xiong Ling, Director of the Dual Carbon Promotion and Development Center at the Shanghai Association for International Cooperation of Small and Medium Enterprises and overall director of the "ESG and Carbon Neutrality Strategy Research Program for Chinese Listed Enterprises," cited extensive research and case studies to provide highly constructive insights on how ESG can empower women. At the social level, ESG emphasizes gender equality, working conditions, and protection of basic rights, creating a favorable working environment for women. At the corporate governance level, ESG encourages increasing the proportion of women in management and decision-making roles, enabling women to play unique roles. Xiong Ling noted that empirical studies show that enhancing women's leadership contributes positively to corporate performance in environmental protection and social responsibility. Many leading companies, under the ESG framework, demonstrate social responsibility by safeguarding women's rights and providing development opportunities. Xiong Ling also emphasized that women's attention to detail and environmental awareness offer unique support for ESG-driven sustainable development. Overall, Xiong Ling elaborated on the critical issue of how ESG supports women's development, which holds significant implications for advancing gender equality and sustainable development, and allows us to witness the rising presence and impact of women across diverse fields.

Keynote Speech:

Women's Academic and Career Development

—Making the Most of University Life


Keynote Speaker: Sherry Zhang


Sherry Zhang, Founder and CEO of HiYouth, Vice Secretary-General of the G20 Young Entrepreneurs' Alliance, shared her rich experiences in university life and career development, delivering a vivid and inspiring story. Sherry Zhang encouraged the audience not to be confined by their academic majors during university, but to actively participate in various student organizations and explore their development through diverse practical experiences. She also advised that if students find their major unsuitable, they could consider pursuing a double degree or minor to adjust their path, or supplement their knowledge through self-study and obtain relevant certifications. Regarding employment, Sherry Zhang suggested choosing a career based on personal interests and strengths, and trying different positions to discover the most passionate direction. On entrepreneurship, she recommended gaining relevant experience first, perhaps by conducting small-scale experiments before transitioning into full-time entrepreneurship. Whether in academics or career development, Sherry Zhang encouraged everyone to maintain a mindset of continuous learning and broadening their horizons at every stage.

Keynote Speech:

Shaping the Future

—Women's Critical Influence in the Age of AI


Keynote Speaker: Li Qianling


Li Qianling, Chief Executive Officer of JLL Greater China and Founder of Beixi Investment Collective, delivered an in-depth analysis of gender bias in AI and proposed recommendations to prevent and reduce such bias—namely, increasing women's participation in the AI field, which is of great significance for enhancing the fairness and ethical integrity of AI systems. Li Qianling first explained that gender bias in AI systems is widespread, evident in areas such as facial recognition, voice assistants, and talent recruitment, due to biased training data, gender-stereotyped labeling, and predominantly male participation in development. She pointed out that women currently make up only about 20% of the AI workforce, resulting in a lack of female perspectives. Therefore, she recommended increasing investment in research on AI bias and enhancing women's participation to improve training processes and increase diversity.

Paper Review Session


To inspire public engagement in gender-related academic research, this forum specially included a paper review session. After rigorous discussion and selection, four representative academic papers were invited for presentation and review.


Paper Reviewer: Bi Yantao


Paper Reviewer: Li Yuling


Paper Reviewer: Shi Chenxia


Paper Reviewer: Zhu Hui


*Listed alphabetically by reviewer's surname


The first presented paper is "Reproductive Rights Under the Influence of American Party Politics" , which uses empirical research methods to analyze the relationship between U.S. politics and reproductive rights. Based on the context of diverging reproductive rights positions between the two major U.S. political parties influencing judicial decisions, the study conducted textual analysis on four representative reproductive rights cases and found that judicial rulings reflect party positions. Li Yuling, managing partner and head of the International Professional Committee at Han Sheng Law Firm and council member of AWDPI China, provided commentary, affirming the paper's framework and contributions while pointing out the need for stronger argumentation and logical coherence to enhance its academic quality. Subsequently, Zhu Hui, vice president of China Fine Wine Group and master's thesis supervisor at Donghua University, offered extensive constructive suggestions, encouraging the author to deepen work on case selection, theoretical explanation, and policy analysis to make the argument more comprehensive and make a deeper contribution to the reproductive rights discourse. Zhu Hui suggested further discussion on how government policies shape reproductive rights, clarifying the connotation and functional positioning of reproductive rights, expanding understanding of the basic structure and normativity of reproductive rights, or considering reproductive freedom as an entry point to discuss how to advance reproductive rights in legislation and practice.


The second presented paper is "Objectification and Empowerment: Chinese Females' Exploration of Western Dating Apps in the United Kingdom" , which uses qualitative methods to analyze Chinese women's experiences and feelings when using dating apps in cross-cultural contexts, finding that women experience both objectification and partial empowerment when interacting with white Western men. In the review, Li Yuling highly praised the paper's structure and argumentation, offering several detailed suggestions for improvement. Zhu Hui noted that the social issue of women's objectification addressed in the paper is highly significant, as it affects not only women's self-perception but also broader societal gender attitudes. Zhu Hui suggested the author deepen the analysis of the sociocultural impacts of objectification and provide recommendations for changing the status quo and promoting gender equality, making the paper more complete and forward-looking.


The third presented paper is "Under the Pendulum—A Labor Game That Manufactures Gender" , which, based on gendered labor theory, attempts to analyze multiple power dynamics and gender tensions by describing the entire labor process of female delivery riders, finding that female riders use emotional labor to reverse gender norms and gain subjectivity. In the review, Shi Chenxia, associate editor and associate researcher at the Journal of International Affairs, Shanghai Institute of International Studies, and postdoctoral researcher at Fudan University's School of Politics, affirmed the significance of the paper's topic and offered constructive suggestions on its framework and key points. Shi Chenxia also suggested that the analysis could delve deeper into root causes, such as the specific pathways through which patriarchy exerts influence. Subsequently, Bi Yantao, professor at the School of International Communications, Hainan University, provided commentary. Bi Yantao praised the paper's critical thinking and broad perspective, which offer new insights into the situation of female delivery riders. Additionally, Bi Yantao suggested incorporating interdisciplinary perspectives such as anthropology in the analytical framework and enhancing persuasiveness by supplementing details such as interviews and survey methods.


The fourth presented paper is "The Menstrual Blood Hidden Online—The Stigmatization of Menstruation Under Chinese Patriarchy" , which analyzes the characteristics of Weibo comments on the high-speed rail sanitary pad incident and the specific content of both supportive and opposing viewpoints. The paper points out that, at the level of discursive practice, opposing views reflect moral rigorism and misogynistic thought. It also analyzes the gendering of public and private spaces at the level of social practice, arguing that the debate reflects women's attempts to claim power in public spaces. Shi Chenxia reviewed first, suggesting further refinement in sample selection, data presentation, and literature review to make the argumentation more systematic and rigorous. In the following review, Bi Yantao affirmed the paper's social value, suggesting in terms of analytical perspective that the influence of other factors such as different cultural backgrounds on attitudes toward women's power be considered, and in terms of research methodology, recommending the addition of more representative samples, clarification of sample selection criteria, and explanation of the reliability of comment coding.

After two days of rich content and interaction, the forum concluded successfully. Experts and scholars from various fields offered a feast of ideas and wisdom. Their extensive life experiences, professional skills, and passion for protecting women's rights have advanced the process of gender equality. We believe that every Asian woman possesses infinite potential. We hope the insights gained from this forum will continue to empower each audience member in their future lives and promote the development of every Asian woman. Although this forum has concluded, it also marks a new beginning. We will continue our efforts on the path toward gender equality.
