"The law is an organic thing. It is defined by us. It is built by us, based on our experiences and the experiences of all of us, so there can be no more excuses. This must change, because the truth is, one in three women is sexually assaulted, and their voices—they need to be heard, they need to be believed, so that justice can be served."

 

"Somewhere, at some point, no matter what, something has to change." —*Prima Facie*, 2022.


Tessa is a barrister at a London law firm. Coming from a working-class background in Liverpool, she has fought her way to success in the male-dominated legal profession. A brilliant and formidable courtroom advocate, she deeply believes in the justice delivered by the British legal system—until…


It was an ordinary evening after work. Tessa had just won a case and decided to celebrate with a (secret) romantic interest from her firm. After several drinks, Tessa suddenly felt too drunk and physically unwell, and refused to continue the date. But her companion did not agree, and forcibly had sex with her. After the assault, Tessa felt both physical pain and a deep violation of her dignity. Strong-willed as she was, she decided to sue her male colleague. She wanted to win another victory—but when the plaintiff seeking justice in court became herself, she realized that the law does not treat everyone equally. The towering system she once believed in collapsed before her eyes. When the law can no longer protect her, what can Tessa still believe in?


The above story comes from *Prima Facie*, a critically acclaimed West End play that has won multiple international theatre awards including the Olivier and Tony Awards. Written by Suzie Miller and starring Jodie Comer (known for *Killing Eve* and *Free Guy*), the play premiered in London's West End in 2022. After receiving an enthusiastic response, it was recorded by the UK's National Theatre Live (NT LIVE) and released in cinemas across Britain, becoming the highest-grossing NT LIVE production in the UK. Through a 90-minute one-act, one-woman performance, *Prima Facie* shines the spotlight directly on "Tessa," and through Jodie Comer's powerful performance, allows audiences to hear the voices of female survivors—voices once silenced in courtrooms and public spaces—from the perspective of a female barrister who has been assaulted. The audience gradually uncovers the systemic flaws in the justice system regarding sexual assault cases, as well as the secondary trauma and structural oppression survivors face.


When sexual consent becomes a courtroom "Rashomon"

The sharpest indictment in *Prima Facie* lies in its focus on "sexual consent." In the play, after experiencing sexual assault, the female barrister Tessa attempts to seek justice through legal channels. Yet she quickly discovers how narrow and rigid the judicial system's understanding of "consent" truly is. "Why didn't you say no?" "Why were you wearing a skirt?" "Why did you drink so much?" "Why didn't you report it immediately?" Tessa finds herself trapped in a vicious cycle of proving she was a "perfect victim." Proving that she did not give sexual consent becomes an almost impossible task. The legal system's definition of consent seems overly dependent on verbal affirmation, while ignoring real-life factors such as power dynamics, social roles, alcohol, pressure, and fear, which often prevent victims from saying "no."

 

At the same time, perpetrators can use reasons such as "what she was wearing," "that she drank with me," or "that we had dated and had sex before" to directly claim they had obtained consent. "Why were you wearing a skirt?" "Why did you drink so much?"—as if a victim dressing up slightly or having a friendly private relationship with the defendant could be interpreted as an "invitation" to sex, and could justify the defendant's "mistaken" interpretation of the victim's "signals." Gender discrimination and power imbalances within the legal profession further make it difficult for women to pursue justice. In court, these seemingly "reasonable" questions not only inflict secondary psychological trauma on victims, but are also used to attack their character as "proper women," forcing them to struggle in the quagmire of proving their own innocence.

 

*Prima Facie* is more than just a play. Through a first-person female perspective, it shines a spotlight on the voices of victims once buried in courtrooms. Tessa's deafening cry—both a criminal defense lawyer and a survivor—forces audiences to re-examine the legal definition of "sexual consent," reflect on gender bias within authoritative judicial systems, and envision a safer, fairer society for the future.

The air begins to shift; all things sprout and grow as yang energy rises. 


March 5th—let us make a pact for justice

On March 5th, let us step into the cinema, into the theatre, and together witness this profound discussion on "sexual consent." We have invited outstanding female guests from the media and legal fields to watch the screening of *Prima Facie* with you, and after the screening, we welcome everyone to join the discussion with our guests on multi-perspective interpretations of the play, as well as on issues such as sexual assault cases, the justice system, and women's rights. AWDPI

Asian Women Development Plan International





AWDPI (Asian Women Development Plan International) is an international nonprofit organization dedicated to eliminating discrimination and gender-based violence against Asian women living overseas, and promoting their empowerment and development. It evolved from Avoice (Overseas Chinese Women Protection Project, Against Violence to Overseas Chinese Women Program), established in 2020. AWDPI operates in multiple countries and regions across Europe, North America, and the Asia-Pacific, with National Office teams established and registered as charitable organizations in the UK, Belgium, and the United States. AWDPI has built a broad network of partnerships with various sectors, including United Nations system organizations, relevant government departments of different countries, international organizations, multinational corporations, and academic institutions. AWDPI advocates for greater attention to the survival challenges faced by Asian women overseas and provides them with sustained protection and support. AWDPI aims to create more opportunities for Asian women overseas to develop in social, economic, cultural, and educational fields, helping them transform from marginalized groups into a vital force driving social progress.
