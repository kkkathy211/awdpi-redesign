02.

Event Introduction



For Asian women living overseas, language often becomes the first barrier they face when seeking social support after experiencing domestic or gender-based violence. Within Australia's legal and community service systems, how does one Chinese woman stand as a linguistic bridge connecting institutions and individuals amid silence and breakdown? As a professional interpreter with over two decades of experience in translation and human rights, Jingjing Lipski uses language to create the possibility of being heard for victims, while also raising authentic and resolute voices for Asian women on the social margins.


In this forum, the AWDPI Australia National Office warmly invites Jingjing to share the motivations and journey that led her into the field of interpreting, as well as her experiences navigating victims' emotional breakdowns and systemic blind spots in cases involving domestic violence, all while steadfastly upholding her professional responsibilities. As the only Chinese interpreter to have received the Liberty Victoria Voltaire Award, she will also reflect on the significance behind this honor and explore how interpreting can evolve from a "technical role" into a form of social responsibility.


The event will also focus on the complex realities faced by Asian women at the intersection of gender, culture, and language. Amidst cultural taboos and family pressures, how does Jingjing understand the roots of "silence," and how does she encourage more women to "speak up"? Drawing from her experience, we will also examine institutional blind spots in Australia's current interpreting system when serving ethnic minority women, and discuss how interpreting can become genuinely effective support rather than a procedural and superficial service.


This is not merely a professional interview about translation, but also a profound conversation about trauma, empathy, and women's lived experiences Through Jingjing's perspective, we will re-examine the weight of language and reconsider the rights and place Asian women deserve in Australian society.
