Arriving in a foreign country,

we may encounter many "firsts."

The first time chatting with someone in a foreign language,

the first time attending a gathering of strangers,

the first time handling life's problems alone.

Sometimes, we can't help but wonder:

If you've ever had these thoughts, you're not alone.


What is social anxiety?


Social anxiety is more than just "shyness."

It may show up as

feeling extremely nervous before speaking with others,

constantly replaying what you just said,

being overly concerned about how others perceive you,

wanting to join activities but not going because of fear,

fearing mistakes, rejection, or judgment.

But please remember,

feeling nervous doesn't mean you're not good enough.


When we enter an unfamiliar cultural environment,

we might worry:

"I don't understand the local culture,"
"My English isn't good enough,"
 "It's hard for me to fit into new social circles,"
 "Will others misunderstand me?"

These feelings are completely normal.

Give yourself some time.

You don't need to fit in with everyone on day one.



What is "interpersonal boundary"?


Simply put: A boundary is about "what makes me comfortable, and what makes me uncomfortable." Healthy interpersonal boundaries can help us:


✅ Protect our time and energy,

✅ Express our true feelings,

✅ Reduce unnecessary stress,

✅ Build healthier relationships,

✅ Learn to respect ourselves and others.


You can try saying things like:

"I need some time for myself right now,"

"Thank you, but I can't do this this time,"

"Let me think about it and get back to you,"

"I'd rather not talk about this topic."

……



Cultural differences don't mean someone dislikes you.

Some people are used to speaking directly,

while others prefer to be more indirect.

Some enjoy frequent conversations,

while others value personal space more.

So when someone communicates differently from you,

don't immediately tell yourself,

"Maybe it's just that

our cultures and communication styles are different."


Don't pressure yourself to

 "become socially confident overnight!"

You can start with one small thing:

Saying "Good morning!" first,

Having a cup of coffee with a classmate,

Asking a simple question,

Meeting one new person at an event.

You don't have to reply to every message immediately.

……

Please remember,

you don't need to be liked by everyone.

You can go at your own pace.

You can say "no."

You can have your own space.

You can choose who you want to be friends with.

You can also walk away from relationships that make you uncomfortable.

Healthy relationships should never require you to constantly sacrifice yourself.
