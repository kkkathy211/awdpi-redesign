Introduction to "Her Mountain, Myriad Paths"

"Her Mountain, Myriad Paths" is an interview initiative launched by the AWDPI Curriculum Development Division member interview program. The name originates from our reinterpretation of the word "mountain": a "mountain" can symbolize separation, pressure, or an insurmountable obstacle. But today, we say: I am the mountain. There is no need to look up, no need to cling—women themselves are majestic, independent, and courageous beings. "Myriad paths" represents the unique growth trajectories and life choices of each woman.


"Her Mountain, Myriad Paths" aims to showcase the rich diversity of life stories within AWDPI's community of women, making different narratives visible and honoring diverse choices. Through one-on-one in-depth conversations, we document real, vivid lives that follow different paths. We believe it is precisely these varied journeys that form the complete picture of women's strength—wherever we go, we can find connection and inspiration in each other's experiences, gaining support and insight through sharing. Here, some climb to the peak, some linger halfway up, some plant flowers at the foot of the mountain. Though our paths differ, this does not prevent us from seeing each other and encouraging one another.


This is not only a testament to members' personal growth, but also a dialogue soon to be opened to the public. We hope to invite more companions to listen, ask questions, converse, and connect—to see reflections of our own paths in each other's stories.


Tracing myriad paths, we will eventually meet; among blossoming mountain flowers, we encounter you.



About Siyu


Qiaoqiao's introduction to Siyu:


Turning what you love into your work, turning what you dream of into reality, and fitting different passions into one life—this may sound like an ideal, high-energy lifestyle. When I first heard Siyu describe her life, I struggled to sort through my thoughts, counting how many main quests, side quests, and even dynamic missions were running simultaneously in her life.


Siyu's story unfolds precisely at these intersections. From her high school fascination with psychology, to cross-disciplinary study and professional training, and eventually stepping into the counseling room, she has gradually transformed her initial interest into a career. At the same time, she is building a mental health public welfare platform, exploring entrepreneurship, developing her personal brand, and integrating handmade creation and life aesthetics into daily living.


The Siyu I know, however, does not seem to fit the conventional image of an "entrepreneur." Amid countless responsibilities, she remains like a still pond—calm, inclusive, stable, yet also very playful and easygoing. Psychological counseling requires professionalism, judgment, and boundaries, as well as sensitivity to emotional and human complexity; entrepreneurship demands confronting costs, markets, and real-world constraints, while personal passions are not always clearly planable or measurable. In Siyu's experience, these seemingly different demands coexist. Beyond providing counseling to clients, Siyu hopes to build a self-care and mutual support platform for psychotherapists. Though we may not have perfect answers yet, it is important to allow space for not having answers—for now.




Therefore, through this interview, we want to discuss how passion moves from interest to practice, how career choices gradually become clear through action, and how one person can hold different ways of thinking and life possibilities at once. At the same time, we will also talk about invisible challenges within the psychological counseling industry, public misconceptions about this field, and the importance of self-care for helpers.
