On the occasion of International Women's Day, UN Women calls on all stakeholders to support the rights, empowerment, and equality of women and girls.

March 6, 2025


   Globally, the rights of women and girls face unprecedented threats: discrimination is intensifying, legal protections are weakening, and funding for programs and institutions that support and protect women is dwindling.


    UN Women's latest report, "Review of Women's Rights 30 Years After the Beijing World Conference on Women," released on March 8 during the 50th United Nations International Women's Day, reveals that in 2024 nearly one in four governments reported setbacks in women's rights.

    Despite significant progress, only 87 countries have ever been led by women, and every ten minutes a woman or girl is killed by an intimate partner or family member. Digital technologies and artificial intelligence are spreading harmful gender stereotypes, while the digital gender divide limits women's employment opportunities.


    Over the past decade, the number of women and girls living in conflict zones has increased by 50%, a deeply troubling trend. Women's rights defenders face harassment, physical attacks, and even death threats every day. Recent global crises—such as the COVID-19 pandemic, climate emergencies, and soaring food and fuel prices—have intensified the urgency of addressing these issues.



    UN Secretary-General António Guterres said: "When women and girls can rise, we all prosper together. Yet globally, women's human rights are under attack. We are not witnessing equality becoming mainstream, but rather misogyny gaining ground. We must stand united and resolutely achieve human rights, equality, and empowerment for all women and girls, everywhere, and for all people."


    Sima Bahous, Executive Director of UN Women, emphasized: "UN Women is committed to ensuring that all women and girls, wherever they are, can fully enjoy their rights and freedoms. Gender equality and women's empowerment face complex challenges, but we will move forward with unwavering ambition and determination. Women and girls are calling for change—this is their rightful demand."


    2025 marks 30 years since the adoption of the landmark Beijing Declaration and Platform for Action at the Fourth World Conference on Women. Based on feedback from 159 governments, this review report highlights progress that must be recognized: gender parity in girls' education has been achieved since 1995, maternal mortality has declined by one-third, women's representation in parliaments has more than doubled, and countries continue to repeal discriminatory laws. Between 1995 and 2024, 189 countries and regions carried out 1,531 legal reforms. The report shows that when women's rights are fully protected in their countries, families, communities, and economies thrive.


    However, significant efforts are still needed to achieve gender equality and bring us closer to the goals of the 2030 Agenda for Sustainable Development. The report proposes a new Beijing+30 Action Agenda, an ambitious roadmap to complete our unfinished business by focusing on the following areas:


  1. A Digital Revolution for All Women and Girls: We must ensure equal access to technology, empower women and girls to lead in artificial intelligence and digital innovation, and safeguard their online safety and privacy.


  2. Ending Poverty: Investments are needed in comprehensive social protection, universal health coverage, education, and robust care systems to help women and girls thrive, and to create millions of green and decent jobs.


  3. Zero Violence: Countries must adopt and implement legislation to end all forms of violence against women and girls, and develop well-resourced programs, including support for community organizations on the front lines of response and prevention.


  4. Full Equality in Decision-Making: In this regard, temporary special measures such as gender quotas have proven effective in rapidly increasing women's participation.


  5. Peace and Security: Adequate funding for national action plans on women, peace and security, and for gender-responsive humanitarian aid, is critical. Women's organizations on the front lines are often first responders in crises and must receive dedicated, sustained funding to build lasting peace.


  6. Climate Justice: The rights of women and girls in climate adaptation must be prioritized, their leadership and knowledge centered, and they must benefit from new green jobs.



    Placing young women and girls at the heart of these six actions is the best way to ensure success both now and in the future. The newly proposed six actions hold the potential to drive breakthrough progress for women's rights and put us back on track toward the 2030 Sustainable Development Goals.


    The Beijing+30 Action Agenda and the upcoming 69th session of the UN Commission on the Status of Women present a valuable opportunity to integrate this action agenda into national policies, regional strategies, and global agreements.


    For women and girls, 2025 is a pivotal year—and a year of unprecedented setbacks and crises. Let us advance women's rights and create a world where all women and girls enjoy equal rights and opportunities. Let us be the first generation to live in an equal world.
