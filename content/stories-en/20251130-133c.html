In July 2025, Asian Women Development Plan International (AWDPI) as an official international partner, was invited to attend the 35th Global Women's Summit, bringing a firm and clear voice for Asian women to Berlin. From high-level dialogues to thematic workshops, from cultural resilience to technology empowerment, AWDPI not only showcased the strength and potential of Asian women, but also joined hands with global partners to launch a new chapter—from "being seen" to "co-creating" together.


"In Berlin, the air in July carried the scent of hope. At the venue of the 35th Global Women's Summit, as a representative of AWDPI (Asian Women Development Plan International), I stood on this stage hailed as the 'Davos for Women' and for the first time felt so clearly: our voices as Asian women are now being truly heard by the world."

—Sylvie, Head of Partnership Development, AWDPI Sylvie



In an era when global gender issues are receiving increasing attention, the presence of Asian women is often obscured within collective narratives. Our struggles, breakthroughs, and contributions need to be fully seen. This is precisely the significance of AWDPI's presence in Berlin—we are not merely attending; we are here to rebuild the narrative for Asian women.

✦

The Berlin Moment: When sincerity meets understanding

✦


"Seven minutes with Lagarde spoke louder than a thousand words"


In front of Christine Lagarde, President of the European Central Bank, we shared AWDPI's practices in combating domestic violence and discrimination, vocational skills training, and building women's community networks, committed to creating more equitable employment opportunities for Asian women overseas. "This is not just charity," I said, "it is real empowerment." She listened intently and responded: "Asian women need platforms like yours. Tell me, what kind of support do you need most?" Those brief seven minutes showed me that international leaders are beginning to view the power of Asian women with fresh eyes.






✦

Rethinking possibilities through tech dialogue

✦


The conversation with Michael Schiebe, CEO of Mercedes-Benz AMG, was equally inspiring. "We are not asking for special treatment," I said, "but to bring different perspectives into the field of technology." We discussed not only the representation of women in high-tech industries, but also how to bring Asian women into decision-making roles, not just execution roles.



✦

Reviving traditional crafts

✦


During the panel discussion "Cultural Resilience: The Digital Rebirth of Traditional Crafts," I proposed "activating intangible cultural heritage through curatorial thinking and integrating it into pathways for women's economic empowerment." This idea received high praise from UN representatives and delegates from multiple countries. After the session, an African delegate approached me and shared: "We have such women too. We need this kind of collaboration." At that moment, I realized that genuine resonance never needs to be shouted.






✦

From resonance to action: the commitments we co-wrote

✦


Summit Chair Irene Natividad particularly affirmed AWDPI's unique value: "You are not just another women's organization—you are a bridge."



This recognition is now turning into concrete collaborations:

  • partnering with Germany's KaDeWe to launch an incubation exhibition for Asian women designers, creating long-term market access

  • co-founding the "Dubai Women in Innovation" program with UAE's Women in Tech

  • advancing cross-regional cooperation in women's education and social innovation


These are not just letters of intent, but tangible entry points for Asian women onto the global stage.



✦

Voices: moving forward with the trust of countless "shes"

✦


We carried the trust of countless "shes" with us throughout every moment of the summit. When I spoke, I represented not only AWDPI, but also the women silently striving in factories, in fields, in offices, and in homes. They may never stand on an international stage, but their stories deserve to be heard just the same.




The conversation with Lagarde, the exchanges with delegates from around the world, the interventions in panel discussions—these seemingly personal moments actually belong to every woman shining in her own field.





✦

Ahead: from "being seen" to "co-creating"

✦


Berlin is only the beginning. AWDPI refuses to become yet another "symbolic presence." We are committed to driving real, sustainable change:


  • accompanying women survivors of violence as they step out of the shadows and rebuild their lives


  • helping immigrant women gain the vocational skills to stand firmly in society


  • ensuring Asian women's voices are truly included in international agendas




On the flight home, I opened my notebook and recalled the words of a Cambodian female artist: "We are not trying to squeeze into an existing world—we want to co-create a broader world with it."

"Strong alone, unstoppable together." These words gained new meaning in Berlin. Our individual strength may be small, but when we connect, we form an unstoppable force.


This trip to Berlin placed AWDPI on the world stage—not just as participants, but as drivers of change. We believe: every single "she" deserves to be seen by the entire world. This time, we witnessed the "power of dialogue." Through each exchange and act of listening, we gained three profound insights:


First, the power of being seen.
The experiences, creativity, and resilience of Asian women are now being taken seriously by the world. When we speak in our own language, from our own positions and through our own actions, we are no longer a group "represented" by others, but actors who can shape agendas and define the future.

Second, the power of connection.
From the European Central Bank to international organizations, from corporate leaders to grassroots representatives, we crossed geographical and cultural boundaries to find a shared language—about respect, equality, and co-creation. These new connections with AWDPI are growing into a collaborative network that will open doors to the world for more women.

Third, the power of action.
Every speech, every meeting, is planting seeds of change. With every new partnership, AWDPI is turning ideas into reality, making "women's protection and empowerment" a sustainable reality.
Though the stage in Berlin has closed, the chapter for Asian women continues to be written.
What we bring back is not just medals and applause, but a firmer conviction—

to create a world of love and equality for women

AWDPI will continue moving forward, transforming the echoes from Berlin into deeper, more lasting global action.
