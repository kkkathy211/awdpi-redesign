After graduating from university, Lusina worked as a Chinese teacher in Malaysia for three and a half years before moving to Scotland in 2019. She loved the small green city with its relaxed pace, and the kindness, simplicity, and genuine humour of the local Scots made her feel a sense of "home." While the long, cold, and depressing Scottish winters didn't deter her, it was her hope of spending the rest of her life with her then-boyfriend in Scotland that made her choose to renew her contract and stay.


When Love Blooms, Dreams Shatter



In May 2020, Lusina met her ex-boyfriend S (pseudonym) at an outdoor climbing area. She was instantly drawn to his athletic build and shy personality. After taking the initiative to strike up a conversation and exchange contact information, they began dating and soon became a couple, quickly moving in together.


The early days of their relationship were always sweet. As the world was engulfed by the pandemic, he cooked while she washed the dishes; he taught her rock climbing, and she taught him Chinese; she watched his favourite films and TV shows and met his family; they went hiking on weekends and travelled during holidays, walking hand in hand through every corner of Scotland.

After moving to the neighbourhood where S lived, Lusina experienced racial discrimination attacks from a few teenagers. S's strong, protective response made Lusina, far from home, feel she finally had someone to rely on. Wanting Lusina to be able to defend herself if physically attacked, S took her to train in Krav Maga (Israeli self-defence/martial art). S, who had earned a 5th-degree black belt early on, patiently sparred with Lusina during class and simulated real-life scenarios as much as possible during private training. Sometimes S went too hard, and Lusina would throw a tantrum. S, genuinely wanting her to master street defence, was always baffled by her playful attitude. While touched by S's good intentions, Lusina also dismissed his serious, over-the-top approach. But Lusina never imagined that the first near-death experience she would face would come from S, the very person who had repeatedly vowed to protect her.


Not long into the relationship, S told Lusina he was against marriage—a shock to her. At first, she didn't understand what being anti-marriage meant. Though she couldn't grasp S's reasoning, she spent a long time coming to terms with this reality, secretly hoping she could change his mind. Gradually, S began displaying some baffling behaviours: falling silent for over ten hours during trips if slightly upset; leaving restaurants without warning and going offline; suddenly telling Lusina to move out without giving any reason; cutting off contact with his parents for over half a year, refusing to answer calls, reply to messages, or meet them; disappearing after drinking a whole bottle of alcohol in a cinema, returning home at 2 a.m. in a police car, completely drunk, then violently punching the sofa…


Because S had once provided Lusina with what felt like a hundred percent emotional value and boyfriend experience, his confusing behaviours doubled her disappointment.



S's passive communication style and drunken outbursts caused Lusina to accumulate more and more doubts. The longer they were together, the more "not wanting marriage" became a real-life problem. In the summer of 2022, Lusina went through a dark and painful period of indecision—whether to stay or leave. Staying meant her contract with her current school allowed her to remain for at most five years, and she was already in her third year, with little room for career advancement at the same workplace. Leaving was difficult too—due to the pandemic, job opportunities in China were prioritised for applicants already in the country. Because of S's refusal to compromise on marriage, Lusina, lacking long-term visa options as an "unmarried civil partner," found it hard to compete for any job.

(Note: An unmarried civil partner refers to a couple who, though not legally married, live together and form a long-term, stable partnership.)


Trapped with no way forward or back, sleepless night after night, crying from frustration, S only offered Lusina verbal comfort, unaware that she had already pictured leaving him countless times in her mind. S said, "I want to marry you, but I don't want to get married. I want to spend the rest of my life with you." But how could they spend the rest of their lives together? S refused to move to another country, and Lusina felt she had no way to stay in Scotland. Looking back, Lusina thinks she might have been stuck in a mental loop, constantly fixating on her difficult situation and her confusion toward her boyfriend. She didn't understand—she didn't necessarily need marriage, but if a marriage certificate could keep the person she loved by her side, what principle was so unchangeable?


Many times, Lusina tried to communicate, wanting to know S's plans for their future, but he either gave no response or drank to avoid the issue, refusing to talk. The longer they stayed together, the less happy they became.



Twisted Love, Silent Violence



Lusina issued a final ultimatum. Last October, after returning from a trip to Greece, she wrote a letter stating, "If we're not getting married, we're breaking up." Because a relationship with no visible future could no longer inspire her to love wholeheartedly. A few days later, while walking together, S for the first time gave in: "Fine, let's get married."


More shocked than thrilled, after repeatedly confirming S was serious, Lusina decided to observe how things unfolded. She asked when they would marry, and he said April. Lusina printed out the requirements for an international marriage—on the man's side, only a birth certificate was needed—but S didn't even glance at the documents and kept delaying going to his parents' house to get the certificate. Later, Lusina printed out the forms for an unmarried civil partnership to show she didn't necessarily insist on marriage, but S still refused to look at the documents or respond.


Later, they had another fight over holiday plans during Christmas. S told Lusina to stay home, threatening to break up if she went out… Faced with his accumulating patterns of heavy drinking, emotional outbursts, silent treatment, and psychological manipulation (PUA), Lusina had had enough of a relationship defined by constant non-responsiveness, and firmly decided to break up and move out.


Two weeks of silence—S's usual tactic—this time, Lusina joined the cold war. When S realised that drunken tantrums no longer worked, he finally attempted communication for the first time. He sincerely apologised, saying he had been under immense pressure, and even before the pandemic, before meeting Lusina, he had struggled with anxiety. He admitted he didn't know how to express himself, couldn't even open up to himself, and promised he would quit drinking and share his unhappy feelings…


But during a trip to Spain, S drank heavily again, claiming he had a cold, fever, and sore throat. He caused a scene at the airport, was denied boarding by the captain, and after taking a flight home the next day, drank again and went on another drunken rampage. Heartbroken, Lusina sent him an email terminating their lease and quickly arranged to stay with a friend.


What happened next was something Lusina never expected. She recalled: "One day, I came back from my friend's place with my suitcase. He was drunk and asked where I was moving to. I didn't respond. I ran to my room, sat on the bed, and started writing in my diary. He came in and started babbling nonsense, even saying he wanted to die. My phone was charging by the bed. When I picked it up to check messages, he suddenly grabbed the charging cable, wrapped it around my neck, and started strangling me. I don't remember how many seconds passed. I looked at him in disbelief, my breath growing weaker… He seemed to suddenly realise what he was doing, let go of the cable, and then pretended to have no memory. I quickly took a selfie to preserve evidence, immediately called the police, but the local emergency line wasn't answered. He acted as if he didn't know why I was calling the police and snatched away my diary. Instinctively, I chased after my diary, which I'd used for nearly two years, but then thought, no—my life comes first. So amid our struggle, I ran away, crying, determined to leave."


Recalling the scene, Lusina still feels deeply terrified—"I never thought he would lay a hand on me, even when I was absolutely certain he wouldn't hurt me while drunk. In that moment, if he hadn't stopped, I would have been strangled to death."



Stepping Out of the Shadows, Embracing a New Life



The next day, Lusina quickly packed and moved out of S's place—leaving the source of domestic violence behind with firm resolve, she felt incredibly strong. But for those who have experienced domestic violence, the shadow lingers deep in memory, resurfacing in related life situations and striking again with full force. "Once, I saw a student playing with a rope and couldn't help feeling afraid…"


To recover from the shadow of domestic violence as quickly as possible, Lusina shared her experience with friends and sought advice, actively participated in social events and travel, and avoided being alone to prevent falling into negative states. At the same time, she kept writing in her diary as evidence, recording her emotional state and tracking her healing journey.


Regarding whether to press charges against S, Lusina went through a long and agonising internal struggle—on one hand, she wanted to forgive and make excuses for him: "Even though I'm the victim, he couldn't control his actions when drunk. He didn't know what he was doing. He was unconscious. The charging cable was just there, and he acted on impulse, instinctively strangling me." "He has mental health issues, but at his core, he's a good person." "He was drunk. He won't do the same thing again." On the other hand, she returned to reason and reality: "I must report this, because I can't let anyone think they can hurt me and face no consequences!" "Why do I keep making excuses for him? Even if drunk, no one should ever harm the person they love—or anyone else."


Lusina left Scotland and returned home for the first time in four years, feeling safe and loved once again. She still believes in love, but she won't throw herself into it recklessly anymore. Loving herself will always come first. If an uncomfortable relationship cannot improve, she will walk away in time and make the best choice for herself.


Although Lusina ultimately did not continue with reporting the incident, she shared her experience on multiple platforms to help others facing similar situations overcome their difficulties. Because she deeply understands that time passes, but the trauma caused by domestic violence does not. An instant of trauma may take a lifetime to heal, but choosing to bravely face it is the first step toward a new life.



Remove the filter, move away from harm



The scope of domestic violence is far broader than the physical assault we commonly associate with it. For example, the "Anti-Domestic Violence Law of the People's Republic of China" stipulates that domestic violence includes not only physical violence but also psychological harm through words, attitudes, or behaviors, such as verbal abuse, intimidation, threats, humiliation, control, and cold violence; it includes economic deprivation, such as restricting access to money or preventing employment or education; it includes social isolation, such as limiting contact with friends and family or prohibiting attendance at school or work; it includes sexual abuse, referring to forced, abusive, humiliating, or non-consensual acts within sexual relationships among family members; and it includes digital violence, referring to harming or controlling family members through digital technology means, such as online bullying, surveillance, or malicious dissemination of personal information.


Violence within romantic or marital relationships is not equivalent to violence between strangers—it is far more complex and profound. Hatred born from love intertwines with lingering affection, forming a tightly woven rope that is difficult to untangle, making it hard to recognize and even harder to let go. At the same time, love often idealizes our perception of our partner. Because of this halo effect and filtering, we automatically screen out traits that might one day harm us, and even place our partner in a position where we believe they could never hurt us.


Because of love, we constantly look inward for the cause of problems, trying to fix things, even making excuses for the other person's violence and deceiving ourselves. When Lusina first met S, her initial impression was filled with words like "bright" and "positive." His athletic appearance and reserved personality created an unconscious contrast, making him seem uniquely charismatic to Lusina, who thus unconsciously placed a halo over him. In reality, S's silence and lack of communication concealed a passive and fragmented attitude toward life—yet his image as an outdoor sports enthusiast masked his vulnerability and destructive tendencies. Therefore, when selecting a partner, one should clearly identify any thoughts or traits in the other person that could potentially harm you. When problems arise, remove the filter and rationally and objectively analyze the traits the other person has revealed, maintaining zero tolerance toward violence.

Every small malicious act in human nature is like a seed—it sprouts with desire and ambition, nourished by negative emotions and jealousy. If not uprooted in time, one day this evil will grow into a towering tree, completely blocking the light in your life.


Learn to say "no" to every early sign of violent harm. Love itself is trust, but love does not grant the person you love the right to hurt you. Even if the harm is unintentional, we should not let it slide or passively accept it. We must learn to address it promptly and distance ourselves when necessary.
