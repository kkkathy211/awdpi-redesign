Introduction to "Her Mountain, Myriad Paths"

"Her Mountain, Myriad Paths" is an interview initiative launched by the AWDPI Curriculum Development Department member interview program. The name originates from our reinterpretation of the word "mountain": "mountain" can symbolize barriers, pressure, and insurmountable obstacles. But today, we say: I am the mountain. There is no need to look up, no need to cling—women themselves are majestic, independent, and courageous beings. "Myriad paths" represents the unique growth trajectories and life choices of each woman.


"Her Mountain, Myriad Paths" aims to showcase the rich diversity of life stories within AWDPI's community of women, making different narratives visible and honoring diverse choices. Through one-on-one in-depth conversations, we document real, vivid, and distinct female life journeys. We believe it is precisely these varied paths that form the complete picture of women's power—no matter where we go, we can find connection and inspiration in each other's experiences, gaining support and insight through sharing. Here, some climb to the peak, some linger halfway up, some plant flowers at the foot of the mountain. Though our paths differ, nothing prevents us from seeing each other and encouraging one another.


This is not only a testament to members' personal growth, but also a dialogue soon to be opened to the public. We look forward to inviting more companions to listen, ask questions, converse, and connect—through each other's stories, illuminating our own paths forward.


On myriad paths we search, yet shall meet at last; among blossoming mountain flowers, we encounter you.



About Dodo


Qiaoqiao's introduction to Dodo:


Everyone who meets Dodo feels the resilient and powerful vitality flowing through her bones, like water. When you look into her eyes, you can't help but believe: the world is her stage. And this is the perfect embodiment of the phrase "growing up means being gentle toward the world."


She jokingly says she has always taken the "unconventional yet righteous path." She doesn't act with strong goal-orientation, nor does she preset outcomes. For her, process and result are one; every step creates new rules of the game. Within these rules, there is no anxiety of over-competition, yet she never settles into complacency—she enjoys the state of constant forward motion.


From theater to applied theater, psychodrama, and then expressive arts therapy, Dodo's life has always been guided by "what I love, what I care about, what moves me." She refuses to be defined or packaged by others, firmly believing that the mindset of "giving possibility a chance" itself constructs meaning.


If you view her life experience as a game, you might see:

  • How to forge one's own professional path in challenging environments;

  • How to build meaning in life through arts-based healing;

  • How to move forward lightly, without struggling against the self, taking that first step with ease.



And the charm of this game lies not elsewhere, but in its "fun"—this very fun is a rare and precious "good thing" in life.
