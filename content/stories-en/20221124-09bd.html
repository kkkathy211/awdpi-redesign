#What is the 16 Days of Activism against Gender-Based Violence?


The annual United Nations "16 Days of Activism" against gender-based violence is approaching. This year's campaign, themed "Activism! UNiTE to end violence against women and girls," calls on more people to join efforts to eliminate gender-based violence and support women and girls worldwide in claiming their rights.


Every year, November 25 marks the UN's International Day for the Elimination of Violence against Women. The 16 Days of Activism begins on this day and continues until December 10, Human Rights Day. The campaign aims to mobilize society, the public, governments, and UN agencies to expand impact and encourage action to prevent and eliminate violence against women and girls (VAWG).

The Accused


Film Introduction:


If she's not a "perfect victim," does that justify rape?


In a tragic incident, Sarah (played by Jodie Foster) is gang-raped in a bar while bystander men remain indifferent or even join in and cheer. Traumatized physically and mentally, Sarah sues her attackers, but public opinion and even her friend who met her at the bar blame her for "inviting" the assault through her behavior. After the trial, the perpetrator receives only a nine-month sentence. The justice system fails her. Catherine, the female prosecutor who defended Sarah, becomes outraged upon seeing how the case unfolds.


Ultimately, driven by anger, Prosecutor Catherine decides to fight back. Through the persistence and efforts of both women, the perpetrators are finally punished. The two women expose the truth, restore dignity to women, and demonstrate the courage of the vulnerable.

Screening Series II

She Said: Moments in a Century of Women Snatches: Moments from Women's Lives


She Said: Moments in a Century of Women

Snatches: 

Moments from Women's Lives


Documentary Introduction:


Rights are not granted from the sky—we must be honest, express ourselves, fight, and shout. This documentary consists of 8 episodes, each 10–12 minutes long. In each episode, a woman narrates her life story in monologue form. The stories are inspired by real women who, between 1918 and 2018—the 100 years since women over 30 in the UK gained voting rights—bravely stood up against injustice and exposed gender discrimination. These stories touch on the Black civil rights movement, workplace sexual assault, marital rape, sexual liberation, judicial misjudgment, curfews on women, and racial discrimination.

Screening Series III The Dressmaker


The Dressmaker

The Dressmaker


Film Introduction:


Set in the 1950s in the fictional Australian town of Dungatar, Tilly Dunnage (played by Kate Winslet), a dressmaker trained in Europe, returns to her childhood hometown. As she steps off the train, smoking a cigarette, her every gesture exudes a powerful, mature charisma. Her return stirs gossip in the town. Labeled a "murderess," this "wicked woman" with revealing clothes and unconventional behavior provokes resentment and jealousy, yet her exquisite fashion skills and beautiful designs win the admiration of many women.


The isolated town lacks genuine care and understanding. People act on rumors and emotions, spreading gossip and judgment. Tilly's past is gradually revealed. Though never a murderer, she was falsely accused; despite the teacher's absurd testimony, her abandoned mother receives no sympathy; her beloved younger brother dies accidentally trying to prove she isn't cursed; a grieving mother is deceived by her husband; a security guard with cross-dressing tendencies hides his true self for fear of losing his job… The fates of the townspeople slowly come to light.


Beautiful clothing may enhance one's outward appearance, but it cannot change inner morality. The contrast between sartorial beauty and human cruelty is fully embodied in Tilly's story.


Fashion in this film serves both as a thematic enhancement and a narrative driver—every plot twist is inextricably linked to fashion.

Film Introduction:


All About My Mother, directed by Pedro Almodóvar and starring Cecilia Roth and Penélope Cruz, is a drama released in Spain on April 16, 1999. The film tells the story of Manuela, who, after her son Esteban dies in a car accident, embarks on a long journey to fulfill his final wish—to meet his father, Lola. In 2000, the film won the Golden Globe Award for Best Foreign Language Film and the Academy Award for Best Foreign Language Film.

Screening Series V Bombshell


Bombshell 

Bombshell


Film Introduction:


Based on true events, this film deeply portrays the dilemmas women face when balancing career advancement, the lure of power, and oppression from those in power. The three female leads represent women at different career stages and life circumstances confronting a shared challenge: when facing abuse of power and sexual harassment, do they choose compromise or resistance? Megan (played by Charlize Theron), the most powerful and influential figure; Gretchen Carlson (played by Nicole Kidman), a fading star host; and Kayla (played by Margot Robbie), an ambitious newcomer—each struggles, compromises, resists, and endures before ultimately making their choice in the face of sexual harassment by Fox News CEO Roger Ailes and then-presidential candidate Donald Trump.


This film explores how, despite an apparent natural alliance among women, the collective interest of the "female community" often gives way to personal choices driven by money and power.


Ailes, who ran Fox News, was also a key Republican figure who helped elect three U.S. presidents—Richard Nixon, Ronald Reagan, and George W. Bush—and advanced the careers of prominent hosts like Sean Hannity, Megyn Kelly, and Gretchen Carlson.


In 2016, Ailes was forced to resign after multiple women accused him of harassment, with Kelly and Carlson playing pivotal roles. The network attempted to pressure Kelly into publicly supporting Ailes, but she refused.

Screening Series VI She and Her Life

Shards of Her


She and Her Life

Shards of Her


Film Introduction:


After a car accident, career-driven Lin Chenxi (played by Hsu Wei-ning) wakes up seemingly in a parallel universe. Her boyfriend Li Haoming has become Officer Xiao Liu (played by Li Cheng-bin), who is investigating the murder of her high school chemistry teacher Xie Zhizhong (played by Chen Yi-wen). Her senior high school classmate Yan Shenghua (played by Alyssa Chia) becomes the prime suspect? As she pieces together her memories day by day, things feel increasingly off. Her seemingly happy family life and close friendship gradually unravel, revealing shocking truths behind the murder case.


She and Her Life begins with a murder, appearing at first to be a suspense thriller. As the protagonist enters different timelines, it seems to shift into science fiction. But as the story reaches its climax, the true core emerges—audiences realize it is, in fact, a "Fang Sijia" story. The parallel universes in the drama are actually imagined realities created by the protagonist suffering from dissociative disorder after trauma.


Beyond that, She and Her Life clearly exposes the pervasive misogynistic mechanisms in society—domestic violence, slut-shaming, workplace sexual harassment, and more. The series bravely and clearly lifts this veil, while sincerely and warmly offering hope for self-rebuilding to women who have been broken.
