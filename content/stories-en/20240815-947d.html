Introduction:


UN Women has called for an immediate humanitarian ceasefire in Gaza to protect women from the ravages of war—an urgent appeal that reminds us that women and children are often the most vulnerable victims in conflict. In politics, although the global proportion of women parliamentarians has risen slightly, women in political roles continue to face serious challenges, including gender discrimination and online harassment. A UNESCO report further reveals gender bias in generative artificial intelligence, highlighting that gender inequality persists even in high-tech fields.


On the other hand, crimes and acts of violence targeting Asians continue to occur around the world, drawing widespread attention and condemnation. In a series of attacks in Portland, Sydney, and Auckland, Asian victims have been targeted due to their ethnic background, showing that hate crimes against Asians have not been effectively curbed. These incidents have caused profound harm to victims and their families and exposed deep-seated racial discrimination in society. At the same time, the voice and influence of Asian communities are gradually growing stronger, with increasing numbers of Asians achieving outstanding success across various fields, promoting broader recognition and appreciation of Asian cultures. These phenomena reflect that while Asian communities still face challenges on the path to combating discrimination and fighting for equal rights, they also demonstrate their undeniable strength and contributions.

UN calls for immediate humanitarian ceasefire to protect women in Gaza


UN Women has recently issued an urgent appeal demanding an immediate humanitarian ceasefire in Gaza to prevent further harm to women from the war. It is estimated that around 9,000 women have been killed in Israeli military operations over the past five months since the conflict began, with the actual number of victims likely higher. UN Women emphasizes that women and children are disproportionately affected in conflict, with approximately 63 women dying each day—37 of them mothers—leading to the breakdown of family structures and children losing essential protection.


Gaza is currently facing a severe food security crisis, with about 2.3 million people on the brink of hunger. Assessments by UN Women show that food intake for most households has dropped to half or less of pre-war levels. Mothers and adult women, responsible for finding food within families, are often the last to eat or completely forgo meals. In 95% of cases, mothers sacrifice at least one of their own meals to ensure their children have food.


In addition, UN Women notes that 10 out of 12 women's organizations in Gaza are currently only partially operational, severely affecting support and protection efforts for local women. In its report, UN Women stresses that immediate action must be taken to achieve a ceasefire, stop killings and destruction, ensure humanitarian aid can enter Gaza without obstruction, and be effectively distributed locally, to prevent further loss of innocent lives and provide urgently needed support and assistance to women in Gaza.

Global proportion of women parliamentarians shows slight increase


Latest data from UN Women shows that the global proportion of women parliamentarians rose to 26.9% in 2023, an increase of 0.4 percentage points from the previous year. This growth reflects a positive trend in women's participation in global politics.


At the regional level, the Americas lead with a women parliamentarian rate of 42.5%. Additionally, the proportion of women speakers of parliament globally has risen to 23.8%, with Cambodia and Côte d'Ivoire electing their first female speakers, signaling breakthroughs for women in political leadership.


Despite significant progress, gender issues remained central in 2023 elections. Abortion rights in Poland and Argentina sparked intense debate and became key campaign issues. At the same time, women in politics face burnout and online harassment, with some prominent female leaders stepping down from public office, highlighting the challenges on the path to gender equality.


UN Women calls on the international community to take strong measures to ensure women's equal participation and safety in politics, including promoting the implementation of quota systems and protecting women politicians from harassment and violence. Through these efforts, the goal is to build a more inclusive and balanced political environment where women's voices and leadership are widely recognized and respected.

UNESCO report reveals generative artificial intelligence exacerbates gender bias


A report released by UNESCO ahead of International Women's Day reveals that large language models (LLMs) commonly exhibit gender bias and stereotypes, especially in depictions of household labor and professional roles. Models such as OpenAI's GPT series and Meta's Llama 2 tend to associate women with domestic activities and men with business and professional roles. UNESCO Director-General Audrey Azoulay warns that even minor gender biases in AI can lead to significant inequalities in the real world.


The report emphasizes that while open-source models are more accessible for academic collaboration to correct biases, they still show negative tendencies when handling content related to LGBTQ+ communities and specific ethnic groups. Gender imbalance in AI development teams—with fewer women in technical and AI research roles—affects the diversity and fairness of these systems.


To address these issues, UNESCO calls for the establishment of regulatory frameworks and encourages private companies to monitor and evaluate bias in AI systems. The "Recommendation on the Ethics of Artificial Intelligence," adopted in 2021, provides a global normative framework for the AI field. In February 2024, tech companies including Microsoft expressed support and committed to taking action to promote gender equality.

Multi-stakeholder partnership meeting held during the 68th session of the UN Commission on the Status of Women to jointly address challenges to gender equality


In 2024, over 2 billion voters worldwide will participate in national and local elections, bringing gender equality once again into the international spotlight. In light of this, UN Women held a conference titled "Advancing Gender Equality: Multi-Stakeholder Partnerships and Practices" on March 20 at its headquarters in New York, aiming to bring together diverse stakeholders to jointly explore effective ways to address the backlash against gender equality.


The meeting was chaired by Ms. Sima Bahous, Executive Director of UN Women, who in her opening remarks pointed out that gender equality worldwide is currently facing serious challenges, with long-established human rights principles being questioned and progress in women's political, economic, and social rights at risk of reversal. Despite this, women's rights advocates, civil society organizations, government officials, and international institutions continue to take active steps to advance gender equality and contribute to achieving the Sustainable Development Goals.


During the meeting, representatives from the United Nations Development Programme, the United Nations Population Fund, and the Office of the United Nations High Commissioner for Human Rights attended and presented recommendations for strengthening gender equality through collective efforts. Participants included government officials, civil society representatives, and academics from around the world, who shared experiences and strategies in advancing gender equality, covering practical cases in areas such as legislative reform, public policy development, and social movements.


One highlight of the meeting was a presentation by a representative from Tanzania on measures to protect women's political participation at the legislative and policy levels. A representative from India shared successful experiences in enhancing young women leaders' capabilities and influence in electoral processes through training, while representatives from Slovenia and Monaco emphasized the importance of international cooperation in advancing gender equality. In addition, the meeting particularly highlighted the positive role of sports in promoting gender equality, noting that sports activities can not only elevate women's social status but also provide young women with platforms to showcase their talents and build confidence. Participants unanimously agreed that only through global cooperation and solidarity can the challenges to gender equality be effectively addressed and true gender equality achieved.

UN human rights experts express concern over forced religious conversion and marriage of young women from minority communities in Pakistan


[April 11, 2024] — Several United Nations human rights experts today voiced serious concern about the forced religious conversion and marriage of young women and girls from minority communities in Pakistan. The experts noted that girls from Christian and Hindu backgrounds are particularly vulnerable to forced religious conversion, abduction, trafficking, child marriage, early marriage, forced marriage, domestic servitude, and sexual violence. These grave human rights violations against young women from religious minorities and the prevailing culture of impunity must be stopped and can no longer be tolerated or justified. The experts emphasized that acts of forced marriage and religious conversion are often condoned by courts, which frequently invoke religious law to leave victims with their abductors, while perpetrators often escape accountability, and police dismiss crimes as "love marriages."


The experts called on Pakistani authorities to strictly enforce laws, ensuring that marriages are only valid when both parties give voluntary consent, and to raise the minimum age of marriage to 18. They stressed that all women and girls, including those from Christian, Hindu, and other religious backgrounds, must be treated equally and without discrimination. The experts urged Pakistan to hold perpetrators accountable, enforce existing laws against child, early, and forced marriage, abduction, and trafficking of minority girls, and fulfill its international human rights obligations.

United Nations reviews progress and challenges 30 years after International Conference on Population and Development


According to the United Nations, on April 29, 2024, during a commemoration of the 30th anniversary of the International Conference on Population and Development held in Cairo, UN General Assembly President Dennis Francis emphasized that global sustainable development will remain stalled unless countries take further measures to bridge gender gaps and reduce poverty and inequality. In 1994, 179 countries adopted a Programme of Action that placed reproductive rights, gender equality, and the empowerment of women, girls, and young people at the heart of global development.


Francis noted that while significant progress has been made in poverty reduction, life expectancy, and food security, progress remains uneven within and between countries, and crises such as climate change and conflict threaten these gains. He called on countries to adopt innovative approaches to overcome challenges in implementing the Programme of Action and reaffirmed the importance of empowering women and girls for achieving peaceful, prosperous, and sustainable societies.


UN Deputy Secretary-General Amina Mohammed, speaking at the 57th session of the United Nations Commission on Population and Development, called for using the anniversary of the Programme of Action as an opportunity to accelerate action and define priorities for the next 30 years. She noted that despite major progress, many countries have not yet met life expectancy targets, and 164 million women of reproductive age worldwide lack access to family planning services. Mohammed stressed that the international community must address the rollback of sexual and reproductive health rights, uphold the dignity of all, ensure no one is left behind, and support rights-based population and development policies.

Gender equality and youth aspirations: Key to achieving sustainable peace


According to senior United Nations officials from the Department of Political and Peacebuilding Affairs on May 28, achieving gender equality and fulfilling youth aspirations are crucial to ensuring sustainable peace. During an open debate on the roles of women and youth, UN Under-Secretary-General Rosemary DiCarlo emphasized that in the context of a global rollback of human rights, women are bearing the brunt of major challenges—especially in Afghanistan, where women have been deprived of basic rights such as education and employment. She called on the international community to support women in realizing equal rights so they can contribute to their nations' futures.


DiCarlo pointed out that Afghanistan is not an isolated case—gender equality is being neglected or even reversed in many regions, and the Secretary-General's "New Agenda for Peace" calls for dismantling patriarchal structures and ensuring equal opportunities for women. She stressed that eliminating structural gender inequality is not only a matter of fairness and rights, but also an effective means of increasing the success of peace and political processes. UN political missions have been actively working to promote women's rights and their meaningful participation in peace processes.


DiCarlo also emphasized the importance of youth inclusion in peace processes, noting that young people already possess the hope, ambition, and energy to contribute. She urged governments to seize opportunities to involve youth in peace processes, rebuild trust in institutions, and strengthen intergenerational solidarity. The United Nations is allocating resources from the Peacebuilding Fund to support national youth, peace, and security agendas and is advancing youth, peace, and security strategies in the African Union and the Arab region. DiCarlo stressed that achieving gender equality and youth aspirations is essential for global sustainable peace and security, and called on the Security Council to support shared responsibility between women and men in building peace and prosperity.

Saudi women's rights activist sentenced to 11 years in prison for supporting women's rights


According to The Guardian on May 1, Saudi women's rights activist Manahel al-Otaibi was secretly sentenced to 11 years in prison by an anti-terrorism court for "choice of clothing and support for women's rights." Saudi officials confirmed to the Office of the United Nations High Commissioner for Human Rights that al-Otaibi was sentenced on January 9, 2024, on charges of so-called "terrorism offenses." She is a certified fitness trainer and artist who frequently advocated for women's rights on social media and was arrested for using the hashtag #societyisready to call for the abolition of the male guardianship system.


Amnesty International and rights group ALQST called on Saudi authorities to immediately and unconditionally release al-Otaibi, stating her imprisonment "directly contradicts the authorities' rhetoric about reform and women's empowerment." Amnesty International activist Bissan Fakih said the verdict exposes the emptiness of the widely publicized women's rights reforms in Saudi Arabia in recent years and reveals the authorities' determination to suppress peaceful dissent. Al-Otaibi's social media accounts portrayed her as a young, progressive figure passionate about fitness, art, yoga, and travel, while also promoting women's rights.


Al-Otaibi's case echoes those of other Saudi women who have received heavy sentences for expressing views on social media. After being forcibly disappeared for five months, she endured severe abuse and solitary confinement, during which she sustained leg injuries. Saudi officials have denied these allegations. Other cases include Salma al-Shehab sentenced to 27 years, Fatima al-Shawarbi to 30 years, Sukaynah al-Aithan to 40 years, and Nourah al-Qahtani to 45 years.

UN Women urges action to end oppression of women in Afghanistan


According to UN Women on June 10, it urged the world to take urgent action to end the oppression of women and girls in Afghanistan. Since the Taliban took power in August 2021, the rights of Afghan women and girls have been unprecedentedly violated. An assessment report released by UN Women points out that the Taliban, through more than 70 decrees and directives, have reversed decades of progress in gender equality, leading to 1.1 million girls out of school, over 100,000 women dropping out of university, and at least a 50% increase in the risk of maternal mortality. Furthermore, social isolation is severe, with 18% of the surveyed women not having met any women outside their immediate family in the past three months.


Despite facing oppression, Afghan women continue to serve their communities and defend their rights. UN Women calls on all parties to provide sustained funding for women's organizations, ensure that at least 30% of humanitarian aid is dedicated to projects promoting gender equality and women's rights, and prevent inadvertently supporting the Taliban's discriminatory policies. The report emphasizes that human rights, especially women's rights, must be integrated into all humanitarian actions and essential needs interventions. Funded by the European Union, this report is UN Women's first country gender profile on Afghanistan since the Taliban took over.

UN calls for action to stop transnational and cross-border female genital mutilation


According to the latest report released on June 14, 2024, by the Office of the United Nations High Commissioner for Human Rights, transnational and cross-border female genital mutilation (FGM) is undermining global efforts to eradicate this harmful practice. Despite measures taken by many countries, this hidden practice continues, putting 4.3 million girls at risk globally in 2023 and affecting over 600,000 women who have undergone FGM in the European Union. The so-called "holiday cutting" phenomenon is particularly prominent, with some families taking their daughters back to their countries of origin during school holidays to undergo the procedure. Girls in border communities are especially vulnerable due to cultural and ethnic ties across national borders that increase their exposure to the practice.


UN High Commissioner for Human Rights Volker Türk stressed that female genital mutilation is a form of gender-based violence and must be eradicated completely. The report calls for global coordinated action, harmonized legal and policy frameworks, and effective implementation. It also urges countries to work with survivors and civil society organizations to develop and implement effective prevention measures, and to consult with affected communities, religious leaders, and traditional leaders to eliminate this harmful practice.

Global lack of women in leadership: only 26 countries have female heads of state


According to the "2024 Report on Women in Political Leadership" released by UN Women on June 24, women remain severely underrepresented in global decision-making. Currently, 113 countries have never had a woman as head of state or government, and only 26 countries are led by women.


June 24 marks International Women in Diplomacy Day, dedicated to recognizing and celebrating women's achievements in diplomacy. Data shows that despite some progress, women remain underrepresented in positions of power and diplomacy. As of January 1, 2024, only 23% of ministerial positions worldwide were held by women. In 141 countries, women make up less than one-third of cabinet members, and seven countries have no women at all in their cabinets. Female permanent representatives to the United Nations in New York, Geneva, and Vienna stand at only 25%, 35%, and 33.5% respectively.


2024 is an election year, with many countries holding major elections that will profoundly impact policy directions and international relations. UN Women Executive Director Sima Bahous said: "When women lead, the world and our planet benefit. This year, many countries will go to the polls, and we must ensure women are at the pinnacle of power where it matters most. Women's equal participation in governance and leadership is key to improving lives for everyone."

Americas: 73-year-old Chinese angler attacked by white man with a bat in Portland, bystanders watched without offering help


A shocking violent incident occurred in Portland, where a 73-year-old Chinese man from China, Xinmin Liang (name transliterated), was suddenly attacked with a bat by a white man while fishing at dawn by the Willamette River. Jie Liang recalled that her father instinctively used his hand to protect his head during the attack, which caused the hand fracture but also saved his life. Seeing her father's face covered in blood and his eye swollen to the size of a golf ball, she was filled with fear and anger.


The Portland Police Bureau has launched an investigation into the incident, initially classifying it as an assault, though police say the nature of the case may be adjusted as the investigation progresses. Authorities are actively searching for the attacker and urging witnesses to come forward with information. Additionally, the attacker left behind a blue knitted hat at the scene bearing the words "Willamette Week." Mr. Liang has handed the hat to police as physical evidence, which may provide a crucial clue in solving the case.

San Francisco designates Vietnamese as an official language


San Francisco has designated Vietnamese as an official language. The decision was unanimously passed by the San Francisco Board of Supervisors on June 11, 2024, making Vietnamese the city's fourth official language, after Spanish, Chinese, and Tagalog. This resolution aims to ensure residents can access city services in the language they are most comfortable with.


According to San Francisco's language data, approximately 6,800 residents primarily speak Vietnamese. Previously, a language needed to have more than 10,000 speakers to be considered for official status. This amendment lowered the threshold to 6,500, allowing Vietnamese to qualify.


This move aims to further improve the accessibility of language services and ensure diverse immigrant communities can better participate in civic life. According to the San Francisco Language Access Compliance Report, the city government will work to increase the number of bilingual staff to remove participation barriers and ensure equitable service for all residents.
