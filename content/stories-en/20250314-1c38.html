OCF Forum .

The Oxford China Forum (OCF) is an annual summit initiated by Oxford students and is one of the largest and most academically rigorous forums on China in the UK. By inviting world-class politicians, entrepreneurs, media figures, and scholars to discuss and exchange ideas, OCF provides diverse perspectives for the public to observe, understand, and reflect on a changing China.










01 Forum Introduction


The 12th Oxford China Forum

Integration and Opening


Founded in 2013, the Oxford China Forum (OCF) has grown into one of the largest and most academically rigorous forums on China in Europe. Past forums have been live-streamed globally by platforms such as Sohu, Douyin, and Phoenix News, accumulating over 10 million viewers. In previous editions, we have had the honor of inviting over 300 distinguished guests, including former UN Secretary-General Ban Ki-moon, former Australian Prime Minister Kevin Rudd, Wang Zhonglei (CEO of Huayi Brothers), Xu Xiaoping (founder of ZhenFund), Michael Sandel (Harvard political philosopher), Li Zexiang (Chairman of DJI), and renowned dancer Jin Xing. This year's Oxford China Forum will continue the spirit of previous forums—we will speak for the audience's genuine needs and respond concretely to these expectations, offering China's solutions from an Oxford perspective. 


On March 15–16, 2025, we welcome you all to gather in our live stream, to perceive the shifts of our era and spark intellectual exchange through integration and opening. This year's online forum will feature five sub-forums, which will be live-streamed on OCF's Xiaohongshu account (Oxford China Forum) and Bilibili account (OCF Oxford Forum). 02 Sub-forum Preview


In recent years, people of Chinese descent have made remarkable achievements in the international cultural arena. In 2020, Chinese American writer Charles Yu's novel *Interior Chinatown* won the National Book Award for Fiction. The novel shines like a brilliant star, illuminating the English literary world with a unique light of Chinese culture. In 2021, Pixar's first Chinese American female director, Domee Shi, released *Turning Red*, which received widespread acclaim upon release, allowing global audiences to experience distinctive stories from a Chinese diasporic cultural perspective through animated film. In 2022, Michelle Yeoh won the Best Actress award at the 95th Academy Awards for *Everything Everywhere All at Once*. Her outstanding performance in a fully Asian-cast science fiction film was not only a highlight of her acting career but also a testament to the growing influence of Chinese people in the film industry. Meanwhile, Chinese theatrical arts have actively expanded overseas, spreading the profound heritage and enduring charm of Chinese culture on the international stage.


These achievements indicate that people of Chinese descent have secured an important place in English-language literature, film, music, and the arts. This is not merely a success in artistic creation, but also reflects a strong global desire for mutual learning and exchange among diverse cultures. Against the backdrop of the current call to "promote Chinese culture globally," the sub-forum "Global China: Chinese Culture Overseas" has emerged accordingly.


This sub-forum has invited scholars, writers, and film and television professionals from relevant fields. It will deeply explore how Chinese people, in cultural expression and creation, represent Chinese communities, shape China's image, and promote cultural exchange. It will examine how cultural expressions such as English literature, film, music, and art can move beyond marginalization in the English-speaking world, using storytelling and visual media to convey Chinese culture, present China's image, and foster humanistic exchange. Finally, it will also focus on audiences themselves, discussing the dissemination and reception of Chinese culture overseas, especially how it influences global perceptions of China.

In the digital era, algorithm-based artificial intelligence has led to the widespread application of automated decision-making in labor management, bringing multi-dimensional challenges to the protection of workers' rights. The core of these challenges lies in the conflict and balance between employers' organized production and workers' autonomy (Tianye, 2024). In responding to the new challenges brought by AI evolution, society's understanding and response to new risks have also transformed, gradually shifting from "replacement" to "regulation" (Ding Xiaodong, 2024). To provide post-hoc remedies for algorithmic inaccuracies, regulate algorithmic power, and ultimately defend workers' human dignity, this sub-forum will discuss legal pathways for regulating algorithmic risks within labor market contexts.


To fully harness the potential of artificial intelligence while minimizing its negative impacts, cross-sectoral collaboration among policymakers, businesses, the financial sector, and academia is crucial. This sub-forum will delve into urgent issues in labor law, including: how should regulators govern employers' use of algorithmic power? How can labor law reshape the human-machine collaboration framework? How can algorithm-driven employment discrimination be prevented? And how can redress mechanisms be improved?


At OCF 2025's "AIXLaw" sub-forum, we will bring together experts from diverse fields to discuss, from their respective perspectives, the impacts brought by AI development. Following this, through a roundtable discussion, we will explore how to collaborate to promote a smooth transition in the labor market and fully unlock the benefits brought by AI.


AI's transformation is reshaping the legal profession and bringing new opportunities and challenges to legal research and practice. In this transformation, no single entity can face the challenges alone. As the younger generation, we must work together to build a more inclusive and sustainable future for the global labor market. Are you ready to embrace the future? OCF looks forward to your participation!

Have you ever imagined that one day your therapist, your closest friend, or even your lover, might be an AI?


The year 2025, once a futuristic setting in science fiction films, is now upon us. In the film *Her*, the protagonist falls in love with an AI; in *Ready Player One*, the virtual world "Oasis" is born. At this moment, we stand on the edge of a cliff of history, as waves of technology and commerce push us toward an unprecedented future.


Ahead lies a lover or friend on the other side of the screen; beneath our feet, the ethical and safety foundations are crumbling. From emotional companionship to mental health, how exactly can AI influence our inner world?


Over the past year, we have witnessed the rapid development of AI technology:

🚀 Apple CEO Tim Cook visited Chinese otome game company Papergames, announcing the integration of *Love and Deep Space* with Vision Pro;

🚀 A U.S. AI chatbot became embroiled in a teenage suicide scandal, sounding an alarm on ethics and technology;

🚀 The love story between social media influencer "Midnight Exploding Husky Dog" and ChatGPT went viral across social media;

🚀 AI-powered mental health and companionship apps have seen explosive growth, with major tech companies rushing into the field;

🚀 AI applications in healthcare are accelerating psychological analysis and diagnosis;

🚀 Home AI assistants have become family members, with children and the elderly becoming target users of the AI companionship industry;


But are we ready? In the face of ethical and compliance issues brought by new technologies, how should we define the boundaries and frameworks for AI's influence on the human inner world?


For this forum, we have invited scholars and experts from fields including digital humanities, AI and psychological counseling, affective computing, otome games, and human-computer interaction. From anthropology, philosophy, and psychology to cutting-edge AI technology, we look forward to exploring with you the new possibilities for the future of human and digital life coexistence.

Among every hundred strangers you pass by, one experiences the city in a way entirely different from yours.


According to a 2021 *Lancet* report, in 2021 there were 61.8 million people globally with Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD, also translated in China as autism), making it one of the top ten causes of non-fatal health burden among people under 20. Whether it is adolescents on the spectrum with high support needs who struggle to independently explore the city, or adults with minimal support needs who still face sensory overload amid the urban hustle and bustle, they all face similar challenges.


The city is noisy, but we are here to guide the way. The OCF 2025 Neurodiversity-Friendly City Forum will join hands with renowned urban planners and frontline professionals in the field of intellectual and developmental disabilities to explore urban planning and design guidelines that are more inclusive, more welcoming, and better meet the needs of neurodivergent communities. We will interpret practical care experiences with the warmest perspective, thus drawing a better blueprint for cities. The philosophy of inclusive urban design is not about "downward compatibility," but about building a harmonious society that is co-created and friendly to all. As rapid urbanization brings benefits to the general public, we are committed to ensuring that every community—regardless of wealth, gender, race, age, or health status—can equally access the opportunities cities offer and participate in productive work. A city that can guide neurodivergent individuals will surely better support the elderly's well-being and children's development.


Enabling autistic individuals to live independently and not be isolated in cities requires the participation and support of every citizen. OCF thanks you for your attention.

The deep integration of art and technology has driven transformative changes in new media art. Cutting-edge technologies such as artificial intelligence (AI), virtual reality (VR), and augmented reality (AR) have completely reshaped the ways in which art is created and experienced, significantly improving creative efficiency. At the same time, immersive interactive exhibitions have broken the constraints of traditional viewing, allowing audiences to "step into" the artwork. Numerous challenges have emerged, including questions about "instant creations" alongside artistic originality, the redefinition of the artist's identity, the sustainability of high-tech art projects, and how to balance innovation with artistic tradition. This forum will focus on how new technologies are transforming paradigms of artistic creation and appreciation, with particular attention to the potential applications of AI, VR, and AR in areas such as the preservation of intangible cultural heritage. It will also analyze the possibilities for cross-sector collaboration between artists and technology companies to realize commercial value, thus promoting the sustainable development of art and society.
