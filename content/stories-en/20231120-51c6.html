November 25 is the United Nations' International Day for the Elimination of Violence against Women, marking the start of the "16 Days of Activism" against Gender-Based Violence, which runs annually from this day until December 10. The 16 Days of Activism aims to encourage people worldwide to take active steps to eliminate gender-based violence.


To date, violence against women and girls remains one of the most widespread human rights violations in the world. According to United Nations statistics, approximately 736 million women globally—nearly one-third of all women—have experienced gender-based violence at least once in their lives, including violence occurring in workplaces and online spaces.


This year, AWDPI (Asian Women Development Plan International) will host five online workshops during the 16 Days of Activism and relaunch the "Moving Mailbox" initiative, joining people across countries who care about gender issues to express support for survivors of domestic violence. In the United Kingdom and Belgium, we will organize offline exhibitions against gender-based violence; in the United States, we will conduct research and interviews on the living conditions of minority ethnic caregivers.


About AWDPI


AWDPI (Asian Women Development Plan International) was founded in 2020 as an international nonprofit organization centered on women's rights. Currently operating in multiple countries and regions across Europe, North America, and the Asia-Pacific, AWDPI advocates for greater attention to the challenges faced by Asian women living overseas. Through a broad network of collaborations with United Nations agencies, international organizations, multinational corporations, and academic institutions, AWDPI provides sustained protection and support to these women, helping them gain greater opportunities for development in social, economic, cultural, and educational fields, transforming them from vulnerable groups into key forces driving social progress.
