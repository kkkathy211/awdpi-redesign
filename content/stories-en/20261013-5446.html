Introduction to "Her Mountain, Ten Thousand Paths"

"Her Mountain, Ten Thousand Paths" is an interview initiative launched by the AWDPI Curriculum Development Division member interview program. The name originates from our reinterpretation of the word "mountain": "mountain" can symbolize barriers, pressure, and insurmountable weight. But today, we say: I am the mountain. There is no need to look up, no need to cling—women themselves are majestic, independent, and courageous beings; "ten thousand paths" represents each woman's unique journey and life choices.


"Her Mountain, Ten Thousand Paths" aims to showcase the rich diversity of life stories within AWDPI's community of women, making different narratives visible and honoring diverse choices. Through one-on-one in-depth conversations, we document real, vivid, and distinct life trajectories of women. We believe it is precisely these varied paths that form the complete picture of women's strength—no matter where we go, we can find connection and inspiration in each other's experiences, gaining support and insight through sharing. Here, some climb to the peak, some linger halfway, some plant flowers at the foot of the mountain. Though the paths differ, they do not prevent us from seeing each other and encouraging one another.


This is not only a testament to members' personal growth, but also a dialogue soon to be opened to the public. We hope to invite more companions to listen, ask questions, converse, and connect—through each other's stories, to see the path ahead of ourselves.


Tracing ten thousand paths, we will eventually meet; among blossoming mountain flowers, we encounter you.



About Liu Liu


Qiao Qiao's introduction to Liu Liu:


In one's life, certain familiar plotlines often appear: childhood, growing up, leaving the original family, work, feelings about growth, and so on. Along the way, one might also choose romance, marriage, or parenthood. Since childhood, we've all heard stories about "how life should be lived when you grow up," so much so that we rarely pause to ask: Is this what I truly want? Or is it what others have told me "I should want"? And if it truly is, then why do I want these things?


But when we actually stand at these life junctures, things are often far more complex than imagined.


Liu Liu is a professional woman and a senior colleague who consistently radiates moonlight-like warmth during our interactions. At the same time, her firmness and reflective spirit constantly teach me her habits of active self-reflection and continuous iteration. She is also a daughter and a mother; we often discuss how multiple roles intertwine in life, shaping its diverse forms.


In this episode, we begin with Liu Liu's personal growth journey, discussing seemingly ordinary experiences from childhood that only gain deeper meaning in hindsight; exploring how she gradually realized that the world does not have just one explanation, nor is there necessarily a ready-made answer to life. We talk about how she carries certain things forward from her family, while choosing to leave others behind; how, through repeated collisions in relationships and daily life, she comes to know herself, handle challenges, and slowly form her own way of judgment, and through continuous iteration, learns to navigate workplace difficulties and broaden her perspective on the world.



We will also talk about intimate relationships, marriage, and parenthood—why did Liu Liu choose marriage? How did she decide to become a mother? When a woman has choices, why does she make the ones she does? After becoming a mother, how have her understandings of herself, family, the previous generation, and life itself changed?


In the first two episodes of "Her Mountain, Ten Thousand Paths," we invited two guests who "don't follow conventional paths." But in fact, the labels of "traditional" and "non-traditional" are themselves just stereotypes. Familiar plotlines do not mean identical stories, and a person's life does not have to follow a fixed script set by others. Therefore, what we truly want to discuss is not which path women should choose, nor do we suggest that certain life choices are the only ones worthy of encouragement, but rather to present the myriad possibilities of lived experience, along with the trade-offs, joys and sorrows involved, and to empathize and embrace as many women as possible.


When we step into plotlines that many have walked before, perhaps we can slowly understand why we entered them, and within them, write our own version. We hope that by sharing the experiences and insights of different women at various life stages and with diverse personalities and thoughts, we can see each other, gain strength, and freely write our own lives.
