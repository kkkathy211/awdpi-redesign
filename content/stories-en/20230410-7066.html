News Commentary

According to the latest poll, Asian American support for the Republican Party has surged, no longer blindly following racial lines to support the Democratic Party

(1) Incident Summary

A recent poll shows that Asian Americans are awakening—from previously blindly supporting the Democratic Party, support for the Republican Party has now exceeded 40% and continues to rise.

In 2018, 77% of Asian Americans supported the Democratic Party, while 23% supported the Republican Party. But by 2022, those figures had shifted to 58% supporting the Democrats and 40% supporting the Republicans. Moreover, this trend is still increasing. The rampant inflation under the Biden administration has placed a heavy burden on the general lower-income population—groups who used to be a solid base of Democratic voters. Whether they will switch sides in the 2024 election remains to be seen.

(2) Commentary Perspective

1. The main reason is the Democratic Party's tolerance of crime and implicit hostility toward Asians, leading to a rise in hate crimes against Asians.

On hate crimes, we have all heard so-called progressive prosecutors repeatedly emphasize fighting hate crimes, yet in reality, they are spreading anti-Asian sentiment. For example, in public education, Democrats repeatedly claim that elite schools have too many Asian students and should reduce their numbers. In California, ballot initiatives in 2012 and 2020 sought to reduce Asian enrollment in universities. Even more absurdly, such clearly anti-Asian measures received support from Asian American Democrats. This is spreading racial hatred by implication, encouraging certain ethnic groups—from lawmakers to ordinary people—to harbor hostility toward Asians.


2. On crime prevention, Asians also hold fundamentally different views from the Democratic Party.

Aside from a few Asian Democratic lawmakers, Asians generally desire social stability and stronger crime control. Yet under the Democratic Party's radical policies, public safety in New York and California has become deeply concerning—"$0 shoplifting" is rampant, people fear their car windows being smashed every time they drive, and even at home, they worry about break-ins. This is not the kind of society Asians wish to see.

Chinese woman in Italy slashed in the throat on the street; perpetrator was her husband


According to Europe Times on March 3, citing Italian media Hua Ren Jie, a 48-year-old Chinese woman was attacked and slashed in the throat at dawn on March 2. She sustained serious injuries, and police are investigating. The Chinese national was treated with a red code emergency response on Via Ciabatti and rushed to the emergency room at Santo Stefano Hospital. Her condition was reported as critical, with deep neck wounds requiring delicate surgery. Doctors remain cautious about her prognosis.


According to the latest update, police believe the attack was due to personal reasons and quickly arrested the suspect. The suspect is the victim's compatriot, possibly her partner, who was intercepted while fleeing in Brescia. He is charged with attempted murder, as the method and type of stab wounds on the victim's throat indicate intent to kill.


Investigators have not yet been able to obtain the victim's testimony, which is crucial for reconstructing the events of the morning of March 2 and determining the motive behind the attack.

Chinese in Spain robbed at highway rest area


China Overseas Net, March 6 — According to "Xi Wen," the WeChat official account of Europe Times Spain edition, Spanish National Police recently arrested two men who robbed a Chinese man at a gas station in Burgos and violently assaulted him.


Cope Radio reported that a Burgos police spokesperson said the two suspects attacked a Chinese man around 6 a.m. local time on March 2 at a service area in the province, stealing personal belongings worth nearly 10,000 euros. During the robbery, the suspects beat the victim.

Police said they responded immediately after receiving the report. At the scene, they arrested the two suspects and found evidence linking them to the robbery.


The victim sustained minor injuries and is not in life-threatening condition. Police said they are investigating to determine the suspects' identities and whether they are involved in other similar cases. The two suspects are now in custody and will face further investigation and trial.

Brazil striving to achieve gender equality in education


The recently released report "UNESCO's Efforts to Achieve Gender Equality in Education: Highlights 2021" introduces the organization's work in 2021 to promote gender equality through education, including actions in Brazil.


Key initiatives in Brazil include: the Gender Flagship Project under the Global Education Coalition, addressing gender impacts during school closures caused by the COVID-19 pandemic; continuation of the 2020 "Let Girls Learn" campaign to support girls' return to school, which reached approximately 400 million people globally; and efforts to ensure digital skills and STEM (science, technology, engineering, and mathematics) education, with a focus on increasing women's participation and improving gender balance in these fields.


The report also includes the UNESCO Prize for Girls' and Women's Education, funded by the Chinese government, which aims to recognize individuals, institutions, and organizations for their innovative contributions to advancing girls' and women's education. In 2021, this $50,000 award was granted to a Brazilian initiative for its work focusing on digital skills development for underrepresented and low-income girls and women, helping to reduce the gender digital divide.

Professional Development Startup Grants for Women Scientists


The Organization for Women in Science for the Developing World (OWSD) offers Professional Development Startup Grants of up to $50,000 to women who have completed doctoral degrees in science, technology, engineering, or mathematics (STEM).


OWSD has announced a new call for applications for its prestigious Early Career Fellowship. This three-year fellowship, worth up to $50,000, supports early-career women scientists seeking to continue research based at their local institutions, engage internationally, and build research groups that attract international visitors.


The Early Career Fellowship is open to women scientists who obtained their PhDs in science, technology, engineering, and mathematics (STEM) disciplines within the past 10 years and are employed at a research or academic center in one of the science and technology lagging countries. Funding is generously provided by the International Development Research Centre (IDRC).

Interested applicants can visit the website www.owsd.net and click on "Fellowships/Early Career Fellowship" to apply. The call for applications opens in the first few months of each year. Eligible countries include El Salvador, Guatemala, Haiti, Honduras, Nicaragua, and Paraguay.

Republicans Leave 22% of Transgender Youth Without Access to Gender-Affirming Care


Twenty-two percent of transgender youth in the United States have lost access to gender-affirming care. A new report from the Human Rights Campaign (HRC) found that out of 300,000 transgender youth aged 13 to 17, 66,600 live in states that have passed laws banning this life-saving care. Another 84,700 transgender youth (28.2%) live in states currently considering such bans.


According to the campaign, eight states currently have complete bans on gender-affirming care for minors. In just the first months of 2023, Mississippi, South Dakota, Tennessee, Arkansas, Iowa, and Utah have enacted laws banning gender-affirming care.


In recent years, Republicans have focused their agenda on attacking the equal rights of transgender youth. A new law in Tennessee, requiring youth already receiving gender-affirming care to detransition, is particularly chilling. Other states, such as Oklahoma and Florida, are considering similar legislation.


These laws contradict pediatric best practices related to transgender care as outlined by the American Academy of Pediatrics, the American Medical Association, and the American Psychological Association. Numerous studies show that lack of social recognition and access to gender-affirming care leads to high suicide rates among transgender youth. Other research finds that gender-affirming care significantly reduces depression and suicide risk among transgender youth.

International Day for the Elimination of Racial Discrimination


United Nations General Assembly Resolution 2142 (XXI), adopted on October 26, 1966, proclaimed March 21 as the "International Day for the Elimination of Racial Discrimination," to be observed annually. On this day in 1960, police opened fire and killed 69 people during a peaceful demonstration against apartheid "pass laws" in Sharpeville, South Africa. In 1966, the General Assembly designated this date, symbolizing the struggle to end apartheid in South Africa, and called on the international community to redouble its efforts to eliminate all forms of racial discrimination.


Over the years, UNESCO has celebrated this international day by organizing events at its headquarters and field offices, and by collaborating with member cities of the International Coalition of Inclusive and Sustainable Cities.

Gender Equality Can Be Achieved Within Our Lifetime

According to the latest report from UN Women, at the current pace of progress, the world will need 286 years to achieve gender equality. The World Economic Forum estimates it will take another 132 years to close the global gender gap. Regardless of which forecast is more accurate, the bottom line is that the current rate of "progress" is unacceptable for something long overdue.


It is these disheartening statistics, along with a desire to offer actionable ways to accelerate progress, that have served as the catalyst for the newly released analytical report and book *Equality in Our Lifetime: How Laws and Policies Can Close or Expand the Gender Gap in the Global Economy*, a powerful call to action by Jody Heymann, Aleta Sprague, and Amy Raub of the World Policy Analysis Center (WORLD). The book explores how gender equality can be achieved and why it is urgently needed to address some of the greatest challenges of our time.

Michelle Yeoh Wins Best Actress at the Oscars


The success of *Everything Everywhere All at Once* at the Oscars is seen as a major victory for Asian and Chinese-descent filmmakers in the "battle for Hollywood representation." The U.S. publication *The Hollywood Reporter* offered a concise and accurate assessment of this year's Oscars—according to its tally, filmmakers of Asian descent, including Chinese and Indian heritage, won a total of eight awards: Best Picture, Best Director, Best Original Screenplay, Best Actress, Best Supporting Actor, Best Makeup and Hairstyling, Best Documentary Short, and Best Original Song, with five of these going to *Everything Everywhere All at Once*. For other filmmakers of color, only one Latino and one Black individual won awards this year. This is highly significant for advancing and showcasing Asian representation!

Donnie Yen Says Foreign Media Never Reports the Real China

Recently, the UK edition of *GQ*'s digital magazine *GQ Hype* featured Donnie Yen on its cover, where the Chinese martial arts film star discussed not only Hollywood's stereotypes of Asians but also criticized Western media for focusing solely on negative stories about China while ignoring its world-leading infrastructure development and levels of daily convenience that many other countries cannot match.


Donnie Yen shared that he has traveled to many countries and found their modernization falling short of China's. He also reflected on his personal journey: as a young man, he longed to prove himself in Hollywood, but now he no longer views Hollywood with special reverence. Instead, before accepting roles, he carefully selects them, asking the production team, "Is this character a cliché?" and "Do they respect Chinese culture?" Donnie Yen stated that if a role lacks meaningful representation, "it's not worth my time."

Dr. Jingliu Wang Becomes the First Female Mainland Chinese-American University President in the U.S.

Jingliu Wang will assume office as the 17th president of Worcester Polytechnic Institute (WPI), often referred to as "Little MIT," on April 3, 2023. She is the first mainland Chinese-American woman to serve as president of a U.S. university and the first person of color to hold the WPI presidency in a non-interim capacity.


Wang earned her bachelor's and master's degrees at Beijing University of Chemical Technology and completed her doctorate in materials science and engineering at Northwestern University in the United States.


In 2009, Wang joined the Division of Industrial Innovation and Partnerships at the U.S. National Science Foundation (NSF), becoming its director in 2012. During her time at NSF, she discovered her growing interest in academia. In 2017, she left NSF to join the State University of New York (SUNY) system as Vice Chancellor for Research and Economic Development. By September 2017, she also served as Interim Provost of the SUNY system and was soon appointed Interim President of SUNY Polytechnic Institute (SUNY Poly), while being promoted to Senior Vice Chancellor for Research and Economic Development. Later, Wang served as Vice President at The Ohio State University, gaining extensive experience in university leadership.

Hundreds of Chinese Americans Historically Flood Texas State Capitol to Testify, Overwhelmingly Opposing Bills 147 and 711


 March 2 is Texas Independence Day, and it is also a significant day for the local Chinese community, as Senate Bills 147 and 711—proposals that have sparked strong opposition among Chinese Americans—held their public hearing today. To make their voices heard at this crucial moment, many concerned individuals drove hundreds of miles from Houston and Dallas to the state capital, Austin, either yesterday or in the early hours of this morning. This morning, they joined local Chinese Americans in attending the hearing, each hoping for a mere two-minute speaking slot. In the end, Chinese Americans overwhelmingly outnumbered other witnesses in opposing both bills! This day can be said to be historic—a milestone for Chinese American political participation in Texas.


To everyone's surprise, Martha Wong, Texas' first female Asian American state representative and a retired Republican, appeared and delivered a powerful 15-minute speech opposing the proposals. She stated that while the intent behind the bills might have been good and not aimed at discrimination, they would nevertheless produce discriminatory consequences, which lawmakers must carefully consider. She joyfully added that she had spent her entire life fighting for greater Chinese American political engagement, and today was the first time she had seen so many Chinese Americans gathered inside the state capitol.


During a Zoom meeting the previous afternoon, Representative Wu Yuanzhi said this was his first time collaborating with the Chinese community on a state capitol hearing, marking the first time the Texas Chinese community had participated in such a hearing at the state capitol. After the hearing, Wu expressed pride in the Chinese American community's performance.
