Over the course of a 14-day inspiring and engaging online book club, we were able to immerse ourselves together in Virginia Woolf's classic work A Room of One's Own. Written in 1929, this enduring masterpiece is far more than a simple literary critique or novel—it is a brilliant, eloquent, and poetic manifesto that profoundly examines the challenges and possibilities women face in literary creation and social structures..



With her distinctive stream-of-consciousness style, Woolf guides us through fiction and history, personal experience and social observation. She presents a powerful central argument: "A woman must have money and a room of her own if she is to write fiction." This seemingly simple demand actually reveals the fundamental social structural barriers that have hindered women's creativity for centuries: economic independence (symbolized by an annual income of 500 pounds) is the foundation for breaking free from dependence and gaining the freedom to think; physical and mental space (a room of one's own) is the necessary condition for undisturbed creative labor.


With sharp and humorous prose, Woolf traces the harsh reality of how women have historically been excluded from the literary canon due to lack of educational opportunities, economic autonomy, and basic personal rights. More importantly, A Room of One's Own has had an immeasurable foundational impact on feminist literature and feminist thought as a whole.

DAY 2

Gender Power Structures in Literary Tradition


DAY 2


When one gender has held the right to write across generations, their experiences are elevated into universal humanity, while the silenced struggles of the other gender are dismissed as special or exceptional.




DAY 2


I recently came across a viewpoint I strongly agree with: literary works should be based on reality and be believable to readers. Writers must dive into life and portray the joys and sorrows within the broader context of our era, rather than writing in isolation or indulging in baseless lamentations.



DAY 2

Men trapped within their own narrow thinking do not choose to grow or transcend themselves. They have never truly understood the meaning of learning—that is, to continuously see and examine the self through books or the world, thereby surpassing existing logical frameworks and moving to the next stage, rather than merely confirming their pre-existing beliefs and prejudices. Such an approach is utterly meaningless.


DAY 2


The section about work reminded me of the recent "mother-friendly job posts." The word "care" itself carries connotations of power dynamics. When we place ourselves within this context, power automatically emerges—it is inherently unequal, regardless of which side one is on.


DAY 2


Sharing this original passage: "Of course, it was all a fantasy—the lilac bushes swayed over the wall in the garden, the brimstone butterflies flew hither and thither, and the pollen was scattered on the air."

DAY 3

Deep Reflections on Gender Inequality


DAY 3




Engaging in a dialogue of thought with the author, feeling her joy and sorrow.








DAY 3

"After becoming aware of these issues, my fear and bitterness turned into pity and tolerance. A year or two later, even pity and tolerance disappeared. I gained the greatest liberation—the freedom to see things objectively."

I love this passage so much.

In the first stage, upon seeing women's unequal circumstances, the author feels bitterness and grievance.

In the second stage, the author further realizes that gender inequality stems from many men relying on degrading women to gain superiority and confidence. This is actually a profound failure of education, because this kind of superiority, built on belittling another group, leads many men into cycles of exploitation and violence. Such a personality is truly tragic. Thus, under the system of gender inequality, men are also victims—no gender truly wins. Therefore, the author develops compassion toward men.

The freedom in the third stage, as I understand it, is the author no longer caring about traditional constraints on women, and firmly walking her own path.

I myself was stuck in the first stage before. Because of the gender oppression I suffered in life, I felt pain, but I also didn't agree with the gender antagonism stirred up by online traffic, so I felt very lonely in both the real world and the online world.

After reading the author's second-stage reflections and third-stage actions, I was deeply inspired. Opposing gender oppression doesn't have to mean fueling gender antagonism—it can also mean building gender confidence.


DAY 3




Women have not only been deprived of the material conditions for creation, but also of their subjectivity in historical narratives. This deprivation was not achieved through violent suppression, but through more subtle and systematic means: excluding women from education, denying their rational abilities, mocking their creative attempts, and ultimately rationalizing all this as a scientific truth about gender essence.







DAY 3




Novels are like spider webs—seemingly swaying in the wind, yet connected to every corner of life. Some of the author's views are indeed quite unique.







DAY1


After finishing Chapter Three, I remain amazed by Virginia's thinking. In an unsupportive context, women's lives are constrained—they dare not record what truly belongs to them, don't know how to unleash their abilities, and even don't realize they have the capacity to do all kinds of things. Meanwhile, men seem to have these rights by birth. In their un"eroded" time, they "purely" complete self-indulgent romantic odes and melancholy. Even more regretfully, to this day, men generally have easier access to resources than women. We still have a long way to go in our continuous struggle.

DAY 4

Female Legacy and Creative Freedom


DAY 4


The inheritance left to Woolf by her aunt Mary was, in fact, the freedom "not to please nor resent." An ordinary woman, through her entire life, paved the way for future female writers. While men gain wealth and status through institutional inheritance, informal transmission among women becomes a channel to break cycles of poverty.



DAY 4


"Artists are naturally extremely sensitive to others' opinions. Literary circles are filled with people who care excessively about what others think." "Their sensitivity intensifies their suffering, because for an artist to fully release the work within, they must possess great strength—a strength that must come from a passionate heart." This sentence gave me a warm embrace. So the reason I'm so sensitive is because I have an artist's temperament!


DAY 4


The world says to men: write if you want to, I don't care; but to women, it responds with a string of laughter: what are you writing? What good does your writing do? Between the lines, the different societal attitudes toward men and women are clearly revealed! Not only ordinary people, but even bishops were filled with contempt for women! Reflecting on today's workplace discrimination and inequality toward women, it's clear that the road to women's pursuit of equality and freedom remains long.


DAY 4



Masterpieces are never created out of thin air or in isolation. They are built upon the thoughts and wisdom of many ordinary people, combined with an individual's unique insights.

DAY 6

The Practical Challenges of Women's Creation


DAY 6


"Books written by women should be shorter and more compact than those by men, with clear structure, without requiring long, uninterrupted periods of concentrated writing. Because they are always being interrupted." A very practical writing suggestion—I think it applies to both women and men today.



DAY 6


The loss of female genius has never been due to a lack of talent, but to an entire social system that refuses to provide space for its survival. Judith's predicament is not a unique phenomenon of Shakespeare's era—since ancient times, countless potential Shakespeares have been strangled in the cradle, and those rare women writers who manage to break through often have to conceal their identities or distort their creativity to conform to societal expectations.

The courage to leap over fences


DAY 7


"The challenge she faces is whether she can ignore the people around her and focus solely on leaping over her own fence. I told her, if you stop to curse, you lose; if you stop to laugh, it's the same. If you hesitate or panic, you're finished. I pleaded with her: just keep jumping, as if I'd bet all my money on her. She cleared the fence, light as a bird on the other side. But there are more fences ahead. I doubted she could endure, because applause and shouts were draining her energy. Yet she tried her best." I love this passage. Don't be distracted by outside voices—move forward with determination.

DAY 9

The androgynous perspective on creation

DAY 9


On one hand, the author says education should emphasize the differences between men and women, and that women should not write like men. On the other hand, the author claims that creators should be androgynous. This seems somewhat contradictory.






DAY 10

Reflection from a modern perspective


DAY 10


As a modern woman, if I were to travel back in time, I would certainly feel outrage and disbelief at the unfair treatment of women back then! But if I actually lived in that era, my thinking would also be constrained and shaped by the norms of the time!




DAY 10

Every generation of women has its own limitations. Compared to the past, women today have much greater room to survive—having a room of one's own and a job to support oneself is relatively less difficult now. But perhaps we should also reflect: are we sometimes trapped in the narrative of "female independence"? For example, some women may believe that "independence" means being able to handle everything alone without asking for help, or taking on more responsibilities in family relationships. I feel that sometimes we fall into a trap disguised as independence. True independence includes not only financial autonomy but also emotional maturity—being unashamed to seek help, and being able to express the need for support when necessary is also a form of independence.


DAY 10


An independent woman does not mean you have to carry all of life's burdens alone! Men should also take on their share of family and social responsibilities.



DAY 10


It's not "not that difficult"—in China, women are competing in the workplace while carrying children, elderly parents, and the kitchen on their backs. For women, achieving a balance between career and family is impossible… There is no balance, only sacrifice.



DAY 10


Among women, there are vast differences in living conditions. A significant portion of women have achieved relative economic independence but are caught in the trap of the independence narrative, while another group still struggles at the margins of survival—each has their own hardships.



DAY 10


I was struck by the mention of "privileged class" in de Beauvoir's interview. Sometimes, when facing the difficulties of female relatives or the different choices of female peers, I wonder whether I possess certain "privileges" in different aspects, and whether such privileges come merely from the courage to take a step forward.


DAY 10

The status of women varies greatly across countries! I once watched a film about female genital mutilation in Africa, and I was deeply shocked and angry at the time! Yet some local women, especially some mothers, believe it is necessary! Therefore, women's self-awareness, especially the enhancement of mothers' rights consciousness, is also very important.



DAY 10


Driven by feelings of unworthiness, shame, and excessive moral standards, many women feel ashamed about earning money, unconsciously "maintaining" an inner image of themselves as some kind of "pure saint." This is a cognitive distortion resulting from patriarchal social conditioning. Money is the real tool that gives women more choices in the future.


DAY 10

True independence requires girls in this land to deeply understand the importance of capital—in plain terms, although most people may seem disconnected from capital, they absolutely, absolutely must know that money is a good thing, because most of one's dignity depends on money. Without money, without the ability to survive independently, any talk of freedom is nothing but a fictional illusion built on a mirage. Without economic freedom, spiritual independence and personal independence are extremely difficult to achieve.

DAY 10


Woman is not a gender, woman is a condition. Girls help girls.

Woolf's words not only reflect the predicament of Western women in the last century, but also transcend time and space to mirror the survival challenges faced by contemporary women. From exploring the stream-of-consciousness writing style to deconstructing the gender power structures in literary traditions, from re-examining women's lives in the last century through a modern perspective to contemplating a trans-historical and transnational creative freedom and perseverance every participant in this shared reading contributed their valuable reflections.


As Woolf said, we need "a room of one's own" a space that is not only a physical boundary separating the body from the surrounding world, but also a place where the mind can freely develop. Let us continue reading, continue thinking, and keep leaping over the fences in life!
