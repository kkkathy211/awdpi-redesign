A report by UN Women indicates that women typically account for the majority of deaths caused by natural disasters. In the 2004 Asian tsunami, over 70% of the deaths in Aceh Province, Indonesia—one of the hardest-hit areas—were women. After Cyclone Nargis struck Myanmar's Irrawaddy Delta in 2008, an estimated 87% of unmarried women and 100% of married women lost their primary source of income.


After disasters strike, women, often as caregivers, fall into another layer of hardship. Due to traditional gender roles, female survivors frequently face the dual burden of family care and post-disaster recovery, while the scarcity of hygiene products for women and children and inadequate reproductive health services place even greater health risks on female survivors.

However, gender sensitivity is often overlooked in post-disaster relief practices. Although in recent years an increasing number of organizations have begun to pay attention to women's needs after disasters, can these services truly cover the broad spectrum of women's needs?


Therefore, from a gender perspective, widely listening to female survivors' post-disaster stories, identifying their unique needs, and recognizing women's strength in the recovery process hold significant reference value for future disaster relief practices by nonprofit organizations.


A study on

women's situations and needs in natural disasters


Study Introduction


This is a study themed "Women's Situations and Needs in Natural Disasters." This study focuses on flooding as a specific disaster, aiming to uncover common yet often overlooked relief needs of female disaster survivors through interviews with women affected by past floods and fieldwork within female communities in flood-stricken areas. It seeks to highlight women's resilience and rebuilding power, providing reference for improving future relief measures tailored to women's needs.


This study sincerely invites women who have experienced floods to participate in this interview. We want to hear about your experiences during the flood, the difficulties you encountered, and the strength of women you witnessed during the disaster. Your story will help us collectively paint a comprehensive picture of women's situations and needs after disasters.


If you:

are a woman who has experienced flooding,

If you know women who have experienced flooding,

Study Information


This interview serves as research data for PeriodPride's "Rebuilding Gender-Friendly Spaces After Disasters" project. The interview will strictly follow research ethics, with all interview materials rigorously anonymized and any information disclosed by respondents kept strictly confidential.

Interview Recruitment Criteria

1. Women who have experienced flooding and were affected by the disaster;

2. Have internet access and be able to participate in online interviews (any device is acceptable) between June 10th and July 30th;

3. No restrictions on other information (such as age, region, occupation, etc.), with priority given to women living in rural or village areas.

Interview Format

Online interview, any device is acceptable.

Interview Duration

Approximately 1 hour

Note

This interview is voluntary, and the information provided will support the development and implementation of PeriodPride's "Rebuilding Gender-Friendly Spaces After Disasters" project.
