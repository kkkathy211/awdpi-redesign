Spring always arrives quietly, awakening everything without a sound.

For AWDPI (Asian Women Development Plan International) AWDPI, this is already the fifth spring. Over the past five years, we have consistently worked to eliminate discrimination and gender-based violence against overseas Asian women, striving to promote greater understanding, respect, and space for growth for them in global society.


This spring of 2025, we are still taking action—and once again extending our invitation for the spring volunteer recruitment.


In 2024, we received over a thousand applications. The questions on the form may have seemed simple:

 • Why do you want to join AWDPI?

 • Why are you applying for this position?

 • Feel free to share anything you'd like to say.


Behind these thousand responses lie a thousand experiences, a thousand voices, a thousand forms of strength.

This spring, some chose to come closer because they have felt pain; some chose to act because they understand; some chose to join because they believe.


These words are not merely answers to questions—they are responses to the world.

They write with courage, answer with experience, and begin a journey of walking together with sincerity.


This article belongs to the 2024 spring recruitment—it is the "first part." The second part, belonging to the 2025 spring recruitment, will meet everyone soon.


Now, please listen to their answers.

Those once silent, struggling, stubborn, and resolute voices are now blooming from each application form.


Below are their stories—



Because of experience, therefore action



Some have personally faced prejudice, some have witnessed the cost of silence, and others understand that merely "being aware" cannot change anything. Joining AWDPI is about speaking up—and even more, about taking action.

For them, and for us.


YJ | Language Support Division - Translation Team Member

One summer, I returned to my grandmother's home. On an afternoon visit to relatives, I half-awoke from a nap and faintly heard adults whispering anxiously, and the muffled sound of crying. With my eyes closed, I listened as an aunt recounted to the family the long history of domestic violence her husband had inflicted on her. The earthen bed beneath me suddenly felt like it was pressing into every bone in my body, as if the shards of beer bottles stuck in the snow in the book *Bearing Witness* were now scattered in this rural northwestern courtyard—any of us women, my grandmother, aunts, mother, and I, would be cut and bleeding if we stood up and pushed open the door.

YSH | UK General Affairs Office

Both my mother and I are victims of domestic violence, and I still carry psychological trauma to this day. As a child, I had no concept of "domestic violence"; I only realized it later in life. I hope to do something to help reduce the number of direct victims like my mother, and even more, I hope no child in any family has to become an indirect victim like me. This is also a form of self-redemption.

WZQ | Head of US Partnership Development Office

I was sexually harassed by teachers during middle and high school, but found it hard to speak up. When I finally mustered the courage to tell my homeroom teacher, the response was, "Are you sure you're not provoking him? Girls should respect and value themselves." Or when I was harassed by a supervisor during an internship and didn't know how to seek help. It is precisely because of these personal experiences that I feel an urgent desire to help women who are suffering as I once did.

LC | Language Support Division - German Team Member

In the fourth grade, on my way home from school, I met a neighbor aunt with a large bruise near her eye. I greeted her, and she quickly waved back before leaving. At dinner that night, my parents vaguely mentioned a big argument between the aunt and her husband the previous night, so serious that the police were called. That was the first time I vaguely realized the real existence of domestic violence. My reason for joining AWDPI isn't complicated—I simply hope that, as a member of the female community, I can use my abilities to offer even a small amount of strength to sisters facing violent situations. In holding them up, perhaps I am also holding myself.

SZX | UK Partnership Development Office

When I sit in a classroom full of white Westerners, as an Asian from the East, I can still clearly feel that I am part of a minority. When I hear men say in feminist discussions that they never need to launch any men's rights movements to fight for themselves, I understand that women's rights must be claimed by women themselves. I recognize an undeniable reality: Chinese women overseas are in a vulnerable position when facing difficulties and unfair treatment. This is no longer just a gender issue—because women do not live only as women; they simultaneously hold richer social identities.




Where you care, there you act



From curiosity to research, from receiving help to mutual support, they respond to reality through action.

For women in war zones, for survivors of domestic violence, to ensure that overseas Asian women's voices are heard, to make more rights a reality—this is also their answer for joining AWDPI.


XY | UK - On-site Legal Support for Emergency Assistance

Asians have long been in a disadvantaged position overseas, and the situation for Asian women is even more difficult. Generation after generation of stereotypes have created an atmosphere where fear permeates every sense of overseas Asian women. If Asian women could have a thorough understanding of local laws or firmly believe they can receive legal help from "people like themselves," the pressure they face could be greatly reduced.

CC | UK - Fundraising Team Member

In East Asian culture, the lack of gender education and sexual awareness has made discussing sex a taboo. Gender awareness has only gradually emerged in recent years and has not yet been integrated into mainstream social discourse. The recent rise of feminism has, due to stereotypes, become a weapon in gender opposition. Severe information asymmetry leads many women to suffer psychological and physical harm to varying degrees due to discrimination or violence, either because they cannot access correct information and resources, or lack the courage to defend their rights. Many men, due to insufficient understanding of gender issues, also perceive women's development and empowerment as a threat. As a minority group, intersectionality adds another layer of identity for overseas Chinese women in the context of gender inequality.

JY | UK Administrative Office Team

As a woman studying economics and finance, my main research topic is inclusive finance. In the process of helping financially vulnerable groups, I found that women make up a larger proportion of this group, and are relatively more disadvantaged in society. As their fellow countrywoman, I understand that "helping them is helping myself."

XD | Legal Affairs Department

When we discuss "measures to protect women from domestic violence in different countries," we usually think of how black-eyed, yellow-skinned Chinese women in China communicate with women's federations and the police to protect themselves, or how native English-speaking white or Black women in the US use government, community, and school support systems to start a new life. We often fail to recognize that in our globalized world, racial migration and mobility are widespread, and cultural barriers and language difficulties may make it harder for immigrant women to access resources and receive help compared to local women.




Where you care, there you act



The world will not heal itself, but they choose to be the ones who drive change.

Providing domestic violence emergency networks, building mutual support communities overseas, advocating for policy improvements, and expanding women's right to choose. Every person who joins is helping to make the future different.


MQ | Advocacy and Influence Department Member

I am now a practitioner in psychology and traditional Chinese medicine, hoping to help more people achieve psychological and health self-support, enabling them to see a bigger world. I have many outstanding female friends around me—women's inherent strength is immense, their potential limitless. Women shine in every field, and at any stage of life, awakening their inner strength can lead them to an infinite life they desire.

LY | Advocacy and Influence Department Member

As someone who has long focused on gender issues and women's empowerment, and who also carries the identity of an "overseas Asian woman," I feel I have the passion, ability, and responsibility to participate in this cause. This is the path I have always pursued and my future goal.

SH | UK - Fundraising Team Member

Women are half the sky. I once endured five years of extremely cruel emotional abuse, and I even thought about suicide. But I knew my death would mean nothing, and only my parents and friends would grieve. For those five years, I never fully lived as my true self or truly experienced happiness. As a woman, I deeply understand how such pain and suffering can force one to become independent and strong. I also deeply feel that I have the duty and responsibility to help women anywhere in the world—to give them warm embraces, to bring them light in the darkness.

DL | Language Support Division - Russian Team Member

Herta Müller said, "We fly in similar postures, and are also likely to fall in the same posture." Although the women we face are strangers to each other, and our life trajectories differ, there must have been moments in life when we shared the same feelings. This distant similarity across time and space brings us together on this platform, where through mutual support and empathy, we create new narratives, making it possible to "fly" once again.



This spring, 235 hands carve new coordinates for women into the walls—the imprints left on seats in a Shanghai screening room, protest posters gripped tightly on New York streets, unspoken words lingering on overseas mental health hotlines late at night—all are traces left by embers that have already begun burning for tomorrow.


In AWDPI's fifth year, we no longer ask "when will change come," but instead respond with a support system that spans Europe, North America, and the Asia-Pacific region, offering professional assistance to every woman in need, publishing reports on the living conditions of Asian women across dozens of countries, and implementing action plans from Shanghai to New York—this is AWDPI's answer to the realities we face.


This spring, may we continue walking together, co-writing the answer to women's empowerment on AWDPI's journey. Right now, action is the future.
