Forum Overview

Introduction of LOCF




The LSE-Oxford China Forum was jointly founded in 2020 by Zhengjing Guolun and Oxford China Forum, and has now completed five successful years. This forum aims to gather outstanding minds, break down knowledge barriers, and create a free, inclusive, open, and equal environment for dialogue and discussion. It brings together diverse perspectives to review and envision China's development journey from multiple angles—reflecting on the past, observing the present, and shaping the future with a global vision.


The lunar year 2024 is not only a once-in-60-years Jia Chen year, but also a "global super election year." In this special year, how China can seize opportunities and forge new development paths amid a complex and changing international landscape is a topic of shared concern. This year, together with our guests, we will explore China's future development directions through three thematic sessions: "New Quality Productivity," "Food Upgrading and Sustainable Development Security," and "Culture," opening brighter prospects from diverse perspectives.


The global political and economic environment resembles a game of chess, with shifting tides like turbulent waves. As one of the most influential forums operated by overseas university alumni, the LSE China Forum hopes to "use boats and oars to navigate uncharted waters," enabling participants to engage with world-class scholars and leaders from politics and business, draw wisdom, and "reach far to benefit all under heaven," jointly drawing a new blueprint for China's sustainable development.


"Among countless boats racing forward, those who row vigorously take the lead." We warmly welcome all such pioneers to join us in this grand gathering.

Sub-Forums

Introduction of Panels





01

New Quality Productivity Sub-Forum

Sailing Toward the Future

In recent years, intelligent electric vehicles have been successively launched in the domestic market, allowing consumers to directly experience improvements in driving. Intelligence has become a new trend in the development of new-energy vehicles. However, opportunities come with challenges. Faced with slowing market growth and distant profitability, how can the electric vehicle industry break through? Amid threats of anti-subsidy investigations from Western countries, how can Chinese automakers survive amid geopolitical turbulence? In this era of transformation led by artificial intelligence, how can autonomous driving technology help build smart cities, and how should it adapt to evolving ethical standards, legal frameworks, and consumer psychology?


The Intelligent Revolution

In an era of rapid advancement in artificial intelligence, technology not only transforms our lifestyles but also sparks boundless creativity and imagination. Join us to explore how AI is moving from theoretical research to widespread application. In this unique forum, experts from diverse fields will gather to engage in cross-disciplinary dialogues, together exploring the future of AI—from ethics and commercial applications to cultural creation and social governance—sparking new thinking and opportunities for collaboration. In this forum, we will not only discuss technological progress but also focus on AI's profound impact on social structures, cultural creation, and humanity's future way of life.






02

Food Upgrading and Sustainable Development Security Sub-Forum

"Among the eight policies in the Hong Fan, food comes first." Food is not only the foundation of people's basic survival but also a guarantee for social harmony, stability, and national long-term security. Currently, international political disputes and military conflicts are erupting in multiple regions, creating a complex global development and security landscape. Against the backdrop of rising anti-globalization sentiments and the clear increase of unilateralism and protectionism, how can we firmly hold our "rice bowl," respond to global transformations, allocate agricultural and industrial resources, regulate the balance between domestic production and imports, and ensure effective food supply and demand?


Meanwhile, climate change adds uncertainty to stable food supply. Rising temperatures, changing precipitation patterns, and increasing extreme weather events pose new challenges to food production. How should agricultural production adapt to these changes to achieve sustainable supply? Can gene-editing technologies and the development of stress-resistant crop varieties be trusted? What is the current status of smart irrigation and water resource utilization? Under climate change, what models of agricultural production should we pursue?


Beyond these challenges, our original mission of improving people's living standards must remain unchanged. Food is paramount to the people; diet, as a key factor affecting happiness and health, significantly influences people's well-being. Therefore, externally, how can we strengthen international cooperation on food security and build a global community of shared development? Internally, how can we adjust policies related to agriculture, rural areas, and farmers (the "three rurals"), how can the market drive innovation, and how can business models evolve to improve the efficiency and transparency of the food supply chain? These are pressing issues of our time.





03

Culture Sub-Forum

Cultural industrialization is not only about inheriting and disseminating traditional culture but also a key pathway to transform cultural resources into economic drivers. How can China's rich cultural resources be converted into economic momentum to promote innovation and development in the cultural industry? This forum will focus on a more inclusive and diverse perspective of the "Chinese nation," exploring how to industrialize traditional culture and how to integrate the cultural industry with technology, business, and other fields to create greater commercial value and social benefits.


China is a country with 56 different ethnic groups, rich cultural backgrounds, and a diverse yet unified cultural landscape. However, the international community's understanding of China remains relatively narrow and superficial, often accompanied by negative narratives about China's ethnic regions. Then, is cultural industrialization an effective way to overcome Western biases, tell China's stories well, and promote traditional culture globally?


Cultural industrialization is not merely about inheriting and innovating traditional culture, but also about driving the transformation of cultural resources into economic drivers. By integrating elements of art, business, and technology, it provides cultural creators with greater creative space and opportunities, while bringing new growth points and vitality to the socio-economy. How to achieve economic benefits while preserving cultural uniqueness, and how to strike a balance between cultural inheritance and commercial operations, are questions we need to consider and discuss.


In recent years, Chinese youth have increasingly demonstrated "cultural confidence," supporting domestic brands. A growing number of homegrown brands featuring traditional culture and aesthetic heritage have emerged. In the 2024 Zhengjing Guolun Culture Sub-Forum, we discussed how contemporary China can overcome Western biases, tell China's stories effectively, and promote traditional culture globally. This year's culture sub-forum aims to go further, exploring how to build and promote a narrative of the "Chinese nation" both domestically and internationally. How can traditional culture be industrialized? How can the cultural industry integrate with technology, business, and other fields to create greater commercial and social value?
