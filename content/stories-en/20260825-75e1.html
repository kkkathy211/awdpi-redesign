    Why "Light and Shadow"?


    In contemporary Western contexts, Asian women often occupy a position of "visible, yet not fully understood." They are seen, defined, but do not necessarily have sufficient opportunities to tell their own stories.


    Therefore, AWDPI uses portrait photography and first-person narratives as media to create a dialogue between the "being seen" image and the "self-telling" voice, attempting to rethink:


    When we see a person, do we truly hear her? We hope this project brings life experiences that are often overlooked into public discourse, so that Asian women are not just an identity label, but real, specific, and vivid individuals.


    From seeing, to listening.


    The event was organized and hosted by Wendy, head of AWDPI's UK Office.


    The image on the left shows host Wendy; the image on the right shows guest Dr. Li.




    The event invited


    Dr. Li to share her life story. From shifts in identity within cross-cultural environments to personal growth and choices, she shared how she came to re-understand herself across different cultures through authentic experiences and reflections. Other participants also shared their moments of confusion, exploration, breakthroughs, and growth in life and career through audio recordings and online interviews.


    Different lives, yet carrying similar inner strength.


    Echoes in the Shadows: Online Life Portraits


    The event also showcased the interim outcomes of the


    "Echoes in the Shadows: Online Life Portraits" project. Selected online portraits


    Through images, text, and sound, we document diverse women's expressions of their life experiences.


    It takes only moments to draw a stroke, but to understand a life, we must quiet our minds and read the story behind the lines.

        Brand introduction

        AWDPI Asian Women Development Plan International (Asian Women Development Plan International) is an international nonprofit organization dedicated to eliminating discrimination and gender-based violence against Asian women living overseas, and promoting their empowerment and development. The organization evolved from Avoice (Overseas Chinese Women Protection Project), established in 2020. AWDPI advocates for attention to the challenges faced by Asian women abroad, providing them with sustained protection and support to help transform them from a marginalized group into a vital force driving social progress. In this project, AWDPI is responsible for the planning and execution of the offline exhibition, as well as portrait photography and voice archive collection for 16 Asian women participants.


        Chinese Culture Week (CCW)  is an international cultural and arts platform based in Edinburgh, held during the Edinburgh Festival Fringe, aiming to connect artists from China, Chinese cultural backgrounds, and diverse global regions with international audiences through theatre, dance, music, exhibitions, traditional crafts, workshops, and cultural exchange activities.


        The official organizer of CCW is Intercultural Connections Initiative (ICI) , a Community Interest Company (CIC) registered in Scotland, primarily focused on cultural exchange, artistic innovation, and education. Officially, CCW is positioned as a platform connecting artists, cultural institutions, and audiences from China, the UK, and around the world.


        Viva la Vida (VIVA) is a global cross-cultural arts brand operating in over 60 countries, committed to using art to document life stories, advance cross-cultural understanding, and support social good. We invite people from diverse cultural backgrounds to answer the question "What is your life?" through a single artwork, thereby recording diverse life stories and fostering cross-cultural understanding. Over its eight years of operation, VIVA has partnered with organizations such as the United Nations, Coca-Cola, and Tencent to host over 400 events, collecting more than 20,000 life portraits and transforming individual expressions into bridges for cultural dialogue.
