Foreword



"The Moment of Lift" is a book written by Melinda Gates. It tells stories of women around the world who actively strive, support one another, and change their destinies. In these gripping stories, we not only come to understand the difficult journeys women have endured in defending their rights, but are also moved and inspired by the radiant strength these resilient women display when refusing to compromise. No woman is insignificant. Every woman deserves respect. Each possesses unique strength, and through effort, they change their own lives and the lives of others.


In her "Letter to Chinese Readers," Melinda Gates shared a story about Chinese scientist Tu Youyou. At age sixteen, Tu Youyou contracted tuberculosis and fortunately recovered. This experience inspired her to become a pharmaceutical researcher. In 1972, Tu Youyou's team discovered artemisinin, an innovative treatment for malaria, and she was awarded the Nobel Prize in Medicine in 2015. In China and around the world, there are many women like Tu Youyou—for example, Principal Zhang Guimei, who transformed the educational fate of impoverished girls; and Mother Teresa, who served society's most vulnerable, practicing respect, care, and love. It is precisely such outstanding role models that inspire us to continue forging ahead on the path of women's empowerment, overcoming obstacles and blazing new trails.

Lifting Their Heads—Women's Education



"If a woman can raise her self-worth, she can change the cultural environment that oppresses her. But most women cannot do this alone—they need support."


Undoubtedly, education is the first and most crucial step in advancing women's rights. With quality education and strong personal development, women can compete with men in the workplace and, through persistent effort, eventually become leaders. In doing so, they not only transform their own lives but also gain the ability to help other women change theirs. Good schools do more than teach academic knowledge—they broaden horizons, encourage kindness, foster independent thinking, and promote positive change. When women, through exceptional education, transform how they see themselves and other women, reshaping their inner sense of self, they open a new chapter—understanding that they and others possess equal rights, and learning to actively defend those rights.


Sister Sudha is a nun who founded a free boarding school. There, every girl learns karate and self-defense courses to protect herself from harm. She also tells her students that women should never place themselves at the bottom of the family hierarchy, and that husbands have no right to beat their wives. Every girl possesses the power to change her own destiny—so long as she believes in herself and takes action.

Creating a New Culture—Women in the Workplace



"When a woman hears her own voice in another woman's story, she becomes brave, and a single, faint voice becomes a chorus."

Research shows that in the workplace, women are more prone to self-doubt than men. Women often underestimate themselves, while men frequently overestimate their abilities. Men tend to be more confident, drawn to opportunities and risks, while women often make more conservative choices when opportunities arise.


In some corporate cultures, rules are set by men, and when women attempt to defend their rights in the face of unfair rules, they hesitate to speak up—because they are uncertain whether they will be supported. Often, only when many women stand shoulder to shoulder, hand in hand, do others truly feel inspired to come forward. And only when women gain power in the workplace, shaping a new workplace culture that promotes gender equality and fairness, enhances inclusivity and multiculturalism, can the status of women in the workplace truly be transformed, allowing every working woman to shine with her brightest light and reveal her true strength.

Male Allies



"Male allies" specifically refer to men who actively advocate for women's rights. Women's empowerment is not solely women's responsibility—it also requires support from men. From a sustainable perspective, empowering women contributes to the development and progress of the entire society, and naturally benefits men as well.


CARE's Pathways program, an international anti-poverty initiative, not only teaches farmers agricultural knowledge but also promotes gender equality among them. Patricia had been solely responsible for the heavy farm work at home, yet the income she earned was meager. She invited her husband to join the Pathways program, where they played a "role-reversal" game in class. This exercise completely transformed their lives—her husband began listening to her opinions, worked with her to increase crop yields, and jointly took out loans with her to purchase improved seeds. That year, they achieved a bountiful harvest and earned substantial income, greatly improving their family's living conditions.


Therefore, men should work together with women to eliminate the barriers and biases that hinder women's development.

"Please draw near to those on the margins of society, so they are not forgotten by the world."


Bringing women together is where empowerment begins. When women come together, raise their voices, and defend their rights, women's empowerment begins. When women trapped in hardship are isolated from others, it is difficult for them to solve problems alone. But when women accept and appreciate one another, we can find strength in each other. Most importantly, every woman must feel love and respect. Equality is just the starting point, not the end goal. Equality is not about opposition, but about better connection. Through the power of women, we can tear down walls, open doors, and create a world filled with love.
