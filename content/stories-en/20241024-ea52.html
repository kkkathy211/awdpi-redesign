From enduring domestic violence to being forced to fight back, Xiaoqing rediscovered courage and strength amid pain, endurance, isolation, and doubt.


Her story is not only a record of personal resistance but also an inspiration of hope, reminding us to reclaim our ability to dwell in this world in the face of systemic violence, to speak for remembrance, to unite for liberation.


Far from home and nearly helpless, Xiaoqing sat weeping in the emergency room, holding her baby whose face was swollen and burning with fever—a scene she will never forget.


The child had just turned one month old, and Xiaoqing's body had not yet recovered, but her self-serving ex-husband cared nothing for this, taking advantage of her vulnerability and plunging her into endless suffering.


1

The web of lies: exposing the true face of the "perfect" suitor.


Xiaoqing worked in the Department of Electronic Engineering and was once a researcher at the University of Nebraska–Lincoln.


After arriving in Lincoln, USA in 2022, she met Xin, a PhD student in the School of Mechanical and Materials Engineering at the same university, during an academic exchange. Xin seemed instantly infatuated with her, pursuing her relentlessly from their first meeting. They soon married, and on Xin's suggestion began preparing for pregnancy and building a new family.



The first incident of domestic violence occurred the night before childbirth, when Xiaoqing suddenly realized that the husband she trusted was nothing but a carefully disguised demon. On the day of delivery, Xin committed the second act of domestic violence. Just five days after giving birth, as soon as Xiaoqing was discharged from the hospital and returned home, the third act of violence struck again.


She couldn't help but wonder how the once-perfect suitor could have become this, only to realize that signs had long been evident: Xiaoqing met the eligibility criteria for a green card; after establishing their relationship, Xin pressured her to apply for U.S. visas for his parents and a green card for himself, and would turn hostile whenever she lacked the energy to manage both work and the complex application processes. Xiaoqing's position as a researcher and her outstanding academic abilities provided Xin with valuable academic resources and networks—an immense advantage to him. Even in her third trimester, Xiaoqing was still doing experiments, writing PowerPoint presentations, and preparing group meeting reports for Xin. It is likely that Xin approached Xiaoqing primarily for access to academic resources and U.S. residency.


2

Accomplices in the hunt: was there no escape? Was there still a way out?


Generally speaking, domestic violence refers to physical, sexual, psychological, or economic violence or threats inflicted by one party upon the other within marriage or other intimate relationships. Due to familial ties and vested interests, such violence often carries a degree of concealment, causing these harmful acts to be covered up or suppressed, with outsiders frequently choosing to stand by and refrain from intervening or offering assistance.[1] 


For Xiaoqing, however, this violence extended beyond the household, becoming more like a premeditated hunt aided by a series of accomplices and harassers. Hunt.



After the third incident of domestic violence, Xin's parents produced a pre-drafted agreement demanding Xiaoqing and her family sign away all ties to the child in exchange for $200,000. When coercion failed, Xin's family contacted a local Asian association, spreading rumors, defamation, and slander against Xiaoqing and her family within the Chinese community. When Xiaoqing sought help from Xin's academic advisor, the advisor chose to continue covering up Xin's academic misconduct and offered Xiaoqing no response. The university remained indifferent and unresponsive. Xin's affair partner continuously smeared Xiaoqing on social media and threatened suicide. Xin's mother—the child's grandmother—went so far as to circulate numerous unredacted photos of the infant to protect her own image.


It is worth noting that when experiencing domestic violence, one should quickly preserve evidence and seek help from law enforcement agencies, women's associations, or domestic violence protection organizations, and should avoid seeking help within the abuser's network of relationships.


After multiple failed attempts to seek help, overwhelmed by immense work, life, and mental pressure, Xiaoqing sank deeper into despair. In July, Xiaoqing reached out to AWDPI AWDPI, and things slowly began to change.


3

A glimmer of dawn: legal protection and social support.


Through AWDPI, Xiaoqing obtained information about relevant legal aid and local women's support associations. With the help of the local association, Xiaoqing finally resolved the issue of her child's identification documents, which had been blocked due to the man's refusal to acknowledge the relationship. The child was able to return to China with Xiaoqing's parents, and court-ordered child support payments from the man began to be enforced.



The local women's association in the U.S. also secured some financial subsidies for the child and provided free diapers and formula. These tangible forms of assistance deeply moved Xiaoqing.


At first, Xiao Qing thought that only if the man acknowledged his relationship with the child and cooperated with the relevant procedures, could the child's identification documents and travel documents be approved, and child support would be paid through mutual agreement. During the process of defending her rights, she gradually became familiar with the legal provisions and procedures: according to U.S. law, as long as there is a biological relationship between the child and the man, child support would be enforced by law.


Xiaoqing's ability to persist in resistance stemmed not only from her own strength and love for her child but also from the support of her parents and the local community. Below are some organizations in the U.S. that can provide assistance to those experiencing domestic violence, offering services such as shelter, psychological counseling, legal aid, and financial support, which help victims rebuild their lives:


1.The National Domestic Violence Hotline (NDVH)

Provides 24-hour hotline services to help victims find emergency shelters, legal aid, and psychological support.

For more information, call:

1-800-799-SAFE(7233)

or visit:

thehotline.org


2.National Coalition Against Domestic Violence (NCADV)

A national organization in the U.S. that provides education, prevention, and policy advocacy, and connects local support services through its network.

For more information, visit:

ncadv.org


3. Women Against Abuse

An organization based in Philadelphia that offers comprehensive support including shelters, legal aid, and counseling.

For more information, visit:

womenagainstabuse.org


4.Futures Without Violence

This organization provides resources and information to assist domestic violence victims and advocates for policy reform.

For more information, visit:

futureswithoutviolence.org


5.Safe Horizon

One of the largest victim assistance organizations in the U.S., providing shelters, legal services, crisis intervention, and support, based in New York.

For more information, visit:

safehorizon.org


6.Asian Pacific Institute on Gender-Based Violence (APIGBV)

Primarily provides support to domestic violence victims in Asian and Pacific Islander communities.

For more information, visit:

api-gbv.org


4

After the dawn breaks, the sky clears.


Xiaoqing's core is undoubtedly strong and stable. When faced with accusations of "seeking sympathy by playing the single mother card," she firmly believes "Being a single mother is not a sign of weakness; becoming one is itself an act of bravery and defiance." Even as she repeatedly confronts past trauma, she bravely breaks the silence: "If I once fell into a deep pit and now lack the strength to fill it, at least I can present the full truth, to warn other girls so they won't follow in my footsteps and fall into the same pit."


Regarding girls who have suffered sexual violence, Xiaoqing believes: "One should still find the courage when it's time to be brave, and actively protect one's own interests. Don't think you can simply turn the page—the harm already done cannot be completely erased. In many cases, violent incidents are foreseeable; don't wait until the final moment of harm to realize the severity of the situation, but instead raise your awareness as early as possible." Xiaoqing also expressed that the help she received from women's protection organizations showed her the power of female solidarity. She hopes to join international women's rights organizations to offer encouragement and support to more women.


Currently, Xiaoqing continues to strive in the U.S., fighting for a better future for herself and her child. We also believe that her life, like her name, will after this dawn, be filled with clear skies ahead.
