UN Women has warned that the expanding "manosphere" in online communities poses a serious threat to gender equality. As a UN agency dedicated to eliminating gender discrimination, UN Women considers the "manosphere" a harmful online group whose presence is increasingly influencing people's attitudes, behaviors, and decisions in the real world.





UN Women points out that the global internet user base has surpassed 5.5 billion, meaning nearly everyone uses social media, and digital platforms have become central hubs for communication.


However, these platforms are now being turned into weapons for spreading misogyny and hate. Once confined to the fringes of online forums, the "manosphere" is now extending its harm into schools and workplaces, and even beginning to damage intimate relationships.


Kalliopi Mingeirou, Head of the Ending Violence against Women and Girls unit at UN Women, said: "We are seeing more and more young men and boys turning to online influencers for guidance on masculinity—on dating, fitness, fatherhood, and more."


Young men seeking responses that make them feel more confident and secure encounter online communities that promote "strength," and these groups spread distorted notions of masculinity and incite misogyny, allowing these harmful ideas to flourish.


Young men seek "identity and belonging" in the digital world


Mingeirou told UN News: "These online spaces exploit young men's insecurities and their need for belonging by spreading messages that degrade the status of women and girls. These views are often filled with misogyny, such as discrediting feminist activists and constructing negative stereotypes."


According to data from Movember Foundation, a leading men's health organization and partner of UN Women, about two-thirds of young men online frequently interact with influencers who promote traditional masculinity.



While some of this content does offer support, much of it continues to promote extreme views and gender discrimination, reinforcing the false belief that men are victims of feminism and social equity movements.


The latest report from the UN Secretariat, "Eliminating All Forms of Violence against Women and Girls," highlights that anti-feminism and portraying women as "controlling" or "dangerous" are common features across various "manosphere" communities.


Social media algorithms amplify inflammatory content and divisive viewpoints, enabling these distorted narratives to gain increasing visibility.


The poison of misogyny harms everyone, regardless of gender


Mingeirou emphasized that the anonymity of the internet facilitates the spread of gender-based discrimination and hate speech. She noted that such rhetoric not only harms the mental and physical health of adolescents but also "poses a serious threat to democratic institutions as a whole."



"The risks and threats posed by digital platforms are steadily eroding women's and girls' sense of safety. We frequently see female journalists and women in politics withdrawing from public discourse due to online abuse."


Mingeirou further pointed out that stereotypes also trigger anxiety and harm men. She stressed the need to create safe online spaces where everyone can receive proper guidance and be protected from the toxicity of misogyny.


The harm of misogyny spreads, and the real world bears the pressure


The "manosphere," lurking in cyberspace, is already infiltrating the real world with its harmful ideologies. Gradually and subtly, mainstream culture and political spheres are being affected. People are beginning to downplay gender-based violence, and gender-discriminatory stereotypes are taking root and spreading throughout society.


In extreme cases, misogynistic ideas intertwine with racism, homophobia, and authoritarianism, merging into a dangerous force. Moreover, online misogyny has spilled out of screens and into real-life society.


Mingeirou further stated: "Growing evidence shows that perpetrators of mass shootings or extreme acts of violence in communities have often been long-term participants in online spaces like the 'manosphere.' The misogynistic views they propagate combine with other extremist ideologies, posing a threat to all communities."


Although different "manosphere" groups may disagree with each other, they share a common trait: viewing feminism as a threat, portraying women as controlling, and depicting men as victims of social equity reforms. Because algorithms prioritize sensational and extremely biased content, the ideologies within the "manosphere" continue to spread and gain increasing acceptance among young men and boys. Meanwhile, the discourse constructed by the "manosphere" is no longer confined to a corner of the digital world—it quietly influences how people think, who they vote for, and how they treat others.


Taking human rights-based responses


In 1995, the Fourth World Conference on Women in Beijing adopted the Beijing Declaration and Platform for Action. Looking back at this milestone in the global women's movement, UN Women stated that the rise of misogyny in cyberspace directly threatens the progress achieved in gender equality.


Therefore, UN Women will take the following steps to intensify actions against harmful digital environments:


  • Collect data and conduct research: Analyze the spread and impact of online hate speech.

  • Support policies and strengthen regulation:: Improve oversight systems to safeguard digital spaces.

  • Follow up and provide volunteer support: Offer assistance to victims of online violence.

  • Educate the public and reshape mindsets: Strengthen education and guidance to help people move beyond harmful notions of masculinity.

  • Empower youth and promote equality: Enhance digital resilience and advance gender equality.

  • Mobilize media and encourage responsibility: Call on media to take further action against harmful online groups.


Education as the solution to prevent misogyny


Fundamentally, education is an effective way to dismantle misogyny and eradicate its roots. Engaging children and adolescents in discussions about gender equality, healthy relationships, and digital citizenship is crucial to preventing the spread of harmful ideologies.


"This is not just about protecting girls," Mingeirou noted, "but about creating a world where both boys and girls can grow up healthy, free from the poisoning of harmful gender norms.". "
