The United Nations' theme for International Women's Day 2024 is "Invest in Women: Accelerate Progress." By 2030, the annual funding gap for gender equality initiatives could reach as high as $36 billion, making it urgent to advance financing for gender equality.


During the United Nations 2023 International Day for the Elimination of Violence against Women "16 Days of Activism," we hosted five online workshops, discussing topics such as "Occupational Illnesses through a Gender Lens" with over 300 audience members. The 2024 International Women's Day theme continues the same thread as the 2023 International Day for the Elimination of Violence against Women: "Eliminating gender-based violence through adequate funding." Without significant investment to address violence against women and girls, expand prevention programs, and implement effective policies, countries will not be able to eliminate gender-based violence by 2030.


This year, AWDPI Asian Women Development Plan International will host six workshops across China, Germany, Ecuador, and the United States during International Women's Day and Women's History Month, standing in solidarity with people around the world who care about gender issues and expressing support for survivors of gender-based violence.


# This Workshop Series



On January 16, 2024, Chen Moumou, a Google engineer, was arrested at his home in Silicon Valley, California. When police arrived, his wife, Yu Mou, was already dead. The investigation revealed that Chen had repeatedly punched Yu in the head the previous night, resulting in her death.


This shocking domestic violence case once again exposed a bloody reality within millions of Chinese diaspora families in the United States. Under layers of oppression including employment, racial, and gender discrimination, even more Chinese women—those with language barriers, low education levels, disabilities, or advanced age—continue to suffer silently from intimate partner violence.


In this workshop, AWDPI has invited Gina, Associate Professor at the Center for the Study of Gender and Sexuality at the University of Chicago, We will use the case of the Google engineer who killed his wife in the U.S. as a starting point to explore the definitions of domestic violence and gender-based violence, understand the physical and mental health impacts on Chinese women who experience violence, discuss how racial and immigration status discrimination affect their ability to seek help, as well as the broader impact of such discrimination on the entire community, and finally, we will explore how to better support those seeking help from gender-based violence.



Unmasking Domestic Violence Among Chinese Americans: How to Better Support Marginalized Population

 

On January 16th, 2024, Google software engineer Liren Chen was arrested under domestic abuse and first-degree murder in his house in Santa Clara, CA. He was accused of beating his wife to death in their home in Santa Clara, California. Chen had admitted to punching Yu on her head until her last breath and ultimately causing her fatal death during the instigation.


Chen and Yu had everything any immigrant to the U.S. could ever wish for – a master's degree from an internationally renowned institution, a top-tier job with a decent salary at Google, and a long-term relationship that dated back to their undergraduate days.


For many, it is hard to believe domestic violence could have happened in today's society, let alone in a family like this. The truth is that there are even many more undereducated, non-English speaking, disabled, and/or elderly Chinese women struggling under the double oppression of intimate relationship violence and economic, racial and gender discrimination. 


As we celebrate International Women's Day and Women's History Month, AWDPI has invited Associate Professor Gina at the University of Chicago, Crown Family School of Social Work, Policy and Practice. We will use the Google engineer murder case as a starting point to explore the definitions of domestic violence and gender-based violence. We aim to understand the impact of violence on physical and psychological health, discuss how discrimination based on race and immigration status can have significant impacts on individuals' willingness and ability to seek help, and explore how such discrimination affects the community in the long run. Finally, we will discuss how to better support survivors of gender-based violence.
