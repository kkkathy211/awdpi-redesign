Brooks International Fellowship Programme 

2026 – Gender and Queer Ecologies

Swipe right to view Chinese

Project type

Fellowship

Host institution

Delfina Foundation, in partnership with Tate (Tate Modern Curatorial Division), supported by the Rory and Elizabeth Brooks Foundation.  The programme brings international visual-arts practitioners to London for six-month research fellowships embedded in Tate and Delfina's residency programme. 

Overview

This fully funded fellowship invites an international practitioner to work with Tate Modern's International Art and Conservation teams on the theme "Gender and Queer Ecologies." The Fellow will reassess early modernist works (c.1860–1945) in Tate's international collection, highlighting overlooked artworks and artists whose practices challenge gender hierarchies and explore bodies, sexuality and ecology. The fellowship combines curatorial research, archival and conservation work, and public-facing presentations, aiming to update collection interpretations through gender and queer theory, decolonial discourse and transnational perspectives. 

Location

London, United Kingdom

Residency at Delfina Foundation; research placement at Tate Modern with visits to other Tate sites in the UK.

Duration and format

Six-month full-time fellowship: 22 June–6 December 2 026 (approximately 36 hours per week). 

The Fellow divides time between Tate (around four days per week) and Delfina Foundation (around one day per week), with a busy schedule of research, internal meetings, seminars, professional-development activities and public events. This is a residential opportunity: the Fellow lives at Delfina Foundation with other residents for the entire period.

Eligibility

The fellowship is aimed at international practitioners based outside the UK who have established experience in visual arts or closely related fields and can demonstrate significant research or curatorial work relevant to gender and queer ecologies. Candidates should have:

  • Demonstrable expertise and ongoing research related to the theme "Gender and Queer Ecologies"

  • Experience with art-historical, curatorial, archival or provenance research

  • Interest in museum-based research and collection interpretation

  • Established professional practice (often mid-career), though formal postgraduate degrees are not required

  • Ability to work collaboratively in a large, multi-site museum and adapt to a busy institutional environment

  • Good working knowledge of English; reading ability in French, Italian or Spanish is desirable but not essential

  • Availability to live and work in London for the full fellowship period (22 June – 6 December 2026) 

There is a second fellowship track ("Ecology of Migrating Identities") under the same programme; candidates may apply to one or both, but can only hold one fellowship. 

Benefits

Selected Fellows receive:

  • Six-month full-time research fellowship at Tate Modern Curatorial, with supported travel to Tate Britain, Tate Liverpool and Tate St Ives and other UK cultural institutions

  • Participation in Delfina Foundation's residency programme, including guided visits, crits, portfolio reviews, and professional-development sessions

  • A single bedroom at Delfina Foundation's communal residence in central London (shared kitchen, library, dining room and other facilities)

  • Return economy-class flight to London and airport transfers in the UK

  • Visa fee reimbursement and support with the visa process (if required)

  • Daily living allowance of £40 and a London local travel pass valid for Underground and buses

  • Access to Tate's collections, archives, staff and networks

  • Opportunities to present research at Tate and Delfina events and to contribute to wider institutional activities and publications 

Application materials

Applicants must complete the official Application Form and Diversity Monitoring Information Form available on Tate's "Working at Tate" page for this opportunity. The open-call document specifies that:

  • Candidates should use the official Word application form (not PDF)

  • CVs or additional attachments are not accepted and will not be seen by the panel

  • Applicants must respond to specific questions about their experience, research interests and motivation in relation to the fellowship theme

  • Alternative formats (video or audio) are accepted for applicants who prefer not to submit written responses, provided they follow the same word-limit guidance

  • Completed applications must be submitted by email to [email protected]

Cost

The fellowship is fully funded. There is no programme fee. The fellowship provides travel, accommodation, visa reimbursement (if needed), daily living allowance and local transport in London. Fellows are expected to cover any personal additional expenses beyond what is outlined in the benefits. 

Deadline

Application deadline: Thursday, 8 January 2026, 17:00 (5 p.m.) GMT.

Indicative timeline: shortlisted candidates are expected to be contacted by 21 January 2026; online interviews will take place between 28 January and 20 February 2026; successful candidates will be notified by 2 March 2026. Fellowship dates are 22 June – 6 December 2026. 

Keywords

gender and queer ecologies; gender studies; queer theory; museum research; curatorial fellowship; modernist art; migration and identity; London arts residency

Gender and Queer Ecologies

Programme Type

Scholarship/Research Residency Programme (fellowship)

Issuing Organization

Co-hosted by Delfina Foundation and Tate Modern Curatorial Division, with support from the Rory and Elizabeth Brooks Foundation. The programme supports visual arts practitioners from around the world through a six-month residency and in-house research collaboration between Tate and Delfina. (Tate Careers website)

Programme Overview

This fellowship is fully funded and invites one international practitioner to join the International Art and Curatorial team at Tate Modern to conduct research on the theme of "Gender and Queer Ecologies." The selected fellow will focus on Tate's international modernist collection from 1860–1945, uncovering artworks and artists overlooked in existing scholarship, with attention to how bodies challenge gender hierarchies, binary divisions, and ecological relations. Combining curatorial, archival, and conservation research with public engagement, the programme aims to renew narratives and interpretations of Tate's collection through gender and queer theory, decolonial perspectives, and transnational research. (Tate Careers website)

Location

London, United Kingdom

The residency is based at Delfina Foundation in central London, with research primarily conducted at Tate Modern and access to Tate venues in other UK cities.

Duration and Format

The programme lasts six months, from 22 June 2026 to 6 December 2026, full-time (approximately 36 hours per week). The fellow spends approximately four days per week conducting research and working within the Tate team, and one day participating in Delfina Foundation's residency and professional development activities. The programme is a concentrated residency; the fellow resides at Delfina Foundation throughout, living and engaging with other resident artists and researchers. (Tate Careers website)

Eligibility Criteria

The programme is open to international practitioners residing outside the UK who have substantial experience in visual arts or related fields, and who can demonstrate a clear research foundation and practical engagement with the theme of "Gender and Queer Ecologies." Key requirements include:

Demonstrated interest and sustained research in themes related to "Gender and Queer Ecologies"

  • Experience in art history, curating, archives, or provenance research

  • Interest in museum studies and collection interpretation

  • Typically mid-career practitioners with significant professional experience (a master's or doctoral degree is not mandatory)

  • Ability to collaborate within large, multi-site institutions and adapt to a fast-paced team environment

  • Strong working proficiency in English; knowledge of French, Italian, or Spanish is beneficial but not required

  • Ability to reside and work in London for the full duration of the programme, from 22 June 2026 to 6 December 2026

Another fellowship position under the same programme focuses on "Ecologies of Migrant Identities." Applicants may apply to both themes, but can only be awarded one fellowship. (Tate Careers website)

Programme Benefits

The selected fellow will receive:

  • A six-month full-time research opportunity with Tate Modern's curatorial team, including supported visits to other Tate venues and partner institutions across the UK

  • Participation in Delfina Foundation's residency programme, including guided visits, workshops, critiques, and professional development activities

  • A private bedroom at Delfina Foundation in central London, with shared access to kitchen, library, and dining areas

  • Round-trip economy airfare to London and local airport transfer

  • Visa fee reimbursement and support with visa application, if required

  • A daily living allowance of £40 and a London public transport travel pass

  • Access to Tate's collections, archives, and professional networks

  • Opportunities to present research outcomes at events hosted by Tate and Delfina, and to participate in related publications and public programmes (Tate Careers website)

Application Materials

Applicants must download and complete the official application form and diversity monitoring form from Tate's "Working at Tate" webpage. The open call specifies:

  • The official Word version of the application form must be used; do not convert it to PDF

  • CVs or other attachments are not accepted; the selection committee will not review any attachments

  • Applicants must respond to questions in the form regarding their experience, research proposal, motivation, and specifically address the theme of this fellowship

  • If submitting a written response is difficult, applicants may choose to respond via video or audio following the provided guidelines, but must adhere to word limits

  • Upon completion, the application form should be emailed to [email protected]  

Fees

The programme is fully funded; no tuition or programme fees are required. The organizers cover round-trip airfare, accommodation in London, visa costs (if applicable), local transport card, and living allowance. Applicants are responsible only for personal expenses.

Application Deadline

Application deadline: 17:00 GMT on 8 January 2026.

Tentative timeline: Shortlisted candidates notified by 21 January 2026; online interviews held between 28 January and 20 February 2026; final results announced by 2 March 2026. Programme implementation period: 22 June 2026 to 6 December 2026.

Interns will assist with cataloguing GBV tools and protocols, maintaining a global repository, facilitating south-south learning, and supporting program follow-up, reporting and coordination across country/regional offices.

"Women at the Centre" Project Internship 

— United Nations Population Fund (UNFPA)

Gender-Based Violence (GBV) Response Team

• Program Type

Internship

• Issuing Organization

United Nations Population Fund (UNFPA) — the United Nations agency responsible for reproductive health, youth development, gender equality, and the elimination of gender-based violence.

• Program Overview

This internship supports UNFPA's Gender-Based Violence Response Team under the "Women at the Centre" initiative, which operates within the Gender, Human Rights, and Inclusion Division. The focus is on strengthening global case management systems for gender-based violence, enhancing the capacity of social service workforces, and building a global knowledge-sharing and technical support platform. Interns will assist in organizing GBV tools and protocols, maintaining global resource databases, promoting South-South learning, and supporting project follow-up, report drafting, and coordination with country and regional offices.

• Location

Fully remote (global)

• Duration and Format

Up to 24 weeks; full-time

• Eligibility Requirements

• Must have fluent English language ability

• Fluency in French is preferred; proficiency in other UN languages (Arabic, Spanish) is an asset

• Strong analytical and writing skills

• Prior experience in gender equality or GBV programs is preferred

• Experience in knowledge management platform development is a plus

• Program Benefits

• Gain practical experience in gender equality and gender-based violence programs at the United Nations level

• Participate in global resource database development, knowledge sharing, and capacity-building initiatives

• Build connections with UNFPA's global team and country/ regional offices

• Enhance skills in project support, report writing, and coordination management

• Application Materials

Resume/CV; cover letter; writing sample related to knowledge management or gender/GBV fields (if available); language proficiency statement

• Fees

No application fee. Unless otherwise stated, interns are responsible for their own travel, accommodation, and insurance costs

• Application Deadline
