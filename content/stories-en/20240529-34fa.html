 Founders' original intentions

 @Yangdi @Jia 


Hi! Hello everyone! I'm Yan Yangdi!

I am a former project coordinator (UNV) for the UNDP Nanyang Poverty Reduction and Sustainable Development Demonstration Project, a Movers mentor, volunteer teacher for the HerDigitalFuture Camp, holder of a bachelor's degree in Women's Studies from China Women's University, a master's degree in Development Studies from the University of Melbourne, and currently studying psychology at Renmin University of China..


In August 2023, while still serving as a UNV volunteer implementing UNDP's first localized sustainable development project in Nanyang County, Henan Province, inspired by UNDP's HerDigitalFuture initiative, I co-organized a one-week HerDigitalFuture Camp for 10 girls from Nanyang together with over eight other young university teachers.


The camp not only enhanced these 10 rural girls' understanding of technological innovation, but also awakened their desire for social development and their aspirations to change the world. Within one week, through local field research and in-depth discussions, I worked with them to design a project called "Girl Planet," aiming to draw attention to the current mental health and cognitive development challenges faced by adolescents and children, and to help more young people achieve healthier psychological and physical development so they can grow into pillars of society.


(The video shows me invited to a summary event, presenting the girls' project design.)


Nearly a year has passed since this project, and many of the girls and even young teachers who participated still ask me, "Tudou" (my nickname), Will our project continue??


Hearing their voices, and also hearing the echo within myself, I want to keep moving forward, to carry on the children's original aspirations, and help more children and adolescents achieve healthy mental states and well-rounded cognitive development..


So, I reached out to Bao Jia, a Movers activist.




Hi! Hello everyone, I'm Bao Jia, a student of Diplomacy at Beijing Foreign Studies University, curator of Joy Reading Public Library, and also a partner at Ming Women Museum in Yunnan.

Myself, and also a girl, During my growth, I have also encountered confusion, uncertainty, the unknown, and challenges, and I deeply wish that during those dim moments, there would be "guide" to point me in the right direction, to take one step forward. Because I've seen, I understand. Because it's needed, I am brave. I hope more people will join us, to do concrete things, solve real problems, protect more blossoms, and light up a beautiful future!

We're all struggling to grow up, but somehow, in the busyness of life, we forget ourselves—forget why we started in the first place. The world we see may differ, but their world is also my world. I simply see the "me" of the past reflected in this world. I hope this beam of light can illuminate each of us, help us preserve our original aspirations, view ourselves with purity, bravely explore the world, and then live life earnestly. Because you're here, we can do even better, can't we?

 Social context and relevant policies



Among the global population of 1.2 billion adolescents aged 10 to 19, about 20% experience mental health issues.


With socioeconomic development, adolescent mental health has gradually become an issue that cannot be ignored. According to data jointly released in 2023 by UNICEF and the World Health Organization, about 20% of the world's 1.2 billion adolescents aged 10 to 19 suffer from mental health problems. Mental health issues account for approximately 16% of the diseases and injuries affecting adolescents aged 10 to 19. Every year, nearly 46,000 adolescents die by suicide globally, making suicide one of the top five causes of death among 10- to 19-year-olds. It is estimated that over 13% of adolescents aged 10 to 19 suffer from mental disorders as defined by the WHO; in upper-middle and high-income countries, nearly 20% of young people aged 15 to 24 frequently feel depressed or lose interest in things.　

In China, the "China National Mental Health Development Report (2021–2022)" released in February 2023 by the Institute of Psychology, Chinese Academy of Sciences, shows that the detection rate of depressive disorders among primary and secondary school students is also on the rise, at 13.5%, 12.3%, and 10.8% respectively. This trend was also confirmed by a 2022 survey conducted by the China Youth and Children Research Center, which surveyed 24,758 primary and secondary school students nationwide. The detection rates for anxiety and depression were as high as 31.3% and 17.9% respectively. Among fourth-grade students, the detection rate of severe depression increased by 0.9 percentage points compared to 2020, a nearly 50% rise, reaching 2.8%.

In response to the increasingly serious issue of adolescent mental health, strengthening mental health education for youth has become a shared consensus. However, at the societal level, problems such as lack of mental health knowledge, insufficient awareness, poor referral systems, and inadequate collaboration among stakeholders still persist. Cynthia McCaffrey, UNICEF Representative in China, emphasized: "Every child has the right to mental health. Although awareness of the impact of mental health issues is growing, stigma continues to prevent children and adolescents from seeking treatment, limiting their opportunities to grow, learn, and develop."



Promote the establishment of an effective, interconnected mental health service system involving families, schools, society, and professional institutions, improve service quality, and promote the healthy physical and mental development of children.


On the policy front, since 2022, the Chinese government and relevant departments have successively introduced a series of major measures to emphasize and strengthen mental health education for minors and students.


On January 1, 2022, the "Family Education Promotion Law of the People's Republic of China" officially came into effect, clearly stipulating that parents or other guardians of minors should not only pay attention to their physical health but also focus on their psychological development in family education. This is the first time China has enacted specific legislation on family education.


On October 16, 2022, the report of the 20th National Congress of the Communist Party of China proposed enhancing people's wellbeing and improving the quality of life. It called for advancing Healthy China initiatives and emphasizing mental health and mental hygiene.


On September 26, 2023, the National Health Commission and the Ministry of Education issued the "Notice on Conducting the 2023 World Mental Health Day Campaign," which designated the national theme for World Mental Health Day on October 10, 2023 as "Promoting Children's Mental Health, Jointly Protecting a Beautiful Future," and set three main goals: First, to popularize mental health knowledge through diverse promotional activities; second, to call on the whole of society to raise awareness and prioritize children and adolescents' mental health; third, to promote the establishment of an effective, interconnected mental health service system involving families, schools, society, and professional institutions, improve service quality, and promote the healthy physical and mental development of children.


In November 2023, the Ministry of Civil Affairs, the Ministry of Education, the National Health Commission, the Communist Youth League Central Committee, and the All-China Women's Federation jointly issued the "Guiding Opinions on Strengthening Mental Health Care Services for Children in Difficult Circumstances," requiring that mental health care services for children in difficult circumstances be placed in a more prominent position, strengthening care measures and improving service quality to better promote their healthy development.


From the "Family Education Promotion Law of the People's Republic of China," the "Notice on Conducting the 2023 World Mental Health Day Campaign," to the "Guiding Opinions on Strengthening Mental Health Care Services for Children in Difficult Circumstances," these successive documents emphasize the importance of mental health services for minors, particularly focusing on building systematic mental health service systems and caring for children in difficult circumstances.



 Project Introduction 

 @Shecho Mind




In a world undergoing great transformation, girls and adolescents especially need the courage to break through the darkness,, the hope to see the stars, and the ability to create the future.


Against this backdrop, by combining our professional backgrounds and experiences

and closely aligning with the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals,

SDG3, SDG4, and SDG5,



we launched the

[Tong Ni Xin Sheng ShechoMind project,

a platform that enables more young women to participate in

supporting girls' psychological and cognitive development and building global competencies,

through active engagement.


Target beneficiaries of the project: 10-1 children and adolescents aged 7 and above


Project slogan: Growing with You, Building the Future Together


Project values: Mutual learning,  Strong mind, integrity, openness, lifelong growth


Project mission and vision:

We hope to create a social support circle that fosters the comprehensive psychological and cognitive development of girls, enabling them to fully explore their potential, enjoy an equitable, healthy, and friendly growth environment, and contribute to the sustainable development of the world.

We are committed to providing professional psychological and cognitive education and learning resources, and coordinating various social forces to help girls develop positive mental health and cognitive growth mindsets, and jointly build a child-friendly growth ecosystem.

Main project activities

At this stage, the project is currently focused on activities related to girls' "mental health" and "cognitive development," as well as education on the Sustainable Development Goals. Key activities include:

· Anonymous letterbox for adolescent girls

· Research on adolescent girls' mental health issues

· Book Si Yi for Girls: Guided reading of books on women's psychology

· "Sister, What Should I Do?" emergency support

· Dialogues on the cognitive development journey of young leaders

· Bilingual guided reading of mental health books for children and adolescents

· SDG education Movers activities

· Rolling poetry and prose radio for children
