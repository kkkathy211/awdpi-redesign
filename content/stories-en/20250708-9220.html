Column Introduction:


In the past month of June, issues related to gender equality and Asian communities have remained focal points around the world. On gender, many countries have seen positive developments: the UK intelligence agency MI6 welcomed its first female leader in history; Zimbabwean Olympic champion Kirsty Coventry was elected as the first female president of the International Olympic Committee (IOC); and Cebu Province in the Philippines inaugurated its first female governor. These changes reflect the rising status of women in politics, sports, and public administration. However, the United Nations reminds us that in many developing countries, achieving gender equality still faces a funding shortfall, with an annual gap of up to 420 billion US dollars needing to be filled.


At the same time, incidents of violence against Asians remain concerning. In the Netherlands, a children's book containing biased content triggered protests by local Chinese residents; in Los Angeles, USA, Xinhua News Agency journalist Gao Shan was hit by a rubber bullet while covering a protest, becoming one of several injured journalists at the scene. Despite these challenges, Asian communities are finding their own ways to speak out: actor Daniel Dae Kim earned a Tony Award nomination for "Best Actor" for his role in *Yellow Face*, becoming the first Asian to receive such a nomination; South Asian Muslim politician Zohran Mamdani won the New York City primary election; and Filipina Gina Ortiz Jones was elected mayor of San Antonio, Texas. These efforts are making Asian communities increasingly visible on cultural, media, and political stages, demonstrating their active pursuit of representation and voice in diverse societies.

United Nations: 420 Billion Dollar Gap

Hinders Global Gender Equality Progress


On June 30, at the Fourth International Conference on Financing for Development (FfD4) held in Seville, Spain, UN Women released a report stating: Developing countries face an annual funding shortfall of approximately 420 billion US dollars in achieving gender equality, a gap that is seriously undermining progress toward the 2030 Sustainable Development Goals.


The report emphasizes that although many countries have made policy commitments to support women's development, actual financial investment remains far from sufficient. Currently, only about one-quarter of countries have effective gender-responsive budgeting systems that can clearly track how much government spending goes toward women-related programs. Many women still lack adequate support in education, health care, childcare, and employment.


UN Women calls on countries to allocate more public resources to services that support women, such as childcare, eldercare, and women's entrepreneurship. This not only improves women's living conditions but also creates numerous new jobs and promotes overall economic development. The conference also adopted the *Seville Commitment*, with countries agreeing unanimously to accelerate the implementation of fiscal policies related to gender equality and ensure funds reach the women and girls most in need.

Cebu Province in the Philippines Makes Historic Breakthrough:

First Female Governor Takes Office


On June 30, the Philippines marked a significant moment in local politics: Pam Baricuatro was sworn in as governor of Cebu Province, becoming the first woman to hold this position since the province's establishment. Previously, she served as head of a charitable organization and civil servant, dedicating herself for years to food rescue, education promotion, and services for vulnerable groups, earning broad support in grassroots communities.


In this election, Baricuatro defeated Gwendolyn Garcia, a long-serving political veteran, winning an overwhelming majority with her campaign slogan of "close to the people" and "rebuilding trust." In her inaugural speech, she pledged to establish more women's empowerment programs and encourage women's participation in local public affairs.


Baricuatro's election not only symbolizes progress in gender representation in Philippine politics but also provides a positive example for women's political participation in Southeast Asia and contributes a new local practice case to the global gender equality agenda. inject new local practice cases.

UK Intelligence System Welcomes First Female Leader


On June 18, the UK government officially announced the appointment of Blaise Metreweli as the new head of the Secret Intelligence Service (MI6), making her the first woman to hold the position of "C" (Chief) since the agency's founding in 1909. This appointment was personally announced by UK Prime Minister Keir Starmer, symbolizing an important step toward greater diversity and inclusion in the country's intelligence and security system.


Blaise Metreweli previously served as MI6's Director of Technology and Innovation ("Q") and held senior roles in MI5. She will formally succeed current chief Richard Moore in the autumn, reporting directly to the Foreign Secretary and assuming full command of intelligence operations.

First Female President Elected at International Olympic Committee


On June 23, Zimbabwean former Olympic champion Kirsty Coventry officially assumed the role of President of the International Olympic Committee (IOC), becoming the first woman to lead the organization since its founding in 1894, as well as the first leader from Africa, marking a significant step forward for diversity within the Olympic system. The 41-year-old athlete represented Zimbabwe in five Olympic Games, winning two gold, four silver, and one bronze medal, making her


one of the most successful female athletes in African history. She joined the IOC Athletes' Commission in 2013, later serving as its chair, and has worked for many years on the Executive Board, accumulating extensive experience in international sports governance. In her inaugural speech, Coventry emphasized: "Women are ready to lead." She stated that she will work to promote more


women into leadership roles in global sports and hopes to use her experience to create more opportunities for African women. This appointment is not only a symbolic breakthrough but also seen as a strong push forward for

Amsterdam Children's Book in the Netherlands Sparks Discrimination Controversy


News


Chinese Community Protest Triggers Official Apology

The City of Amsterdam distributed a commemorative children's book titled *My Birthday City* to elementary school students to celebrate the city's 750th anniversary. A board game section in the book read: "Hello (Ni Hao)! Chinese tourists are blocking the bike lane. To avoid them, go back 39 spaces." This passage, due to


reinforcing racial stereotypes, sparked strong protests from the Chinese community. Around 100 Chinese residents gathered in front of Amsterdam City Hall on June 5, 2025, arguing that the content spreads harmful ethnic bias to children. Following the protest, the mayor of Amsterdam issued a public apology, and the publisher promised to cover the original text with stickers and explain the editorial error. However, community representatives stated that the incident exposed.


the persistent problem of institutional bias against Asians and called for the establishment of more robust publishing review mechanisms and diverse editorial teams. a more comprehensive publication review mechanism and a diverse peer-review team.

Los Angeles Anti-ICE Protest Escalates, Xinhua Journalist Hit by Rubber Bullet


In early June, the US Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) launched large-scale enforcement operations targeting undocumented immigrants across Los Angeles County, triggering days of protest. On June 8 at noon, outside a federal law enforcement facility in downtown Los Angeles, protesters clashed violently with National Guard troops deployed at the scene. Police used tear gas, flashbangs, and rubber bullets to disperse the crowd.


Gao Shan, Xinhua News Agency's Los Angeles correspondent, reported live from the scene, stating that he was struck twice by non-lethal projectiles while filming. The accompanying cameraman was also hit in the leg, leaving visible signs of severe bleeding. Despite wearing clear identification equipment, the journalists were still caught in the use of force by law enforcement.

Female Intern Attacked on Campus at Indian Institute of Technology Madras

Raising Gender Safety Concerns


At 9:15 p.m. on June 25, a sexual assault case targeting a female intern occurred on the campus of Indian Institute of Technology Madras (IIT Madras). A 20-year-old female intern was attacked by Roshan Kumar, a contract cafeteria worker, while walking near an open-air theater on campus. The man threatened her with a wooden stick, pulled her hair, and molested her.


Though terrified, the victim promptly reported the incident. Campus security and Kotturpuram police launched an investigation, identified the suspect through surveillance footage, and arrested Roshan Kumar on June 28. Kumar was an employee at the "Mumbai Snacks" stall and had remained on campus despite taking a day off.


After the incident, IIT Madras issued a statement declaring zero tolerance for any form of sexual harassment and pledged to provide women with a safe and respectful learning and working environment. The victim's identity was not disclosed in accordance with rulings by India's Supreme Court.

Daniel Dae Kim Makes History with Tony Nomination

Breaking Barriers for Asian Representation in Mainstream Theater


On June 8, the 78th Tony Awards announced its nominations, with American actor Daniel Dae Kim nominated for "Best Actor in a Play" for his leading role in David Henry Hwang's adapted play *Yellow Face*, becoming the first Asian American nominated in the Tony Award category for Best Actor in a Play. The play premiered on Broadway in 2024, and Kim's nomination is seen as a significant milestone in the growing voice and cultural visibility of Asian actors on mainstream stages.

South Asian Muslim Zohran Mamdani

wins New York City primary election

Rising Asian political influence


On June 25, 33-year-old state representative Zohran Mamdani defeated several high-profile opponents in New York City's Democratic mayoral primary, becoming the first Asian American and Muslim candidate in New York's history with a viable chance of becoming mayor. Born in Uganda to Indian immigrant parents, he only became a U.S. citizen in 2018.


His victory has greatly inspired New York's South Asian and Muslim communities, with many young people saying they feel motivated to pay attention to and participate in politics. During his campaign, Mamdani focused on issues such as housing, transportation, and anti-discrimination, while actively building connections across different communities—such as posting videos in Hindi and visiting mosques—to demonstrate respect and belonging. This win is still seen as an important step forward for Asian communities in expanding their influence on the American political stage. .

Filipino American woman Gina Ortiz Jones

elected Mayor of San Antonio

making Asian American and LGBTQ+ history


On June 7, 2025, Filipino American Gina Ortiz Jones won the mayoral runoff election in San Antonio, Texas, defeating opponent Rolando Pablos with 54% of the vote, becoming the first Asian American and LGBTQ+ individual to serve as mayor in the city's history. The 44-year-old Jones previously served as U.S. Deputy Secretary of the Air Force in the Biden administration and had run for Congress twice before.


Her campaign faced numerous challenges, including a barrage of attack ads from conservative opponents, yet Jones consistently treated voters with "respect and dignity," emphasizing issues such as expanding preschool access, affordable housing, and workforce training. Her victory symbolizes the growing voice of marginalized communities in American local governance..


Jones's win has not only boosted Democratic morale in Texas but also demonstrated the increasing political influence of Asian American women in southern U.S. cities..
