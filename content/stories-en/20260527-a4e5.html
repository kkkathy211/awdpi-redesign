On May 5, 2026, to enhance the academic research, paper writing, and professional output capabilities of its members, the Legal Affairs Department of the international nonprofit AWDPI (Asian Women Development Plan International) launched AI-Era Academic Tools: Learning and Practical Skills online specialized training. The training was hosted by Attorney Xia Mengya, head of the department, with volunteer Zhou Shuxun as the keynote speaker, and all members participated in the learning session. By leveraging AI tools to empower professional research, the training aims to support efficient production of public-interest legal outcomes. At the beginning, Attorney Xia Mengya explained the core significance of the course. With the rapid development of AI technology, academic research and professional writing have entered an era of tool-enabled empowerment. Mastering intelligent tools can significantly improve the efficiency of literature retrieval, data organization, and paper writing.



Volunteer Zhou Shuxun delivered a systematic presentation on the theme of IMA Smart Workspace Hands-On Practice. She introduced in detail IMA's functional positioning, core modules, and operational logic, covering four major components: knowledge base construction, intelligent Q&A, document interpretation, and intelligent writing, providing step-by-step guidance on operation methods and frequently used techniques. She also demonstrated practical features such as literature import, AI summarization, cross-language translation, framework generation, and citation annotation within academic research contexts, helping members quickly get started and apply the skills immediately.


During the hands-on session, Attorney Xia Mengya, drawing from her experience publishing multiple academic papers, conducted a full-process practical teaching on legal paper writing. Using hot topics such as digital RMB and RWA as examples, she broke down the complete process from topic selection, literature screening, framework construction, content writing, to standardized citation, and demonstrated on-site how to use IMA to quickly extract core insights from literature, refine paper titles, and organize argumentative logic. She particularly emphasized that AI is an assistive tool, not a replacement for creation, and academic integrity and standards must be strictly upheld. AI should be used to enhance efficiency and optimize logic, not to directly generate content. Meanwhile, Attorney Xia Mengya shared frontline experiences on journal selection, submission communication, and publication for academic promotion, guiding members to avoid common pitfalls in publication and encouraging them to actively write and publish articles to strengthen both individual professional competence and the department's professional influence.



This training was practice-oriented and application-focused, deeply integrating AI tools with legal research and academic writing. The content was highly practical and actionable. Going forward, the AWDPI Legal Affairs Department will continue to conduct skill-building and professional empowerment trainings, promoting the routine use of AI tools in legal research, compliance management, and content production, and driving forward more robust and efficient legal support for the public-interest cause of protecting Asian women's rights through the dual engines of professional expertise and digital tools.
