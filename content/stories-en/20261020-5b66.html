From a young age, we are taught the standard answer to "hearing and being heard,"

defaulting to the presence or absence of sound to divide "normal" from "different."

But the ruler that defines "normal" should never come in just one measure.

Within lives that "cannot hear,"

there is also deep professional dedication, creative romance, and spiritual self-coherence,

vibrant lives that do not rely on an "inspirational narrative."


This time, Peking University Love Society Sign Language Branch × AWDPI jointly present the public welfare event themed "Voices Unsilenced • Echoes of Light"

We have invited 6 guests from different fields—

each bringing their life experiences and professional insights,

not selling sorrow, not glorifying suffering,

but simply asking with us:

If the world had never had sound to begin with,

what is normal, and who gets to define it?

Speaker Lineup


JUAN

Wang Juan

🔸Recipient of the Beijing March 8th Red Banner Medal

🔸Chairperson of Beijing Ai De Fen Bei Public Welfare Foundation

From a voice professional on CCTV screens to a deep contributor in the field of hearing loss public welfare. A group of people whose careers are built on sound have stepped into the silent world, building a bridge between sound and silence through professionalism and perseverance. In 2012, she co-founded "Ai De Fen Bei" with over 30 broadcasters and hosts, and over fourteen years has led the team from a single rescue project into a 5A-level foundation, serving over 40,000 hearing-impaired families.


YILIN

Zhu Yilin

🔸Torchbearer at the 2008 Beijing Paralympic Winter Games

🔸Chairperson of Xicheng District Deaf Persons Association, Beijing

🔸Advocate for information accessibility for the hearing-impaired community

True normality means every life state has the right to live equally. From learning to enter mainstream schools by feeling vibrations in the throat and reading lips, to undergoing two cochlear implant surgeries, and then advocating for information accessibility for the community—she does not want to be an inspirational role model, but simply fights for "the freedom to listen." Hearing aids and cochlear implants are never medals, but tools just like eyeglasses.


BUBU

Yu Yang (Bu Bu)

🔸Bilingual Deaf director & screenwriter 

🔸Founder of the "Hearing" series of interviews with Deaf community figures

🔸Finalist director in the 14th FIRST Youth Film Festival's pitching section

From Beijing to Lhasa, from monasteries to film sets. After her hearing completely faded, she began using words and lenses to document the Deaf community: the "Hearing" series recovers overlooked life stories, and works like *Zhuoga* and *Water* bring Deaf narratives to the silver screen. She firmly believes that interviews are a path. Deaf people should not merely be subjects filmed or explained by others, but should be the creators themselves.


GUIHUA

Wang Yiming (Gui Hua)

🔸Deaf theater actor

🔸Sign language advisor for the 2025 CCTV Spring Festival Gala accessible broadcast

🔸Promoter of diversity and inclusion

Not "giving voice to the Deaf," but creating together with Deaf and hearing people. On stage, she always takes the lead in the most challenging roles. From the dance "Hello, Stars" at the opening ceremony of the Beijing Paralympics, to China's first stage play co-created by Deaf and hearing performers, *Beyond the Sound of Water*, to the nationally unprecedented national science popularization drama *Ma Liang and the Magic Brush*, she has elevated sign language beyond an "auxiliary tool" into a complete and independent stage language. Off stage, she runs sign language classes and advocates for mutual understanding. Using theater as light and sign language as a bridge, she has walked a long path from self-expression to collective inclusion.


DENGKE

Gao Dengke

🔸Assistant Researcher, Institute of Literature, Chinese Academy of Social Sciences 

🔸Director of Academic Committee, Aesthetic Education Experimental Center, Tsinghua Academy of Arts and Sciences

Specializing in visual culture and aesthetic education research, he proposes that images form a complete and independent language system—deeply resonating with the essence of sign language. In his view, true accessibility goes beyond physical access; it requires cultural inclusion and aesthetic equality. Only by breaking the habit of "auditory centrism" can we achieve genuine cognitive integration.


YUZE

Qin Yuzhe (moderator of the roundtable)

🔸PhD in Art History, University of Paris

🔸International curator, documentary producer

🔸Head of Cooperation Development & Chair of International Culture and Education Development, Asian Women Development Plan International (AWDPI)

Exploring the expressive power of the silent world, discussing the future of diversity and inclusion. Using art as a bridge, she transcends boundaries between academia, curation, education, and public welfare. Specializing in cross-cultural communication and innovative aesthetic education, she believes there is never just one path to expression and perception.
