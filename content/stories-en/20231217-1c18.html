November 25 is the United Nations' International Day for the Elimination of Violence against Women, marking the start of the "16 Days of Activism" against Gender-Based Violence, which runs annually until December 10. The 16 Days of Activism aims to encourage people worldwide to take active steps to eliminate gender-based violence.


To date, violence against women and girls remains one of the most widespread human rights violations in the world. According to United Nations statistics, approximately 736 million women globally—nearly one-third of all women—have experienced gender-based violence at least once in their lives, including violence in the workplace and online spaces.


This year, AWDPI (Asian Women Development Plan International) hosted five online workshops during the 16 Days of Activism and relaunched the "Avoice" mobile mailbox, joining people across countries in showing support for domestic violence survivors. In the United Kingdom and Belgium, we held offline exhibitions against gender-based violence; in the United States, we conducted research and interviews on the living conditions of minority ethnic care workers.

16 Days of Activism Online Workshops


From November 25 to December 10, AWDPI hosted five high-quality gender empowerment workshops, reaching approximately 330 participants.

 

"Occupational Diseases through a Gender Lens"


The speaker explored the gendered burdens faced by women in pneumoconiosis-affected families and female pneumoconiosis patients themselves, starting from social gender roles, and discussed how the construction of "masculinity" impacts male patients and their families. The audience discussed the social construction of masculinity and how to rebuild more inclusive gender relations.

 

"Advancing Sustainable Development by Bridging the Gender Gap—The Story of the Galápagos Islands"


The speaker introduced anti-gender-based violence policies and NGO work in Ecuador's island province; the audience discussed how to integrate anti-gender-based violence content into education systems and how to engage men in preventing gender-based violence.

 

"Psychological and Social Factors in Domestic Violence"


The speaker discussed the psychological and social factors contributing to domestic violence, as well as follow-up interventions and treatments for both perpetrators and survivors, exchanging insights with the audience on related psychological traits and details of therapeutic approaches.

 

"Exploring Innovative Pathways for Girls' STEM Education in Underserved Areas"


The speaker presented the global disparity in access to STEM education for women, showcased the content and implementation strategy of the UNDP "She x Digital Future" project, and shared personal reflections with the audience on delivering STEM education to rural girls.

 

"How Sex Education Promotes Gender Equality"

The speaker discussed how sex education can break down gender discrimination and help people better express and receive. The audience discussed current developments in sex education and related topics.
