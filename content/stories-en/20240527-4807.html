As an Asian woman in the fields of public health, human services, and social welfare, have you ever felt that your identity made job hunting particularly challenging? Have you felt uncertain about your future career path? Would you like to hear advice from accomplished Asian women who have shared similar experiences? Do you want to have deeper conversations with them? Join us now!


The Chicago office of the Asian Women Development Plan International (AWDPI) will host a career-themed lecture, and we welcome all members majoring in social work, public policy, psychology, public health, and related fields. If you are interested in the human services sector, you are also welcome to join!


Here, outstanding Asian women leaders will share their experiences and insights in the field of human services. Through their stories—challenges they've faced, victories they've achieved—we will learn why they chose this career path and how they overcame obstacles.


Come network with accomplished Asian women leaders in the industry, gain valuable resources and experiences, and spark deeper reflections on your future career planning!


We firmly believe the contributions and energy of Asian women should transcend race and gender, bringing value, meaning, and inspiration to more people. Therefore, this event is open to all racial and gender identities—everyone is welcome!

★ Featured Speakers ★


*Note: Listed in alphabetical order



Lin Bian (Lin Bian)

Assistant Professor, Department of Psychology, University of Chicago


Dr. Lin Bian is an Assistant Professor in the Department of Psychology at the University of Chicago. She earned her bachelor's degree in psychology from Zhejiang University and her Ph.D. in developmental psychology from the University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign. Currently based at the University of Chicago, Dr. Bian conducts in-depth research on children's social cognitive development, exploring how children perceive social groups (such as gender and race), stereotypes, and moral concepts. Her research is highly influential, frequently published in top-tier journals, and has received numerous awards. Prior to joining the University of Chicago, she served as the Evalyn Edwards Milman Assistant Professor at Cornell University and completed postdoctoral research at Stanford University.




Lori Yi Long

Lotus Root Counseling (Lotus Root Counseling) Founder

Licensed Clinical Social Worker


Lori was born in Wuhan and raised in Guangzhou before moving to Chicago to pursue a Master of Social Work at the University of Chicago. Lori has also lived and studied in the Philippines and India. As a first-generation Asian immigrant, she is committed to serving marginalized communities, including Asian and bi/multicultural populations, immigrants, and LGBTQIA+ individuals. She has extensive experience working with individuals of diverse racial or ethnic backgrounds, sexual orientations, immigration statuses, gender expressions, and socioeconomic conditions. Her specialties include helping clients discover inner strength, build healthy relationships, and mediate cultural and intergenerational conflicts. Lori understands the challenges of expressing and navigating multiple cultural identities. While focusing on personal growth, she also considers cultural systems and unique individual experiences.




Shilin Liu (Shilin Liu)

Executive Director of Partnerships and Credential Programs (PCP), Harris School of Public Policy, University of Chicago


Shilin Liu currently serves as the Executive Director of Partnerships and Credential Programs (PCP) at the Harris School of Public Policy, University of Chicago. She holds a bachelor's degree in sociology from Fudan University, China, and a Master in Public Policy (MPP) from the Harris School of Public Policy. Shilin began her career as a general management trainee at Mars China (a global food company). She held various roles at Mars China, including in marketing, manufacturing, and human resources. Later, she moved to the United States to pursue a master's degree in public policy and gained professional experience as a consultant with organizations such as Save the Children and Education Pioneers. Shilin is dedicated to helping young professionals discover their true calling and create pathways for lifelong learning and self-development. In addition to her full-time role, she is an active career and life coach. She is a Certified Professional Co-Active Coach (CPCC) and an Associate Certified Coach (ACC).




Yejin Sohn

Licensed Clinical Social Worker

Ph.D. Student, University of Chicago


Yejin, from Seoul, South Korea, has worked with individuals affected by interpersonal violence for over a decade. Before pursuing her Ph.D. at the Crown Family School of Social Work at the University of Chicago, Yejin served as the coordinator and case manager for the Survivor Advocacy Program (SAP) at Ohio University, a program dedicated to "advocating for and providing confidential support to students who have experienced sexual assault, sexual harassment, dating/family violence, and stalking." Yejin is skilled in cross-cultural practice and has worked in diverse settings, including higher education institutions and community organizations in both South Korea and the United States. Throughout her career, she has contributed to multiple research teams and published several articles focusing on child abuse and sexual violence.
