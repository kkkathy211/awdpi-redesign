#Why do we blame ourselves?


Psychology describes a concept known as the "Just-World Hypothesis." People are inclined to believe that the world is fair, and that bad things do not happen randomly to good people. As a result, survivors may think:

"If I had done something differently, this wouldn't have happened."


This is a misplaced attribution of responsibility.

Many individuals experience a freeze response—being unable to speak or move in the moment. This is not weakness; it is a neurobiological survival response.

Scenario One: Public space + stranger → Confront directly, call it out loudly


Don't be polite, don't be indirect. Your first sentence should be a public, police-level accusation:


"He is sexually harassing me! I don't know him! There's sexual harassment happening here—please help record it, I'm calling the police right now!"


 Core idea Drag the perpetrator from the shadows into the light—onlookers will become your protection.


Scenario Two: Dinner party or drinking gathering + acquaintance → Immediately name it, break through the "just joking" disguise


The other person uses the atmosphere or alcohol as cover, betting you won't speak up. Your job is to break the unspoken rule:


"Are you making a sexual comment about me? Please note, your words constitute sexual harassment."


"Your behavior just now was sexual harassment—please show some self-respect."


  • If they say, "Just a joke, why so sensitive?" → "Do you think sexual harassment is a joke? I don't."

  • If they say, "You're overreacting" → "I wouldn't think that if you hadn't done it. If you don't want to be misunderstood, watch your behavior."

  • If they laugh it off and change the subject → Hold your ground. You've already shown you're not easy to mess with. If they don't stop, escalate: "I have a habit of recording. If you continue, I'm calling the police immediately."


"Anyone who can't show you basic respect will never give you anything good. Cutting your losses is wise."


Scenario Three: Private space + one-on-one → Priority one: leave, establish external connection


Don't focus first on what to say—assess safety immediately and act:

  • Pretend to take a call: "I'm with [name] right now (state name + location), and he knows he's being seen by a third party."

  • Move toward an exit or public area: "Excuse me, my friend is coming to pick me up."

  • After escaping, in a safe environment, use the second type of verbal strategy to clearly define the incident and warn of consequences.

#WhatIsSexualHarassment?


Deliberately pressing against or touching private areas on public transit; vulgar sexual jokes or innuendos; sending pornographic messages via social media; using power to pressure others in workplaces or schools; secretly filming, sharing private content, or maliciously photoshopping images online—these acts are recognized as sexual harassment worldwide.

"

"Any unwanted, sexually related conduct that violates your will constitutes unlawful behavior in most jurisdictions."

"


#What constitutes sexual harassment?


  • Unwanted physical contact in public spaces

  • Verbal sexual remarks or innuendo

  • Sending explicit content without consent

  • Abuse of power in workplaces or educational settings

  • Non-consensual recording, image manipulation, or distribution

These behaviors are widely recognized as legally actionable misconduct.

First layer: Civil protection against sexual harassment

  • Standard for determination: Your willingness is the only criterion. It doesn't matter if the other person thinks "it's fine"—if you feel uncomfortable, offended, or unwilling, it constitutes sexual harassment.

  • Organizations have a legal duty: government agencies, companies, and schools must take preventive measures, accept complaints, and conduct investigations. Ignoring reports or handling them negligently is itself illegal.

  • You can legally demand: cessation of harassment, public apology, elimination of negative impact, and compensation for damages. If severe psychological harm is caused, you may also claim compensation for mental distress.


Second layer: Forced indecency or insult (criminal offense)

Forcibly touching, molesting, or insulting others through violence, threats, drugging, or intimidation will result in imprisonment. Harsher penalties apply if committed in public, against multiple people, repeatedly, or targeting minors.


Third layer: Rape (the most serious legal boundary)

Rape committed against someone's will through violence, coercion, or other means results in long-term imprisonment. In severe cases or if causing serious injury or death, the penalty can be life imprisonment or even the death penalty.


Level 1: Civil Liability

  • Standard: Based on the victim's subjective experience of discomfort or violation

  • Employers and institutions have a legal duty to prevent and address harassment

  • Survivors may claim:

    • cessation of harm

    • formal apology

    • reputational restoration

    • financial compensation (including emotional distress damages)


Level 2: Criminal Offense — Indecent Assault / Coercion

  • Use of force, threats, intoxication, or coercion

  • More severe penalties in public settings, repeated offenses, or cases involving minors


Level 3: Rape (Severe Criminal Offense)

  • Non-consensual sexual acts involving force, coercion, or incapacitation

  • Subject to severe criminal penalties, including long-term imprisonment

Lawyer Xia's "Five-Step Rights Protection Method"

1. Clearly refuse: Say directly, "I don't consent—this is harassment, stop immediately." Your refusal is the most critical legal fact.


2. Prioritize safety: Leave immediately if possible, go to a place with people or surveillance. Your life and safety come first.


3. Preserve evidence immediately: Do not delete or alter anything—save chat logs, voice messages, images, videos; note the time, location, and camera positions; seek medical help immediately if injured, and keep medical records and photos of injuries.


4. Assert your rights immediately: Report criminal acts directly to the police; for workplace or campus harassment, file a complaint with the institution and contact women's federations or legal aid organizations; for civil claims, apply for legal aid.


5. Don't fear it's been too long: The statute of limitations for criminal cases is relatively long. The earlier you report, the stronger your evidence—but it's never too late to seek justice. **Justice may be delayed, but it will never be absent.

Debunking the "perfect victim" myth


Many women worry after being violated: "I'm not a perfect victim—did I somehow allow it?"


This is a fundamental attribution error. The pursuit of a "perfect victim" makes people ignore objective factors like power imbalances and the perpetrator's intent, leading to victim-blaming.


Your reactions under extreme threat—freezing, trembling, insomnia, panic—


are normal human responses under threat, not signs that something is wrong with you.
