Caring about social issues is where change begins.


But moving from "I really care" to "What can I do?" is often not easy.


Many young women care about educational equity, gender equality, community development, and public policy, yet may not know where to start due to lack of experience, resources, formal positions, or institutional support. They might ask:

  • Can you be a leader without a formal title?

  • Can one person's idea really bring change to a community?

  • How can actions initiated by young people further influence public policy and institutions?


On August 15, Asian Women Development Plan International (AWDPI) will invite education advocate, researcher, and founder of Raízes Startup, Bianca Galves Gombos, to host an online dialogue on "Young Women and Civic Leadership."


Drawing from Bianca's personal experiences and practices, this event will explore how young women can transform their concern for social issues into concrete actions, create real impact through youth-led community projects, and engage in public life, public policy, and social change even without formal authority or institutional resources.



Guest Introduction






Bianca Galves Gombos She is an education advocate, researcher, and social entrepreneur who has long focused on the intersection of educational equity, youth participation, and systemic reform.


Her research examines how unequal access to educational resources affects democratic participation and social mobility, and how public policies and government programs can respond to these challenges. She is committed to transforming research, policy analysis, and project management into community-centered actions.


Raízes Startup




Bianca is the founder of Raízes Startup, a youth-led nonprofit organization.


The organization provides tools, social networks, and leadership support to help young people design and implement community projects focused on educational opportunity and civic engagement, enabling youth not only to express concern about social issues but also to actively participate in solving them.


She has spoken at the ECOSOC Youth Forum of the United Nations Economic and Social Council and shared insights at the Brookings Institution's "Global Workshop on Collaborative Research and Action for Education Systems Transformation."


Bianca is also an alumna of the Latin America Leadership Academy and of Centre College.



We will discuss together:



(1) From Concern to Action




When facing social issues you care about, where can young women begin? How can vague concern be turned into specific, actionable first steps?

(2) Leadership Without a Title




When we lack formal positions, institutional support, or public influence, how can we build confidence, express our views, and lead others to participate together?

(3) Community Engagement and Sustainable Change




Why is genuine, lasting social change impossible without community participation? How can youth projects understand local needs, build trust, and avoid remaining at a short-term level?

(4) From Community Action to Public Policy




How can the experience and evidence gathered from community projects enter public discourse? In what ways can young women participate in policy-making, institutional reform, and civic life?

(5) Skills Needed for Civic Leadership




Communication, listening, research, organizing, project management, resource mobilization, and cross-sector collaboration—what skills help young women drive community action more effectively?

(6) Taking Action with Limited Resources




Can community projects still be initiated without experience, funding, connections, or a team? How can one start from local problems, existing resources, and small-scale practices?


Why do we need more young women participating in civic life?

(7) Civic leadership does not belong only to those with formal titles.




It can begin with truly listening to community needs, launching a small youth-led project, conducting sustained research on a public issue, or connecting different people to jointly seek solutions.



Young women possess their own lived experiences, community observations, and issue analysis. They are not merely recipients of policies and social services, but should also be initiators of public issues, organizers of community actions, and participants in institutional change.


Yet in reality, young women's voices may still be constrained by factors such as age, gender, resources, and social identity. They may be seen as "lacking experience," lack access to public institutions, or even before taking action, doubt their own abilities due to fear of failure.


Therefore, promoting young women's participation in civic life involves more than encouraging them to "speak bravely"—it also means equipping them with knowledge, tools, networks, and real opportunities to turn concern into action and community practice into social impact.



Significance of the Event



Leadership does not necessarily start with a title—it can also start with a sense of responsibility;


Social action does not require abundant resources—it can begin with one specific issue and one small step;


Community engagement is not just about serving others—it is also a crucial process for understanding public issues, gathering evidence, and driving policy change.


Whether you are a student, young professional, community volunteer, educator, or someone wondering how to take action on issues you care about, we sincerely invite you to join this dialogue.
