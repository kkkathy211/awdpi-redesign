Everyone is an artist of life





I eat my meal in silence

calling out "Xiaowu, Xiaowu" and tossing some meat to it

It wags its tail, barking joyfully

When he grabs my hair and slams me against the wall

Xiaowu keeps wagging its tail

For someone who fears no pain, he is powerless


 ——Yu Xiuhua, "My Dog Is Called Xiaowu"


Through plain yet powerfully montage-like language, Yu Xiuhua projects scenes of domestic violence experienced by the poem's narrator like cinematic frames.


Sadly, the suffering of the "I" in the poem is not unique, but a microcosm of countless victims who have endured or are enduring gender-based violence.


Discussions on women's rights protection have never ceased, transcending forms of text and language, breaking through expressive limitations, aiming only to spark deep reflection on feminist issues.




On the evening of November 18, VIVA co-hosted with Avoice the online workshop "Born Rebellious, I Define the Rules: Women's Legal Rights" successfully launched..


This sharing session was honored to invite Teacher Zhao Tiansong and Senior student Zhang Yuyan, two professional guests engaged in research and work in the public welfare fields of anti-domestic violence and women's rights protection, to explore with participants the future development path of women in public affairs.





01

Guest Sharing Content


Teacher Zhao Tiansong focused on the theme of "conflict and breakthrough" for women in public spaces.


He analyzed the "conflicts" women may face in society from three aspects: sources (Cultural perception differences, negative interactions), occurrence (family, public spaces, emergencies), and response (self-help, seeking help from formal/informal organizations).


But where does breakthrough come from?


Traditional beliefs such as "women's opinions are trivial" and "men work outside, women manage inside" are rule frameworks set for women by nature, society, and family. Hidden behind traditional moral teachings, they bring real frustration to women caught within. This is precisely why women's path to breakthrough must begin here.



At the end of his talk, Teacher Zhao summarized the goals and degrees of "breakthrough":


"One goal is called comfort...... The second is called development— does this breakthrough benefit the future development of our group?"


"From conflict to breakthrough" embodies the journey of women as both individuals and a collective, moving from "breaking" to "building" when confronting constraints and discrimination within the vast network of public relations..



If Teacher Zhao summarized the path of women's "conflict to breakthrough" from a purely theoretical perspective, then Senior Student Zhang Yuyan illustrated real-life examples from her work experience in the Singapore team of Avoice's Legal Research Division, showcasing her unique path of breakthrough..


In this session, she introduced in detail the historical development of Singapore's anti-domestic violence laws and educated participants about the Avoice Singapore Anti-Domestic Violence Legal Handbook.


compiled by Avoice. This handbook covers 16 topics including the definition of domestic violence, civil and criminal liabilities, civil and criminal litigation, marriage, divorce, and social services and support beyond legislation. It is currently in the manuscript revision stage.



Since Singapore's legal system is based on the English common law tradition, with primary legal scope determined by precedent, the legal provisions related to anti-domestic violence have not formed a complete system and remain relatively fragmented.


Therefore, one of Avoice's goals in compiling the anti-domestic violence legal handbook is to "establish a comprehensive database of legal and related assistance resources" for victims of domestic violence..


With this relatively comprehensive database, victims can directly search for legal statutes and case precedents to better use legal tools to protect their legitimate rights.




Breaking through conflict is a fearless act, yet the question of "where should Nora go after leaving home" has never stopped being debated, directly pointing to the difficulty of the "building" phase after "breaking."


Zhang Yuyan's work at Avoice offers us one such perspective.


We hope that when a domestic violence victim finally gathers the courage to fight back, she will realize she is not alone—comprehensive anti-domestic violence legal provisions from the government, and official and grassroots rescue organizations ready to help are her strongest support.






02

"Painting Life" Co-Creation Segment


As the event neared its end, participants engaged in a fun online "Painting Life" co-creation session.


At the center of the canvas was a map of Singapore, overlaid in the sky above by a purple ribbon symbolizing "against violence," an initial artwork created by Senior Student Zhang Yuyan based on the theme of the session.



Participants then used this as a starting point for bold and free drawing. The brush is emotionless, but the hand holding it is full of feeling; stroke by stroke, "painting life," "painting" women's strength.



When the last participant lifted her brush, the online workshop officially concluded. Yet the enthusiasm and active sharing among participants did not end.


Everyone hoped the host would share the recorded session in the community, and they actively joined discussions in the group, sharing the meanings behind their own artworks.:


The sun I drew: each of our lives is like the sun, giving ourselves energy and warmth to others, letting us warm each other and care for one another.

               ------ Teacher Zhao Tiansong

I drew a ☯ symbol, corresponding to the guest speaker's theme of conflict and breakthrough—how to reconcile contradictions; and several small icons representing women raising their voices.

        ------ HYT

The dog I drew—because I think dogs are healing. They are always honest, optimistic, full of vitality and hope.

                                             ------ Muzhiyi





03

Epilogue


After the event, a VIVA member refined the collaborative artwork completed by participants and sent the enhanced version to the community as a gift to the two speakers and over thirty participants.


The original artwork was created spontaneously by participants, with every image carrying their thoughts and blessings.


The path ahead for women's "breakthrough and reconstruction" is full of unknowns and challenges, yet the spirit of optimism and beautiful hopes for the future shown in the original painting will not be erased.



In China, the Law on the Protection of Women's Rights and Interests of the People's Republic of China was passed by the National People's Congress on April 3, 1992, and has been in effect ever since; the international community has also never stopped advocating for women's rights.


As women's rights advocate Melinda Gates stated: "Being a feminist means believing that every woman should be able to use her voice and pursue her potential, and that women and men should work together to remove barriers and end the biases that still hold women back."


In this refined artwork, we amplify the fearless optimism it conveys, hoping to inspire more partners to join us in building a gender-friendly society for the next generation of women.


We hope that when Nora leaves home and breaks through gender constraints, what awaits her is companions fighting alongside her and a social environment that lifts her toward a broader sky..
