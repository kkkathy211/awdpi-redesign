On the path of protecting women, there are always many people quietly giving of themselves. Some have once been deeply trapped in hardship, and thus are more eager to reach out to those in similar distress; others, though never having experienced it personally, can still empathize, hoping to melt the ice of violence with tiny sparks and help society hear women's voices and demands.


For the same aspirations and ideals, countless people from around the world with diverse backgrounds have joined AWDPI, hoping that even their faint light can illuminate the way forward for those in darkness. Here, they have gained unforgettable practical experiences and thrived in a diverse and inclusive environment, enhancing their personal abilities through repeated challenges and expanding the boundaries of their thinking through constant exchange. Let us continue to get closer to these humble yet great volunteers and listen to their stories with AWDPI.

✦




Support and Rescue Department – Lin Qi

As a social work student, Lin Qi has always paid close attention to gender equality and hopes to use her skills to help women in need. Introduced by a senior in the same major, she applied and joined AWDPI, serving in multiple roles including case liaison officer, head of the case liaison team, case supervision member, and daily operator of the assistant team.


Since joining AWDPI, she believes her greatest reward has been seeing clients gradually improve with their support—this process has given her tremendous strength. The experience that left the deepest impression on Lin Qi was witnessing a client, after recovering with their help, decide to join the project and use her professional skills to support others. This further strengthened Lin Qi's sense of mission and responsibility in volunteer work. She also believes that everyone can become a messenger of love. Through these activities, she has come to appreciate even more the significant meaning and value of AWDPI and other women's protection organizations in society. As volunteers, they not only provide practical assistance but also spread positive energy through their actions, raising broader social awareness of women's rights issues.


At the same time, Lin Qi has met many like-minded partners at AWDPI—people who, like her, are passionate about women's protection and work together to support women in crisis. This sense of unity and collaboration makes her feel less alone and fills her with hope and anticipation for the future of women's protection organizations and gender equality. Lin Qi hopes these organizations can continue their efforts to support women in distress, and she deeply wishes to see society pay greater attention to women's rights, with the public actively participating in efforts to protect women's rights, jointly building a fairer and more inclusive society through collective action.


"I hope everyone can work for women's rights protection with a sincere heart." Lin Qi says this—and lives by it. "Even if it's just a tiny glimmer, when gathered together, it can light up the path ahead. Let us join hands and contribute our strength to the cause of women's rights protection!"



Support and Rescue Department – Ma Mengyuan

Ma Mengyuan is currently studying social work at China Women's College. Being in a women's college, where over 90% of the students are female, gender equality and anti-gender violence activities are frequently held. Influenced by her school and major, Ma Mengyuan joined AWDPI as a case liaison officer since her freshman year and recently joined the case supervision team. She has completed five cases, all successfully closed, and one case supervision has also been successfully concluded.


Throughout the process, Ma Mengyuan encountered many challenges—for example, time differences often caused delays in communication and coordination. But these challenges helped her further improve her communication skills and work capacity. The moments closest to case closure are the most memorable and fulfilling for her. Though their work may not fully resolve a client's problems, they can provide sexual health knowledge and, more importantly, give the client a sense that "someone is helping me; I'm not alone." At the end of a case, the client may even shift from crisis to a calmer transitional phase, beginning to reflect on how to move forward in life. Positive feedback collected during follow-ups has given her strong encouragement and a deep sense of achievement.


For Ma Mengyuan, AWDPI has been a crucial practical experience—it is the only organization she has been involved with since the beginning of her university life, one that has continuously accompanied and grown with her. This experience means far more to her than textbook knowledge gained in class. She believes AWDPI brings people's strengths together, allowing each volunteer to contribute their unique advantages. Those with ability and available time invest themselves in the organization, pooling their strength to take action, create change, and ultimately achieve a 1+1>2 effect.


Looking ahead, Ma Mengyuan has many hopes for women's protection organizations and gender rights advocacy. "First is gender equality." She notes that for many people today, gender equality remains just a slogan or an awareness, but she hopes these slogans and awareness can be put into practice. "Whether it's protecting women workers' rights or improving the inequalities women face in the workplace and daily life, we can all try to speak up, identify issues, and drive change."



Support and Rescue Department – Yang Qinwen

Systemic gender-based violence against women has been an issue Yang Qinwen has studied and cared about since her undergraduate years, and she has constantly thought about how to use her own strength to combat gender violence. In the summer of 2022, she saw AWDPI's recruitment announcement and seized the opportunity to join the Legal Support Team. She currently serves as both a member and head of the Legal Support Team, primarily responsible for providing legal support to women seeking help and managing internal case assignments.


Among many tasks, the case that left the deepest impression on Yang Qinwen was her first Dutch case. There was an urgent situation requiring immediate police contact, and as it was her first time handling such a case, she felt anxious and unsure how to communicate with the client. Fortunately, the local police responded quickly, and she swiftly adjusted her mindset, providing emotional support to the client while contacting the police, successfully helping the client escape danger in time. In subsequent case support, Yang Qinwen often recalls this experience and draws on the lessons learned. Through repeated volunteer activities, she has also gained a clearer understanding of the limits of support that rescue organizations can offer to survivors of violence. In her view, AWDPI and other women's protection organizations are essential in eliminating discrimination and violence and creating a diverse and equal society, bringing volunteers together through such organizations and platforms to jointly resist structural oppression.


In the future, Yang Qinwen hopes to see more multidimensional support that enables more people to stay involved and participate in gender-related public welfare. "People working on gender-related public welfare will inevitably feel the difficulty of breaking through structural problems from time to time, and their hopes for equality and a violence-free world may even fade. But what matters is that we are injecting energy and leaving behind traces of our efforts."



Program Quality Department – Qian Siyu

Qian Siyu is currently a senior at Arizona State University in the United States, majoring in Public Policy and Public Service. She has joined AWDPI's Academic Research Team, responsible for writing regular and special reports. Her first regular report focused on child marriage among Vietnamese women; later, she conducted research on Cambodian women, all centering on women in Southeast Asia. Last year, she began focusing on special reports in Europe, such as reports on the workplace experiences of Chinese diaspora women in Belgium and the UK, and most recently, a report on the living conditions of minority ethnic caregivers in the United States.


In Qian Siyu's view, women's protection organizations like AWDPI act as a bond, bringing together people who care about women's issues and gender equality, and under scientific and efficient organizational management, ultimately amplifying greater impact. "I noticed that the organization was originally called Avoice, mainly focusing on anti-domestic violence, but has now been renamed AWDPI—this reflects the expansion of its influence." Beyond that, Qian Siyu sees deeper significance. "I think anti-domestic violence is a basic response to women's fundamental need for personal safety, whereas AWDPI now responds to more comprehensive needs, such as women's workplace environments. This represents a leap in psychological needs—moving up the hierarchy in Maslow's theory of needs. It's also a reflection of the organization's deepening impact and growing influence."


In the future, she hopes to see these organizations gain even greater influence, truly enabling policymakers to recognize existing problems and even influencing the formulation or implementation of public policies.


As a volunteer, Qian Siyu believes courage is essential. Volunteers are not only practitioners but also advocates. If a volunteer truly feels the pain of women in need, they should never hesitate or worry about negative social opinions—instead, they must turn that empathy into concrete, on-the-ground action and raise their voice as they wish to be heard. "Girls can do anything, and can do everything. Women can exert their strength in any setting—never let yourself be confined by stereotypes." Only in this way can more women be genuinely influenced and empowered in various meaningful ways.



Project Quality Department, Zhou Xuan

When Zhou Xuan first joined AWDPI, she was a member of the Partnerships Development Department, responsible for business development. Later, she became the head of the Monitoring and Evaluation Unit.


In 2023, she primarily served in the Monitoring and Evaluation Unit. Over the past year, the entire organization underwent significant structural adjustments and personnel reforms. As a department head, her main responsibility was to assist in policy implementation. She and her team improved the volunteer management and incentive systems, including establishing comprehensive policies, conducting training for department heads, and ensuring implementation. Additionally, her department collaborated with departments facing challenges, helping them establish new work systems and operational manuals. Later, she also advanced the development of a volunteer service agreement.


Although Zhou Xuan does not belong to a frontline service department, she can support the organization's positive development from behind the scenes and promote the implementation of aid activities. This makes her feel deeply fulfilled and proud. At the same time, thanks to her position, she can observe the organization and developments across different countries from a strategic perspective, and she has come to realize that NGOs face many limitations and regulations—there is still a long way to go for NGO development. "The foundation for NGO development in China is very weak. There are many social problems, but the government may not be able to directly address many of them. As a result, supply in society is far below demand." Therefore, she greatly looks forward to the growth and strengthening of women's rights organizations that are not directly controlled by the government, hoping that in the future, these nonprofit organizations can do more, fill market gaps, and solve more social problems.


At the same time, Zhou Xuan also holds great hope for the awakening of women's consciousness in the information age. She has noticed that in recent years, due to the popularity of social media and the breaking down of information barriers, most of the women around her have gradually begun to understand the various forms of hidden discrimination women face under patriarchal society, and how women should normally advocate for equal rights. She hopes this awakening of awareness can gradually drive changes in social systems and correct social norms.


Xuan Yuchen, Head of the Project Quality Department, highly praised Zhou Xuan's work: "Head of the Monitoring and Evaluation Unit, K-pop star of Xishuangbanna, and the sunshine figure of the Project Quality Department. With great enthusiasm and responsibility, she moves across departments, actively fulfilling the role of the Monitoring and Evaluation Unit. From bi-monthly reports to annual reports, from volunteer incentives to resolving departmental issues, she is busy in every corner of the organization."



UK National Office, Chen Xuanyue

Chen Xuanyue first learned about the AWDPI organization through a socially responsible individual and joined the organization with the intention of broadening her experience. Currently, she serves as Deputy Director of the UK Partnerships Development Department and mainly participated in communication and collaboration activities in 2023, including the anti-violence art exhibition.


The moment that gives Chen Xuanyue the greatest sense of achievement is seeing the regular AWDPI monthly reports or summaries. She feels she hasn't been with the organization for very long and hasn't made many significant contributions yet, but whenever she sees the entire organization gradually thriving and delivering help to those in need, she feels deeply proud. In her view, AWDPI and other women's protection organizations not only help women in need through action, but more importantly, they transmit feminist values, letting countless overseas women know that there is a place they can turn to when facing difficulties. "I hope all women can grow strong and shine brightly in every field, unafraid to show their talents, free from being brainwashed by stereotypes, brave in trying, confident in their abilities, forever self-assured, and receiving the rewards they truly deserve."


In addition, Chen Xuanyue has met many like-minded friends within the organization. Although they come from different backgrounds, they all share noble ideals and lofty aspirations, each striving toward the same goal from their own perspectives and in their own ways. When countless individuals can join such nonprofit organizations and contribute their part, the day of achieving equality will surely come sooner.



UK National Office, Yuan Sihao

Yuan Sihao first encountered AWDPI through a social media post shared by a friend. Having personally experienced a case—being an indirect victim of domestic violence—he hopes to do his part in calling for greater societal care and protection for women. Currently, Yuan Sihao serves as Secretary Assistant at the UK National Office and is a member of the Language Support Unit. His involvement includes coordinating guest arrangements for the 2023 AWDPI Asian Women Development Forum, daily office operations, organizing meetings, translating the interview transcript of Ewelina, founder of the charity HSTAR, and translating the interview with the director of the UK women's aid organization Shakti Women's Aid.


As a man, Yuan Sihao has his own understanding of anti-domestic violence. He believes that in cases of domestic violence, most perpetrators are men. Therefore, the awareness of protecting women should be more actively instilled in men. While women's self-protection is important, the root cause must ultimately be addressed—by stopping the perpetrators. If men lack this awareness and fail to actively participate in protecting women, combating domestic violence will be difficult to achieve. "There are still too few men working in women's protection today. To truly protect women from the threat of domestic violence and ultimately achieve gender equality, the involvement of men is indispensable." Therefore, he believes the urgent task is to raise awareness of gender equality among men in society, helping more and more men understand the seriousness of the issue, so they can spontaneously protect and respect women, and ultimately promote the advancement of women's status.


The Head of the UK General Affairs Department gave Yuan Sihao high praise: "Yuan Sihao has performed exceptionally well in the Avoice Overseas Chinese Women Protection Project General Affairs Department, and we are all deeply grateful. In the General Office, he is like the backbone of the department. Whether handling initial HR tasks or later assisting in organizing welcome events and various other activities, Sihao has consistently demonstrated exceptional professionalism and diligence. His work is not only completed thoroughly, but always delivered efficiently and with high quality, setting an excellent example for the department. We hope everyone can learn from Sihao and work together to elevate our project to new heights."


Yuan Sihao looks forward to more male participation in the future in the action to protect women's rights. "The ultimate goal of women's rights is to achieve gender equality. True success will come when society no longer needs to deliberately emphasize the word 'women.'"



Maintaining passion, staying true to their hearts, continuously learning, and persistently contributing their part, they bring help to women in distress, use words to amplify women's voices, and gather countless sparks into a brilliant galaxy, shining with warmth and light.
