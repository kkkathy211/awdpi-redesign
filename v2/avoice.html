A large group with no one looking out for them

Around the world, one in three women has experienced domestic violence, according to the World Health Organization.

No study had counted how many overseas Chinese women face it. Avoice was set up to fill that gap: to help them, and to help them grow from victims into advocates for gender equality.

Why they rarely get help

Five problems we found in our first years of casework.

1

Relief systems don't fit

Local help often can't give them money and safety fast enough.

2

Official channels feel out of reach

Little knowledge of local law and migrant anxiety keep them from the police.

3

Few people to turn to

Language, culture and nationality make outside support hard to find.

4

Little self-protection

Vague ideas of what abuse is and old gender norms weaken self-rescue.

5

Too little attention

No in-depth study or full intervention program existed for them.

Six kinds of support, one team around her

Behind every case, several teams work as one, so she only has to tell her story once.

1Language

In her own language

English, French, German, Russian, Dutch, Czech, Portuguese, Italian and Spanish, plus online sign language. We translate, interpret and help gather documents.

9languages, plus sign language
2Legal

Knowing her rights

Anti-violence laws translated into Chinese, case analysis and advice, and contacts for local lawyers.

7countries covered by our legal handbooks
3Psychological

Room to heal

Counselling tailored to each woman, and courses on mental health.

4Community

Never on her own

A mutual-aid community, and online learning on prevention, self-protection and the law.

5Network

The right door, sooner

A database of shelters and medical, legal, psychological and financial aid by country, with referrals.

700+aid organizations in our database
6Interview and writing

Her story, in her words

We listen to survivors, record their experience and share what helped them get free.

From a first message to lasting support

Five steps, entirely on WeChat and in Chinese, from the first hello to ongoing care.

  1. 1

    Find us on WeChat

    Search Avoice-WomenAid or scan our QR code.

  2. 2

    Tell us where you are

    Add a note with your country or region and a name. A nickname is fine.

  3. 3

    Meet your two volunteers

    We open a small WeChat group: just you and two volunteers.

  4. 4

    Plan the way forward

    Your case lead works out short- and long-term help with you.

  5. 5

    We stay beside you

    We follow up, keep notes and connect you with local services.
