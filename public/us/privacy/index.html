Privacy notice

This privacy notice explains how AWDPI United States collects, uses and protects personal information when you ask us for support, contact us, donate, volunteer with us or work with us.

Who we are

This notice is issued by ASIAN WOMEN DEVELOPMENT PLAN INTERNATIONAL US INC, a California nonprofit corporation recognised by the IRS as tax-exempt under Section 501(c)(3), known as AWDPI United States.

  • Address: 1060 Lincoln Ave Suite 20 1127, San Jose, CA 95125
  • EIN: 93-2511417
  • Email: us@awdpi.org
  • Phone: +1 404 310 6818

AWDPI United States is an independent organisation within the AWDPI international network. AWDPI United Kingdom, AWDPI Belgium and AWDPI Australia are separate organisations. Each is responsible for the personal information it handles and has its own privacy notice.

The information we collect

What we collect depends on how you deal with us.

  • If you ask us for support: the name or nickname you give us, your contact details (for example your WeChat ID, WhatsApp number or email address), the state and city where you live, and what you choose to tell us about your situation and needs. This can include sensitive information, such as your health, your immigration status, or violence you have experienced.
  • If you donate: your name, contact details, the amount and date of your gift, and what we need to send you a receipt.
  • If you contact us or work with us as a partner: your name, your organisation, your contact details and the content of your messages.
  • If you volunteer with us: your name, contact details, languages, skills and availability, and records of the work you do with us.
  • If you visit awdpi.org: see Our website below.

You do not need to give us your real name to get support. You can use a nickname, and you decide what you tell us.

How we use it

  • To give you the support you ask for, such as language support, legal information, emotional support, referrals to shelters, legal services or other support, and follow-up.
  • To protect someone's life or physical safety in an emergency.
  • To share your story, for example in an interview published in our Stories section, only if you agree, and only in the form you agree to.
  • To process donations, send receipts and keep the records that US tax law requires.
  • To answer enquiries, work with partner organisations and organise volunteering.
  • To produce research and statistics on gender-based violence, using only information that no longer identifies anyone.

Who we share it with

We do not sell personal information, and we do not share it for advertising.

We keep what you tell us about your situation confidential. We only share it with others with your agreement, except where the law requires us to (for example, to report suspected abuse of a child) or where it is needed to protect someone's life.

  • Our team. The staff and volunteers handling your request see only what they need, and they must keep it confidential.
  • Support organisations, such as shelters, lawyers, counsellors or interpreters, only with your agreement.
  • Other AWDPI organisations, only when this is needed to help you (for example if you move to another country) and with your agreement.
  • Service providers that handle information for us, such as our email and document storage provider, payment providers and the services described under Our website. They may only use the information to provide their service to us.
  • Authorities, such as the police or courts, only where the law requires it or to protect someone's life.

When we accept donations online, card details are handled by the payment provider. We do not receive or store your full card number.

Messaging apps

Many people contact us through WeChat or WhatsApp. These apps are run by Tencent and Meta under their own privacy policies, and these companies may process your information outside the United States. If you prefer, you can contact us by email or phone instead. If someone else may look at your phone, consider deleting your conversations with us after reading them.

Our website

  • awdpi.org does not use analytics or advertising cookies, and it does not track you across other websites. Because of this, we do not respond differently to "Do Not Track" signals.
  • Pages on awdpi.org load fonts from Google Fonts. To do this, your browser connects to Google's servers, which receive your IP address.
  • A few older articles in our Stories section were first published on WeChat and load images or videos from Tencent's servers.

How long we keep it

We keep personal information only as long as we need it:

  • Support records: while we are supporting you, and then for up to 3 years after your case is closed, after which we delete or anonymise them. You can ask us to delete them sooner.
  • Donation records: as long as US tax and accounting rules require.
  • Enquiries and partner correspondence: up to 2 years after our last contact.
  • Volunteer records: while you volunteer with us and up to 2 years afterwards.

Your choices

You can ask us to:

  • tell you what personal information we hold about you and give you a copy;
  • correct it if it is wrong;
  • delete it, unless the law requires us to keep it;
  • stop contacting you.

Write to us@awdpi.org. We reply within 30 days. If we need to confirm who you are, we will do it in a way that keeps you safe. Depending on the state where you live, you may have further rights under state law.

Children

Our services are for adults. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children under 13 through our website. If you are under 18 and need help, please tell us and we will help you find services for young people.

How we protect it

We limit access to personal information to the people who need it, keep case information confidential, and take reasonable technical and organisational measures to protect it against loss and misuse.

Your safety

If you are in immediate danger, call 911.

You can also call the National Domestic Violence Hotline at 1-800-799-7233, or text START to 88788, at any time.

The Quick exit button at the top of every page takes you straight to Google, and so does pressing Esc twice. Leaving a page this way does not erase it from your browser history, so if someone else uses your device, consider using a private window or clearing your history.
